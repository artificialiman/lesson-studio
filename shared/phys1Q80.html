AFundamental & Derived Quantities and Units

Question 1

Which of the following is NOT a fundamental quantity?

Solution

The seven fundamental quantities are length, mass, time, electric current, thermodynamic temperature, amount of substance, and luminous intensity.

Force is derived from mass × acceleration, so it is not fundamental.

Answer: Force.

Question 2

The SI unit of luminous intensity is:

Solution

Length → metre. Mass → kilogram. Time → second. Electric current → ampere. Temperature → kelvin. Amount of substance → mole. Luminous intensity → candela.

Answer: Candela (cd).

Question 3

Express the unit of force in SI base units.

Solution 
F = ma
[m] = kg
[a] = m/s²
[F] = kg × m s⁻² = kg m s⁻²

Answer: kg m s⁻² (the newton, N).

Question 4

The dimensional formula for power is:

Solution 
Power = Work / time
[Work] = [M L² T⁻²]
[time] = [T]
[Power] = [M L² T⁻³]

Answer: [M L² T⁻³].

Question 5

Which of the following pairs has the same dimensions?

Solution 
Work:       [M L² T⁻²]
Energy:     [M L² T⁻²]
Force:      [M L T⁻²]
Momentum:   [M L T⁻¹]
Impulse:    [M L T⁻¹]
Pressure:   [M L⁻¹ T⁻²]

Work and energy have the same dimensions. So do momentum and impulse.

Answer: Work and energy (or momentum and impulse).

Question 6

Convert 3.5 g/cm³ to kg/m³.

Solution 
1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³
3.5 g/cm³ = 3.5 × 1000 = 3500 kg/m³

Answer: 3500 kg/m³.

Question 7

Which of the following is a derived quantity?

Solution

Length, mass and time are fundamental. Volume is length × length × length, so it is derived.

Answer: Volume.

Question 8

The dimension of acceleration is:

Solution 
Acceleration = velocity / time
[velocity] = [L T⁻¹]
[acceleration] = [L T⁻¹] / [T] = [L T⁻²]

Answer: [L T⁻²].

Question 9

If x = aⁿ, and a has a fractional error of 0.02, what is the fractional error in x when n = 4?

Solution 
Δx/x = n × (Δa/a)
     = 4 × 0.02 = 0.08

Answer: 0.08 (i.e. 8%).

Question 10

Which of the following is dimensionless?

Solution 
Strain = change in length / original length = [L]/[L] = dimensionless
Refractive index, relative density, angle (in radians) are also dimensionless.

Answer: Strain (also refractive index, relative density).

Question 11

Which of these is a correct dimensional equation?

Solution 
v = u + at
[L T⁻¹] = [L T⁻¹] + [L T⁻²][T]
        = [L T⁻¹] + [L T⁻¹]  ✓

Answer: v = u + at is dimensionally correct.

Question 12

A quantity has dimensions [M L² T⁻²]. Which physical quantity is it?

Solution 
[M L² T⁻²] = kg m² s⁻² = joule
This is the dimension of work or energy.

Answer: Energy (or work).

BMeasurement of Mass, Weight, Length and Time

Question 13

The instrument best suited for measuring the internal diameter of a narrow tube is:

Solution

A metre rule cannot reach inside a narrow tube. A vernier caliper can grip the inside diameter to 0.1 mm, and a micrometer screw gauge can measure external diameters but usually not internal ones.

Answer: Vernier caliper.

Question 14

A micrometer screw gauge has a zero error of −0.03 mm. A reading gives 4.56 mm. What is the corrected reading?

Solution 
Corrected = observed − zero error
          = 4.56 − (−0.03)
          = 4.56 + 0.03 = 4.59 mm

Answer: 4.59 mm.

Question 15

A body has a mass of 60 kg. Find its weight on a planet where g = 3.7 m/s².

Solution 
W = mg = 60 × 3.7 = 222 N

Answer: 222 N.

Question 16

A student measures the mass of a body five times and gets 4.98 g, 4.99 g, 5.00 g, 5.01 g and 5.02 g. The true mass is 5.50 g. The measurements are:

Solution

The readings cluster tightly (spread only ±0.02 g), so they are precise. But their average (5.00 g) is far from the true value (5.50 g), so they are not accurate.

Answer: Precise but not accurate.

Question 17

Which of the following is a systematic error?

Solution

Systematic errors have the same size and direction every time. A zero error on a vernier caliper shifts every reading the same way, so it is systematic.

Random errors vary unpredictably. Parallax depends on the observer's eye position — it can be random.

Answer: Zero error.

Question 18

A pendulum's period is timed as 20 oscillations in 40.0 s. If the stopwatch least count is 0.1 s, what is the uncertainty in one period?

Solution 
Period = 40.0 / 20 = 2.00 s
Uncertainty per oscillation = 0.1 / 20 = 0.005 s

Answer: ±0.005 s.

Question 19

How many significant figures are there in 0.03020 m?

Solution

Leading zeros are never significant. The digits 3, 0, 2, 0 (after the decimal point) are significant.

Answer: 4 significant figures.

Question 20

The length and width of a rectangle are (8.0 ± 0.1) cm and (3.0 ± 0.1) cm. Find the fractional error in the area.

Solution 
Fractional error = 0.1/8.0 + 0.1/3.0
                 = 0.0125 + 0.0333 = 0.0458

Answer: Approximately 0.046 (4.6%).

Question 21

A vernier caliper reads 2.35 cm. Given that 1 vernier division = 0.01 cm, what is the least count of the instrument?

Solution

The least count is the smallest measurement the instrument can take. For a vernier caliper with main scale in mm and 10 vernier divisions, the least count is 0.1 mm = 0.01 cm.

Answer: 0.01 cm (0.1 mm).

Question 22

The mass of a body is 250 g. What is its weight on Earth? (g = 10 m/s²)

Solution 
m = 250 g = 0.25 kg
W = mg = 0.25 × 10 = 2.5 N

Answer: 2.5 N.

Question 23

Which instrument measures weight directly?

Solution

A beam balance compares masses — it measures mass. A spring balance measures the extension of a spring under load, which depends on g, so it measures weight.

Answer: Spring balance.

Question 24

A physical quantity is measured as 5.0 m. If the true value is 5.2 m, find the percentage error.

Solution 
Error = |5.0 − 5.2| = 0.2 m
Percentage error = (0.2 / 5.2) × 100 = 3.85%

Answer: Approximately 3.85%.

CPosition, Distance and Displacement

Question 25

A boy walks 6 m east, then 4 m west, then 8 m east. Find the distance and the displacement.

Solution 
Distance = 6 + 4 + 8 = 18 m
Displacement = +6 − 4 + 8 = 10 m east

Answer: Distance = 18 m; displacement = 10 m east.

Question 26

A runner completes half a lap of a circular track of radius 100 m. Find the distance and displacement.

Solution 
Half-circumference = πr = π × 100 ≈ 314 m
Displacement = diameter = 2r = 200 m (along the diameter)

Answer: Distance ≈ 314 m; displacement = 200 m.

Question 27

A girl moves 5 m north, 12 m east, 5 m south. Find the distance and displacement.

Solution 
Distance = 5 + 12 + 5 = 22 m
Net north–south = 5 − 5 = 0
Net east = 12 m
Displacement = 12 m east

Answer: Distance = 22 m; displacement = 12 m east.

Question 28

Which of the following is a vector quantity?

Solution

Displacement, velocity and force are vectors. Distance, speed and mass are scalars.

Answer: Displacement (or velocity or force).

Question 29

A particle starts at x = +5 m and ends at x = −7 m. Find its displacement.

Solution 
Δx = x₂ − x₁ = −7 − 5 = −12 m
Magnitude = 12 m, direction negative

Answer: −12 m (12 m in the negative direction).

Question 30

A body travels from A to B (distance 8 m) then from B back to A. What is the total distance and the total displacement?

Solution 
Distance = 8 + 8 = 16 m
Displacement = 0 (start and finish at the same point)

Answer: Distance = 16 m; displacement = 0.

Question 31

A man walks 3 km north, then 4 km east. Find the magnitude of the displacement.

Solution 
Magnitude = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5 km
Direction: tan θ = 4/3 = 1.333, θ = 53.1° east of north

Answer: 5 km at 53.1° east of north.

Question 32

A car travels 100 km due north, then 100 km due south. Find the average speed if the total journey takes 4 hours, and the average velocity.

Solution 
Total distance = 100 + 100 = 200 km
Total time = 4 h
Average speed = 200 / 4 = 50 km/h
Displacement = 100 − 100 = 0
Average velocity = 0 / 4 = 0 km/h

Answer: Average speed = 50 km/h; average velocity = 0.

Question 33

Can the magnitude of displacement ever be greater than the distance travelled?

Solution

No. Displacement is the straight-line change in position. Distance is the total path length. The straight-line distance between two points can never exceed the path length.

Answer: No. |Displacement| ≤ Distance.

Question 34

A body moves 4 m to the right, then 3 m up, then 4 m to the left, then 3 m down. Find the distance and displacement.

Solution 
Distance = 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 14 m
Net x = 4 − 4 = 0
Net y = 3 − 3 = 0
Displacement = 0

Answer: Distance = 14 m; displacement = 0.

Question 35

A plane flies 300 km north in 1 h, then 400 km east in 2 h. Find the average velocity over the whole trip.

Solution 
Resultant displacement = √(300² + 400²) = √250000 = 500 km
Total time = 1 + 2 = 3 h
Average velocity = 500 / 3 ≈ 166.7 km/h
Direction: tan θ = 400/300 = 1.333, θ = 53.1° east of north

Answer: Approximately 166.7 km/h at 53.1° east of north.

Question 36

A man walks along a rectangular field of length 30 m and width 20 m. He walks along the perimeter once. Find the distance and the displacement.

Solution 
Distance = perimeter = 2(30 + 20) = 100 m
Displacement = 0 (returns to starting point)

Answer: Distance = 100 m; displacement = 0.

DScalars and Vectors

Question 37

Two forces of 8 N and 6 N act at right angles. Find the resultant.

Solution 
R = √(8² + 6²) = √100 = 10 N
Direction: tan θ = 6/8 = 0.75, θ = 36.9° from the 8 N force

Answer: 10 N at 36.9° from the 8 N force.

Question 38

A force of 100 N acts at 37° to the horizontal. Find its horizontal and vertical components. (cos 37° = 0.8, sin 37° = 0.6)

Solution 
Horizontal = 100 cos 37° = 100 × 0.8 = 80 N
Vertical = 100 sin 37° = 100 × 0.6 = 60 N

Answer: 80 N horizontal, 60 N vertical.

Question 39

Two forces of 5 N and 5 N act at 120° to each other. Find the resultant.

Solution 
R = √(5² + 5² + 2 × 5 × 5 × cos 120°)
cos 120° = −0.5
R = √(25 + 25 − 25) = √25 = 5 N

Answer: 5 N.

Question 40

Three forces act on a body: 10 N east, 6 N west, 8 N north. Find the resultant.

Solution 
Net east–west = 10 − 6 = 4 N east
Net north–south = 8 N north
R = √(4² + 8²) = √80 = 8.94 N
Direction: tan θ = 8/4 = 2, θ = 63.4° north of east

Answer: 8.94 N at 63.4° north of east.

Question 41

A body is acted on by 12 N east and 5 N north. What third force keeps it in equilibrium?

Solution 
Resultant of the two = √(12² + 5²) = 13 N
Direction: tan θ = 5/12 = 0.4167, θ = 22.6° north of east
Third force = 13 N at 22.6° south of west

Answer: 13 N at 22.6° south of west.

Question 42

Two vectors of magnitudes 7 and 24 units act at right angles. Their resultant has magnitude:

Solution 
R = √(7² + 24²) = √(49 + 576) = √625 = 25 units

Answer: 25 units.

Question 43

A vector of magnitude 10 units is inclined at 60° to the x-axis. Its y-component is:

Solution 
y-component = 10 sin 60° = 10 × 0.866 = 8.66 units

Answer: 8.66 units.

Question 44

Which of the following operations is valid for scalars but not for vectors?

Solution

Ordinary arithmetic addition (3 + 4 = 7) is valid for scalars but not for vectors, because vector addition must account for direction.

Answer: Ordinary arithmetic addition.

Question 45

The resultant of two forces of 3 N and 5 N cannot be:

Solution 
Maximum = 3 + 5 = 8 N
Minimum = 5 − 3 = 2 N
Any value between 2 N and 8 N is possible.
A resultant of 9 N is outside this range.

Answer: 9 N.

Question 46

A boy pulls a box with a force of 200 N at 30° to the horizontal. Find the effective horizontal force.

Solution 
Horizontal = 200 cos 30° = 200 × 0.866 = 173.2 N

Answer: 173.2 N.

Question 47

A vector A = 3i + 4j. Find its magnitude and direction.

Solution 
|A| = √(3² + 4²) = 5
Direction: tan θ = 4/3 = 1.333, θ = 53.1° from the x-axis

Answer: 5 units at 53.1° to the x-axis.

Question 48

Three forces act on a body at the same point: 4 N east, 4 N north and 4 N south. Find the resultant.

Solution 
North–south: 4 − 4 = 0
East–west: 4 N east
Resultant = 4 N east

Answer: 4 N east.

Question 49

A ship sails 20 km on a bearing of 060°, then 20 km on a bearing of 150°. Find the resultant displacement.

Solution 
Bearing 060°: 60° from north, so components = (20 sin 60°, 20 cos 60°) = (17.32, 10)
Bearing 150°: 150° from north, so components = (20 sin 150°, 20 cos 150°) = (10, −17.32)
Sum = (27.32, −7.32)
Resultant = √(27.32² + 7.32²) = √(746.4 + 53.6) = √800 = 28.28 km
Direction: tan θ = −7.32/27.32 = −0.268, θ = −15° from east
Bearing = 90° + 15° = 105°

Answer: Approximately 28.28 km on a bearing of 105°.

Question 50

Explain why 3 N + 4 N can give resultants of 7 N, 1 N, or 5 N, but not 8 N.

Solution 
Same direction: R = 3 + 4 = 7 N
Opposite direction: R = 4 − 3 = 1 N
Right angle: R = √(3² + 4²) = 5 N
Maximum possible = 7 N
8 N exceeds the maximum, so it is impossible.

Answer: Because the maximum resultant of two forces is their sum (7 N), and 8 N exceeds this limit.

ESpeed, Velocity and Acceleration

Question 51

A car travels 150 km in 2 h, then 90 km in 1.5 h. Find the average speed for the whole journey.

Solution 
Total distance = 150 + 90 = 240 km
Total time = 2 + 1.5 = 3.5 h
Average speed = 240 / 3.5 = 68.6 km/h

Answer: Approximately 68.6 km/h.

Question 52

A car accelerates from 15 m/s to 35 m/s in 5 s. Find its acceleration.

Solution 
a = (35 − 15) / 5 = 20 / 5 = 4 m/s²

Answer: 4 m/s².

Question 53

A body moving at 40 m/s decelerates uniformly at 8 m/s². How long does it take to stop?

Solution 
v = u + at  →  0 = 40 + (−8)t
8t = 40  →  t = 5 s

Answer: 5 s.

Question 54

A body accelerates uniformly from rest at 3 m/s² for 8 s. Find its final velocity and distance travelled.

Solution 
v = u + at = 0 + 3 × 8 = 24 m/s
s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½ × 3 × 64 = 96 m

Answer: v = 24 m/s; s = 96 m.

Question 55

A train slows from 72 km/h to 36 km/h in 10 s. Find its deceleration.

Solution 
u = 72 km/h = 20 m/s
v = 36 km/h = 10 m/s
a = (10 − 20) / 10 = −1 m/s²
Deceleration = 1 m/s²

Answer: 1 m/s².

Question 56

A body moves 300 m in 20 s, then 500 m in 30 s, then 200 m in 10 s. Find the average speed.

Solution 
Total distance = 300 + 500 + 200 = 1000 m
Total time = 20 + 30 + 10 = 60 s
Average speed = 1000 / 60 = 16.67 m/s

Answer: Approximately 16.67 m/s.

Question 57

Two bodies move in opposite directions at 15 m/s and 10 m/s. Find their relative velocity.

Solution 
Opposite directions: relative velocity = 15 + 10 = 25 m/s

Answer: 25 m/s.

Question 58

A body starts from rest and moves with uniform acceleration. After 6 s it has a velocity of 18 m/s. Find the acceleration and the distance travelled.

Solution 
a = (18 − 0) / 6 = 3 m/s²
s = ½(0 + 18) × 6 = 54 m

Answer: a = 3 m/s²; s = 54 m.

Question 59

A car covers the first half of a journey at 40 km/h and the second half at 60 km/h. Find the average speed.

Solution 
Let the total distance be 2d.
Time for first half = d / 40
Time for second half = d / 60
Total time = d/40 + d/60 = (3d + 2d)/120 = 5d/120 = d/24
Average speed = 2d / (d/24) = 48 km/h

Answer: 48 km/h.

Question 60

Can a body have constant speed and still be accelerating?

Solution

Yes. A body moving in a circle at constant speed has a changing direction of velocity, so its velocity is changing and it is accelerating (centripetal acceleration).

Answer: Yes — for example, uniform circular motion.

Question 61

A body moves with initial velocity 5 m/s and uniform acceleration 2 m/s². Find its velocity after 10 s and the distance travelled in that time.

Solution 
v = u + at = 5 + 2 × 10 = 25 m/s
s = ut + ½at² = 5 × 10 + ½ × 2 × 100 = 50 + 100 = 150 m

Answer: v = 25 m/s; s = 150 m.

Question 62

A car travelling at 30 m/s decelerates at 5 m/s². Find the distance travelled before it stops.

Solution 
v² = u² + 2as
0 = 30² + 2(−5)s
10s = 900  →  s = 90 m

Answer: 90 m.

FEquations of Motion, Free Fall and Projectiles

Question 63

A stone is dropped from a height of 45 m. Find the time to reach the ground. Use g = 10 m/s².

Solution 
h = ½gt²  →  45 = ½ × 10 × t²
45 = 5t²  →  t² = 9  →  t = 3 s

Answer: 3 s.

Question 64

A stone is dropped from a height and hits the ground at 40 m/s. Find the height. Use g = 10 m/s².

Solution 
v² = u² + 2gh
40² = 0 + 2 × 10 × h
1600 = 20h  →  h = 80 m

Answer: 80 m.

Question 65

A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 25 m/s. Find the maximum height reached. Use g = 10 m/s².

Solution 
At maximum height, v = 0
0 = 25² − 2 × 10 × h
20h = 625  →  h = 31.25 m

Answer: 31.25 m.

Question 66

A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 20 m/s. Find the total time of flight. Use g = 10 m/s².

Solution 
Time to reach the top: t = u/g = 20/10 = 2 s
Total time of flight = 2 × 2 = 4 s

Answer: 4 s.

Question 67

A body falls freely from rest. Find its velocity after 4 s and the distance fallen. Use g = 10 m/s².

Solution 
v = gt = 10 × 4 = 40 m/s
h = ½gt² = ½ × 10 × 16 = 80 m

Answer: v = 40 m/s; h = 80 m.

Question 68

A body is projected horizontally from a height of 80 m with a horizontal velocity of 30 m/s. Find the time of flight and the horizontal range. Use g = 10 m/s².

Solution 
Vertical: h = ½gt²  →  80 = 5t²  →  t = 4 s
Horizontal: R = uₓ t = 30 × 4 = 120 m

Answer: t = 4 s; R = 120 m.

Question 69

A projectile is launched at 50 m/s at 30° to the horizontal. Find its time of flight. Use g = 10 m/s².

Solution 
u_y = 50 sin 30° = 25 m/s
T = 2u_y / g = 2 × 25 / 10 = 5 s

Answer: 5 s.

Question 70

A projectile is launched at 40 m/s at 60° to the horizontal. Find the maximum height. Use g = 10 m/s².

Solution 
u_y = 40 sin 60° = 40 × 0.866 = 34.64 m/s
H = u_y² / (2g) = (34.64)² / 20 = 1200 / 20 = 60 m

Answer: 60 m.

Question 71

A projectile is launched at 30 m/s at 45° to the horizontal. Find its range. Use g = 10 m/s².

Solution 
R = u² sin 2θ / g = 30² × sin 90° / 10
R = 900 × 1 / 10 = 90 m

Answer: 90 m.

Question 72

For a projectile on level ground, at what angle of projection is the range maximum?

Solution 
R = u² sin 2θ / g
R is maximum when sin 2θ = 1
2θ = 90°  →  θ = 45°

Answer: 45°.

GGraphical Analysis of Motion

Question 73

A distance–time graph is a straight line passing through (0, 0) and (6, 18). Find the speed.

Solution 
Speed = slope = (18 − 0) / (6 − 0) = 3 m/s

Answer: 3 m/s.

Question 74

A velocity–time graph rises uniformly from 0 to 20 m/s over 5 s, then stays at 20 m/s for 10 s. Find the total displacement.

Solution 
Phase 1 (triangle): area = ½ × 5 × 20 = 50 m
Phase 2 (rectangle): area = 10 × 20 = 200 m
Total displacement = 50 + 200 = 250 m

Answer: 250 m.

Question 75

A velocity–time graph has a straight line from (3, 9) to (9, 27). Find the acceleration.

Solution 
Acceleration = slope = (27 − 9) / (9 − 3) = 18 / 6 = 3 m/s²

Answer: 3 m/s².

Question 76

A velocity–time graph shows a body decelerating uniformly from 24 m/s to 0 in 8 s. Find the deceleration and distance travelled.

Solution 
Deceleration = 24 / 8 = 3 m/s²
Distance = area of triangle = ½ × 8 × 24 = 96 m

Answer: Deceleration = 3 m/s²; distance = 96 m.

HTrap Set — Confusing JAMB Cases Solved Carefully

Question 77 — The average-speed trap

A car travels from A to B at 60 km/h and returns from B to A at 40 km/h. Find the average speed for the whole journey.

Solution

The tempting answer is (60 + 40)/2 = 50 km/h. This is wrong, because average speed is total distance divided by total time, not the average of the two speeds.

Let AB = d.
Time going = d / 60
Time returning = d / 40
Total distance = 2d
Total time = d/60 + d/40 = (2d + 3d)/120 = 5d/120 = d/24
Average speed = 2d / (d/24) = 48 km/h

Answer: 48 km/h. Average speed is a harmonic mean when the distances are equal, not an arithmetic mean.

Question 78 — The zero-velocity trap

A ball is thrown vertically upwards. At the highest point of its flight, its velocity is zero. What is its acceleration at that instant?

Solution

The tempting answer is 0, reasoning that "if velocity is zero and it stops, acceleration must be zero." This is wrong.

Velocity is zero at the top, but the velocity is still changing — it is changing from upward to downward. The rate of change of velocity at that instant is the acceleration due to gravity.

Answer: 9.8 m/s² (or 10 m/s²) directed downwards. Zero velocity does not mean zero acceleration.

Question 79 — The displacement-vs-distance trap

A man walks 6 m east, then 8 m west, then 8 m east. Find (a) the total distance, and (b) the displacement.

Solution

The trap is to treat distance and displacement as the same. They are not.

Distance = 6 + 8 + 8 = 22 m (path length only)
Displacement = +6 − 8 + 8 = +6 m (net change in position)

Answer: (a) Distance = 22 m; (b) Displacement = 6 m east.

Question 80 — The resultant-range trap

Two forces of magnitudes 9 N and 12 N act on a body. Which of the following resultants is impossible: 3 N, 15 N, 20 N, or 10 N?

Solution

The trap is to think any resultant is possible. The resultant of two vectors is bounded.

Maximum = 9 + 12 = 21 N (same direction)
Minimum = 12 − 9 = 3 N (opposite directions)
Possible range: 3 N ≤ R ≤ 21 N
Check each option:
  3 N  → possible (opposite directions)
  15 N → possible (some acute angle)
  20 N → possible (small angle)
  10 N → possible (some angle)

But a resultant of 22 N or more, or less than 3 N, would be impossible. In the given list, all four values fall within the range.

Important: If the question had offered 2 N or 22 N, those would be impossible. The bound 3 N ≤ R ≤ 21 N is the key fact: |A − B| ≤ R ≤ A + B.

Answer: All four listed values (3, 15, 20, 10 N) are possible. A resultant outside 3–21 N would be impossible.
