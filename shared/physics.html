Question 1:
A waterfall is 420m high, calculate the difference in temperature of the water between the top and bottom of the waterfall assuming no heat is lost. [specific heat capacity of water = 4.20 × 10³ Jkg⁻¹K⁻¹, g = 10ms⁻²]
  • A. 1.0°C
  • B. 42.0°C
  • C. 4.2°C
  • D. 0.1°C
Solution
Principle: Conservation of Energy. The potential energy (P.E.) lost by the water as it falls is converted into heat energy (Q), which raises the temperature of the water.
P.E. lost = Heat gained
mgh = mcΔT

Where:

m = mass of water

g = acceleration due to gravity = 10 ms⁻²

h = height = 420 m

c = specific heat capacity = 4.20 × 10³ Jkg⁻¹K⁻¹

ΔT = change in temperature


Substitute the values:

m × 10 × 420 = m × 4.20 × 10³ × ΔT

Divide both sides by m:

4200 = 4200 × ΔT

ΔT = 4200 / 4200

ΔT = 1.0°C

Answer: A. 1.0°C
Question 2:
A body when slightly displaced is said to be in neutral equilibrium only if it tends to:
  • A. comes to rest in a new position with the centre of gravity and height remaining unchanged
  • B. return to its original position
  • C. remain in its displaced position
  • D. move further away from its original position while its centre of mass changes.
Solution
Principle: Types of Equilibrium.
  • Stable Equilibrium: When displaced, the center of gravity rises and the body returns to its original position.
  • Unstable Equilibrium: When displaced, the center of gravity lowers and the body moves further away.
  • Neutral Equilibrium: When displaced, the center of gravity remains at the same height, and the body comes to rest in the new position.
Answer: A. comes to rest in a new position with the centre of gravity and height remaining unchanged
Question 3:
A string of length 5m is extended by 0.04m when a load of 0.8kg is suspended at the end. How far will it extend if a force of 16N is applied? [g = 10ms⁻²]
  • A. 0.04m
  • B. 0.12m
  • C. 0.01m
  • D. 0.08m
Solution
Principle: Hooke's Law. The extension of a spring is directly proportional to the force applied, provided the limit of proportionality is not exceeded.
F = ke => F₁ / e₁ = F₂ / e₂

First, calculate the initial force (F₁):

F₁ = mass × gravity = 0.8 kg × 10 ms⁻² = 8 N

Given: e₁ = 0.04 m, F₂ = 16 N

Using the formula:

8 / 0.04 = 16 / e₂

Cross-multiply:

8 × e₂ = 16 × 0.04

8 × e₂ = 0.64

e₂ = 0.64 / 8

e₂ = 0.08 m

Answer: D. 0.08m

Which of the following has negligible mass?

Question 6:
Calculate the upthrust on an object of volume 50cm³ which is immersed in liquid of density 10³kgm⁻³. [g = 10ms⁻²]
  • A. 0.8 N
  • B. 2.5 N
  • C. 0.5 N
  • D. 1.0 N
Solution
Principle: Archimedes' Principle. The upthrust (buoyant force) on an object immersed in a fluid is equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the object.
Upthrust (U) = ρ × V × g

Given:

Density (ρ) = 10³ kgm⁻³

Volume (V) = 50 cm³ = 50 × 10⁻⁶ m³

g = 10 ms⁻²


Substitute values:

U = 10³ × (50 × 10⁻⁶) × 10

U = 1000 × 0.00005 × 10

U = 0.5 N

Answer: C. 0.5N

Question 12:
The volume of a fixed mass of gas is 10cm³ when its pressure is 400cmHg. Determine the volume of the gas when its pressure is 200cmHg.
  • A. 30 cm³
  • B. 40 cm³
  • C. 50 cm³
  • D. 20 cm³
Solution
Principle: Boyle's Law. For a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, the volume of the gas is inversely proportional to its pressure.
P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

Given:

Initial Pressure (P₁) = 400 cmHg

Initial Volume (V₁) = 10 cm³

Final Pressure (P₂) = 200 cmHg

Final Volume (V₂) = ?


Substitute values:

400 × 10 = 200 × V₂

4000 = 200 × V₂

V₂ = 4000 / 200

V₂ = 20 cm³

Answer: D. 20cm³
Question 13:
The product PV where P is pressure and V is volume has the same unit as:
  • A. impulse
  • B. power
  • C. force
  • D. work
Solution
Principle: Dimensional Analysis / Units. Pressure (P) is Force/Area (N/m²), and Volume (V) is Area × Length (m³). Therefore, PV = (N/m²) × m³ = N·m. The unit N·m is the Joule (J), which is the unit of work or energy.
Answer: D. work

Calculate the height of the image formed by a pinhole camera of length 12 cm used to photograph an object 60cm away from the hole and 70cm high.

Given:

Image distance (v) = 12 cm (camera length)

Object distance (u) = 60 cm

Object height (h_o) = 70 cm

Image height (h_i) = ?


Substitute values:

12 / 60 = h_i / 70

0.2 = h_i / 70

h_i = 0.2 × 70

h_i = 14 cm

Answer: D. 14cm

Calculate the time taken for a mango fruit that fall from the tree 20 m to the ground. [g = 10ms⁻²]

h = ut + ½gt²

Given:

Height (h) = 20 m

Initial velocity (u) = 0 m/s

g = 10 ms⁻²


Substitute values:

20 = 0 × t + 0.5 × 10 × t²

20 = 5t²

t² = 20 / 5

t² = 4

t = 2 s

Answer: D. 2s

Question 27:
Calculate the voltage across a 5μF capacitor connected to an a.c source of frequency 50/π Hz when a current of 0.25A flows through the circuit.
  • A. 1591.3 V
  • B. 2000.0 V
  • C. 500.0 V
  • D. 397.8 V
Solution
Principle: Capacitive Reactance. In an AC circuit with a capacitor, the opposition to current flow is called capacitive reactance (Xc). The voltage across the capacitor is given by Ohm's law using Xc.
Xc = 1 / (2πfC)
V = I × Xc

Given:

Capacitance (C) = 5 μF = 5 × 10⁻⁶ F

Frequency (f) = 50/π Hz

Current (I) = 0.25 A


Step 1: Calculate Xc

Xc = 1 / (2 × π × (50/π) × 5 × 10⁻⁶)

Xc = 1 / (100 × 5 × 10⁻⁶)

Xc = 1 / (500 × 10⁻⁶)

Xc = 1 / 0.0005

Xc = 2000 Ω


Step 2: Calculate Voltage (V)

V = I × Xc

V = 0.25 A × 2000 Ω

V = 500 V

Answer: C. 500.0 V

Question 35:
From the diagram above, calculate the total current in the circuit. [Resistors: 18Ω, 12Ω, 6Ω in parallel, connected to 12V battery]
  • A. 5.0A
  • B. 3.7A
  • C. 4.5A
  • D. 4.0A
Solution
Principle: Resistors in Parallel. The reciprocal of the equivalent resistance (Rp) of resistors in parallel is the sum of the reciprocals of the individual resistances. The total current is given by Ohm's Law (I = V / Rp).
1/Rp = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃
I = V / Rp

Given:

R₁ = 18 Ω, R₂ = 12 Ω, R₃ = 6 Ω, V = 12 V


Step 1: Calculate Equivalent Resistance (Rp)

1/Rp = 1/18 + 1/12 + 1/6

Find a common denominator (36):

1/Rp = 2/36 + 3/36 + 6/36

1/Rp = (2 + 3 + 6) / 36

1/Rp = 11 / 36 Ω⁻¹

Rp = 36 / 11 Ω ≈ 3.27 Ω


Step 2: Calculate Total Current (I)

I = V / Rp

I = 12 / (36/11)

I = 12 × 11 / 36

I = 132 / 36

I ≈ 3.67 A

Answer: B. 3.7A

Solution

Given:

n = 1.5

A = 60°


Substitute values:

1.5 = sin((60 + Dm) / 2) / sin(60 / 2)

1.5 = sin((60 + Dm) / 2) / sin(30°)

1.5 = sin((60 + Dm) / 2) / 0.5

sin((60 + Dm) / 2) = 1.5 × 0.5

sin((60 + Dm) / 2) = 0.75

Given that sin⁻¹(0.75) = 49°:

(60 + Dm) / 2 = 49°

60 + Dm = 98°

Dm = 98° - 60°

Dm = 38°

Answer: A. 38.00°

Step 2: Calculate Work Done (W)

W = 40 N × 0.8 m

W = 32 J

Question 7:
The length of a side of a metallic cube at 20°C is 5.0cm. Given that the linear expansivity of the metal is 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹, find the volume of the cube at 120°C.
  • A. 126.50cm³
  • B. 126.25cm³
  • C. 126.00cm³
  • D. 125.00cm³
Solution
Principle: Thermal Expansion (Volume). The volume expansivity (γ) of a solid is three times its linear expansivity (α). The new volume (V₂) is given by the formula.
γ = 3α
V₂ = V₁[1 + γ(T₂ - T₁)]

Given:

Initial side length (L₁) = 5.0 cm

Initial Volume (V₁) = L₁³ = 5³ = 125 cm³

Initial Temp (T₁) = 20°C

Final Temp (T₂) = 120°C

α = 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹


Step 1: Calculate γ

γ = 3 × 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ = 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ K⁻¹


Step 2: Calculate V₂

ΔT = 120 - 20 = 100°C

V₂ = 125 [1 + (1.2 × 10⁻⁴ × 100)]

V₂ = 125 [1 + 0.012]

V₂ = 125 × 1.012

V₂ = 126.5 cm³

Answer: A. 126.50cm³
Question 8:
A lorry travels 10km northwards, 4km eastwards, 6km southwards and 4km westwards to arrive at a point T. What is the total displacement?
  • A. 6km south
  • B. 4km north
  • C. 6km north
  • D. 4km east
Solution
Principle: Vector Addition (Displacement). Displacement is a vector quantity. We can resolve the motion into North-South and East-West components.

North-South component:

North = +10 km, South = -6 km

Net North-South = 10 - 6 = 4 km North


East-West component:

East = +4 km, West = -4 km

Net East-West = 4 - 4 = 0 km


Total displacement is the resultant of these components:

Resultant = 4 km North

Answer: B. 4km north
Question 9:
A force of 10N drags a mass 10kg on a horizontal table with an acceleration of 0.2ms⁻². If the acceleration due to gravity is 10ms⁻², the coefficient of friction between the moving mass and the table is:
  • A. 0.02
  • B. 0.08
  • C. 0.20
  • D. 0.80
Solution
Principle: Newton's Second Law and Friction. The net force (F_net) acting on the body is the applied force (F) minus the frictional force (f). F_net = ma, and f = μR, where R is the normal reaction (R = mg for a horizontal surface).
F - f = ma
f = μmg

Given:

F = 10 N, m = 10 kg, a = 0.2 ms⁻², g = 10 ms⁻²


Substitute values into the equation:

10 - (μ × 10 × 10) = 10 × 0.2

10 - 100μ = 2

100μ = 10 - 2

100μ = 8

μ = 8 / 100

μ = 0.08

Answer: B. 0.08

  • B. 182.6 °C
  • C. 306.1 °C
  • D. 335.3 °C
Solution
Principle: Thermal Expansion (Linear). The change in length (or diameter) is given by ΔL = L₀ α ΔT.
ΔL = L₀ α (T₂ - T₁)

Given:

L₀ = 50.0 cm

L₂ = 50.29 cm

ΔL = 0.29 cm

T₁ = 30 °C

α = 1.9 × 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹


0.29 = 50.0 × (1.9 × 10⁻⁵) × ΔT

0.29 = 9.5 × 10⁻⁴ × ΔT

ΔT = 0.29 / (9.5 × 10⁻⁴)

ΔT ≈ 305.26 °C


T₂ = T₁ + ΔT

T₂ = 30 + 305.26 = 335.26 °C

Answer: D. 335.3°C

  • D. 5.5 m

v² = u² + 2as

Given:

F_forward = 1240 N

F_resistive = 800 N

m = 1220 kg

u = 0 m/s

v = 4 m/s

a = F_net / m = 440 / 1220 ≈ 0.3607 ms⁻²

v² = u² + 2as

4² = 0² + 2 × 0.3607 × s

16 = 0.7214 × s

s = 16 / 0.7214

s ≈ 22.18 m
