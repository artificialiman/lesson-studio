2. Units, dimensions, measurement

Prefixes: T $10^{12}$, G $10^{9}$, M $10^{6}$, k $10^{3}$, c $10^{-2}$, m $10^{-3}$, μ $10^{-6}$, n $10^{-9}$, p $10^{-12}$. SI base units: m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd.

Derived: $\u{N=kg\,m\,s^{-2}}$; $\u{J=kg\,m^2\,s^{-2}}$; $\u{W=kg\,m^2\,s^{-3}}$; $\u{Pa=kg\,m^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$; $\u{C=A\,s}$; $\u{V=kg\,m^2\,s^{-3}\,A^{-1}}$; $\u{\Omega=kg\,m^2\,s^{-3}\,A^{-2}}$; $\u{F=kg^{-1}\,m^{-2}\,s^4\,A^2}$; $\u{H=kg\,m^2\,s^{-2}\,A^{-2}}$; $\u{T=kg\,s^{-2}\,A^{-1}}$.

Dimensions: velocity $LT^{-1}$; force $MLT^{-2}$; energy $ML^2T^{-2}$; power $ML^2T^{-3}$; pressure and Young's modulus $ML^{-1}T^{-2}$; $G$: $M^{-1}L^3T^{-2}$; $h$: $ML^2T^{-1}$. Dimensions cannot give constants such as $\frac{1}{2}$ or $2\pi$.

Instruments: metre rule 0.1 cm; vernier 0.01 cm (main scale + division × 0.01); micrometer 0.01 mm. True reading = observed − zero error. Spherometer $R=\frac{l^2}{6h}+\frac{h}{2}$. Errors: % error = (absolute error ÷ value) × 100; products and quotients add % errors; a power n multiplies it by n.

3. Motion

Scalars: distance, speed, mass, time, energy, work, power, temperature, pressure. Vectors: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum, impulse, weight, electric field.

Vector addition

$R^2=A^2+B^2+2AB\cos\theta$ ($\theta$ between the vectors, tail to tail)
Right angles: $R=\sqrt{A^2+B^2}$, $\tan\alpha=\frac{B}{A}$
Components: $A\cos\theta$ and $A\sin\theta$

Constant acceleration

$v=u+at$
$s=ut+\frac{1}{2}at^2$
$v^2=u^2+2as$
$s=\frac{1}{2}(u+v)\,t$
Distance in the $n$th second: $s_n=u+a\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)$

Average speed = total distance ÷ total time. Average velocity = displacement ÷ time. Free fall: $a=g$ downward; time to top $=\frac{u}{g}$; maximum height $=\frac{u^2}{2g}$. Graphs: gradient of displacement–time = velocity; gradient of velocity–time = acceleration; area under velocity–time = displacement; area under acceleration–time = change in velocity.

Projectile (ground level, speed u, angle θ)

$T=\frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}$
$H=\frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}$
$R=\frac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}$
Horizontal launch from height $h$: $t=\sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}$, range $=ut$

Horizontal velocity $u\cos\theta$ is constant; initial vertical velocity is $u\sin\theta$. Maximum range $\frac{u^2}{g}$ at $45\u{°}$. Angles $\theta$ and $(90\u{°}-\theta)$ give the same range.

Circular motion

$v=\omega r$, $\omega=2\pi f=\frac{2\pi}{T}$
$a=\frac{v^2}{r}=\omega^2 r$ (towards the centre)
$F=\frac{mv^2}{r}=m\omega^2r$
Flat road: $v_{max}=\sqrt{\mu rg}$
Banked, no friction: $\tan\theta=\frac{v^2}{rg}$
Banked, friction: $v_{max}=\sqrt{\frac{rg(\tan\theta+\mu)}{1-\mu\tan\theta}}$
Vertical circle on a string: top $\sqrt{rg}$ minimum, bottom $\sqrt{5rg}$

Centripetal force does no work.

Simple harmonic motion

$a=-\omega^2x$, $\omega=2\pi f=\frac{2\pi}{T}$
$v=\pm\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}$
$v_{max}=\omega A$ (equilibrium), $a_{max}=\omega^2A$ (extremes)
$E=\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2A^2=\frac{1}{2}kA^2$, $KE=\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2(A^2-x^2)$
$T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$ (pendulum)
$T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}$ (spring)

The pendulum period (small angles) does not depend on mass or amplitude. The spring period does not depend on $g$. A seconds pendulum has $T=2\ \u{s}$, so $l\approx1\ \u{m}$ ($0.994\ \u{m}$ at $g=9.8$).

Gravitation

$F=\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}$
$g=\frac{GM}{R^2}$, $g_h=g\left(\frac{R}{R+h}\right)^2$
$U=-\frac{GMm}{r}$
$v_{orbit}=\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$, $T=\frac{2\pi r}{v}$
$v_{esc}=\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}=\sqrt{2gR}\approx1.12\times10^{4}\ \u{m\,s^{-1}}$

Kepler: orbits are ellipses with the Sun at one focus; equal areas in equal times; $T^2\propto r^3$. Geostationary orbit: period 24 h, above the equator, orbital radius $\approx4.2\times10^{7}\ \u{m}$, height $\approx3.6\times10^{7}\ \u{m}$. Weightlessness in orbit: body and craft fall together, so there is no contact force.

4. Forces, work, fluids and solids

Newton: $F=ma=\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$, $W=mg$. Action and reaction act on different bodies.

Momentum

$p=mv$, $J=Ft=\Delta p$
$m_1u_1+m_2u_2=m_1v_1+m_2v_2$
Stick together: $m_1u_1+m_2u_2=(m_1+m_2)v$
Recoil: $Mv_{gun}=-mv_{bullet}$

Area under a force–time graph = impulse. Elastic collision: kinetic energy conserved. Inelastic: not conserved.

Friction and connected bodies

$f=\mu R$ ($R=mg$ on a horizontal surface)
$\tan\lambda=\mu$; a body slides on a slope when $\tan\theta=\mu$
Down a rough slope: $a=g(\sin\theta-\mu\cos\theta)$
Lift: $R=m(g+a)$ going up, $R=m(g-a)$ going down
Two masses over a pulley: $a=\frac{(m_2-m_1)g}{m_1+m_2}$, $T=\frac{2m_1m_2g}{m_1+m_2}$
$m_1$ on a smooth table pulled by hanging $m_2$: $a=\frac{m_2g}{m_1+m_2}$

Moments and equilibrium

Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot. Equilibrium needs $\Sigma F=0$ and $\Sigma M=0$. A couple = force × perpendicular distance between the forces. Stable: centre of gravity rises when displaced. Unstable: it falls. Neutral: it stays level. A low centre of gravity and a wide base give stability.

Work, energy, power, machines

$W=Fd\cos\theta$, $P=\frac{W}{t}=Fv$
$KE=\frac{1}{2}mv^2$, $PE=mgh$
$MA=\frac{\u{load}}{\u{effort}}$, $VR=\frac{d_{\u{effort}}}{d_{\u{load}}}$
$\eta=\frac{MA}{VR}\times100\%=\frac{\u{work out}}{\u{work in}}\times100\%$
Inclined plane $VR=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}$; wheel and axle $VR=\frac{R}{r}$; screw jack $VR=\frac{2\pi R}{p}$

Pulley system: $VR$ = number of rope segments supporting the load. A real machine has $MA

Fluids

$\rho=\frac{m}{V}$, relative density $=\frac{\rho}{\rho_{water}}$
$P=\frac{F}{A}$, $P=\rho gh$
Hydraulic press: $\frac{F_1}{A_1}=\frac{F_2}{A_2}$
$U=\rho_{fluid}Vg$, apparent weight $=W-U$
Relative density $=\frac{\u{weight in air}}{\u{weight in air}-\u{weight in water}}$

Floating: weight of body = weight of fluid displaced; fraction submerged $=\frac{\rho_{body}}{\rho_{fluid}}$. Mercury barometer: $P=\rho gh$ with $h=0.76\ \u{m}$.

Elasticity

$F=ke$ (up to the elastic limit)
$\u{stress}=\frac{F}{A}$, $\u{strain}=\frac{e}{L}$, $Y=\frac{FL}{Ae}$
$E=\frac{1}{2}Fe=\frac{1}{2}ke^2$, energy per unit volume $=\frac{1}{2}\times\u{stress}\times\u{strain}$
Springs: series $\frac{1}{k}=\frac{1}{k_1}+\frac{1}{k_2}$; parallel $k=k_1+k_2$

Force–extension graph: gradient = $k$; area = energy stored.

5. Heat and gases

Temperature: $T=\theta+273$. Fixed points: ice $0\ \u{°C}$, steam $100\ \u{°C}$. For a thermometric property $X$: $\theta=\frac{X_\theta-X_0}{X_{100}-X_0}\times100$.

Expansion

$\Delta L=\alpha L_0\Delta\theta$
$\beta=2\alpha$, $\gamma=3\alpha$ (small changes)
Liquids: $\gamma_{real}=\gamma_{apparent}+\gamma_{container}$

Water has maximum density at $4\ \u{°C}$. In a bimetallic strip the metal with the larger expansivity lies on the outside of the curve when heated.

Gas laws (fixed mass, kelvin)

$P_1V_1=P_2V_2$ ($T$ constant)
$\frac{V_1}{T_1}=\frac{V_2}{T_2}$ ($P$ constant)
$\frac{P_1}{T_1}=\frac{P_2}{T_2}$ ($V$ constant)
$\frac{P_1V_1}{T_1}=\frac{P_2V_2}{T_2}$
$PV=\v{ngas}RT$

Kinetic theory

$P=\frac{1}{3}\rho\,c_{\u{rms}}^{2}$
Mean kinetic energy per molecule $=\frac{3}{2}k_BT$
$c_{\u{rms}}=\sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$

Gas pressure comes from molecular impacts on the walls. At constant volume a higher temperature raises impact speed and pressure.

Heat quantities and transfer

$Q=mc\Delta\theta$, $C=mc$
$Q=ml$ (change of state, no temperature change)
Electrical: $IVt=mc\Delta\theta$ or $IVt=ml$
Mixtures: heat lost = heat gained
Relative humidity $=\frac{\u{SVP at dew point}}{\u{SVP at air temperature}}\times100\%$
Conduction: $\frac{Q}{t}=\frac{kA\Delta\theta}{L}$

Evaporation occurs at any temperature at the surface; boiling occurs when saturated vapour pressure equals external pressure. Convection needs a fluid; radiation needs no medium. Dull black surfaces absorb and emit best; shiny surfaces worst. Vacuum flask: the vacuum stops conduction and convection; silvered walls cut radiation; the stopper cuts conduction and convection at the top.

6. Waves and sound

$v=f\lambda$, $T=\frac{1}{f}$
Young's double slit: $x=\frac{\lambda D}{a}$
String fixed at both ends: $f_n=\frac{nv}{2L}$, $v=\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}$
Open pipe: $f_n=\frac{nv}{2L}$ (all harmonics), $\lambda=2L$
Closed pipe: $f=\frac{(2n-1)v}{4L}$ (odd harmonics only), $\lambda=4L$
Resonance tube: $l_1+e=\frac{\lambda}{4}$, $l_2+e=\frac{3\lambda}{4}$, $v=2f(l_2-l_1)$, $e\approx0.6r$
Beats: $|f_1-f_2|$; the unknown is $f\pm f_{beat}$

Waves: transverse vibration is perpendicular to travel; longitudinal is along it. Reflection keeps frequency, wavelength and speed. Refraction changes speed and wavelength, not frequency. Diffraction is greatest when the gap is about one wavelength. Interference: constructive path difference $n\lambda$; destructive $(n+\frac{1}{2})\lambda$. Nodes have zero amplitude and are $\frac{\lambda}{2}$ apart.

Doppler effect ($v$ sound, $v_s$ source, $v_o$ observer)

Source approaching: $f'=\frac{fv}{v-v_s}$
Source receding: $f'=\frac{fv}{v+v_s}$
Observer approaching: $f'=\frac{f(v+v_o)}{v}$
Observer receding: $f'=\frac{f(v-v_o)}{v}$

Sound

Echo: $d=\frac{vt}{2}$
$I=\frac{P}{4\pi r^2}$
$\beta=10\log\frac{I}{I_0}$, $I_0=10^{-12}\ \u{W\,m^{-2}}$

Sound is longitudinal and needs a medium. Speed: solids > liquids > gases. In a gas $v\propto\sqrt{T}$ (kelvin); at constant temperature it does not depend on pressure or frequency. Audible range 20 Hz to 20 kHz; ultrasound is above 20 kHz. Pitch follows frequency, loudness follows amplitude (intensity $\propto$ amplitude²), quality follows waveform. Resonance occurs when the driving frequency equals the natural frequency.

7. Light

Reflection and mirrors

Mirror turned through $\theta$: reflected ray turns through $2\theta$
Two mirrors at angle $\theta$: images $=\frac{360\u{°}}{\theta}-1$ (when whole)
$\frac{1}{f}=\frac{1}{u}+\frac{1}{v}$, $f=\frac{R}{2}$, $m=\frac{v}{u}=\frac{h_i}{h_o}$

Plane mirror: virtual, erect, same size, laterally inverted, as far behind as the object is in front. Concave: object beyond C: image between F and C, real, inverted, diminished; at C: at C, real, inverted, same size; between C and F: beyond C, real, inverted, magnified; at F: image at infinity; inside F: virtual, erect, magnified (shaving and dental mirrors). A lamp at F gives a parallel beam. Convex: always virtual, erect, diminished (driving mirrors).

Refraction and prisms

$n=\frac{\sin i}{\sin r}=\frac{c}{v}=\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}$, $n_1\sin\theta_1=n_2\sin\theta_2$
real depth ÷ apparent depth $=n$
$\sin C=\frac{1}{n}$ (generally $\sin C=\frac{n_2}{n_1}$)
Prism: $n=\frac{\sin\frac{A+D_{min}}{2}}{\sin\frac{A}{2}}$; thin prism $D=(n-1)A$
$A=60\u{°}$, $n=1.5$: $D_{min}\approx37.2\u{°}$

Typical $n$: water 1.33, glass 1.5. Total internal reflection needs light going from the denser to the less dense medium with incidence above $C$ (optical fibres, prism periscopes, binoculars, mirage, diamond brilliance). Dispersion: violet is deviated most, red least.

Lenses and instruments

$\frac{1}{f}=\frac{1}{u}+\frac{1}{v}$, $m=\frac{v}{u}$
$P=\frac{1}{f}$ (dioptre, $f$ in metres), contact $P=P_1+P_2$
Simple microscope: $M=1+\frac{D}{f}$ (near point), $M=\frac{D}{f}$ (infinity)
Compound microscope: $M=M_oM_e$
Telescope, normal adjustment: $M=\frac{f_o}{f_e}$, separation $f_o+f_e$
Pinhole camera: $\frac{h_i}{h_o}=\frac{\u{image distance}}{\u{object distance}}$

Real-is-positive convention: $f$ positive for converging, negative for diverging; $v$ positive for real images, negative for virtual. An object inside $f$ of a converging lens gives a virtual, erect, magnified image. Short sight is corrected by a diverging lens; long sight by a converging lens.

Colour and the electromagnetic spectrum

Additive primaries: red, green, blue. Red + green = yellow; red + blue = magenta; green + blue = cyan; all three = white. Complementary pairs: red–cyan, green–magenta, blue–yellow. An object reflects its own colour and absorbs the rest; a filter transmits its own colour. Magenta absorbs green; cyan absorbs red; yellow absorbs blue. Pigment primaries (subtractive): cyan, magenta, yellow.

Spectrum by rising frequency: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma. All are transverse and travel at $c$ in vacuum; $E=hf$ rises with frequency. Only ultraviolet (upper end), X-rays and gamma ionise. Penetration depends on the material. Uses: radio (communication), microwave (radar, cooking), infrared (remote controls, thermal imaging), ultraviolet (sterilisation, forged-note detection), X-rays (imaging, flaw detection), gamma (sterilisation, radiotherapy).

8. Electricity and magnetism

Electrostatics and capacitors

$F=\frac{kQ_1Q_2}{r^2}$, $E=\frac{F}{q}$, $E=\frac{V}{d}$
$E=\frac{kQ}{r^2}$, $V=\frac{kQ}{r}$, $U=qV$
$C=\frac{Q}{V}$, $C=\frac{\epsilon_0\epsilon_rA}{d}$, $E=\frac{1}{2}CV^2=\frac{Q^2}{2C}$
Series: $\frac{1}{C}=\frac{1}{C_1}+\frac{1}{C_2}$ (same charge). Parallel: $C=C_1+C_2$ (same voltage)

Like charges repel; unlike attract. Charge leaks fastest from points (lightning conductor).

Current electricity

$I=\frac{Q}{t}$, $V=\frac{W}{Q}$, $V=IR$ (constant temperature)
$R=\frac{\rho L}{A}$
Series: $R=R_1+R_2+R_3$. Parallel: $\frac{1}{R}=\frac{1}{R_1}+\frac{1}{R_2}+\frac{1}{R_3}$; two: $\frac{R_1R_2}{R_1+R_2}$
$E=I(R+r)=V+Ir$; short circuit $I=\frac{E}{r}$
$n$ cells in series: emf $nE$, resistance $nr$. In parallel: emf $E$, resistance $\frac{r}{n}$
$P=IV=I^2R=\frac{V^2}{R}$, $W=Pt$, $1\ \u{kWh}=3.6\times10^{6}\ \u{J}$
Potential divider: $V_{out}=V_{in}\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}$
Wheatstone: $\frac{P}{Q}=\frac{R}{S}$. Metre bridge: $\frac{R}{S}=\frac{l}{100-l}$. Potentiometer: $\frac{E_1}{E_2}=\frac{l_1}{l_2}$
Voltmeter: $R=\frac{V}{I_g}-R_g$ in series. Ammeter: $S=\frac{I_gR_g}{I-I_g}$ shunt
Electrolysis: $m=ZIt$; $96\,500\ \u{C}$ per mole of electrons

The resistance of a metal rises with temperature. Cost = kW × hours × rate. Dry cell: zinc case, carbon rod, manganese(IV) oxide depolariser, ammonium chloride; amalgamated zinc removes local action. Lead-acid cell: lead and lead(IV) oxide plates in dilute sulphuric acid, about 2 V per cell, rechargeable.

Magnetic effects and induction

$F=BIL\sin\theta$, $F=qvB\sin\theta$
$B=\frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}$, $B=\mu_0nI$, coil centre $B=\frac{\mu_0NI}{2r}$
$\frac{F}{L}=\frac{\mu_0I_1I_2}{2\pi d}$
$\Phi=BA\cos\theta$, $\varepsilon=-N\frac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t}$, $\varepsilon=BLv$
$\varepsilon=-L\frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t}$, energy $\frac{1}{2}LI^2$
Transformer: $\frac{V_s}{V_p}=\frac{N_s}{N_p}$, $\eta=\frac{V_sI_s}{V_pI_p}\times100\%$

Parallel currents in the same direction attract; opposite directions repel. Left hand (motor): first finger field, second finger current, thumb force. Right hand (generator): thumb motion, first finger field, second finger induced current. Lenz: the induced current opposes the change that causes it. Transformer losses: copper, eddy currents, hysteresis, flux leakage. Earth's field: declination is the angle between magnetic and geographic meridians; dip is the angle of the field below horizontal.

Alternating current

$V_{rms}=\frac{V_{peak}}{\sqrt{2}}\approx0.707\,V_{peak}$, mean power $\frac{1}{2}I_0^2R$
$X_L=2\pi fL$, $X_C=\frac{1}{2\pi fC}$
$Z=\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}$, $\tan\varphi=\frac{X_L-X_C}{R}$
Resonance: $X_L=X_C$, $f_0=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}$, $Z=R$ (minimum), current maximum
$P=V_{rms}I_{rms}\cos\varphi$

In a pure inductor voltage leads current by $90\u{°}$; in a pure capacitor current leads voltage by $90\u{°}$. A capacitor blocks direct current; an inductor opposes alternating current.

9. Modern physics and electronics

Atomic models: Thomson (1897) found the electron: positive sphere with embedded electrons. Rutherford (1911), alpha scattering by gold foil: most pass straight through, a few deflect through large angles, so the atom has a small, dense, positive nucleus. Neutrons were found by Chadwick in 1932. Bohr (1913): fixed energy levels; a transition emits or absorbs one photon. Line spectra are the evidence.

Bohr: $hf=E_i-E_f$, $E_n=\frac{-13.6}{n^2}\ \u{eV}$
Photoelectric: $hf=\phi+KE_{max}$, $f_0=\frac{\phi}{h}$, $eV_s=KE_{max}$
de Broglie: $\lambda=\frac{h}{p}=\frac{h}{mv}$
X-rays: $\lambda_{min}=\frac{hc}{eV}$

Below $f_0$ no electrons are emitted, whatever the intensity. Above $f_0$, $KE_{max}$ rises with frequency and does not depend on intensity; intensity sets the number of electrons, so the current. X-rays form when fast electrons strike a metal target; they travel straight, are not deflected by fields, blacken film, ionise gases and diffract in crystals.

Radioactivity

$N=N_0e^{-\lambda t}$, $A=\lambda N$, $t_{1/2}=\frac{0.693}{\lambda}$
fraction left $=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n}$, $n=\frac{t}{t_{1/2}}$
$E=\Delta m\,c^2$, $1\ \u{u}=931.5\ \u{MeV}$

Alpha: helium nucleus, charge +2e, mass 4 u; stopped by paper; strongest ionisation; bends slightly toward the negative plate. Beta (β⁻): fast electron; stopped by a few mm of aluminium; medium ionisation; bends strongly toward the positive plate. Gamma: electromagnetic, uncharged; reduced only by thick lead or concrete; weak ionisation; not deflected. Alpha decay: $A$ falls by $4$, $Z$ by $2$. Beta decay: $A$ unchanged, $Z$ rises by $1$. Gamma: neither changes. Fission splits a heavy nucleus (uranium-235); fusion joins light nuclei. Detectors: Geiger–Müller tube, cloud chamber, scintillation counter. Carbon-14 half-life 5730 years.

Semiconductors and logic

n-type: pentavalent dopant (phosphorus, arsenic), electrons carry. p-type: trivalent dopant (boron, gallium), holes carry. A p–n diode conducts in forward bias and blocks in reverse; one diode gives half-wave rectification, a four-diode bridge full-wave. Transistor (npn or pnp): emitter, base, collector; a small base current controls a larger collector current, as a switch or amplifier. Gates: AND outputs 1 only if both inputs are 1; OR if either is 1; NOT inverts; NAND is NOT-AND; NOR is NOT-OR.

Corrected from the earlier factfile: the ionising and penetrating orderings of the spectrum by frequency (only ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma ionise; penetration depends on the material); neutrons were not part of Rutherford's model; prism $D_{min}\approx37.2\u{°}$, not $38\u{°}$; $340\ \u{m\,s^{-1}}$ is one of several values in use for sound; short-circuit current is $\frac{E}{r}$, not unlimited; projectile motion and transistors now have content.
