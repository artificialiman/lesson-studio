AConversion of Energy

Question 1

State the law of conservation of energy.

Solution

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form to another. The total energy of an isolated system remains constant.

Question 2

A body of mass 4 kg falls from a height of 15 m. Find its kinetic energy just before striking the ground. (g = 10 m/s²)

Solution 
PE lost = KE gained
KE = mgh = 4 × 10 × 15 = 600 J

Answer: 600 J.

Question 3

A machine takes in 800 J and delivers 640 J of useful work. Find its efficiency.

Solution 
η = (useful output / total input) × 100%
η = (640 / 800) × 100% = 80%

Answer: 80%.

Question 4

Convert 5 kWh to joules.

Solution 
1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
5 kWh = 5 × 3.6 × 10⁶ = 1.8 × 10⁷ J

Answer: 1.8 × 10⁷ J.

Question 5

An electric kettle draws 5 A from a 240 V supply for 10 minutes. Find the electrical energy converted.

Solution 
t = 10 × 60 = 600 s
E = VIt = 240 × 5 × 600 = 7.2 × 10⁵ J

Answer: 7.2 × 10⁵ J.

Question 6

A diesel engine has an efficiency of 40%. How much useful work does it deliver per litre of diesel? (1 litre of diesel ≈ 38 MJ)

Solution 
Useful work = η × energy input
= 0.40 × 38 MJ = 15.2 MJ

Answer: 15.2 MJ.

Question 7

Two machines are in series. Their efficiencies are 90% and 70%. Find the overall efficiency.

Solution 
η = η₁ × η₂ = 0.90 × 0.70 = 0.63 = 63%

Answer: 63%.

Question 8

Name the main energy conversion in (a) an electric motor, (b) a dynamo, (c) a solar panel, (d) a battery, (e) a filament lamp.

Solution

(a) Electrical → Mechanical

(b) Mechanical → Electrical

(c) Light → Electrical

(d) Chemical → Electrical

(e) Electrical → Light + Heat

Question 9

Why can no real machine have an efficiency of 100%?

Solution

Because part of the input energy is always dissipated as heat from friction between moving parts, as resistance heat in electrical parts, and as sound. This wasted energy cannot be fully recovered, so the useful output is always less than the input.

Question 10

Write the energy chain for a coal-fired thermal power station, from fuel to consumer.

Solution 
Chemical (fuel)
→ Heat (boiler)
→ KE (expanding steam)
→ Mechanical (turbine)
→ Electrical (generator)
→ Electrical (transmission line)
→ Light / Heat / Motion (consumer)

Question 11

A car of mass 1 200 kg moves at 25 m/s. Find its kinetic energy.

Solution 
KE = ½mv² = ½ × 1200 × 25²
KE = 600 × 625 = 375 000 J = 375 kJ

Answer: 375 kJ.

Question 12

A pump lifts 500 kg of water through 12 m in 20 s. Find its power. (g = 10 m/s²)

Solution 
Work done = mgh = 500 × 10 × 12 = 60 000 J
P = W/t = 60 000 / 20 = 3 000 W = 3 kW

Answer: 3 kW.

BTransmission Systems

Question 13

A belt drive has driver pulley diameter 30 cm and driven pulley diameter 90 cm. If the driver rotates at 400 rpm, find the driven speed.

Solution 
N₁D₁ = N₂D₂
400 × 30 = N₂ × 90
N₂ = 12 000 / 90 = 133.3 rpm

Answer: ≈ 133 rpm.

Question 14

A gear of 15 teeth drives a gear of 75 teeth at 600 rpm. Find the speed of the driven gear.

Solution 
N₁T₁ = N₂T₂
600 × 15 = N₂ × 75
N₂ = 9000 / 75 = 120 rpm

Answer: 120 rpm.

Question 15

A transformer has 800 turns in the primary and 40 turns in the secondary. If the primary voltage is 240 V, find the secondary voltage.

Solution 
Vₛ/Vₚ = Nₛ/Nₚ
Vₛ / 240 = 40 / 800
Vₛ = 240 × 0.05 = 12 V

Answer: 12 V.

Question 16

An ideal transformer has a primary of 500 V, 2 A and a secondary current of 20 A. Find the secondary voltage.

Solution 
VₚIₚ = VₛIₛ
500 × 2 = Vₛ × 20
Vₛ = 1000 / 20 = 50 V

Answer: 50 V.

Question 17

A transmission line of resistance 10 Ω carries 30 A. Find the power wasted in the line.

Solution 
P_loss = I²R = 30² × 10 = 9 000 W = 9 kW

Answer: 9 kW.

Question 18

A power station transmits 200 kW over a line of resistance 4 Ω. Find the power loss when the transmission voltage is (a) 2 kV and (b) 20 kV.

Solution 
(a) V = 2 kV:  I = 200 000 / 2 000 = 100 A
    P_loss = 100² × 4 = 40 000 W = 40 kW
(b) V = 20 kV: I = 200 000 / 20 000 = 10 A
    P_loss = 10² × 4 = 400 W

Answer: (a) 40 kW; (b) 400 W.

Question 19

A transformer has an output of 2 400 W and an input of 2 500 W. Find its efficiency.

Solution 
η = (2400 / 2500) × 100% = 96%

Answer: 96%.

Question 20

Why is the core of a transformer laminated?

Solution

To reduce eddy-current loss. Lamination breaks the core into thin, insulated sheets, so the induced eddy currents are confined to small loops of higher resistance. The energy lost as heat is greatly reduced.

Question 21

Why is a transformer said to work only on alternating current?

Solution

A transformer works by electromagnetic induction. The primary current must change to produce a changing magnetic flux in the core, which then induces an e.m.f. in the secondary. Direct current is steady, so the flux is steady and no e.m.f. is induced in the secondary.

Question 22

A compound gear train has stage ratios 4 and 5. If the input speed is 1 200 rpm, find the output speed.

Solution 
Overall VR = 4 × 5 = 20
N_out = 1 200 / 20 = 60 rpm

Answer: 60 rpm.

Question 23

A chain drive has a front sprocket of 18 teeth and a rear sprocket of 54 teeth. If the front sprocket rotates at 90 rpm, find the rear sprocket speed.

Solution 
N₁T₁ = N₂T₂
90 × 18 = N₂ × 54
N₂ = 1620 / 54 = 30 rpm

Answer: 30 rpm.

Question 24

State two differences between a belt drive and a chain drive.

Solution

Belt drive: relies on friction, can slip, quiet in operation, cheap.

Chain drive: relies on positive engagement of links with sprocket teeth, no slip, keeps exact timing, needs lubrication, noisier.

Question 25

State the standard transmission and distribution voltages in the Nigerian grid, and the supply frequency.

Solution 
Transmission: 330 kV and 132 kV
Sub-transmission: 33 kV
Distribution: 11 kV
Consumer: 415 V (3-phase) / 240 V (single phase)
Frequency: 50 Hz

Question 26

Name three causes of energy loss in a transformer and state how each is reduced.

Solution

Copper loss (I²R heat in windings): use thick copper wire of low resistance.

Eddy-current loss: use a laminated core.

Hysteresis loss: use soft iron core.

(Also: flux leakage — wind coils on top of one another on a closed core.)

CMachines: MA, VR and Efficiency

Question 27

Define mechanical advantage and velocity ratio.

Solution

MA = load / effort. A ratio of two forces; no unit.

VR = distance moved by effort / distance moved by load in the same time. Depends only on the geometry of the machine; no unit.

Question 28

A machine lifts a load of 600 N with an effort of 120 N. Find its mechanical advantage.

Solution 
MA = L / E = 600 / 120 = 5

Answer: 5.

Question 29

A machine has VR = 6 and MA = 4.5. Find its efficiency.

Solution 
η = MA/VR = 4.5/6 = 0.75 = 75%

Answer: 75%.

Question 30

A machine with VR = 5 and η = 60% is used to raise a load of 300 N. Find the effort.

Solution 
MA = η × VR = 0.60 × 5 = 3
E = L / MA = 300 / 3 = 100 N

Answer: 100 N.

Question 31

An effort of 80 N moves through 4 m to raise a load of 200 N through 1 m. Find (a) work input, (b) work output, (c) efficiency.

Solution 
(a) W_in = E × d_E = 80 × 4 = 320 J
(b) W_out = L × d_L = 200 × 1 = 200 J
(c) η = (200/320) × 100% = 62.5%

Answer: (a) 320 J; (b) 200 J; (c) 62.5%.

Question 32

Give the formula for velocity ratio of (a) a lever, (b) a wheel and axle, (c) an inclined plane, (d) a screw jack, (e) a hydraulic press.

Solution 
(a) Lever:      VR = effort arm / load arm
(b) Wheel-axle: VR = R / r
(c) Incline:    VR = length / height
(d) Screw jack: VR = 2πr / pitch
(e) Hydraulic:  VR = A / a (areas)

Question 33

A lever has an effort arm of 80 cm and a load arm of 20 cm. Find VR. What effort is needed to raise a 400 N load in the ideal case?

Solution 
VR = 80 / 20 = 4
Ideal: E × 80 = 400 × 20
E = 8000 / 80 = 100 N

Answer: VR = 4; E = 100 N.

Question 34

An inclined plane is 8 m long and rises 2 m. Find its VR and the ideal effort to push a 500 N load up it.

Solution 
VR = 8 / 2 = 4
Ideal: E × 8 = 500 × 2
E = 1000 / 8 = 125 N

Answer: VR = 4; E = 125 N.

Question 35

A screw jack has a handle length of 40 cm and a pitch of 4 mm. Find its VR.

Solution 
r = 0.40 m, p = 0.004 m
VR = 2πr / p = 2π(0.40)/0.004
VR = 2.513 / 0.004 = 628

Answer: ≈ 628.

Question 36

A wheel and axle has wheel radius 50 cm and axle radius 10 cm. Find its VR.

Solution 
VR = R/r = 50/10 = 5

Answer: 5.

Question 37

A hydraulic press has small piston area 8 cm² and large piston area 240 cm². An effort of 60 N is applied. Find the load (ideal).

Solution 
E/a = L/A
60 / 8 = L / 240
L = (60 × 240) / 8 = 1 800 N

Answer: 1 800 N.

Question 38

A block and tackle has 5 supporting strands and an efficiency of 80%. Find its MA and the effort needed to raise a load of 600 N.

Solution 
VR = 5
MA = η × VR = 0.80 × 5 = 4
E = 600/4 = 150 N

Answer: MA = 4; E = 150 N.

Question 39

Why does the efficiency of a simple machine increase as the load increases?

Solution

The work wasted against friction in the machine is roughly constant (it depends on the machine, not on the load). As the load increases, the useful work grows while the wasted work stays about the same. The wasted fraction therefore becomes smaller and efficiency rises. Beyond the machine's rated load, friction and distortion increase, and efficiency falls again.

Question 40

State two machines that are self-locking, and explain what self-locking means.

Solution

Self-locking machines: screw jack; worm gear.

Meaning: The machine holds its load without any applied effort. Their efficiency is below 50%, so friction alone is enough to prevent the load from reversing the motion.

DMachine Types and Lever Classes

Question 41

Classify each of the following as class 1, 2 or 3 lever: (a) crowbar, (b) wheelbarrow, (c) tongs, (d) bottle opener, (e) forceps, (f) scissors.

Solution

(a) Crowbar — class 1 (F between L and E)

(b) Wheelbarrow — class 2 (L between F and E)

(c) Tongs — class 3 (E between F and L)

(d) Bottle opener — class 2

(e) Forceps — class 3

(f) Scissors — class 1

Question 42

Which class of lever always has MA less than 1? Give two examples.

Solution

Class 3. The effort sits between the fulcrum and the load, so the effort arm is shorter than the load arm.

Examples: tongs, human forearm (bicep curl), fishing rod.

Question 43

Which class of lever always has MA greater than 1? Give two examples.

Solution

Class 2. The load sits between the fulcrum and the effort, so the effort arm is always longer than the load arm.

Examples: wheelbarrow, nutcracker, bottle opener.

Question 44

A block and tackle has 4 movable rope strands. What is its VR and, ignoring friction, what effort lifts a 400 N load?

Solution 
VR = 4 strands
Ideal MA = 4
E = 400 / 4 = 100 N

Answer: VR = 4; E = 100 N (ideal).

Question 45

State the main advantage of (a) a wedge and (b) a screw over a simple lever.

Solution

(a) Wedge: Very high VR (slant length ÷ thickness). Small effort at the blunt end produces a very large splitting force at the sharp edge.

(b) Screw: Very high VR (2πr ÷ pitch) in a small, compact device. Also typically self-locking, so it holds its position once set.

Question 46

Name the machine used in each case and its class where relevant: (a) a crane jib, (b) an excavator arm, (c) lifting a car corner, (d) a car brake pedal.

Solution

(a) Crane jib — lever combined with pulley and gear.

(b) Excavator arm — hydraulic system with levers.

(c) Screw jack or hydraulic bottle jack.

(d) Brake pedal — class 2 lever (foot effort, load at master-cylinder push-rod).

Question 47

Give the VR of a gear train with driver of 24 teeth and driven of 72 teeth.

Solution 
VR = T_driven / T_driver = 72/24 = 3

Answer: 3. Speed is reduced by 3; torque is multiplied by 3 (ideal).

Question 48

A door is 0.9 m wide and is opened by pushing at the outer edge with 40 N. What torque is applied?

Solution 
τ = F × r = 40 × 0.9 = 36 N m

Answer: 36 N m.

Question 49

Explain why using a longer spanner loosens a tight nut more easily.

Solution

The spanner is a lever with the nut as the fulcrum and the hand as the effort. A longer spanner increases the effort arm, so the same hand force produces a greater turning moment (torque) on the nut, and the nut turns more easily.

Question 50

A force of 30 N is applied at the rim of a wheel of radius 0.4 m, turning an axle of radius 0.08 m. Find the ideal load that can be lifted.

Solution 
VR = R/r = 0.4/0.08 = 5
Ideal MA = 5
L = MA × E = 5 × 30 = 150 N

Answer: 150 N.

Question 51

The efficiency of a certain pulley system is 75%. What is the mechanical advantage if VR = 8?

Solution 
MA = η × VR = 0.75 × 8 = 6

Answer: 6.

Question 52

A machine has VR = 10 and efficiency 40%. What effort is needed to raise a 500 N load? Comment on the result.

Solution 
MA = η × VR = 0.40 × 10 = 4
E = 500 / 4 = 125 N

The machine is only 40% efficient, which means 60% of the input energy is wasted. Despite the loss, the machine still gives a force gain of 4 because its VR (10) is much greater than the loss factor.

ERepair and Maintenance of Machines

Question 53

Distinguish between maintenance and repair.

Solution

Maintenance is the work done to keep a machine in good working order. It is done before failure.

Repair is the work done to restore a machine that has already failed. It is done after failure.

Question 54

State four functions of lubrication.

Solution

1. Reduces friction between moving surfaces.

2. Reduces wear.

3. Carries away heat generated by friction.

4. Prevents rust and corrosion by sealing the metal from moisture and air.

(Also: seals gaps, flushes out dirt.)

Question 55

List five common faults in machines and state a remedy for each.

Solution

Wear — lubricate, replace worn parts.

Overheating — check lubrication and cooling, reduce load.

Corrosion — paint or grease, keep dry.

Loose fastenings — tighten and add lock washers.

Leakage — replace seals and gaskets, top up fluid.

Question 56

Compare preventive and corrective maintenance in terms of cost, downtime and timing.

Solution 
Preventive:  before failure, on schedule
             downtime short and planned
             cost small and regular
Corrective:  after failure
             downtime long and unplanned
             cost often large; secondary damage possible

Preventive maintenance is cheaper overall.

Question 57

Explain the purpose of the first oil change in a new engine.

Solution

During the first hours of running, the metal surfaces bed in and produce fine metal particles. These mix with the oil and, if left, act as an abrasive. The first oil change flushes out these run-in particles and prevents accelerated wear. Manufacturers commonly recommend this change after about 20 running hours.

Question 58

State one advantage of predictive (condition-based) maintenance over preventive maintenance.

Solution

Predictive maintenance replaces parts only when a measurement (vibration, temperature, oil analysis) shows wear, so parts are used for their full remaining life. This avoids the cost of replacing parts that are still in good condition, and it also prevents failures between scheduled services.

FDams and Energy Production

Question 59

Water flows at 300 m³/s through a turbine from a head of 60 m. Find the power available. (ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 10 m/s²)

Solution 
P = ρQgh = 1000 × 300 × 10 × 60
P = 1.8 × 10⁸ W = 180 MW

Answer: 180 MW.

Question 60

A hydroelectric station has head 50 m, flow rate 200 m³/s and overall efficiency 85%. Find the electrical output.

Solution 
P_available = 1000 × 200 × 10 × 50 = 1.0 × 10⁸ W
P_out = 0.85 × 1.0 × 10⁸ = 8.5 × 10⁷ W = 85 MW

Answer: 85 MW.

Question 61

Name the four largest hydroelectric dams in Nigeria and give their installed capacities.

Solution 
Kainji (Niger River)  — 760 MW
Jebba (Niger River)   — 578.4 MW
Shiroro (Kaduna River) — 600 MW
Zungeru (Kaduna River) — 700 MW

Question 62

Which Nigerian dam was the first hydropower plant, and in what year was it commissioned?

Solution

Kainji Dam, on the Niger River, commissioned in 1968. It was designed for 960 MW but only 8 of the 12 planned turbines (760 MW) were installed.

Question 63

State three functions of a dam.

Solution

1. Generation of hydroelectric power.

2. Storage of water for irrigation and drinking.

3. Flood control downstream.

(Also: fisheries, navigation, recreation.)

Question 64

State two environmental and social problems caused by large dams.

Solution

1. Displacement of communities (for example, the town of Bussa and Foge Island were drowned by the Kainji reservoir).

2. Downstream river flow and silt deposition patterns are changed, affecting farming and fishing.

(Also: reservoir silting, loss of forest and wildlife, downstream erosion.)

Question 65

Write the energy chain for a hydroelectric station from reservoir to consumer.

Solution 
Gravitational PE of stored water
→ KE of falling water
→ Mechanical energy of turbine
→ Electrical energy from generator
→ Electrical energy along transmission lines
→ Usable energy at the consumer (light, heat, motion)

Question 66

Why does a hydroelectric station's output fall during the dry season?

Solution

Because P = ρQgh. Dry-season river flow Q decreases, and the reservoir level drops, so the head h is also smaller. Since P depends on both Q and h, the output falls on both counts.

Question 67

A dam holds back water to a depth of 40 m. Find the pressure at the base of the dam due to the water column. (ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 10 m/s²)

Solution 
P = ρgh = 1000 × 10 × 40 = 4.0 × 10⁵ Pa

Answer: 4.0 × 10⁵ Pa (≈ 4 bar).

Question 68

What is the function of the spillway of a dam?

Solution

The spillway is a channel that releases surplus water from the reservoir safely when the level rises above the designed maximum, for example during a flood. It protects the dam from overtopping and structural failure.

GRockets and Satellites

Question 69

State the principle on which a rocket works.

Solution

Newton's third law of motion: to every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. The rocket expels hot gas backward; the gas pushes the rocket forward with equal force (thrust).

Question 70

A rocket of mass 4 000 kg expels gas at 2 500 m/s at the rate of 12 kg/s. Find (a) the thrust, and (b) the initial acceleration. (g = 10 m/s²)

Solution 
(a) F = (dm/dt) × v_e = 12 × 2500 = 30 000 N
(b) Weight = Mg = 4000 × 10 = 40 000 N
    Since F (30 000) < Mg (40 000), the rocket will not lift off.
For lift-off, thrust must exceed weight.

Answer: (a) 30 000 N; (b) rocket cannot lift off.

Question 71

A rocket has a mass ratio M₀/M_f = 5 and exhaust speed 3 000 m/s. Find the change in velocity. (ln 5 = 1.609)

Solution 
Δv = v_e × ln(M₀/M_f)
Δv = 3000 × 1.609 = 4 827 m/s ≈ 4.83 km/s

Answer: ≈ 4.83 km/s.

Question 72

State two differences between a solid-fuel rocket and a liquid-fuel rocket.

Solution

Solid-fuel: simple, cannot be throttled or shut down once lit, stored ready for long periods. Specific impulse ≈ 250 s.

Liquid-fuel: complex, can be throttled and restarted, fuelled shortly before launch. Specific impulse ≈ 300–350 s (kerosene) or ≈ 450 s (hydrogen).

Question 73

What are the three conditions for a satellite to be geostationary?

Solution

1. It must orbit directly above the equator.

2. It must orbit west-to-east, in the same sense as the Earth's rotation.

3. Its period must equal one sidereal day (23 h 56 min), which places it at about 35 786 km above the equator.

Question 74

A geostationary satellite is 35 786 km above the equator. Find the one-way signal delay to Earth. (c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s)

Solution 
t = d / c = 3.5786 × 10⁷ / 3 × 10⁸
t = 0.119 s ≈ 0.12 s (one way)
Round trip ≈ 0.24 s

Answer: ≈ 0.12 s one way; ≈ 0.24 s for the round trip.

Question 75

A satellite is in a circular orbit at radius r = 7.0 × 10⁶ m. Find its orbital speed. (GM = 4.0 × 10¹⁴ m³/s²)

Solution 
v = √(GM/r) = √(4.0 × 10¹⁴ / 7.0 × 10⁶)
v = √(5.71 × 10⁷) ≈ 7.56 × 10³ m/s ≈ 7.6 km/s

Answer: ≈ 7.6 km/s.

Question 76

Give the typical altitude and use of each satellite orbit: (a) LEO, (b) MEO (GPS), (c) GEO.

Solution

(a) LEO (160–2 000 km): Earth observation, weather, space station; period ≈ 90 min.

(b) MEO (≈ 20 200 km): navigation (GPS); period ≈ 12 h.

(c) GEO (35 786 km): communication and weather; period = 24 h (sidereal), so the satellite stays over one point.

HNigeriaSat-1 and NigComSat-1

Question 77

State four facts about NigeriaSat-1.

Solution

1. Launched 27 September 2003 from Plesetsk, Russia, on a Kosmos-3M rocket.

2. Earth-observation microsatellite, about 100 kg, built by Surrey Satellite Technology Ltd (SSTL), UK.

3. Part of the Disaster Monitoring Constellation (DMC).

4. Imaged at 32 m resolution in three bands (green, red, near-infrared). Used for desertification monitoring, flood warning, mapping, and studying the environment–disease link.

Question 78

State four facts about NigComSat-1 and explain why it failed.

Solution

Facts:

1. Launched 13 May 2007 from Xichang, China, on a Long March 3B rocket.

2. Nigeria's first communications satellite; geostationary at 42.5° E, 35 786 km above the equator.

3. Built on the Chinese DFH-4 platform, mass ≈ 5 150 kg, carrying 28 transponders (4 C-band, 14 Ku-band, 8 Ka-band, 2 L-band).

4. Used for telephone and data links, internet, TV and radio broadcasting; operated by NIGCOMSAT Ltd.

Failure: In April 2008, the satellite lost power from one solar array. In November 2008, it lost all power after another solar-array anomaly and was sent to a graveyard orbit. It had operated for about 18 months, against a 15-year design life.

ITrap Set — Confusing JAMB Cases Solved Carefully

Question 79 — The "efficiency tells you the whole story" trap

Machine A has VR = 4 and efficiency 90%. Machine B has VR = 10 and efficiency 40%. Which machine gives the greater force multiplication? Show your working.

Solution

The tempting answer is Machine A, because its efficiency is higher. This is wrong. Force multiplication is MA, not efficiency.

Machine A: MA = η × VR = 0.90 × 4 = 3.6
Machine B: MA = η × VR = 0.40 × 10 = 4.0

Machine B multiplies force more, even though it wastes much more energy. Efficiency and force multiplication are different quantities.

Answer: Machine B has the greater MA (4 vs 3.6).

Question 80 — The "high voltage is dangerous, so use low voltage for transmission" trap

A power company must deliver 500 kW over a line of resistance 5 Ω. Compare the power loss if the transmission voltage is 1 kV and if it is 50 kV.

Solution

The tempting logic is that low voltage is safer, so the line should run at low voltage. The loss figures show why real grids run high voltage.

At 1 kV:  I = P/V = 500 000 / 1 000 = 500 A
          P_loss = I²R = 500² × 5 = 1 250 000 W = 1 250 kW
          This is 250% of the delivered power — impossible in practice.

At 50 kV: I = 500 000 / 50 000 = 10 A
          P_loss = 10² × 5 = 500 W
          This is 0.1% of the delivered power.

Raising the transmission voltage by 50× cut the loss by a factor of 2 500 (because loss ∝ 1/V²). This is why the Nigerian grid transmits at 330 kV and 132 kV, not at 240 V. Safety at the consumer end is achieved by stepping the voltage down at substations, not by transmitting low voltage over long distances.

Answer: Loss at 1 kV = 1 250 kW; loss at 50 kV = 500 W.
