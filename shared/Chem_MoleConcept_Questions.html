Set 1 · Moles from Mass (n = m / M)

Q1  [A · Teacher model]

How many moles of water molecules are present in 36 g of water? (H = 1, O = 16)

Answer: 2 mol

M(H₂O) = 2(1) + 16 = 18 g mol⁻¹. n = m/M = 36/18 = 2 mol.

Common error: 36 mol skips the division by M. 0.5 mol inverts the ratio (18/36).

Q2  [B · Board variant (student)]

How many moles of sodium carbonate are present in 5.3 g of Na₂CO₃? (Na = 23, C = 12, O = 16)

Answer: 0.05 mol

M(Na₂CO₃) = 2(23) + 12 + 3(16) = 106 g mol⁻¹. n = 5.3/106 = 0.05 mol.

Common error: 0.10 mol uses M = 53 (counting one Na). 20 mol inverts m/M. Count every atom with its subscript.

Q3  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

What is the mass of 0.25 mol of hydrated copper(II) sulphate, CuSO₄·5H₂O? (Cu = 64, S = 32, O = 16, H = 1)

Answer: 62.5 g

M = 64 + 32 + 4(16) + 5(18) = 160 + 90 = 250 g mol⁻¹. m = nM = 0.25 × 250 = 62.5 g.

Common error: Exception: water of crystallisation is part of the molar mass. 40 g is the anhydrous salt (0.25 × 160); 22.5 g is the water alone; 250 g is the mass of 1 mol.

Set 2 · Avogadro's Constant and Particle Counts (N = nL)

Q4  [A · Teacher model]

How many molecules are present in 8 g of methane, CH₄? (C = 12, H = 1; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 3.01 × 10²³

M(CH₄) = 16 g mol⁻¹. n = 8/16 = 0.5 mol. N = 0.5 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.01 × 10²³ molecules.

Common error: 1.51 × 10²⁴ counts the 5 atoms in each molecule (0.5 × 5 × L). The question asks for molecules.

Q5  [B · Board variant (student)]

How many atoms are present in 3.4 g of ammonia, NH₃? (N = 14, H = 1; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 4.82 × 10²³

M(NH₃) = 17 g mol⁻¹. n = 3.4/17 = 0.2 mol of molecules. Each molecule has 4 atoms: moles of atoms = 0.2 × 4 = 0.8 mol. N = 0.8 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 4.82 × 10²³ atoms.

Common error: 1.20 × 10²³ is the number of molecules. 2.41 × 10²³ counts only 2 atoms per molecule.

Q6  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

What is the total number of ions in 11.1 g of calcium chloride, CaCl₂? (Ca = 40, Cl = 35.5; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 1.81 × 10²³

M(CaCl₂) = 40 + 2(35.5) = 111 g mol⁻¹. n = 11.1/111 = 0.1 mol of formula units. Each gives Ca²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ = 3 ions, so 0.3 mol of ions. N = 0.3 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.81 × 10²³.

Common error: Exception: an ionic solid has no molecules; count ions per formula unit. 6.02 × 10²² counts formula units only; 1.20 × 10²³ counts 2 ions.

Set 3 · Molar Volume of Gases (22.4 dm³ at STP)

Q7  [A · Teacher model]

What volume does 8 g of oxygen gas occupy at STP? (O = 16; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: 5.6 dm³

Oxygen gas is O₂: M = 32 g mol⁻¹. n = 8/32 = 0.25 mol. V = 0.25 × 22.4 = 5.6 dm³.

Common error: Exception: oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen and the halogens are diatomic. 11.2 dm³ uses M = 16 (a single O atom).

Q8  [B · Board variant (student)]

What is the mass of carbon dioxide in 5.6 dm³ of the gas at STP? (C = 12, O = 16; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: 11 g

n = V/22.4 = 5.6/22.4 = 0.25 mol. M(CO₂) = 44 g mol⁻¹. m = 0.25 × 44 = 11 g.

Common error: 44 g is the mass of 1 mol. 22 g would need 11.2 dm³ (0.5 mol).

Q9  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A gas mixture contains 2.8 g of nitrogen (N₂) and 3.2 g of oxygen (O₂). What volume does the mixture occupy at STP? (N = 14, O = 16; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: 4.48 dm³

n(N₂) = 2.8/28 = 0.1 mol. n(O₂) = 3.2/32 = 0.1 mol. Total n = 0.2 mol. V = 0.2 × 22.4 = 4.48 dm³. At STP, 1 mol of any gas occupies the same volume, so the moles of different gases simply add.

Common error: 2.24 dm³ counts only one gas. 8.96 dm³ uses atomic masses (14 and 16), doubling the moles.

Set 4 · Molar Mass from Gas Data and Isotope Abundance

Q10  [A · Teacher model]

0.88 g of a gas occupies 448 cm³ at STP. Find its molar mass (molar volume = 22 400 cm³).

Answer: 44 g mol⁻¹

n = 448/22 400 = 0.02 mol. M = m/n = 0.88/0.02 = 44 g mol⁻¹.

Common error: Keep units matched: 22 400 cm³ goes with cm³, 22.4 dm³ goes with dm³. 88 g mol⁻¹ is the mass of 2 mol, from halving n.

Q11  [B · Board variant (student)]

A gas has a density of 2.86 g dm⁻³ at STP. Find its molar mass (molar volume = 22.4 dm³).

Answer: 64 g mol⁻¹

Density is the mass of 1 dm³. Mass of 22.4 dm³ (1 mol) = 2.86 × 22.4 = 64 g. Molar mass = density × molar volume = 64 g mol⁻¹.

Common error: The mass of one mole is the density multiplied by 22.4, never divided.

Q12  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

Element Y has three isotopes: ²⁴Y (79%), ²⁵Y (10%) and ²⁶Y (11%). Find its relative atomic mass.

Answer: 24.32

Weighted mean = (24 × 79 + 25 × 10 + 26 × 11)/100 = (1 896 + 250 + 286)/100 = 24.32.

Common error: 25.00 is the plain average of the three masses. 24.00 is the most abundant isotope only. 25.68 swaps the abundances. Abundances must weight each mass.

Set 5 · Valency by the Equivalent Mass Method (valency = atomic mass ÷ equivalent mass)

Q13  [A · Teacher model]

An element M has atomic mass 27 and equivalent mass 9. Find its valency.

Answer: 3

Valency = atomic mass / equivalent mass = 27/9 = 3.

Common error: Do not invert: 9/27 is not a whole number. Valency is always a whole number.

Q14  [B · Board variant (student)]

1.2 g of a metal (atomic mass 24) combines with 0.8 g of oxygen. Find the valency of the metal (O = 16).

Answer: 2

Equivalent mass = mass of metal combining with 8 g of oxygen = (1.2/0.8) × 8 = 12. Valency = 24/12 = 2. (Check by moles: 1.2/24 = 0.05 mol metal and 0.8/16 = 0.05 mol O, so the oxide is MO.)

Common error: Equivalent mass is defined against 8 g of oxygen. Using 16 g gives 24 and valency 1.

Q15  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

5.6 g of iron (Fe = 56) reacts with chlorine to give 16.25 g of iron chloride (Cl = 35.5). Find the valency of iron and the formula of the chloride.

Answer: 3 and FeCl₃

Mass of chlorine = 16.25 − 5.6 = 10.65 g. n(Fe) = 5.6/56 = 0.1 mol. n(Cl) = 10.65/35.5 = 0.3 mol. Ratio Fe : Cl = 1 : 3, so the formula is FeCl₃ and Fe is trivalent.

Common error: Exception: iron shows variable valency (2 and 3). The data decide, not memory: 3 Cl per Fe means FeCl₃. Fe₃Cl contradicts the 1 : 3 ratio.

Set 6 · Empirical Formula from Percentage Composition

Q16  [A · Teacher model]

A compound contains 40.0% carbon, 6.7% hydrogen and 53.3% oxygen by mass. Find its empirical formula (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16).

Answer: CH₂O

Treat the percentages as grams in 100 g. Moles: C 40.0/12 = 3.33; H 6.7/1 = 6.7; O 53.3/16 = 3.33. Divide by the smallest (3.33): C 1 : H 2 : O 1. Empirical formula CH₂O.

Common error: C₂H₄O₂ is a molecular formula (a multiple). The empirical formula is the simplest whole-number ratio.

Q17  [B · Board variant (student)]

A compound contains 43.4% sodium, 11.3% carbon and 45.3% oxygen by mass. Find its empirical formula (Na = 23, C = 12, O = 16).

Answer: Na₂CO₃

Moles: Na 43.4/23 = 1.887; C 11.3/12 = 0.942; O 45.3/16 = 2.831. Divide by 0.942: Na 2.00 : C 1.00 : O 3.01. Empirical formula Na₂CO₃.

Common error: Always divide by the smallest number of moles, then read off the ratio.

Q18  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A compound contains 72.4% iron and 27.6% oxygen by mass. Find its empirical formula (Fe = 56, O = 16).

Answer: Fe₃O₄

Moles: Fe 72.4/56 = 1.293; O 27.6/16 = 1.725. Divide by 1.293: Fe 1 : O 1.33. A ratio of 1.33 = 4/3 is not whole, so multiply by 3: Fe 3 : O 4. Empirical formula Fe₃O₄.

Common error: Exception: if a ratio ends in .33 or .67, multiply by 3; if .5, multiply by 2. Rounding 1.33 to 1 gives FeO; 1.5 would give Fe₂O₃.

Set 7 · Empirical Formula from Reacting and Combustion Masses

Q19  [A · Teacher model]

2.4 g of carbon combines completely with 0.6 g of hydrogen to form a hydrocarbon. Find its empirical formula (C = 12, H = 1).

Answer: CH₃

n(C) = 2.4/12 = 0.2 mol. n(H) = 0.6/1 = 0.6 mol. Ratio C : H = 0.2 : 0.6 = 1 : 3. Empirical formula CH₃.

Common error: C₃H reverses the ratio. Masses are converted to moles of atoms before comparing.

Q20  [B · Board variant (student)]

A hydrocarbon of mass 0.42 g burns completely to give 1.32 g of carbon dioxide and 0.54 g of water. Find its empirical formula (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16).

Answer: CH₂

n(C) = n(CO₂) = 1.32/44 = 0.03 mol. n(H₂O) = 0.54/18 = 0.03 mol, and each H₂O holds 2 H atoms, so n(H) = 0.06 mol. Ratio C : H = 0.03 : 0.06 = 1 : 2. Empirical formula CH₂. (Check: 0.03 × 12 + 0.06 × 1 = 0.42 g.)

Common error: Exception: 1 mol of H₂O contains 2 mol of H atoms, but 1 mol of CO₂ contains only 1 mol of C. Using 0.03 for H gives CH.

Q21  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

0.92 g of an organic compound containing carbon, hydrogen and oxygen burns completely to give 1.76 g of carbon dioxide and 1.08 g of water. Find its empirical formula (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16).

Answer: C₂H₆O

C: 1.76/44 = 0.04 mol, mass 0.48 g. H: 1.08/18 = 0.06 mol H₂O, so 0.12 mol H, mass 0.12 g. O by difference: 0.92 − 0.48 − 0.12 = 0.32 g, which is 0.32/16 = 0.02 mol. Ratio C : H : O = 0.04 : 0.12 : 0.02 = 2 : 6 : 1. Empirical formula C₂H₆O.

Common error: Exception: the oxygen in the products comes mostly from the air, so the oxygen in the compound is found only by difference from the sample mass.

Set 8 · Molecular Formula from Empirical Formula and Molar Mass

Q22  [A · Teacher model]

A compound has empirical formula CH₂ and molar mass 70 g mol⁻¹. Find its molecular formula (C = 12, H = 1).

Answer: C₅H₁₀

Empirical formula mass = 12 + 2 = 14. n = 70/14 = 5. Molecular formula = (CH₂)₅ = C₅H₁₀.

Common error: Molecular formula = n × empirical formula, with n = molar mass ÷ empirical formula mass.

Q23  [B · Board variant (student)]

A compound of empirical formula CH₂O has a molar mass of 180 g mol⁻¹. Find its molecular formula (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16).

Answer: C₆H₁₂O₆

Empirical formula mass = 12 + 2 + 16 = 30. n = 180/30 = 6. Molecular formula = (CH₂O)₆ = C₆H₁₂O₆.

Common error: Multiply every subscript by n, not just the carbon.

Q24  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A hydrocarbon contains 92.3% carbon and 7.7% hydrogen by mass and has molar mass 78 g mol⁻¹. Find its molecular formula (C = 12, H = 1).

Answer: C₆H₆

Moles: C 92.3/12 = 7.69; H 7.7/1 = 7.7. Ratio 1 : 1, so the empirical formula is CH (mass 13). n = 78/13 = 6. Molecular formula = C₆H₆.

Common error: Two steps: composition gives the empirical formula; molar mass gives n. C₂H₂ (mass 26) and C₄H₄ (mass 52) are multiples of CH but do not match 78.

Set 9 · Vapour Density (vapour density = molar mass ÷ 2)

Q25  [A · Teacher model]

Calculate the vapour density of ammonia, NH₃. (N = 14, H = 1)

Answer: 8.5

M(NH₃) = 14 + 3 = 17. Vapour density = density of gas ÷ density of hydrogen = M/2 = 17/2 = 8.5.

Common error: 17 is the relative molecular mass, not the vapour density. Hydrogen gas is H₂ (mass 2), which is why we divide by 2.

Q26  [B · Board variant (student)]

A compound of empirical formula CH₂O has a vapour density of 30. Find its molecular formula (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16).

Answer: C₂H₄O₂

M = 2 × vapour density = 60. Empirical formula mass = 30. n = 60/30 = 2. Molecular formula = C₂H₄O₂.

Common error: CH₂O results from using 30 as the molar mass. Always double the vapour density first.

Q27  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A hydrocarbon contains 85.7% carbon by mass and has a vapour density of 28. Find its molecular formula (C = 12, H = 1).

Answer: C₄H₈

Hydrogen = 100 − 85.7 = 14.3%. Moles: C 85.7/12 = 7.14; H 14.3/1 = 14.3. Ratio C : H = 1 : 2, so the empirical formula is CH₂ (mass 14). M = 2 × 28 = 56. n = 56/14 = 4. Molecular formula = C₄H₈.

Common error: C₂H₄ (mass 28) comes from treating the vapour density as the molar mass.

Set 10 · Integrated: Gas Volume, Vapour Density and Formulae

Q28  [A · Teacher model]

7.0 g of a gas occupies 5.6 dm³ at STP. Find its vapour density (molar volume = 22.4 dm³).

Answer: 14

n = 5.6/22.4 = 0.25 mol. M = 7.0/0.25 = 28 g mol⁻¹. Vapour density = 28/2 = 14.

Common error: 28 is the molar mass; one more step (÷ 2) gives the vapour density.

Q29  [B · Board variant (student)]

What is the mass of 2.24 dm³ of a gas of vapour density 17, measured at STP? (molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: 3.4 g

M = 2 × 17 = 34 g mol⁻¹. n = 2.24/22.4 = 0.1 mol. m = 0.1 × 34 = 3.4 g.

Common error: 1.7 g uses the vapour density as the molar mass; 34 g is the mass of 22.4 dm³.

Q30  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A gaseous hydrocarbon contains 85.7% carbon by mass. 1.4 g of the gas occupies 1.12 dm³ at STP. Find its molecular formula (C = 12, H = 1; molar volume = 22.4 dm³).

Answer: C₂H₄

Composition: C 85.7/12 = 7.14 and H 14.3/1 = 14.3, ratio 1 : 2, so the empirical formula is CH₂ (mass 14). Molar mass: n = 1.12/22.4 = 0.05 mol; M = 1.4/0.05 = 28 g mol⁻¹. n(formula) = 28/14 = 2. Molecular formula = C₂H₄.

Common error: Composition gives the ratio and the gas volume gives the molar mass. CH₂ stops at the empirical formula.
