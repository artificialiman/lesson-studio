Part 1 — Exam Architecture & Strategy

1.1 The JAMB Mathematics Syllabus

The syllabus tests conceptual understanding, computational accuracy, and the ability to apply mathematical reasoning to real-world problems. The exam carries 40 questions drawn from five major sections:

  • Number and Numeration — number bases, fractions/decimals/approximations, indices, logarithms, surds, sets, ratio/proportion, percentages, interest, permutation and combination, probability.
  • Algebra and Functions — polynomials, quadratic equations, simultaneous equations, inequalities, sequences and series, variation, functions, binomial expansion, matrices.
  • Geometry and Trigonometry — angles, polygons, circles (theorems), area and volume, latitude and longitude, trigonometry ratios, sine and cosine rules, bearings, vectors.
  • Calculus — differentiation (polynomial, trig, exponential, logarithmic), applications of differentiation (rates, maxima/minima), integration (indefinite and definite), area under curves.
  • Statistics — mean/median/mode, measures of dispersion, cumulative frequency, histograms, probability distributions.

1.2 Paper Types & the Question Bank

Old system (pre-2013): Fixed paper types (A, B, C, D). First question asked "What type is this?"

CBT transition (2013–2024): Full computer-based testing. Code words like "DIBU" and "SELF" served as anti-cheating markers.

Modern system (2025+): Full-scale shuffling of both questions and answer options. Every candidate receives a unique permutation. The old alphabetical system is obsolete.

Key Fact

JAMB Mathematics maintains a large item bank. Each candidate sees roughly 40 questions per session. Exact prediction is statistically impossible — but the topic blueprint is structurally guaranteed.

3Geometry & Trigonometry (circle theorems, polygons, sine/cosine rules, bearings, vectors)~20–25%Diagram-heavy. Circle theorems appear without exception.

The 70% Rule

Algebra + Number + Geometry = over 65% of every exam. Add Calculus and you reach ~80%. This distribution is forced by the blueprint. Master these four blocks.

1.4 How JAMB Constructs the Exam

Test Blueprint (Table of Specification): Maps exactly how many questions come from each topic. Forces high-weight areas to appear every year.

Item Response Theory (IRT): Balances difficulty and discrimination across parallel test forms. Every candidate receives a statistically equivalent exam.

Part 6 — How to Use This Archive

Mastery Protocol
  1. Do the 50-question General Diagnostic first. Score each section. Identify your weakest block.
  2. Do the 20-question Algebra Diagnostic. Map your weak variations. Algebra is the highest-weight block — you cannot afford gaps here.
  3. Work through the 55-question Algebra Mastery Set in batches of 10. For every wrong answer, identify the variation and drill 10 more of that single type.
  4. Study the diagram supplement. Redraw every circle theorem, every 3D solid, and the parabola from memory.
  5. Complete the 25 diagram-based questions. These test visual reasoning — a skill JAMB rewards consistently.
  6. Do not move to the next topic until you score 50+/55 on the Algebra set and 22+/25 on the diagram questions.
