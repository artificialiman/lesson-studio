# 1. STOICHIOMETRY & THE MOLE
**Exam Frequency: 15-20% of all questions**

## CORE FORMULAS

**n = m/M** (moles = mass ÷ molar mass)  
**n = V/22.4** (gases at STP only)  
**n = C × V** (solutions, V in liters)  
**n = N/NA** (NA = 6.02 × 10²³)

---

## MOLAR MASS

Element: Use periodic table value  
Compound: Sum of (atoms × atomic mass)

H₂SO₄ = 2(1) + 32 + 4(16) = 98 g/mol

---

## EMPIRICAL → MOLECULAR FORMULA

1. Mass % → divide by atomic mass → moles
2. Divide all by smallest mole value
3. Multiply to get whole numbers → empirical formula
4. Molecular mass ÷ empirical mass = n
5. Molecular formula = n × empirical formula

---

## STOICHIOMETRIC CALCULATIONS

For: aA + bB → cC + dD

**Mole ratio = a : b : c : d**

Steps:
1. Balance equation
2. Convert given to moles
3. Use mole ratio
4. Convert to required unit

---

## LIMITING REAGENT

1. Calculate moles of each reactant
2. Divide each by its coefficient
3. Smallest = limiting reagent

**% Yield = (actual/theoretical) × 100**

---

## GAS LAWS (STP)

**STP:** 273K, 1 atm  
**1 mole gas = 22.4 L at STP**

**PV = nRT**  
R = 0.0821 L·atm/(mol·K)  
T must be in Kelvin

---

## CONCENTRATION

**M = n/V** (molarity)  
**C₁V₁ = C₂V₂** (dilution)  
**ppm = (mass solute/mass solution) × 10⁶**

---

## COMMON EXAM TRAPS

❌ Not balancing first  
❌ Using 24 L instead of 22.4 L at STP  
❌ Forgetting °C → K conversion  
❌ Atomic mass ≠ molar mass confusion

---

# 2. ATOMIC STRUCTURE & PERIODICITY
**Exam Frequency: 10-15%**

## ATOMIC BASICS

**Z = protons = electrons** (neutral atom)  
**A = protons + neutrons**

Notation: ᴬXᶻ or X-A

---

## ISOTOPES

Same Z, different A (different neutrons)

**RAM = Σ(isotope mass × % abundance)/100**

---

## ELECTRON CONFIGURATION

**Order:** 1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p...

**Rules:**
- Aufbau: Fill lowest first
- Pauli: Max 2 per orbital
- Hund: Fill singly before pairing

**Exceptions:**
- Cr: [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵
- Cu: [Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰

---

## PERIODIC TRENDS

**Atomic Radius:**  
Across → decreases | Down → increases

**Ionization Energy:**  
Across → increases | Down → decreases

**Electronegativity:**  
Across → increases | Down → decreases  
Most EN: Fluorine (4.0)

---

## IONS

**Cations:** Lose electrons (metals)  
Remove from highest n first

**Anions:** Gain electrons (nonmetals)

**Isoelectronic:** O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺ all = [Ne]

---

# 3. CHEMICAL BONDING
**Exam Frequency: 10-12%**

## BOND TYPES

**ΔEN Scale:**
- <0.4: Nonpolar covalent
- 0.4-1.7: Polar covalent
- >1.7: Ionic

---

## IONIC BONDING

Metal → nonmetal (electron transfer)

**Properties:**
- High melting point
- Conduct when molten/dissolved
- Brittle crystals
- Soluble in water

---

## COVALENT BONDING

Nonmetal + nonmetal (electron sharing)

**Strength:** Triple > Double > Single

**Properties:**
- Lower melting points
- Don't conduct electricity
- Soluble in nonpolar solvents

---

## LEWIS STRUCTURES

1. Count valence electrons
2. Connect with single bonds
3. Complete octets on outer atoms
4. Remaining on central atom
5. Form multiple bonds if needed

**Exceptions:**
- H: 2 electrons only
- B, Be: Can be <8
- P, S, Cl: Can be >8 (period 3+)

---

## MOLECULAR SHAPES (VSEPR)

| Pairs (B/L) | Shape | Angle | Example |
|-------------|-------|-------|---------|
| 2/0 | Linear | 180° | CO₂ |
| 3/0 | Trigonal planar | 120° | BF₃ |
| 4/0 | Tetrahedral | 109.5° | CH₄ |
| 3/1 | Pyramidal | <109.5° | NH₃ |
| 2/2 | Bent | <109.5° | H₂O |

---

## MOLECULAR POLARITY

Polar bonds + asymmetric = Polar  
Polar bonds + symmetric = Nonpolar

CO₂: Linear (symmetric) → Nonpolar  
H₂O: Bent (asymmetric) → Polar

---

## INTERMOLECULAR FORCES

**Weakest → Strongest:**

1. London dispersion (all molecules)
2. Dipole-dipole (polar molecules)
3. Hydrogen bonding (H-N, H-O, H-F)

H-bonding explains water's high BP

---

# 4. ACIDS, BASES & SALTS
**Exam Frequency: 12-15%**

## DEFINITIONS

**Arrhenius:**
- Acid: H⁺ in water
- Base: OH⁻ in water

**Brønsted-Lowry:**
- Acid: H⁺ donor
- Base: H⁺ acceptor

---

## STRONG VS WEAK

**Strong Acids (memorize):**  
HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃, H₂SO₄, HClO₄

**Strong Bases:**  
Group 1 hydroxides (NaOH, KOH)  
Group 2 hydroxides (Ca(OH)₂, Ba(OH)₂)

**Weak:** Everything else

---

## pH SCALE

**pH = -log[H⁺]**  
**pOH = -log[OH⁻]**

**pH + pOH = 14** (at 25°C)

pH < 7: Acidic  
pH = 7: Neutral  
pH > 7: Basic

---

## NEUTRALIZATION

**Acid + Base → Salt + Water**

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

**Titration formula:**  
**nₐVₐ/nᵦVᵦ = Mₐ/Mᵦ**

---

## SALT PREPARATION

**Soluble salts:**
- Acid + metal
- Acid + base
- Acid + carbonate

**Insoluble salts:**
- Precipitation (double decomposition)

---

## INDICATORS

**Litmus:**  
Red in acid, Blue in base

**Methyl Orange:**  
Red in acid, Yellow in base

**Phenolphthalein:**  
Colorless in acid, Pink in base

---

## COMMON EXAM QUESTIONS

- pH calculations
- Neutralization equations
- Titration calculations
- Salt solubility rules
- Indicator color changes

# 5. REDOX & ELECTROCHEMISTRY
**Exam Frequency: 12-15%**

## OXIDATION STATES

**Rules (priority order):**
1. Free element = 0
2. Ion = its charge
3. F = -1 always
4. H = +1 (except -1 with metals)
5. O = -2 (except -1 in peroxides)
6. Group 1 = +1, Group 2 = +2
7. Sum = overall charge

---

## OIL RIG

**Oxidation Is Loss** (of electrons)  
**Reduction Is Gain** (of electrons)

**Oxidized** = reducing agent  
**Reduced** = oxidizing agent

---

## BALANCING REDOX (ACIDIC)

1. Split into half-reactions
2. Balance atoms (not O, H)
3. Balance O with H₂O
4. Balance H with H⁺
5. Balance charge with e⁻
6. Equalize electrons
7. Add, cancel common

**Basic:** Add OH⁻ to neutralize H⁺

---

## ELECTROCHEMICAL CELLS

**Galvanic:** Spontaneous, makes electricity  
**Electrolytic:** Non-spontaneous, needs electricity

**Anode:** Oxidation, negative (galvanic)  
**Cathode:** Reduction, positive (galvanic)

**E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode**

E° > 0 → Spontaneous  
E° < 0 → Non-spontaneous

---

## FARADAY'S LAWS

**Q = It**

**Moles = It/nF**

Where:
- I = current (A)
- t = time (s)
- n = electrons transferred
- F = 96,500 C/mol

---

## COMMON APPLICATIONS

- Batteries
- Electroplating
- Purification of metals
- Water electrolysis
- Rusting (corrosion)

# 7. CHEMICAL EQUILIBRIUM
**Exam Frequency: 8-10%**

## EQUILIBRIUM CONSTANT

For: aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD

**Kc = [C]^c[D]^d / [A]^a[B]^b**

Exclude pure solids/liquids

**Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn** (gases)

---

## K INTERPRETATION

K >> 1: Products favored  
K ≈ 1: Equal amounts  
K << 1: Reactants favored

**Only temperature changes K**

---

## LE CHATELIER'S PRINCIPLE

System shifts to oppose stress

**Add reactant:** Shift right →  
**Remove product:** Shift right →  
**Increase pressure:** Shift to fewer moles  
**Increase temperature:**
- Endo: Shift right
- Exo: Shift left

**Catalyst:** No shift, faster equilibrium

---

## Q vs K

**Q = current [products]/[reactants]**

Q < K → Forward shift  
Q = K → At equilibrium  
Q > K → Reverse shift

---

## ICE TABLE

```
     A  +  B  ⇌  C
I:   a₀    b₀    c₀
C:   -x    -x    +x
E:  a₀-x  b₀-x  c₀+x
```

# 8. REACTION RATES (KINETICS)
**Exam Frequency: 5-8%**

## FACTORS AFFECTING RATE

1. **Concentration:** Higher → faster
2. **Temperature:** Higher → faster (rate doubles per 10°C)
3. **Surface area:** Larger → faster
4. **Catalyst:** Lowers activation energy
5. **Pressure (gases):** Higher → faster

---

## COLLISION THEORY

Molecules must collide with:
- Sufficient energy (≥ Ea)
- Proper orientation

---

## RATE LAW

**Rate = k[A]^m[B]^n**

m, n = reaction orders (experimental only)  
k = rate constant (temperature-dependent)

---

## ACTIVATION ENERGY

**Arrhenius:**  
k = Ae^(-Ea/RT)

Or: ln(k₂/k₁) = -Ea/R(1/T₂ - 1/T₁)

Higher Ea → slower reaction  
Catalyst lowers Ea

---

# 9. SEPARATION TECHNIQUES
**Exam Frequency: 8-10%**

## METHODS

**Filtration:** Separate insoluble solid from liquid

**Evaporation:** Remove solvent, get solid

**Distillation:** Separate by boiling points
- Simple: Large BP difference
- Fractional: Small BP difference

**Crystallization:** Purify solid by controlled cooling

**Chromatography:** Separate based on movement through medium

**Sublimation:** Solid → Gas → Solid (no liquid)

---

## MIXTURES VS COMPOUNDS

**Mixture:**
- Variable composition
- Separated physically
- Properties of components retained

**Compound:**
- Fixed composition
- Separated chemically only
- New properties

# 10. THERMOCHEMISTRY
**Exam Frequency: 5-8%**

## ENTHALPY CHANGE (ΔH)

**Exothermic:** ΔH < 0 (heat released)  
**Endothermic:** ΔH > 0 (heat absorbed)

---

## HESS'S LAW

**ΔH(overall) = ΣΔH(steps)**

Total enthalpy change independent of pathway

---

## CALCULATIONS

**q = mcΔT**

- q = heat (J)
- m = mass (g)
- c = specific heat capacity
- ΔT = temperature change (K or °C)

---

## STANDARD ENTHALPY

**ΔH°f:** Formation (from elements)  
**ΔH°c:** Combustion  
**ΔH°neut:** Neutralization

# 11. WATER & HARDNESS
**Exam Frequency: 3-5%**

## TYPES OF HARDNESS

**Temporary Hardness:**
- Caused by: Ca(HCO₃)₂, Mg(HCO₃)₂
- Removed by: Boiling
- Reaction: Ca(HCO₃)₂ → CaCO₃↓ + H₂O + CO₂

**Permanent Hardness:**
- Caused by: CaSO₄, MgSO₄, CaCl₂, MgCl₂
- NOT removed by boiling
- Removed by: Adding washing soda (Na₂CO₃) or ion exchange

---

## REMOVAL METHODS

**Boiling:** Temporary only

**Washing Soda (Na₂CO₃):**
CaSO₄ + Na₂CO₃ → CaCO₃↓ + Na₂SO₄

**Ion Exchange (Permutit):**
Exchanges Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ for Na⁺

**Distillation:** Removes all impurities (both types)

---

## DISADVANTAGES OF HARD WATER

- Wastes soap (forms scum)
- Scale in pipes/boilers
- Higher fuel costs (scale reduces heat transfer)

# 12. SOLUBILITY & Ksp
**Exam Frequency: 3-5%**

## SOLUBILITY PRODUCT (Ksp)

For: AB(s) ⇌ A⁺(aq) + B⁻(aq)

**Ksp = [A⁺][B⁻]**

Higher Ksp = more soluble

---

## COMMON ION EFFECT

Adding common ion decreases solubility

Example: Adding NaCl to AgCl solution
- Increases [Cl⁻]
- Shifts left (Le Chatelier)
- AgCl precipitates

---

## PRECIPITATION

**If Q > Ksp:** Precipitate forms
**If Q < Ksp:** No precipitate
**If Q = Ksp:** Saturated solution

---

## SELECTIVE PRECIPITATION

Add reagent that forms precipitate with only one ion

Example: Separate Ag⁺ from Cu²⁺ using HCl
- AgCl precipitates (Ksp very low)
- Cu²⁺ remains in solution

---

# 14. RADIOACTIVITY
**Exam Frequency: 4-6%**

## TYPES OF DECAY

**Alpha (α):** ⁴He nucleus, +2 charge
- Stopped by paper
- A decreases by 4, Z decreases by 2

**Beta (β):** Electron, -1 charge
- Stopped by aluminum
- A same, Z increases by 1

**Gamma (γ):** EM radiation, no charge
- Stopped by lead
- A and Z unchanged

---

## NUCLEAR EQUATIONS

**Alpha decay:**
²³⁸U₉₂ → ²³⁴Th₉₀ + ⁴He₂

**Beta decay:**
¹⁴C₆ → ¹⁴N₇ + ⁰e₋₁

**Balance:** Mass numbers and atomic numbers must balance

---

## HALF-LIFE (t₁/₂)

Time for half of sample to decay

**N = N₀(½)ⁿ**

Where: n = t/t₁/₂

Or: **N = N₀e^(-λt)**

---

## APPLICATIONS

- Carbon dating (¹⁴C)
- Medical tracers
- Cancer treatment
- Smoke detectors
- Food preservation

---

# 15. METALS & EXTRACTION
**Exam Frequency: 6-8%**

## REACTIVITY SERIES (High → Low)

K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Ag > Au

**More reactive:**
- Extracted by electrolysis
- React with water/acids
- Displace less reactive metals

---

## EXTRACTION METHODS

**Electrolysis:** K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al
- Most reactive metals
- From molten compounds

**Reduction with Carbon:** Zn, Fe, Pb
- Moderately reactive
- From oxides in blast furnace

**No reduction needed:** Cu, Ag, Au
- Least reactive
- Found native or easily reduced

---

## BLAST FURNACE (Iron)

**Raw materials:**
- Iron ore (Fe₂O₃)
- Coke (C)
- Limestone (CaCO₃)

**Reactions:**
1. C + O₂ → CO₂
2. CO₂ + C → 2CO
3. Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
4. CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
5. CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ (slag)

**Product:** Pig iron (impure)

---

## ALUMINUM EXTRACTION

**Hall-Héroult Process:**
- Ore: Bauxite (Al₂O₃)
- Dissolved in molten cryolite
- Electrolysis at 900°C

**Cathode:** Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al
**Anode:** 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻ (carbon anode burns)

---

## CORROSION

**Rusting (iron):**
- Needs O₂ + H₂O
- Forms Fe₂O₃·xH₂O

**Prevention:**
- Painting, oiling (barrier)
- Galvanizing (zinc coating)
- Sacrificial anode (more reactive metal)
- Cathodic protection

---

# 16. INDUSTRIAL PROCESSES
**Exam Frequency: 5-7%**

## HABER PROCESS (Ammonia)

**N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃** (ΔH = -92 kJ/mol)

**Conditions:**
- Temperature: 450-500°C (compromise)
- Pressure: 200-300 atm (high)
- Catalyst: Iron

**Le Chatelier:**
- High pressure favors products (4 moles → 2 moles)
- Low temp favors products (exothermic)
- But low temp = slow rate → use 450°C

---

## CONTACT PROCESS (Sulfuric Acid)

**Step 1:** S + O₂ → SO₂

**Step 2:** 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃
- Catalyst: V₂O₅
- Temperature: 450°C
- Pressure: 2 atm

**Step 3:** SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇ (oleum)

**Step 4:** H₂S₂O₇ + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄

---

## FRACTIONAL DISTILLATION (Petroleum)

**Fractions (top to bottom):**

| Fraction | Boiling Range | Carbon Atoms | Use |
|----------|---------------|--------------|-----|
| Gas | <40°C | C₁-C₄ | Fuel |
| Petrol | 40-100°C | C₅-C₁₀ | Cars |
| Naphtha | 100-150°C | C₇-C₁₄ | Chemicals |
| Kerosene | 150-250°C | C₁₀-C₁₆ | Aviation fuel |
| Diesel | 250-350°C | C₁₅-C₂₅ | Engines |
| Lubricating oil | >350°C | C₂₀-C₅₀ | Lubrication |
| Bitumen | Residue | >C₅₀ | Roads |

**Cracking:** Breaking large hydrocarbons into smaller ones
- Thermal or catalytic
- Produces alkenes

# 17. QUALITATIVE ANALYSIS
**Exam Frequency: 6-8%**

## CATION TESTS

**Flame Tests:**
- Na⁺: Yellow
- K⁺: Lilac/violet
- Ca²⁺: Brick red
- Cu²⁺: Blue-green
- Ba²⁺: Apple green

**Precipitation:**
- Add NaOH to solution

| Cation | NaOH (few drops) | NaOH (excess) |
|--------|------------------|---------------|
| Fe²⁺ | Green ppt | Insoluble |
| Fe³⁺ | Brown ppt | Insoluble |
| Al³⁺ | White ppt | Soluble |
| Zn²⁺ | White ppt | Soluble |
| Cu²⁺ | Blue ppt | Insoluble |
| Pb²⁺ | White ppt | Soluble |

**Add NH₃:**
- Cu²⁺: Deep blue complex
- Ag⁺: White ppt (soluble in excess NH₃)

---

## ANION TESTS

**Carbonate (CO₃²⁻):**
Add acid → CO₂ gas (turns limewater milky)

**Sulfate (SO₄²⁻):**
Add BaCl₂ → white ppt (BaSO₄)

**Chloride (Cl⁻):**
Add AgNO₃ → white ppt (AgCl, soluble in NH₃)

**Bromide (Br⁻):**
Add AgNO₃ → cream ppt (AgBr, slightly soluble in NH₃)

**Iodide (I⁻):**
Add AgNO₃ → yellow ppt (AgI, insoluble in NH₃)

**Nitrate (NO₃⁻):**
Brown ring test (Fe²⁺ + conc. H₂SO₄)

# 18. ORGANIC CHEMISTRY - ADVANCED
**Exam Frequency: 8-10%**

## ALCOHOLS (Alkanols)

**Primary (1°):** OH on end carbon
**Secondary (2°):** OH on middle carbon
**Tertiary (3°):** OH on carbon with 3 bonds

**Oxidation:**
- 1° → Aldehyde → Carboxylic acid
- 2° → Ketone (stops here)
- 3° → No reaction

**Esterification:**
Alcohol + Acid → Ester + Water

CH₃OH + CH₃COOH → CH₃COOCH₃ + H₂O

---

## CARBOXYLIC ACIDS

**General formula:** CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH or RCOOH

**Properties:**
- Weak acids (partially ionize)
- React with metals: 2CH₃COOH + Mg → (CH₃COO)₂Mg + H₂
- React with bases: CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O
- React with carbonates: 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂

---

## ESTERS

**Formation:** Acid + Alcohol → Ester + Water (conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst)

**Properties:**
- Sweet/fruity smell
- Used in perfumes, flavoring

**Hydrolysis:**
- Acid hydrolysis: Ester + H₂O → Acid + Alcohol
- Alkaline hydrolysis (Saponification): Ester + NaOH → Salt + Alcohol

---

## FATS & OILS

**Triglycerides:** Glycerol + 3 fatty acids

**Saturated fats:** No C=C bonds (solid at room temp, animal fats)
**Unsaturated fats:** C=C bonds (liquid at room temp, vegetable oils)

**Saponification:**
Fat/Oil + 3NaOH → Glycerol + 3 Soap molecules

**Soap structure:** Long hydrocarbon chain (hydrophobic) + COO⁻Na⁺ (hydrophilic)

---

## POLYMERS

**Addition Polymerization:**
- Monomers with C=C
- Double bond opens, monomers link
- Example: nC₂H₄ → (C₂H₄)ₙ (polythene)

**Condensation Polymerization:**
- Two different monomers
- Small molecule (H₂O, HCl) eliminated
- Example: Nylon, polyester, proteins

---

## PROTEINS

**Amino acids:** H₂N-CHR-COOH

**Peptide bond:** -CO-NH- (condensation of -COOH and -NH₂)

**Structure:**
- Primary: Amino acid sequence
- Secondary: Helix/sheet (H-bonds)
- Tertiary: 3D folding
- Quaternary: Multiple chains

**Denaturation:** Loss of structure (heat, pH, chemicals)

---

## ACID RAIN

**Formation:**
- SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃ (sulfurous acid)
- SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄ (sulfuric acid)
- NO₂ + H₂O → HNO₃ (nitric acid)

**Effects:**
- Corrodes buildings, statues
- Kills fish (lakes become acidic)
- Damages plants
- Leaches nutrients from soil

**Control:**
- Use low-sulfur fuels
- Flue gas desulfurization
- Catalytic converters (cars)

---

## GREENHOUSE EFFECT

**Greenhouse gases:** CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs

**Mechanism:**
1. Solar radiation reaches Earth
2. Earth emits infrared (heat)
3. Greenhouse gases trap heat
4. Temperature increases

**Effects:**
- Global warming
- Ice caps melting
- Sea level rise
- Climate change

**Control:**
- Reduce fossil fuel use
- Renewable energy
- Reforestation

---

## OZONE LAYER

**Stratospheric O₃:** Protects from UV radiation

**Depletion:**
- CFCs break down O₃
- CFCl₃ + UV → CFCl₂ + Cl
- Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂
- ClO + O → Cl + O₂ (Cl regenerated = catalyst)

**Effects:**
- Increased UV → skin cancer
- Eye cataracts
- Immune suppression

**Solution:** Montreal Protocol (banned CFCs)

# 20. GAS LAWS - ADVANCED
**Exam Frequency: 4-6%**

## GRAHAM'S LAW (Diffusion/Effusion)

**Rate₁/Rate₂ = √(M₂/M₁)**

Where M = molar mass

Lighter gases diffuse faster

---

## DALTON'S LAW (Partial Pressures)

**Ptotal = P₁ + P₂ + P₃ + ...**

For mixture of non-reacting gases

**Partial pressure:** Pᵢ = Xᵢ × Ptotal

Where Xᵢ = mole fraction

---

## IDEAL VS REAL GASES

**Ideal gas assumptions:**
- No intermolecular forces
- Negligible particle volume
- Perfectly elastic collisions

**Real gases deviate at:**
- High pressure (volume matters)
- Low temperature (IMF matters)

**Van der Waals equation:** (P + a/V²)(V - b) = RT
- a = IMF correction
- b = volume correction

# 21. LAWS OF CHEMICAL COMBINATION
**Exam Frequency: 2-3%**

## LAW OF DEFINITE PROPORTIONS

Compound always has same elements in same mass ratio

H₂O always has H:O = 1:8 by mass

---

## LAW OF MULTIPLE PROPORTIONS

If two elements form multiple compounds, mass ratios are simple whole numbers

CO: C:O = 12:16 = 3:4
CO₂: C:O = 12:32 = 3:8
Ratio of O masses = 4:8 = 1:2 ✓

---

## LAW OF RECIPROCAL PROPORTIONS

Masses of elements combining with fixed mass of third element are in ratio they combine with each other

---

## GAY-LUSSAC'S LAW

Volumes of reacting gases (same T, P) are in simple whole number ratios

N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃
1 vol : 3 vol : 2 vol
