AFundamental and Derived Quantities and Units

Question 1

Which of the following is a fundamental quantity?

Solution

Length, mass, time, electric current, temperature, amount of substance and luminous intensity are the seven fundamental quantities.

Force, energy and pressure are derived.

Answer: Length (or mass, time, etc.).

Question 2

Derive the SI base unit of force from the equation F = ma.

Solution 
F = mass × acceleration
Unit of mass = kg
Unit of acceleration = m/s²
Unit of force = kg × m/s² = kg m s⁻² = newton (N)

Answer: kg m s⁻², which is the newton (N).

Question 3

Show that the equation s = ut + ½at² is dimensionally consistent.

Solution 
Left side: [s] = [L]
Right side, term 1: [u t] = [L T⁻¹] × [T] = [L]
Right side, term 2: [a t²] = [L T⁻²] × [T²] = [L]
All terms have dimensions [L]. The equation is dimensionally consistent.

Answer: Consistent, since every term reduces to [L].

Question 4

Find the dimensional formula for pressure, given P = F / A.

Solution 
[F] = [M L T⁻²]
[A] = [L²]
[P] = [M L T⁻²] / [L²] = [M L⁻¹ T⁻²]

Answer: [M L⁻¹ T⁻²].

Question 5

Convert a density of 4.2 g/cm³ to kg/m³.

Solution 
1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³
4.2 g/cm³ = 4.2 × 1000 = 4200 kg/m³

Answer: 4200 kg/m³.

Question 6

A quantity has dimensions [M L² T⁻³]. Identify it.

Solution 
[M L² T⁻²] = energy (or work)
[M L² T⁻³] = energy / time = power

Answer: Power.

Question 7

Which of the following equations is dimensionally incorrect? (i) v = u + at, (ii) s = ut + at², (iii) v² = u² + 2as.

Solution 
(i)  [L T⁻¹] = [L T⁻¹] + [L T⁻²][T] = [L T⁻¹] + [L T⁻¹]  ✓
(ii) [L] = [L T⁻¹][T] + [L T⁻²][T²] = [L] + [L]  ✓ (with ½ absorbed)
(iii)[L² T⁻²] = [L² T⁻²] + [L T⁻²][L] = [L² T⁻²] + [L² T⁻²]  ✓

Answer: All three are dimensionally consistent. The factor ½ is dimensionless and does not affect the check.

Question 8

Express 72 km/h in m/s.

Solution 
72 km/h = 72 × 1000 m / 3600 s
        = 72000 / 3600 = 20 m/s

Answer: 20 m/s.

BMeasurement of Mass, Weight, Length and Time

Question 9

Five readings of a length are 4.12 cm, 4.13 cm, 4.11 cm, 4.14 cm and 4.10 cm. Find the average length and state the spread of the readings.

Solution 
Average = (4.12 + 4.13 + 4.11 + 4.14 + 4.10) / 5
        = 20.60 / 5 = 4.12 cm
Spread = ±0.02 cm

Answer: Average = 4.12 cm, spread ±0.02 cm.

Question 10

A body has a mass of 8 kg. Find its weight on Earth (g = 9.8 m/s²) and on the Moon (g = 1.6 m/s²).

Solution 
On Earth: W = mg = 8 × 9.8 = 78.4 N
On Moon:  W = mg = 8 × 1.6 = 12.8 N
Mass remains 8 kg on both.

Answer: 78.4 N on Earth; 12.8 N on the Moon.

Question 11

A rectangle has length (12.0 ± 0.1) cm and width (4.0 ± 0.1) cm. Find the area and its uncertainty.

Solution 
Area = 12.0 × 4.0 = 48.0 cm²
Fractional error = 0.1/12.0 + 0.1/4.0 = 0.0083 + 0.025 = 0.0333
Absolute error = 0.0333 × 48.0 = 1.6 cm²
Area = (48.0 ± 1.6) cm²

Answer: (48.0 ± 1.6) cm².

Question 12

A stopwatch with least count 0.1 s times 25 oscillations of a pendulum as 50.0 s. Find the period and its uncertainty.

Solution 
Period = 50.0 / 25 = 2.00 s
Uncertainty per oscillation = 0.1 / 25 = 0.004 s
Period = (2.00 ± 0.004) s

Answer: (2.00 ± 0.004) s.

Question 13

How many significant figures are present in 0.00450 m?

Solution 
Leading zeros are not significant.
The digits 4, 5 and the trailing 0 are significant.
That is 3 significant figures.

Answer: 3 significant figures.

Question 14

The mass of a body is measured five times as 2.50 kg, 2.51 kg, 2.49 kg, 2.50 kg and 2.50 kg. A beam balance reads to 0.01 kg. State whether the measurements are accurate, precise, or both.

Solution 
The readings cluster tightly around 2.50 kg, so the measurement is precise.
Since 2.50 kg is close to the expected value for such a body, the measurement is also accurate.

Answer: Both accurate and precise.

Question 15

A vernier caliper has a zero error of +0.05 cm. If a reading is 3.20 cm on the scale, what is the corrected length?

Solution 
Corrected length = observed reading − zero error
Corrected = 3.20 − 0.05 = 3.15 cm

Answer: 3.15 cm.

Question 16

Why is the period of a pendulum timed over many oscillations rather than just one?

Solution

The reaction time of the observer introduces a fixed uncertainty each time the stopwatch is started or stopped. By timing many oscillations, that fixed uncertainty is shared among all of them, so the uncertainty per oscillation is reduced.

Answer: To reduce the effect of the observer's reaction-time error on the measurement of one period.

CPosition, Distance and Displacement

Question 17

A boy walks 10 m east, then 4 m west. Find the distance travelled and the displacement.

Solution 
Distance = 10 + 4 = 14 m
Displacement = 10 − 4 = 6 m east

Answer: Distance = 14 m; displacement = 6 m east.

Question 18

A runner completes one lap of a circular track of radius 70 m. Find the distance and the displacement.

Solution 
Distance = circumference = 2πr = 2π(70) ≈ 440 m
Displacement = 0 (start and finish coincide)

Answer: Distance ≈ 440 m; displacement = 0.

Question 19

A girl walks 6 m east, then 8 m north. Find her displacement.

Solution 
Magnitude = √(6² + 8²) = √100 = 10 m
Direction: tan θ = 8/6 = 1.333, θ = 53.1° north of east

Answer: 10 m at 53.1° north of east.

Question 20

A cyclist travels 40 km east in 2 h, then 40 km west in 2 h. Find the average speed and the average velocity.

Solution 
Total distance = 40 + 40 = 80 km
Total time = 2 + 2 = 4 h
Average speed = 80 / 4 = 20 km/h
Displacement = 40 − 40 = 0 km
Average velocity = 0 / 4 = 0 km/h

Answer: Average speed = 20 km/h; average velocity = 0.

Question 21

A particle is at x = −3 m and moves to x = 7 m. Find the displacement.

Solution 
Δx = x₂ − x₁ = 7 − (−3) = 10 m

Answer: 10 m in the positive direction.

Question 22

A body moves 5 m north, then 5 m east, then 5 m south. Find the distance and the displacement.

Solution 
Distance = 5 + 5 + 5 = 15 m
Net north–south = 5 − 5 = 0
Net east = 5 m
Displacement = 5 m east

Answer: Distance = 15 m; displacement = 5 m east.

Question 23

An object is 20 m from a reference point. It moves to a point 12 m on the other side of the reference. Find the distance and the displacement.

Solution 
Distance = 20 + 12 = 32 m (total path length)
If we take the initial side as positive:
Displacement = −12 − 20 = −32 m
Magnitude = 32 m in the direction across the reference

Answer: Distance = 32 m; displacement = 32 m across the reference point.

Question 24

Explain, with one example, why distance and displacement are different quantities.

Solution

Distance is the total length of the path travelled, regardless of direction. Displacement is the straight-line change in position with direction. They have different values for any journey that changes direction.

Example: A person walks 4 m east then 4 m west. Distance = 8 m; displacement = 0.

DScalars and Vectors

Question 25

Classify each of the following as a scalar or a vector: mass, velocity, time, force, speed, displacement.

Solution 
Scalars: mass, time, speed
Vectors: velocity, force, displacement

Answer: As listed above.

Question 26

Two forces of 9 N and 12 N act at right angles. Find the resultant.

Solution 
R = √(9² + 12²) = √(81 + 144) = √225 = 15 N
Direction: tan θ = 12/9 = 1.333, θ = 53.1° from the 9 N force

Answer: 15 N at 53.1° from the 9 N force.

Question 27

A force of 80 N acts at 60° to the horizontal. Find its horizontal and vertical components.

Solution 
Horizontal = 80 cos 60° = 80 × 0.500 = 40 N
Vertical   = 80 sin 60° = 80 × 0.866 = 69.3 N

Answer: 40 N horizontal, 69.3 N vertical.

Question 28

Two forces of 6 N and 8 N act at 60° to each other. Find the resultant.

Solution 
R = √(6² + 8² + 2 × 6 × 8 × cos 60°)
R = √(36 + 64 + 48) = √148 = 12.2 N

Answer: 12.2 N.

Question 29

A body is acted on by 7 N east and 24 N north. What third force keeps it in equilibrium?

Solution 
Resultant of the two forces = √(7² + 24²) = √625 = 25 N
Direction: tan θ = 24/7 = 3.43, θ = 73.7° north of east
For equilibrium, the third force must be equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

Answer: 25 N at 73.7° south of west.

Question 30

Can two vectors of magnitudes 5 units and 3 units produce a resultant of (a) 8 units, (b) 2 units, (c) 10 units?

Solution 
Maximum resultant (same direction) = 5 + 3 = 8
Minimum resultant (opposite direction) = 5 − 3 = 2
All intermediate values between 2 and 8 are possible.
(a) 8 — yes (same direction)
(b) 2 — yes (opposite direction)
(c) 10 — no (exceeds the maximum)

Answer: (a) yes, (b) yes, (c) no.

Question 31

A vector of magnitude 20 makes an angle of 30° with the x-axis. Express it in component form.

Solution 
x-component = 20 cos 30° = 20 × 0.866 = 17.3
y-component = 20 sin 30° = 20 × 0.500 = 10.0
Vector = 17.3 i + 10.0 j

Answer: 17.3 i + 10.0 j.

Question 32

Three forces act on a body: 5 N east, 5 N north, and 5 N west. Find the resultant.

Solution 
East–west: 5 east − 5 west = 0
North–south: 5 north
Resultant = 5 N north

Answer: 5 N north.

Question 33

Explain why two forces of 5 N and 5 N can produce resultants of 10 N, 0 N, or 5√2 N.

Solution 
Same direction: R = 5 + 5 = 10 N
Opposite direction: R = 5 − 5 = 0 N
At right angles: R = √(5² + 5²) = √50 = 5√2 N

Answer: Because vector addition depends on direction, not just magnitude.

ESpeed, Velocity and Acceleration

Question 34

A car travels 240 km in 3 h. Find its average speed.

Solution 
v̄ = d / t = 240 / 3 = 80 km/h

Answer: 80 km/h.

Question 35

A car accelerates from 12 m/s to 28 m/s in 4 s. Find its acceleration.

Solution 
a = (v − u) / t = (28 − 12) / 4 = 16 / 4 = 4 m/s²

Answer: 4 m/s².

Question 36

A train moving at 30 m/s brakes uniformly and stops in 10 s. Find its retardation.

Solution 
a = (0 − 30) / 10 = −3 m/s²
Retardation = 3 m/s²

Answer: 3 m/s².

Question 37

A body moves with uniform acceleration from 5 m/s to 15 m/s in 4 s. Find the distance travelled.

Solution 
Average velocity = (5 + 15) / 2 = 10 m/s
Distance = 10 × 4 = 40 m

Answer: 40 m.

Question 38

Two cars move in the same direction at 25 m/s and 18 m/s. Find their relative velocity. Then find it when they move in opposite directions.

Solution 
Same direction: 25 − 18 = 7 m/s
Opposite direction: 25 + 18 = 43 m/s

Answer: 7 m/s and 43 m/s.

Question 39

A body moves 600 m in 30 s, then 400 m in 20 s. Find the average speed for the whole journey.

Solution 
Total distance = 600 + 400 = 1000 m
Total time = 30 + 20 = 50 s
Average speed = 1000 / 50 = 20 m/s

Answer: 20 m/s.

Question 40

Can a body have zero velocity and non-zero acceleration? Give an example.

Solution

Yes. Velocity is zero at the highest point of a ball thrown vertically upwards, but the acceleration due to gravity is still 9.8 m/s² downwards.

Answer: Yes — at the top of the flight of a ball thrown vertically upwards.

Question 41

A body moves at 20 m/s for 10 s, then at 10 m/s for 15 s. Find the average speed for the whole journey.

Solution 
Distance 1 = 20 × 10 = 200 m
Distance 2 = 10 × 15 = 150 m
Total distance = 350 m
Total time = 25 s
Average speed = 350 / 25 = 14 m/s

Answer: 14 m/s.

FEquations of Uniformly Accelerated Motion

Question 42

A car starts from rest and accelerates at 4 m/s² for 6 s. Find its final velocity and the distance travelled.

Solution 
v = u + at = 0 + 4 × 6 = 24 m/s
s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½ × 4 × 36 = 72 m

Answer: v = 24 m/s; s = 72 m.

Question 43

A car travelling at 25 m/s decelerates uniformly at 5 m/s². Find the distance it travels before stopping.

Solution 
v² = u² + 2as  →  0 = 25² + 2(−5)s
10s = 625  →  s = 62.5 m

Answer: 62.5 m.

Question 44

A stone is dropped from a height of 125 m. Find the time to reach the ground and the velocity just before impact. Use g = 10 m/s².

Solution 
h = ½gt²  →  125 = ½ × 10 × t²  →  t² = 25  →  t = 5 s
v = gt = 10 × 5 = 50 m/s

Answer: t = 5 s; v = 50 m/s.

Question 45

A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 30 m/s. Find the maximum height reached and the total time of flight. Use g = 10 m/s².

Solution 
At maximum height v = 0:
0 = 30² − 2 × 10 × h  →  h = 900 / 20 = 45 m
Time to reach top: t = 30 / 10 = 3 s
Total time of flight = 2 × 3 = 6 s

Answer: Height = 45 m; total time = 6 s.

Question 46

A train accelerates from rest at 2 m/s² for 15 s, then travels at constant velocity for 30 s, then decelerates uniformly to rest in 10 s. Find the total distance travelled.

Solution 
Phase 1: v = 0 + 2 × 15 = 30 m/s
         s₁ = ½(0 + 30) × 15 = 225 m
Phase 2: s₂ = 30 × 30 = 900 m
Phase 3: s₃ = ½(30 + 0) × 10 = 150 m
Total = 225 + 900 + 150 = 1275 m

Answer: 1275 m.

Question 47

A projectile is launched at 40 m/s at 30° to the horizontal. Find its time of flight, maximum height and range. Use g = 10 m/s².

Solution 
u_y = 40 sin 30° = 20 m/s
u_x = 40 cos 30° = 34.6 m/s
Time of flight T = 2u_y / g = 2 × 20 / 10 = 4 s
Maximum height H = u_y² / 2g = 400 / 20 = 20 m
Range R = u² sin 2θ / g = 1600 × sin 60° / 10
        = 1600 × 0.866 / 10 = 138.6 m

Answer: T = 4 s; H = 20 m; R ≈ 138.6 m.

GGraphical Analysis of Motion

Question 48

A distance–time graph is a straight line passing through (0, 0) and (8, 32). Find the speed.

Solution 
Speed = slope = (32 − 0) / (8 − 0) = 4 m/s

Answer: 4 m/s.

Question 49

A velocity–time graph rises uniformly from 0 to 16 m/s over 4 s, then remains at 16 m/s for 8 s. Find the total displacement.

Solution 
Phase 1 (triangle): area = ½ × 4 × 16 = 32 m
Phase 2 (rectangle): area = 8 × 16 = 128 m
Total displacement = 32 + 128 = 160 m

Answer: 160 m.

Question 50

A velocity–time graph has three straight segments: (0–5 s) from 0 to 20 m/s; (5–12 s) constant at 20 m/s; (12–18 s) from 20 m/s to 0. Find (a) the acceleration in the first segment, (b) the deceleration in the third segment, and (c) the total distance travelled.

Solution 
(a) a = (20 − 0) / 5 = 4 m/s²
(b) a = (0 − 20) / 6 = −3.33 m/s²  →  deceleration = 3.33 m/s²
(c) Distance = area under graph
    Segment 1: ½ × 5 × 20 = 50 m
    Segment 2: 7 × 20 = 140 m
    Segment 3: ½ × 6 × 20 = 60 m
    Total = 50 + 140 + 60 = 250 m

Answer: (a) 4 m/s²; (b) 3.33 m/s²; (c) 250 m.
