Set 1 · Molar Mass of Compounds (add every atom, apply every bracket)

Q1  [A · Teacher model]

Calculate the molar mass of calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂. (Ca = 40, O = 16, H = 1)

Answer: 74 g mol⁻¹

M = 40 + 2(16 + 1) = 40 + 34 = 74 g mol⁻¹.

Common error: 57 g mol⁻¹ ignores the bracket: the subscript 2 multiplies both O and H.

Q2  [B · Board variant (student)]

Calculate the molar mass of aluminium sulphate, Al₂(SO₄)₃. (Al = 27, S = 32, O = 16)

Answer: 342 g mol⁻¹

M = 2(27) + 3(32 + 4 × 16) = 54 + 3(96) = 54 + 288 = 342 g mol⁻¹.

Common error: 150 g mol⁻¹ ignores the 3 outside the bracket: there are 3 sulphate groups.

Q3  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

Calculate the molar mass of Mohr's salt, (NH₄)₂SO₄·FeSO₄·6H₂O. (N = 14, H = 1, S = 32, O = 16, Fe = 56)

Answer: 392 g mol⁻¹

(NH₄)₂SO₄ = 2(18) + 96 = 132. FeSO₄ = 56 + 96 = 152. 6H₂O = 6 × 18 = 108. M = 132 + 152 + 108 = 392 g mol⁻¹.

Common error: 284 g mol⁻¹ leaves out the water of crystallisation.

Set 2 · Moles from Mass (n = m / M)

Q4  [A · Teacher model]

How many moles of water molecules are present in 36 g of water? (H = 1, O = 16)

Answer: 2 mol

M(H₂O) = 2(1) + 16 = 18 g mol⁻¹. n = m/M = 36/18 = 2 mol.

Common error: 36 mol skips the division by M. 0.5 mol inverts the ratio (18/36).

Q5  [B · Board variant (student)]

How many moles of sodium carbonate are present in 5.3 g of Na₂CO₃? (Na = 23, C = 12, O = 16)

Answer: 0.05 mol

M(Na₂CO₃) = 2(23) + 12 + 3(16) = 106 g mol⁻¹. n = 5.3/106 = 0.05 mol.

Common error: 0.10 mol uses M = 53 (counting one Na). 20 mol inverts m/M. Count every atom with its subscript.

Q6  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

What is the mass of 0.25 mol of hydrated copper(II) sulphate, CuSO₄·5H₂O? (Cu = 64, S = 32, O = 16, H = 1)

Answer: 62.5 g

M = 64 + 32 + 4(16) + 5(18) = 160 + 90 = 250 g mol⁻¹. m = nM = 0.25 × 250 = 62.5 g.

Common error: Exception: water of crystallisation is part of the molar mass. 40 g is the anhydrous salt (0.25 × 160); 22.5 g is the water alone; 250 g is the mass of 1 mol.

Set 3 · Mass from Moles (m = n × M), Including an Element Inside a Compound

Q7  [A · Teacher model]

What is the mass of 0.5 mol of sodium hydroxide, NaOH? (Na = 23, O = 16, H = 1)

Answer: 20 g

M(NaOH) = 23 + 16 + 1 = 40 g mol⁻¹. m = nM = 0.5 × 40 = 20 g.

Common error: 40 g is the mass of 1 mol, not 0.5 mol.

Q8  [B · Board variant (student)]

What is the mass of 0.2 mol of aluminium sulphate, Al₂(SO₄)₃? (Al = 27, S = 32, O = 16)

Answer: 68.4 g

M = 2(27) + 3(96) = 342 g mol⁻¹. m = 0.2 × 342 = 68.4 g.

Common error: 30 g comes from M = 150, which ignores the 3 outside the bracket.

Q9  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

What mass of sulphur is present in 0.2 mol of aluminium sulphate, Al₂(SO₄)₃? (S = 32)

Answer: 19.2 g

Each formula unit contains 3 S atoms, so n(S) = 3 × 0.2 = 0.6 mol. m(S) = 0.6 × 32 = 19.2 g.

Common error: 6.4 g counts one S per formula unit. 68.4 g is the mass of the whole compound.

Set 4 · Moles from the Volume of a Liquid (convert to mass with density first)

Q10  [A · Teacher model]

Calculate the number of moles of water in 18 cm³ of water. (density of water = 1 g cm⁻³; H = 1, O = 16)

Answer: 1.0 mol

Mass = density × volume = 1 × 18 = 18 g. M(H₂O) = 18 g mol⁻¹. n = 18/18 = 1.0 mol.

Common error: 18 mol treats the volume in cm³ as if it were moles.

Q11  [B · Board variant (student)]

How many molecules are present in 36 cm³ of water? (density = 1 g cm⁻³; H = 1, O = 16; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 1.20 × 10²⁴

Mass = 36 g. n = 36/18 = 2 mol. N = 2 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.20 × 10²⁴ molecules.

Common error: 6.02 × 10²³ is the answer for 1 mol (18 cm³), not 2 mol.

Q12  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

How many moles of ethanol, C₂H₅OH, are present in 90 cm³ of ethanol of density 0.8 g cm⁻³? (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16)

Answer: 1.57 mol

Mass = 0.8 × 90 = 72 g. M(C₂H₅OH) = 24 + 6 + 16 = 46 g mol⁻¹. n = 72/46 = 1.57 mol.

Common error: 1.96 mol uses 90 as a mass. Volume must be turned into mass with the density before dividing by M.

Set 5 · Avogadro's Constant and Particle Counts (N = nL)

Q13  [A · Teacher model]

How many molecules are present in 8 g of methane, CH₄? (C = 12, H = 1; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 3.01 × 10²³

M(CH₄) = 16 g mol⁻¹. n = 8/16 = 0.5 mol. N = 0.5 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.01 × 10²³ molecules.

Common error: 1.51 × 10²⁴ counts the 5 atoms in each molecule (0.5 × 5 × L). The question asks for molecules.

Q14  [B · Board variant (student)]

How many atoms are present in 3.4 g of ammonia, NH₃? (N = 14, H = 1; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 4.82 × 10²³

M(NH₃) = 17 g mol⁻¹. n = 3.4/17 = 0.2 mol of molecules. Each molecule has 4 atoms: moles of atoms = 0.2 × 4 = 0.8 mol. N = 0.8 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 4.82 × 10²³ atoms.

Common error: 1.20 × 10²³ is the number of molecules. 2.41 × 10²³ counts only 2 atoms per molecule.

Q15  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

What is the total number of ions in 11.1 g of calcium chloride, CaCl₂? (Ca = 40, Cl = 35.5; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 1.81 × 10²³

M(CaCl₂) = 40 + 2(35.5) = 111 g mol⁻¹. n = 11.1/111 = 0.1 mol of formula units. Each gives Ca²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ = 3 ions, so 0.3 mol of ions. N = 0.3 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.81 × 10²³.

Common error: Exception: an ionic solid has no molecules; count ions per formula unit. 6.02 × 10²² counts formula units only; 1.20 × 10²³ counts 2 ions.

Set 6 · Mass of a Single Particle and Atoms Inside Compounds

Q16  [A · Teacher model]

Calculate the mass of one molecule of water. (H = 1, O = 16; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 2.99 × 10⁻²³ g

1 mol (18 g) contains 6.02 × 10²³ molecules. Mass of one molecule = 18 / 6.02 × 10²³ = 2.99 × 10⁻²³ g.

Common error: Multiplying 18 by L gives a huge number. The mass of one particle is the molar mass divided by L.

Q17  [B · Board variant (student)]

Calculate the mass of one atom of copper. (Cu = 64; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 1.06 × 10⁻²² g

Mass of one atom = 64 / 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.06 × 10⁻²² g.

Common error: Copper is monatomic: use the atomic mass, not a doubled value.

Q18  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

How many oxygen atoms are present in 17.1 g of aluminium sulphate, Al₂(SO₄)₃? (Al = 27, S = 32, O = 16; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 3.61 × 10²³

M = 342 g mol⁻¹, so n = 17.1/342 = 0.05 mol. Oxygen atoms per formula unit = 3 × 4 = 12. n(O) = 0.05 × 12 = 0.6 mol. N = 0.6 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.61 × 10²³.

Common error: 1.20 × 10²³ counts only the 4 oxygens of one sulphate group. The 3 outside the bracket multiplies them.

Set 7 · Ions in Ionic Compounds

Q19  [A · Teacher model]

How many moles of chloride ions are present in 0.5 mol of magnesium chloride, MgCl₂?

Answer: 1.0 mol

MgCl₂ → Mg²⁺ + 2Cl⁻. Each formula unit gives 2 Cl⁻, so n(Cl⁻) = 2 × 0.5 = 1.0 mol.

Common error: 0.5 mol ignores the subscript 2.

Q20  [B · Board variant (student)]

How many sodium ions are present in 10.6 g of sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃? (Na = 23, C = 12, O = 16; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 1.20 × 10²³

M = 106 g mol⁻¹. n = 10.6/106 = 0.1 mol. Na₂CO₃ → 2Na⁺ + CO₃²⁻, so n(Na⁺) = 0.2 mol. N = 0.2 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.20 × 10²³.

Common error: 6.02 × 10²² is the number of formula units, not the number of Na⁺ ions.

Q21  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

Calculate the total number of ions in 17.1 g of aluminium sulphate, Al₂(SO₄)₃. (Al = 27, S = 32, O = 16; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 1.51 × 10²³

n = 17.1/342 = 0.05 mol. Al₂(SO₄)₃ → 2Al³⁺ + 3SO₄²⁻ = 5 ions per formula unit. n(ions) = 0.05 × 5 = 0.25 mol. N = 0.25 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.51 × 10²³.

Common error: 5.12 × 10²³ counts atoms (17 per formula unit). A sulphate group is one ion, not five atoms.

Set 8 · Molar Volume of Gases (22.4 dm³ at STP)

Q22  [A · Teacher model]

What volume does 8 g of oxygen gas occupy at STP? (O = 16; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: 5.6 dm³

Oxygen gas is O₂: M = 32 g mol⁻¹. n = 8/32 = 0.25 mol. V = 0.25 × 22.4 = 5.6 dm³.

Common error: Exception: oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen and the halogens are diatomic. 11.2 dm³ uses M = 16 (a single O atom).

Q23  [B · Board variant (student)]

What is the mass of carbon dioxide in 5.6 dm³ of the gas at STP? (C = 12, O = 16; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: 11 g

n = V/22.4 = 5.6/22.4 = 0.25 mol. M(CO₂) = 44 g mol⁻¹. m = 0.25 × 44 = 11 g.

Common error: 44 g is the mass of 1 mol. 22 g would need 11.2 dm³ (0.5 mol).

Q24  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A gas mixture contains 2.8 g of nitrogen (N₂) and 3.2 g of oxygen (O₂). What volume does the mixture occupy at STP? (N = 14, O = 16; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: 4.48 dm³

n(N₂) = 2.8/28 = 0.1 mol. n(O₂) = 3.2/32 = 0.1 mol. Total n = 0.2 mol. V = 0.2 × 22.4 = 4.48 dm³. At STP, 1 mol of any gas occupies the same volume, so the moles of different gases simply add.

Common error: 2.24 dm³ counts only one gas. 8.96 dm³ uses atomic masses (14 and 16), doubling the moles.

Set 9 · Particles and Volumes of Gases at STP

Q25  [A · Teacher model]

How many molecules are present in 4.48 dm³ of oxygen at STP? (molar volume = 22.4 dm³; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 1.20 × 10²³

n = 4.48/22.4 = 0.2 mol. N = 0.2 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.20 × 10²³ molecules.

Common error: 2.41 × 10²³ counts atoms (2 per O₂ molecule).

Q26  [B · Board variant (student)]

What volume do 3.01 × 10²³ molecules of ammonia occupy at STP? (L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: 11.2 dm³

n = 3.01 × 10²³ / 6.02 × 10²³ = 0.5 mol. V = 0.5 × 22.4 = 11.2 dm³.

Common error: 22.4 dm³ is the volume of a full mole.

Q27  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

How many atoms are present in 11.2 dm³ of ozone, O₃, at STP? (molar volume = 22.4 dm³; L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

Answer: 9.03 × 10²³

n(O₃) = 11.2/22.4 = 0.5 mol. Each molecule has 3 atoms, so n(atoms) = 1.5 mol. N = 1.5 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 9.03 × 10²³.

Common error: Exception: ozone is triatomic. Treating it as O₂ gives 6.02 × 10²³.

Set 10 · Gas Density and Comparisons at STP

Q28  [A · Teacher model]

Calculate the density of carbon dioxide at STP. (C = 12, O = 16; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: 1.96 g dm⁻³

M(CO₂) = 44 g mol⁻¹ occupies 22.4 dm³. Density = 44/22.4 = 1.96 g dm⁻³.

Common error: Multiplying 44 by 22.4 gives a mass for 22.4 dm³ squared, not a density.

Q29  [B · Board variant (student)]

What volume does 10 g of hydrogen gas occupy at STP? (H = 1; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: 112 dm³

Hydrogen gas is H₂: M = 2 g mol⁻¹. n = 10/2 = 5 mol. V = 5 × 22.4 = 112 dm³.

Common error: 224 dm³ uses M = 1, the mass of a single H atom.

Q30  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

Which occupies the larger volume at STP, 16 g of oxygen (O₂) or 16 g of methane (CH₄), and by how much? (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: Methane, by 11.2 dm³

n(O₂) = 16/32 = 0.5 mol, V = 11.2 dm³. n(CH₄) = 16/16 = 1 mol, V = 22.4 dm³. Methane is larger by 22.4 − 11.2 = 11.2 dm³.

Common error: Equal masses do not give equal volumes. Equal moles do.

Set 11 · Molar Mass from Gas Data and Isotope Abundance

Q31  [A · Teacher model]

0.88 g of a gas occupies 448 cm³ at STP. Find its molar mass (molar volume = 22 400 cm³).

Answer: 44 g mol⁻¹

n = 448/22 400 = 0.02 mol. M = m/n = 0.88/0.02 = 44 g mol⁻¹.

Common error: Keep units matched: 22 400 cm³ goes with cm³, 22.4 dm³ goes with dm³. 88 g mol⁻¹ is the mass of 2 mol, from halving n.

Q32  [B · Board variant (student)]

A gas has a density of 2.86 g dm⁻³ at STP. Find its molar mass (molar volume = 22.4 dm³).

Answer: 64 g mol⁻¹

Density is the mass of 1 dm³. Mass of 22.4 dm³ (1 mol) = 2.86 × 22.4 = 64 g. Molar mass = density × molar volume = 64 g mol⁻¹.

Common error: The mass of one mole is the density multiplied by 22.4, never divided.

Q33  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

Element Y has three isotopes: ²⁴Y (79%), ²⁵Y (10%) and ²⁶Y (11%). Find its relative atomic mass.

Answer: 24.32

Weighted mean = (24 × 79 + 25 × 10 + 26 × 11)/100 = (1 896 + 250 + 286)/100 = 24.32.

Common error: 25.00 is the plain average of the three masses. 24.00 is the most abundant isotope only. 25.68 swaps the abundances. Abundances must weight each mass.

Set 12 · Gas Mixtures and Average Molar Mass

Q34  [A · Teacher model]

A gas mixture contains equal numbers of moles of oxygen (O₂) and nitrogen (N₂). Find its average molar mass. (N = 14, O = 16)

Answer: 30 g mol⁻¹

Average M = (32 + 28)/2 = 30 g mol⁻¹.

Common error: 60 g mol⁻¹ adds the two molar masses instead of averaging them.

Q35  [B · Board variant (student)]

Dry air is taken as 80% nitrogen (N₂) and 20% oxygen (O₂) by volume. Calculate its average molar mass. (N = 14, O = 16)

Answer: 28.8 g mol⁻¹

For gases, volume % = mole %. Average M = 0.80 × 28 + 0.20 × 32 = 22.4 + 6.4 = 28.8 g mol⁻¹.

Common error: 30 g mol⁻¹ is the simple mean and ignores the proportions.

Q36  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A gas mixture is 60% methane (CH₄) and 40% carbon dioxide (CO₂) by volume. Find the vapour density of the mixture. (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16)

Answer: 13.6

Average M = 0.60 × 16 + 0.40 × 44 = 9.6 + 17.6 = 27.2 g mol⁻¹. Vapour density = 27.2/2 = 13.6.

Common error: 27.2 is the average molar mass. Divide by 2 for the vapour density.

Set 13 · Percentage Composition by Mass

Q37  [A · Teacher model]

Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃. (N = 14, H = 1, O = 16)

Answer: 35.0%

M = 14 + 4 + 14 + 48 = 80 g mol⁻¹. Nitrogen = 2 × 14 = 28. % N = 28/80 × 100 = 35.0%.

Common error: 17.5% counts only one nitrogen atom. There are two: one in NH₄⁺, one in NO₃⁻.

Q38  [B · Board variant (student)]

Calculate the percentage of water of crystallisation in magnesium sulphate heptahydrate, MgSO₄·7H₂O. (Mg = 24, S = 32, O = 16, H = 1)

Answer: 51.2%

M = 24 + 32 + 64 + 7(18) = 120 + 126 = 246 g mol⁻¹. % water = 126/246 × 100 = 51.2%.

Common error: 48.8% is the percentage of anhydrous salt (120/246).

Q39  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

An iron ore contains 80% iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃. What mass of iron can be obtained from 200 kg of the ore? (Fe = 56, O = 16)

Answer: 112 kg

Mass of Fe₂O₃ = 0.80 × 200 = 160 kg. Fraction of iron in Fe₂O₃ = 112/160 = 0.70. Mass of Fe = 0.70 × 160 = 112 kg.

Common error: 160 kg is the oxide, not the iron. 140 kg applies 70% to the whole ore, ignoring the 80% purity.

Set 14 · Purity and Mole Calculations

Q40  [A · Teacher model]

Calculate the number of moles of calcium carbonate in 25 g of limestone that is 80% pure CaCO₃. (Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16)

Answer: 0.2 mol

Mass of pure CaCO₃ = 0.80 × 25 = 20 g. M = 100 g mol⁻¹. n = 20/100 = 0.2 mol.

Common error: 0.25 mol treats the whole sample as pure CaCO₃.

Q41  [B · Board variant (student)]

A 5.85 g sample of common salt contains 0.09 mol of sodium chloride. Calculate its percentage purity. (Na = 23, Cl = 35.5)

Answer: 90%

Mass of NaCl = 0.09 × 58.5 = 5.265 g. Purity = 5.265/5.85 × 100 = 90%.

Common error: 0.09 is an amount in moles, not a fraction of the sample. Convert to mass first.

Q42  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A 10 g sample contains 80% copper(II) oxide, CuO. What mass of copper is in the sample? (Cu = 64, O = 16)

Answer: 6.4 g

Mass of CuO = 8 g. Fraction of copper in CuO = 64/80 = 0.8. Mass of Cu = 0.8 × 8 = 6.4 g.

Common error: 8 g is the mass of oxide in the sample, not of copper.

Set 15 · Valency by the Equivalent Mass Method (valency = atomic mass ÷ equivalent mass)

Q43  [A · Teacher model]

An element M has atomic mass 27 and equivalent mass 9. Find its valency.

Answer: 3

Valency = atomic mass / equivalent mass = 27/9 = 3.

Common error: Do not invert: 9/27 is not a whole number. Valency is always a whole number.

Q44  [B · Board variant (student)]

1.2 g of a metal (atomic mass 24) combines with 0.8 g of oxygen. Find the valency of the metal (O = 16).

Answer: 2

Equivalent mass = mass of metal combining with 8 g of oxygen = (1.2/0.8) × 8 = 12. Valency = 24/12 = 2. (Check by moles: 1.2/24 = 0.05 mol metal and 0.8/16 = 0.05 mol O, so the oxide is MO.)

Common error: Equivalent mass is defined against 8 g of oxygen. Using 16 g gives 24 and valency 1.

Q45  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

5.6 g of iron (Fe = 56) reacts with chlorine to give 16.25 g of iron chloride (Cl = 35.5). Find the valency of iron and the formula of the chloride.

Answer: 3 and FeCl₃

Mass of chlorine = 16.25 − 5.6 = 10.65 g. n(Fe) = 5.6/56 = 0.1 mol. n(Cl) = 10.65/35.5 = 0.3 mol. Ratio Fe : Cl = 1 : 3, so the formula is FeCl₃ and Fe is trivalent.

Common error: Exception: iron shows variable valency (2 and 3). The data decide, not memory: 3 Cl per Fe means FeCl₃. Fe₃Cl contradicts the 1 : 3 ratio.

Set 16 · Valency from Formulae and Compound Masses

Q46  [A · Teacher model]

In the oxide X₂O₅, what is the valency of X? (valency of oxygen = 2)

Answer: 5

Total valency of oxygen = 5 × 2 = 10. This is shared by 2 atoms of X, so valency of X = 10/2 = 5.

Common error: 10 forgets to share the total between the two X atoms.

Q47  [B · Board variant (student)]

Element M (atomic mass 56) forms an oxide of formula M₂O₃. Calculate the percentage of M in the oxide. (O = 16)

Answer: 70%

M(M₂O₃) = 2(56) + 3(16) = 160. % M = 112/160 × 100 = 70%.

Common error: 77.8% comes from using the formula MO (56/72). Use the subscripts.

Q48  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

The chloride of an element X has formula XCl₂ and molar mass 111 g mol⁻¹. Find the atomic mass of X and its valency. (Cl = 35.5)

Answer: Atomic mass 40; valency 2

Mass of 2Cl = 71. Atomic mass of X = 111 − 71 = 40. Two Cl⁻ per X means valency 2 (the element is calcium).

Common error: 75.5 subtracts only one chlorine atom.

Set 17 · Equivalent Mass by Hydrogen Displacement

Q49  [A · Teacher model]

Find the equivalent mass of aluminium. (atomic mass = 27, valency = 3)

Answer: 9

Equivalent mass = atomic mass / valency = 27/3 = 9.

Common error: 27 is the atomic mass; equivalent mass is the mass per unit of valency.

Q50  [B · Board variant (student)]

0.6 g of magnesium displaces 560 cm³ of hydrogen at STP from dilute acid. Calculate the equivalent mass of magnesium. (H = 1; molar volume = 22 400 cm³)

Answer: 12

n(H₂) = 560/22 400 = 0.025 mol, which is 0.05 g of hydrogen. Equivalent mass = mass of metal that displaces 1 g of hydrogen = 0.6/0.05 = 12.

Common error: 24 divides by 0.025, the moles of H₂, instead of the mass of hydrogen (0.05 g).

Q51  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

0.9 g of a metal of atomic mass 27 displaces 1.12 dm³ of hydrogen at STP. Find its equivalent mass and valency. (molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: Equivalent mass 9; valency 3

n(H₂) = 1.12/22.4 = 0.05 mol, which is 0.10 g of hydrogen. Equivalent mass = 0.9/0.10 = 9. Valency = 27/9 = 3.

Common error: A fractional valency (such as 1.5) is a signal of an arithmetic slip, because valency is always a whole number.

Set 18 · Empirical Formula from Percentage Composition

Q52  [A · Teacher model]

A compound contains 40.0% carbon, 6.7% hydrogen and 53.3% oxygen by mass. Find its empirical formula (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16).

Answer: CH₂O

Treat the percentages as grams in 100 g. Moles: C 40.0/12 = 3.33; H 6.7/1 = 6.7; O 53.3/16 = 3.33. Divide by the smallest (3.33): C 1 : H 2 : O 1. Empirical formula CH₂O.

Common error: C₂H₄O₂ is a molecular formula (a multiple). The empirical formula is the simplest whole-number ratio.

Q53  [B · Board variant (student)]

A compound contains 43.4% sodium, 11.3% carbon and 45.3% oxygen by mass. Find its empirical formula (Na = 23, C = 12, O = 16).

Answer: Na₂CO₃

Moles: Na 43.4/23 = 1.887; C 11.3/12 = 0.942; O 45.3/16 = 2.831. Divide by 0.942: Na 2.00 : C 1.00 : O 3.01. Empirical formula Na₂CO₃.

Common error: Always divide by the smallest number of moles, then read off the ratio.

Q54  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A compound contains 72.4% iron and 27.6% oxygen by mass. Find its empirical formula (Fe = 56, O = 16).

Answer: Fe₃O₄

Moles: Fe 72.4/56 = 1.293; O 27.6/16 = 1.725. Divide by 1.293: Fe 1 : O 1.33. A ratio of 1.33 = 4/3 is not whole, so multiply by 3: Fe 3 : O 4. Empirical formula Fe₃O₄.

Common error: Exception: if a ratio ends in .33 or .67, multiply by 3; if .5, multiply by 2. Rounding 1.33 to 1 gives FeO; 1.5 would give Fe₂O₃.

Set 19 · Empirical Formula from Percentage Composition (extended)

Q55  [A · Teacher model]

A compound contains 27.3% carbon and 72.7% oxygen by mass. Find its empirical formula. (C = 12, O = 16)

Answer: CO₂

Moles: C 27.3/12 = 2.275; O 72.7/16 = 4.544. Divide by 2.275: C 1 : O 2.0. Empirical formula CO₂.

Common error: CO would need equal numbers of moles.

Q56  [B · Board variant (student)]

A compound contains 32.4% sodium, 22.6% sulphur and 45.0% oxygen by mass. Find its empirical formula. (Na = 23, S = 32, O = 16)

Answer: Na₂SO₄

Moles: Na 32.4/23 = 1.409; S 22.6/32 = 0.706; O 45.0/16 = 2.813. Divide by 0.706: Na 2.0 : S 1 : O 3.98 ≈ 4. Empirical formula Na₂SO₄.

Common error: Use 3.98 ≈ 4. Small rounding differences from the data are expected.

Q57  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A compound contains 43.7% phosphorus and 56.3% oxygen by mass. Find its empirical formula. (P = 31, O = 16)

Answer: P₂O₅

Moles: P 43.7/31 = 1.410; O 56.3/16 = 3.519. Divide by 1.410: P 1 : O 2.5. Multiply by 2: P 2 : O 5. Empirical formula P₂O₅.

Common error: Exception: a ratio ending in .5 must be doubled. Rounding 2.5 down gives PO₂.

Set 20 · Empirical Formula from Reacting and Combustion Masses

Q58  [A · Teacher model]

2.4 g of carbon combines completely with 0.6 g of hydrogen to form a hydrocarbon. Find its empirical formula (C = 12, H = 1).

Answer: CH₃

n(C) = 2.4/12 = 0.2 mol. n(H) = 0.6/1 = 0.6 mol. Ratio C : H = 0.2 : 0.6 = 1 : 3. Empirical formula CH₃.

Common error: C₃H reverses the ratio. Masses are converted to moles of atoms before comparing.

Q59  [B · Board variant (student)]

A hydrocarbon of mass 0.42 g burns completely to give 1.32 g of carbon dioxide and 0.54 g of water. Find its empirical formula (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16).

Answer: CH₂

n(C) = n(CO₂) = 1.32/44 = 0.03 mol. n(H₂O) = 0.54/18 = 0.03 mol, and each H₂O holds 2 H atoms, so n(H) = 0.06 mol. Ratio C : H = 0.03 : 0.06 = 1 : 2. Empirical formula CH₂. (Check: 0.03 × 12 + 0.06 × 1 = 0.42 g.)

Common error: Exception: 1 mol of H₂O contains 2 mol of H atoms, but 1 mol of CO₂ contains only 1 mol of C. Using 0.03 for H gives CH.

Q60  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

0.92 g of an organic compound containing carbon, hydrogen and oxygen burns completely to give 1.76 g of carbon dioxide and 1.08 g of water. Find its empirical formula (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16).

Answer: C₂H₆O

C: 1.76/44 = 0.04 mol, mass 0.48 g. H: 1.08/18 = 0.06 mol H₂O, so 0.12 mol H, mass 0.12 g. O by difference: 0.92 − 0.48 − 0.12 = 0.32 g, which is 0.32/16 = 0.02 mol. Ratio C : H : O = 0.04 : 0.12 : 0.02 = 2 : 6 : 1. Empirical formula C₂H₆O.

Common error: Exception: the oxygen in the products comes mostly from the air, so the oxygen in the compound is found only by difference from the sample mass.

Set 21 · Empirical Formula from Reacting Masses

Q61  [A · Teacher model]

2.4 g of magnesium burns in oxygen to give 4.0 g of magnesium oxide. Find the empirical formula of the oxide. (Mg = 24, O = 16)

Answer: MgO

Mass of oxygen = 4.0 − 2.4 = 1.6 g. n(Mg) = 2.4/24 = 0.1 mol. n(O) = 1.6/16 = 0.1 mol. Ratio 1 : 1. MgO.

Common error: Using 4.0 g as the mass of oxygen ignores that the oxide mass includes the metal.

Q62  [B · Board variant (student)]

1.12 g of iron forms 1.60 g of an oxide of iron on heating in air. Find the empirical formula of the oxide. (Fe = 56, O = 16)

Answer: Fe₂O₃

Mass of oxygen = 1.60 − 1.12 = 0.48 g. n(Fe) = 1.12/56 = 0.02 mol. n(O) = 0.48/16 = 0.03 mol. Ratio Fe : O = 2 : 3. Fe₂O₃.

Common error: Rounding 1.5 to 1 gives FeO. Double 1 : 1.5 to get 2 : 3.

Q63  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

4.5 g of an organic base contains 2.4 g of carbon and 0.7 g of hydrogen, the rest being nitrogen. Find its empirical formula. (C = 12, H = 1, N = 14)

Answer: C₂H₇N

Mass of N = 4.5 − 2.4 − 0.7 = 1.4 g. n(C) = 0.2 mol; n(H) = 0.7 mol; n(N) = 1.4/14 = 0.1 mol. Divide by 0.1: C 2 : H 7 : N 1. C₂H₇N.

Common error: The third element is found by difference; it is never ignored.

Set 22 · Combustion Analysis (extended)

Q64  [A · Teacher model]

0.58 g of a hydrocarbon burns completely to give 1.76 g of carbon dioxide and 0.90 g of water. Find its empirical formula. (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16)

Answer: C₂H₅

n(C) = 1.76/44 = 0.04 mol. n(H₂O) = 0.90/18 = 0.05 mol, so n(H) = 0.10 mol. (Check: 0.48 + 0.10 = 0.58 g.) Ratio C : H = 0.04 : 0.10 = 2 : 5. C₂H₅.

Common error: Using 0.05 for H (the moles of water) gives C₄H₅. Each H₂O holds two H atoms.

Q65  [B · Board variant (student)]

0.60 g of an organic acid containing carbon, hydrogen and oxygen burns to give 0.88 g of carbon dioxide and 0.36 g of water. Find its empirical formula. (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16)

Answer: CH₂O

n(C) = 0.88/44 = 0.02 mol, mass 0.24 g. n(H) = 2 × 0.36/18 = 0.04 mol, mass 0.04 g. Mass of O = 0.60 − 0.24 − 0.04 = 0.32 g, n(O) = 0.02 mol. Ratio C : H : O = 1 : 2 : 1. CH₂O.

Common error: Oxygen in the products comes from the air. Find oxygen in the sample only by difference.

Q66  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

0.30 g of a hydrocarbon of molar mass 30 g mol⁻¹ burns to give 0.88 g of carbon dioxide and 0.54 g of water. Find its molecular formula. (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16)

Answer: C₂H₆

n(C) = 0.88/44 = 0.02 mol. n(H) = 2 × 0.54/18 = 0.06 mol. Ratio 1 : 3, so empirical formula CH₃ (mass 15). n = 30/15 = 2. Molecular formula C₂H₆.

Common error: CH₃ is only the empirical formula. Compare its mass with the molar mass.

Set 23 · Water of Crystallisation

Q67  [A · Teacher model]

25 g of hydrated copper(II) sulphate, CuSO₄·xH₂O, loses 9 g of water on strong heating. Find x. (Cu = 64, S = 32, O = 16, H = 1)

Answer: x = 5

Mass of anhydrous CuSO₄ = 25 − 9 = 16 g; n = 16/160 = 0.1 mol. n(H₂O) = 9/18 = 0.5 mol. x = 0.5/0.1 = 5.

Common error: Using 25 g as the anhydrous mass gives x = 3.2, which is not a whole number.

Q68  [B · Board variant (student)]

Magnesium sulphate crystals, MgSO₄·xH₂O, contain 51.2% water by mass. Find x. (Mg = 24, S = 32, O = 16, H = 1)

Answer: x = 7

In 100 g: MgSO₄ = 48.8 g, n = 48.8/120 = 0.407 mol. Water = 51.2 g, n = 51.2/18 = 2.84 mol. x = 2.84/0.407 = 7.

Common error: Divide the water by 18, not by 16 (that is oxygen).

Q69  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

14.3 g of hydrated sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃·xH₂O, leaves 5.3 g of residue on heating. Find x and the molar mass of the crystals. (Na = 23, C = 12, O = 16, H = 1)

Answer: x = 10; M = 286 g mol⁻¹

Mass of water = 14.3 − 5.3 = 9.0 g, n = 0.5 mol. n(Na₂CO₃) = 5.3/106 = 0.05 mol. x = 0.5/0.05 = 10. M = 106 + 10(18) = 286 g mol⁻¹.

Common error: x is the ratio of moles of water to moles of salt, not the moles of water alone.

Set 24 · Molecular Formula from Empirical Formula and Molar Mass

Q70  [A · Teacher model]

A compound has empirical formula CH₂ and molar mass 70 g mol⁻¹. Find its molecular formula (C = 12, H = 1).

Answer: C₅H₁₀

Empirical formula mass = 12 + 2 = 14. n = 70/14 = 5. Molecular formula = (CH₂)₅ = C₅H₁₀.

Common error: Molecular formula = n × empirical formula, with n = molar mass ÷ empirical formula mass.

Q71  [B · Board variant (student)]

A compound of empirical formula CH₂O has a molar mass of 180 g mol⁻¹. Find its molecular formula (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16).

Answer: C₆H₁₂O₆

Empirical formula mass = 12 + 2 + 16 = 30. n = 180/30 = 6. Molecular formula = (CH₂O)₆ = C₆H₁₂O₆.

Common error: Multiply every subscript by n, not just the carbon.

Q72  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A hydrocarbon contains 92.3% carbon and 7.7% hydrogen by mass and has molar mass 78 g mol⁻¹. Find its molecular formula (C = 12, H = 1).

Answer: C₆H₆

Moles: C 92.3/12 = 7.69; H 7.7/1 = 7.7. Ratio 1 : 1, so the empirical formula is CH (mass 13). n = 78/13 = 6. Molecular formula = C₆H₆.

Common error: Two steps: composition gives the empirical formula; molar mass gives n. C₂H₂ (mass 26) and C₄H₄ (mass 52) are multiples of CH but do not match 78.

Set 26 · Vapour Density (vapour density = molar mass ÷ 2)

Q76  [A · Teacher model]

Calculate the vapour density of ammonia, NH₃. (N = 14, H = 1)

Answer: 8.5

M(NH₃) = 14 + 3 = 17. Vapour density = density of gas ÷ density of hydrogen = M/2 = 17/2 = 8.5.

Common error: 17 is the relative molecular mass, not the vapour density. Hydrogen gas is H₂ (mass 2), which is why we divide by 2.

Q77  [B · Board variant (student)]

A compound of empirical formula CH₂O has a vapour density of 30. Find its molecular formula (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16).

Answer: C₂H₄O₂

M = 2 × vapour density = 60. Empirical formula mass = 30. n = 60/30 = 2. Molecular formula = C₂H₄O₂.

Common error: CH₂O results from using 30 as the molar mass. Always double the vapour density first.

Q78  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A hydrocarbon contains 85.7% carbon by mass and has a vapour density of 28. Find its molecular formula (C = 12, H = 1).

Answer: C₄H₈

Hydrogen = 100 − 85.7 = 14.3%. Moles: C 85.7/12 = 7.14; H 14.3/1 = 14.3. Ratio C : H = 1 : 2, so the empirical formula is CH₂ (mass 14). M = 2 × 28 = 56. n = 56/14 = 4. Molecular formula = C₄H₈.

Common error: C₂H₄ (mass 28) comes from treating the vapour density as the molar mass.

Set 27 · Vapour Density (extended)

Q79  [A · Teacher model]

Calculate the vapour density of chlorine gas, Cl₂. (Cl = 35.5)

Answer: 35.5

M(Cl₂) = 2 × 35.5 = 71 g mol⁻¹. Vapour density = 71/2 = 35.5.

Common error: 71 is the molar mass. 35.5 happens to equal the atomic mass because Cl₂ is diatomic: the 2s cancel.

Q80  [B · Board variant (student)]

A gas has a vapour density of 8. What is the mass of 5.6 dm³ of the gas at STP? (molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: 4 g

M = 2 × 8 = 16 g mol⁻¹. n = 5.6/22.4 = 0.25 mol. m = 0.25 × 16 = 4 g.

Common error: 2 g uses the vapour density as the molar mass.

Q81  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A gas has a density of 3.17 g dm⁻³ at STP. Calculate its vapour density and identify the gas. (Cl = 35.5; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: Vapour density 35.5; chlorine, Cl₂

M = density × 22.4 = 3.17 × 22.4 = 71 g mol⁻¹. Vapour density = 71/2 = 35.5. M = 71 matches Cl₂.

Common error: 71 g mol⁻¹ is the molar mass. Halve it for the vapour density.

Set 28 · Vapour Density and Thermal Dissociation

Q82  [A · Teacher model]

Calculate the theoretical vapour density of phosphorus pentachloride, PCl₅, before it dissociates. (P = 31, Cl = 35.5)

Answer: 104.25

M = 31 + 5(35.5) = 208.5 g mol⁻¹. Vapour density = 208.5/2 = 104.25.

Common error: 208.5 is the molar mass.

Q83  [B · Board variant (student)]

At a certain temperature PCl₅ is 50% dissociated: PCl₅ ⇌ PCl₃ + Cl₂. Calculate the vapour density of the equilibrium mixture. (P = 31, Cl = 35.5)

Answer: 69.5

Start with 1 mol. At 50% dissociation: 0.5 PCl₅ + 0.5 PCl₃ + 0.5 Cl₂ = 1.5 mol. Total mass is unchanged (208.5 g), so average M = 208.5/1.5 = 139. Vapour density = 139/2 = 69.5.

Common error: 104.25 ignores that dissociation increases the number of moles and so lowers the density.

Q84  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

The observed vapour density of partly dissociated PCl₅ is 83.4. Calculate the percentage dissociation. (theoretical vapour density = 104.25)

Answer: 25%

Degree of dissociation α = (D − d)/d = (104.25 − 83.4)/83.4 = 0.25, so 25%. (Check: moles = 1 + α = 1.25; 104.25/1.25 = 83.4.)

Common error: 20% divides by D instead of d.

Set 29 · Empirical and Molecular Formula: Concept Checks

Q85  [A · Teacher model]

A compound has empirical formula CH₂ and molar mass 42 g mol⁻¹. How many atoms are present in one molecule? (C = 12, H = 1)

Answer: 9 atoms

n = 42/14 = 3. Molecular formula C₃H₆: 3 + 6 = 9 atoms.

Common error: 3 counts the atoms in the empirical formula only.

Q86  [B · Board variant (student)]

Two hydrocarbons each contain 85.7% carbon and 14.3% hydrogen by mass. Their molar masses are 28 and 56 g mol⁻¹. Write their molecular formulae. (C = 12, H = 1)

Answer: C₂H₄ and C₄H₈

Both have empirical formula CH₂ (mass 14). 28/14 = 2 gives C₂H₄. 56/14 = 4 gives C₄H₈.

Common error: Same percentage composition does not mean same molecular formula. Only molar mass separates them.

Q87  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

Ethyne (C₂H₂) and benzene (C₆H₆) have the same empirical formula. State it and calculate the percentage of carbon in each. (C = 12, H = 1)

Answer: CH; 92.3% in each

Both simplify to CH. Ethyne: 24/26 × 100 = 92.3%. Benzene: 72/78 × 100 = 92.3%. Identical percentage composition follows from identical empirical formulae.

Common error: Percentage composition cannot distinguish them; molar mass is needed.

Set 30 · Integrated: Gas Volume, Vapour Density and Formulae

7.0 g of a gas occupies 5.6 dm³ at STP. Find its vapour density (molar volume = 22.4 dm³).

Answer: 14

n = 5.6/22.4 = 0.25 mol. M = 7.0/0.25 = 28 g mol⁻¹. Vapour density = 28/2 = 14.

Q89  [B · Board variant (student)]

What is the mass of 2.24 dm³ of a gas of vapour density 17, measured at STP? (molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: 3.4 g

M = 2 × 17 = 34 g mol⁻¹. n = 2.24/22.4 = 0.1 mol. m = 0.1 × 34 = 3.4 g.

Common error: 1.7 g uses the vapour density as the molar mass; 34 g is the mass of 22.4 dm³.

Q90  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

A gaseous hydrocarbon contains 85.7% carbon by mass. 1.4 g of the gas occupies 1.12 dm³ at STP. Find its molecular formula (C = 12, H = 1; molar volume = 22.4 dm³).

Answer: C₂H₄

Composition: C 85.7/12 = 7.14 and H 14.3/1 = 14.3, ratio 1 : 2, so the empirical formula is CH₂ (mass 14). Molar mass: n = 1.12/22.4 = 0.05 mol; M = 1.4/0.05 = 28 g mol⁻¹. n(formula) = 28/14 = 2. Molecular formula = C₂H₄.

Common error: Composition gives the ratio and the gas volume gives the molar mass. CH₂ stops at the empirical formula.

Set 31 · Integrated: Composition, Density, Vapour Density and Gas Volume

Q91  [A · Teacher model]

A compound contains 27.3% carbon and 72.7% oxygen by mass. Its density at STP is 1.96 g dm⁻³. Find its molecular formula. (C = 12, O = 16; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: CO₂

Empirical formula: C 27.3/12 = 2.275, O 72.7/16 = 4.54, ratio 1 : 2, so CO₂ (mass 44). Molar mass = 1.96 × 22.4 = 43.9 ≈ 44 g mol⁻¹. n = 44/44 = 1. Molecular formula CO₂.

Common error: Density times 22.4 gives the molar mass directly. Do not stop at the empirical formula without checking n.

Q92  [B · Board variant (student)]

A compound contains 40.0% carbon, 6.7% hydrogen and 53.3% oxygen by mass. 3.0 g of its vapour occupies 1.12 dm³ at STP. Find its molecular formula. (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: C₂H₄O₂

Empirical formula CH₂O (mass 30). n(vapour) = 1.12/22.4 = 0.05 mol; M = 3.0/0.05 = 60 g mol⁻¹. n = 60/30 = 2. Molecular formula C₂H₄O₂.

Common error: Composition gives the ratio and the gas volume gives M; both are needed.

Q93  [C · Class activity (deeper)]

0.44 g of a gaseous hydrocarbon of vapour density 22 burns to give 1.32 g of carbon dioxide and 0.72 g of water. Find its molecular formula and the volume of the gas at STP. (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16; molar volume = 22.4 dm³)

Answer: C₃H₈; 0.224 dm³ (224 cm³)

M = 2 × 22 = 44 g mol⁻¹, so n(gas) = 0.44/44 = 0.01 mol. n(C) = 1.32/44 = 0.03 mol, so 3 C per molecule. n(H) = 2 × 0.72/18 = 0.08 mol, so 8 H per molecule. Molecular formula C₃H₈. (Check: 0.36 + 0.08 = 0.44 g.) V = 0.01 × 22.4 = 0.224 dm³.

Common error: Dividing by the moles of hydrocarbon (0.01) gives atoms per molecule directly, so no empirical step is needed.
