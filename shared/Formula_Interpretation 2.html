PART 1Definitions for the Term

TermDefinition
FormulaA rule that links quantities by equal signs and operations, so that known values give an unknown one.
QuantitySomething that can be measured: it has a number and a unit.
VariableA quantity that can take different values in a problem.
ConstantA quantity that stays fixed in the problem (g, c, G, or a quantity held steady).
Constant of proportionality (k)The fixed number that turns a proportion into an equation: y ∝ x becomes y = kx.
Directly proportional (y ∝ x)y and x rise and fall together in the same ratio; y ÷ x stays fixed; the graph is a straight line through the origin.
Inversely proportional (y ∝ 1 ÷ x)y falls as x rises in the same ratio; y × x stays fixed; the graph is a curve (hyperbola).
Square law (y ∝ x²)y changes by the square of the change in x: x doubled gives y four times.
Inverse square law (y ∝ 1 ÷ x²)y changes by the inverse square of the change in x: x doubled gives y one quarter.
Square root relation (y ∝ √x)y changes by the square root of the change in x: x four times gives y two times.
Joint variationy depends on several quantities at once; their factors multiply (P = ρQgh).
RateHow much a quantity changes for each unit of another, usually time: change ÷ time. 'Per' means divided by.
Δ (delta, 'change in')Final value minus initial value: Δx = x(final) − x(initial). It can be negative.
δ (small delta)A very small change.
Gradient (slope)Rise ÷ run on a graph; the rate of change of y with x.
InterceptThe value of y where the graph meets the y-axis (x = 0).
Net (resultant)The single value that has the same effect as several acting together; for forces, the vector sum ΣF.
Σ (sigma, 'sum of')Add up every term of the kind shown.
ScalarA quantity with size only (mass, time, energy).
VectorA quantity with size and direction (force, velocity, momentum).
AverageTotal of a quantity ÷ total time (or number), not the mean of the end values unless the change is uniform.
InstantaneousThe value at one moment; the gradient of the graph at that point.
RatioOne quantity divided by another of the same kind; it has no unit (efficiency, MA, relative density).
Percentage change(new − old) ÷ old × 100 %.
Dimension and unitDimension is the kind of quantity (length, time, mass); unit is the measure of it (m, s, kg). Both sides of a formula must match.
Limiting caseA check at an extreme value (zero, very large) to see if a formula behaves sensibly.
Order of magnitudeThe nearest power of ten of a number: 4 × 10⁻⁷ is of order 10⁻⁶.

PART 2How to Read a Formula

The Six Reading Moves

MoveWhat to doExample: F = GMm ÷ r²
1Name every symbol: quantity, unit, scalar or vectorF force (N, vector); G constant; M, m masses (kg); r distance between centres (m)
2Find the structure: product, quotient, power, root, sum, difference, trig, logProduct on top (G, M, m); r² below
3Read each symbol's role: top is direct, below is inverse, power sets the scalingF ∝ M; F ∝ m; F ∝ 1 ÷ r²
4Hold everything else fixed and state the proportionalityWith M and m fixed, F ∝ 1 ÷ r²
5Run the doubling test: double or halve one quantity at a timer × 2 gives F × ¼; M × 2 gives F × 2
6Check units and limits: what if x → 0 or very large?N = N m² kg⁻² × kg × kg ÷ m². As r → ∞, F → 0; as r → 0 the formula fails (point masses)

PART 8Interpretation Drills: Triples

QQuestionWorking and answer
8.1 Direct proportion: y ∝ x: the ratio y ÷ x stays fixed; factors multiply across several variables
8.1aIn F = ma with the mass fixed, the acceleration goes from 2 to 6 m s⁻². By what factor does the net force change?F ∝ a, so factor = 6 ÷ 2 = 3. Ans: × 3
8.1bIn V = IR, the voltage across a resistor is made 3 times larger and the resistance 2 times larger. By what factor does the current change?I = V ÷ R: factor = 3 ÷ 2 = 1.5. Ans: × 1.5
8.1cA wire has resistance 8 Ω. Another wire of the same metal and thickness is 25 % longer. Find its resistance.R ∝ L, so R = 8 × 1.25 = 10 Ω. Ans: 10 Ω
8.2 Inverse proportion: y ∝ 1 ÷ x: y × x stays fixed; a rise of p % in x does not give a fall of p % in y
8.2aA wave travels in a medium where its speed is fixed. Its frequency goes from 200 Hz to 800 Hz. By what factor does the wavelength change?v = fλ fixed, so λ ∝ 1 ÷ f: factor = 200 ÷ 800 = 0.25. Ans: × 0.25
8.2bIn I = V ÷ R, the voltage is doubled and the resistance is made four times larger. By what factor does the current change?Factor = 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5. Ans: × 0.5
8.2cAt constant temperature a gas has its pressure raised by 20 %. By what percentage does the volume change?PV = constant, so V₂ ÷ V₁ = P₁ ÷ P₂ = 1 ÷ 1.2 = 0.833. Change = −16.7 %, a fall of 16.7 %, not 20 %. Ans: −16.7 %
8.3 Square law: y ∝ x²: change x by a factor k and y changes by k²
8.3aA car's speed becomes 3 times larger. By what factor does its kinetic energy change?KE ∝ v²: factor = 3² = 9. Ans: × 9
8.3bIn P = I²R, the current is doubled and the resistance is made three times larger. By what factor does the power change?Factor = 2² × 3 = 12. Ans: × 12
8.3c(i) A car's speed rises by 10 %: find the percentage change in its kinetic energy (and in its stopping distance). (ii) Its kinetic energy rises by 21 %: find the percentage change in speed.(i) Factor = 1.1² = 1.21, a rise of 21 %. (ii) v ∝ √KE: factor = √1.21 = 1.1, a rise of 10 %. Ans: (i) +21 %; (ii) +10 %
8.4 Inverse square law: y ∝ 1 ÷ x²: change x by a factor k and y changes by 1 ÷ k²
8.4aAt twice the distance from Earth's centre, by what factor does g change?g ∝ 1 ÷ r²: factor = 1 ÷ 2² = 0.25. Ans: × 0.25
8.4bIn F = kQ₁Q₂ ÷ r², both charges are made 3 times larger and the separation 3 times larger. By what factor does the force change?Factor = (3 × 3) ÷ 3² = 1. The force is unchanged. Ans: × 1
8.4cA lamp gives 36 W m⁻² at 2 m. Find the intensity at 5 m and the distance at which it is 4 W m⁻².I ∝ 1 ÷ r²: I = 36 × (2 ÷ 5)² = 5.76 W m⁻². For 4: r = 2 × √(36 ÷ 4) = 6 m. Ans: 5.76 W m⁻²; 6 m
8.5 Square root relation: y ∝ √x: change x by a factor k and y changes by √k; a root is applied to everything under it
8.5aA pendulum is made 4 times longer. By what factor does its period change?T = 2π√(l ÷ g): T ∝ √l, factor = √4 = 2. Ans: × 2
8.5bOn the Moon g is one sixth of Earth's. By what factor does the speed of an object dropped from the same height change on landing (v = √(2gh)), and by what factor does a pendulum's period change?v ∝ √g: factor = √(1 ÷ 6) = 0.408. T ∝ 1 ÷ √g: factor = √6 = 2.45. Ans: speed × 0.408; period × 2.45
8.5cFor a stretched string f = (1 ÷ 2L)√(T ÷ μ). The tension is made 9 times larger, μ 4 times larger and the length 3 times larger. By what factor does f change?f ∝ √T ÷ (L√μ): factor = √9 ÷ (3 × √4) = 3 ÷ 6 = 0.5. Ans: × 0.5
8.6 Cubes and joint variation: y ∝ x³; several quantities multiply in joint variation
8.6aA solid sphere's radius is doubled (same material). By what factor does its mass change?m ∝ r³: factor = 2³ = 8. Ans: × 8
8.6bIn P = ηρQgh the flow is doubled, the head is made 1.5 times larger and the efficiency falls from 90 % to 80 %. By what factor does the output power change?Factor = 2 × 1.5 × (0.8 ÷ 0.9) = 2.67. Ans: × 2.67
8.6cA satellite at 4.2 × 107 m has a period of 1 day. By Kepler's law (T ∝ r^1.5), find the period at 3.84 × 108 m (the Moon's orbit).Ratio of radii = 3.84 × 108 ÷ 4.2 × 107 = 9.14. T = 1 day × 9.14^1.5 = 27.6 days. Ans: 27.6 days
8.7 Reading graphs: gradient and intercept: y = mx + c: the gradient m is the rate; the intercept c is the starting value
8.7aA velocity–time line passes through (0 s, 5 m s⁻¹) and (4 s, 17 m s⁻¹). Find the initial velocity, the acceleration and the velocity at 10 s.u = intercept = 5 m s⁻¹. a = gradient = (17 − 5) ÷ 4 = 3 m s⁻². v = u + at = 5 + 3 × 10 = 35 m s⁻¹. Ans: u = 5 m s⁻¹; a = 3 m s⁻²; v = 35 m s⁻¹
8.7bA graph of T² against l for a pendulum is a straight line through the origin with gradient 4.0 s² m⁻¹. Find g.T² = (4π² ÷ g) l, so gradient = 4π² ÷ g and g = 4π² ÷ 4.0 = 9.87 m s⁻². Ans: 9.87 m s⁻²
8.7cA resistance is 100 Ω at 0 °C and 139 Ω at 100 °C, rising linearly. Find the temperature coefficient α (R = R₀(1 + αθ)) and the resistance at 60 °C.α = (R − R₀) ÷ (R₀θ) = (139 − 100) ÷ (100 × 100) = 3.9 × 10-3 K⁻¹. R(60) = 100 × (1 + 3.9 × 10-3 × 60) = 123.4 Ω. Ans: α = 3.9 × 10-3 K⁻¹; R = 123.4 Ω
8.8 Rate and change in (Δ): rate = Δquantity ÷ Δt; the gradient of a graph is the rate
8.8aA body's velocity goes from 12 m s⁻¹ to 30 m s⁻¹ in 6 s. Find the average acceleration.a = Δv ÷ Δt = (30 − 12) ÷ 6 = 3 m s⁻². Ans: 3 m s⁻²
8.8bA 0.15 kg ball moving at 20 m s⁻¹ is stopped in 0.02 s. Find the net force, using force = rate of change of momentum.F = Δp ÷ Δt = 0.15 × 20 ÷ 0.02 = 150 N. Ans: 150 N
8.8cA heater warms water at 2 °C per minute using 500 W. For the same water, find the power for 5 °C per minute, and the temperature after 8 minutes at the first rate starting from 20 °C.Rate of warming ∝ power: P = 500 × 5 ÷ 2 = 1 250 W. Temperature = 20 + 2 × 8 = 36 °C. Ans: 1 250 W; 36 °C
8.9 Net and sum (Σ): net = sum with signs or directions; equilibrium means the net is zero
8.9aA 2 kg body has forces of 12 N to the right, 5 N to the left and friction of 3 N to the left. Find the net force and the acceleration.Net = 12 − 5 − 3 = 4 N to the right. a = 4 ÷ 2 = 2 m s⁻². Ans: net 4 N; a = 2 m s⁻²
8.9bForces of 10 N east, 6 N west and 4 N north act on a body. What fourth force gives equilibrium?Net so far: east 10 − 6 = 4 N and north 4 N, so the resultant is √(4² + 4²) = 5.66 N at 45° north of east. The fourth force is equal and opposite: 5.66 N at 45° south of west. Ans: 5.66 N at 45° south of west
8.9cA 5 kg block starts from rest and is pushed 8 m by a 30 N force against 10 N of friction. Use net work = ΔKE to find its final speed.Net work = (30 − 10) × 8 = 160 J = ½mv², so v = √(2 × 160 ÷ 5) = 8 m s⁻¹. Ans: 8 m s⁻¹
8.10 Percent and 'times as': new ÷ old = 1 ± p ÷ 100; percentages are always taken on the old value
8.10aA quantity falls from 80 to 60. Find the percentage fall. Then find the percentage rise needed to go from 60 back to 80.Fall = (80 − 60) ÷ 80 × 100 = 25 %. Rise = (80 − 60) ÷ 60 × 100 = 33.3 %. The two are different because the 'old' value differs. Ans: fall 25 %; rise 33.3 %
8.10bA car's speed rises by 20 % and its mass falls by 20 %. Find the percentage change in kinetic energy.KE ∝ mv²: factor = 1.2² × 0.8 = 1.152. Change = 15.2 %. Ans: +15.2 %
8.10cR = V ÷ I: V is measured with 2 % error and I with 3 % error. Find the percentage error in R.For a quotient the percentage errors add: 2 + 3 = 5 %. Ans: 5 %
8.11 Angles: sin, cos, tan: sin θ and cos θ lie between −1 and 1; cos 0° = 1, cos 90° = 0, sin 90° = 1
8.11aIn W = Fd cos θ, find the fraction of Fd that becomes work at θ = 0°, 60° and 90°.cos 0° = 1; cos 60° = 0.5; cos 90° = 0. Ans: 1; 0.5; 0
8.11bA projectile's range is ∝ sin 2θ. Which angle gives the same range as 20°, and which gives the greatest range?sin 2θ = sin(180° − 2θ), so θ and 90° − θ give equal range: 70°. The greatest sin 2θ = 1 is at 2θ = 90°: 45°. Ans: 70°; 45°
8.11cIn F = BIl sin θ, find F as a fraction of its maximum at θ = 30°, and the angle at which F = 0.8 of the maximum.At 30°: sin 30° = 0.5. sin θ = 0.8 gives θ = 53.1°. Ans: 0.5 of maximum; 53.1°
8.12 Exponential and logarithm: equal steps give equal factors (exponential); equal factors give equal steps (logarithm)
8.12aIn N = N₀(½)^(t ÷ T), find the fraction left after 3 half-lives and after 2.5 half-lives.(½)³ = 0.125. (½)^2.5 = 0.177. Ans: 0.125; 0.177
8.12bSound intensity level L = 10 log(I ÷ I₀) in decibels. Find the rise in L when the intensity becomes 100 times larger, and when it doubles (two identical machines).×100: ΔL = 10 log 100 = 20 dB. ×2: ΔL = 10 log 2 = 3.01 dB. Ans: +20 dB; +3.01 dB
