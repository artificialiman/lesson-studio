## Segment 2: Chemistry Content Established in the Session

Everything in this segment is **[Claude-supplied, unreviewed]** unless marked. Every numeric value is a claim to be checked against Iman's exam board data sheet.

### 2.1 The eight families of secondary school chemistry calculations

| Family | Governing relations |
|---|---|
| 1. Atomic and mass foundations | Ar from isotopic abundance = Σ(mass × abundance) / 100; Mr = sum of Ar; neutrons = A − Z |
| 2. The mole concept | n = m/M; n = N/L; n = V/V_m; n = C × V; percentage composition; empirical and molecular formula |
| 3. Stoichiometry | Reacting masses and volumes; limiting reagent; percentage yield; percentage purity |
| 4. Gas laws | Boyle, Charles, combined, ideal gas, Dalton, Graham, Gay-Lussac |
| 5. Solutions and titration | g/dm³ = mol/dm³ × M; C₁V₁ = C₂V₂; (C_A V_A)/(C_B V_B) = n_A/n_B; solubility |
| 6. Energetics | q = mcΔT; ΔH = q/n; Hess's law; bond energies |
| 7. Electrochemistry | Q = It; m = MQ/nF; E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode |
| 8. Equilibrium, rates, pH | Kc, Kp; pH = −log[H⁺]; pH + pOH = 14; rate; half-life |

### 2.2 The mole as a family of counting units

The Avogadro constant is 6.022 × 10²³ (I also wrote 6.02 × 10²³ in the first list; the second is the rounded form).

| Mole of | Entity counted | Bridge to measurable | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| Substance | Particles | n = m / M | Stoichiometry, titration, solubility |
| Gas | Molecules at stated condition | n = V / V_m | Gas laws |
| Solution | Solute particles per dm³ | n = C × V | Titration, dilution |
| Electrons | Electrons | 1 mol e⁻ = 96,500 C | Electrolysis |
| Nuclei | Radioactive nuclei | N = N₀(½)^(t/t½) | Nuclear |

**Hub structure:** mass, gas volume, concentration × volume, charge and particle count all convert through n.

**Linkage stated:** F = N_A × e, where e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. Numerically, 6.022 × 10²³ × 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ ≈ 96,470 C mol⁻¹, which rounds to 96,500 at classroom precision. **[Open]** which value of F Iman prefers, given his two-layer precision rule (see 4.1).

**Terminology I proposed:** "amount of substance" for the quantity, "mole" for the unit, "molar mass" for M. Always ask "1 mole of what?"

### 2.3 Graphs

Five shapes:

| Shape | Equation form | Chemistry examples |
|---|---|---|
| Line through origin | y = kx | P vs 1/V, V vs T (K), m vs n, m vs Q |
| Line with intercept | y = mx + c | V vs T (°C), intercept near −273 °C |
| Hyperbola | xy = k | P vs V (Boyle) |
| Exponential | y = y₀e^(−kt) or (½)^(t/t½) | Decay, first-order reactions |
| Plateau or S-curve | Rise then level, or step | Heating/cooling, titration, solubility |

Five reading questions: name the axes and units; name the shape; state what slope means; state what area means; state what intercepts mean.

Graph families covered: gases, energetics, rates and equilibrium, solutions, electrochemistry and nuclear. Teaching order I proposed: straight lines with m vs n first, then electrolysis m vs Q, then gases, energy profiles and heating curves, rate and decay, titration and solubility last.

### 2.4 The many names of pressure

| Quantity | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure | Force per unit area | Pa, kPa, atm, mmHg |
| Partial pressure | Pressure a single gas would exert alone | Same |
| Concentration (solution) | Moles of solute per dm³ | mol dm⁻³ |
| Concentration (gas) | n/V | mol dm⁻³ |
| Density | Mass per unit volume | g cm⁻³, g dm⁻³ |
| Molar volume | Volume per mole | dm³ mol⁻¹ |
| Mole fraction | Share of total moles | None |
| Vapour pressure | Pressure of vapour at equilibrium with liquid | Pa |
| Osmotic pressure | Pressure needed to stop solvent flow across a membrane | Pa |

**Unifying identity offered:** P = (n/V)RT, so at fixed T, pressure and concentration are proportional; density follows from ρ = PM/RT. Also Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn.

### 2.5 Reference conditions

Iman raised this omission: "you did not discuss ATP, STP, vapor density, saturation… those connect too."

| Label | Temperature | Pressure | Molar volume | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| STP | 0 °C (273 K) | 101.3 kPa | 22.4 dm³ | Older texts, JAMB-style |
| RTP | 25 °C (298 K) | 101.3 kPa | 24 dm³ | WAEC/NECO and most current syllabi |
| SATP | 25 °C | 100 kPa | 24.8 dm³ | IUPAC-style, newer texts |
| ATP (ambient) | Actual room | Actual air | Must be calculated | Real lab conditions |

**Cautions I raised:** ATP means something else in biology; the word "standard" has meant 0 °C in older sources and 25 °C in newer; 22.4 vs 24 dm³ is roughly a 7% difference. **[Open]** which convention Iman treats as primary for his students.

**Vapour density:** VD = Mr(gas) / Mr(H₂) = Mr / 2, unitless. Relative density against air uses Mr / 29.

**Saturation family:** vapour pressure, saturated vapour, boiling point (vapour pressure equals surrounding pressure), saturated solution, supersaturated. One idea in two settings: a limit set by temperature, reached when two opposite processes run at equal rates.

**Collecting gas over water:** total pressure = gas pressure + water vapour pressure (Dalton); water vapour pressure depends only on temperature; dry gas pressure = atmospheric − water vapour pressure.

### 2.6 The mathematics of tables

Iman's framing: "Graphs are usually Accompanied by tables… I'd like to sort the math of that out before we continue discussing the chemistry, because I personally use tables to condense related concepts across the text/course in one picture."

**Three table types I distinguished:**

| Type | Holds | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| Data table | Measurements | A graph and a relationship |
| Calculation table | Derived quantities, stepwise | An answer with an audit trail |
| Matrix table | Concepts against attributes | A cross-course map |

**Pattern-detection order:**

| Pattern in the table | Constant | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| y/x same each row | Ratio | Direct proportion |
| Equal steps in x give equal steps in y | First difference | Line with intercept |
| x × y same each row | Product | Inverse proportion |
| Equal steps in x multiply y by a fixed factor | Ratio of successive y | Exponential |
| Halving interval constant | Half-life | Decay |

**Table-to-graph rules:** independent variable on x; axis label equals column heading with unit; fixed scale intervals; best-fit line not dot-to-dot; slope from two far-apart points on the line; slope units are y unit over x unit.

**Uncertainty rules I stated:** same decimal places per instrument; repeat trials in extra columns; concordant titres within 0.10 cm³; significant figures follow the least precise input. **[Open]** whether Iman uses 0.10 cm³ as the concordance threshold; it is a common convention I supplied, not something he stated.

### 2.7 Tables as algebra with the letters removed

Iman's observation: "I usually see algebra get simplified into tables before being shoved as a shortcut/trick, especially those questions about age and interest."

**[Stated]** Iman also said my explanation of tables "was too jargon heavy" and asked that I "articulate it simpler… try to mimic my vocab/tone and rhythm, taxonomies it real good (matter of fact, use tables)."

**Three moves I described:** list, step, match.

**Age problem worked:** father 40, son 10; row at 5 years gives 45 and 15, ratio 3. The shortcut (difference is constant, so 2 × son = 30) is the constant column written as a rule.

**Interest problems worked:** simple interest ₦10,000 at 10% gives a straight line (total = 10,000 + 1,000 × years; 16,000 at 6 years). Compound interest gives a growing interest column and Total = 10,000 × 1.1^years.

**Pattern table linking money to chemistry:**

| Down the column | Pattern | Shortcut | Chemistry echo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Same amount added | Straight line | start + step × count | Mass deposited vs time at constant current |
| Same factor multiplied | Exponential | start × factor^count | Half-life |
| Two columns multiply to a constant | Inverse | product = constant | Boyle |
| Two columns keep a constant ratio | Direct | y = kx | m vs n |

**Where tables stop working (Iman's response: "I love that where tables stop working part, it's helpful for my math pedagogy"):**

| Situation | Why it fails | What takes over |
|---|---|---|
| Hundreds of rows needed | Too slow | Formula |
| Answer not a whole number | Rows skip past it | Algebra or graph |
| Several unknowns | No obvious next row | Simultaneous equations |

**[Inference]** I observed that this mirrors the electrolysis three-tier structure (formula that sometimes fails, steps that take over, a selection skill above both). Iman has not confirmed the parallel.
