MEANING OF DIGITAL TECHNOLOGY
What is Digital Technology? Digital technology refers to electronic tools and devices that use
digital information (0s and 1s) to create, store, and share information.
Digital vs. Analog:
 Analog: Uses continuous signals (e.g., old clock with hands)
 Digital: Uses 0s and 1s (e.g., digital clock with numbers)
Why "Digital"? Computers understand only two states: ON (1) and OFF (0). All information is
converted to combinations of 0s and 1s called binary code.

WEEK 6: BASIC COMPUTER HARDWARE - STORAGE UNITS
MEANING OF STORAGE
Computer Storage: Space where data and information are kept.
Why Needed: Save work, store files, keep programs, backup data
TYPES OF STORAGE
PRIMARY STORAGE (Main Memory)
Characteristics: Inside computer, temporary, fast, loses data when power is off
26
1. RAM (Random Access Memory)
 Temporary "working memory"
 Holds currently running programs and data
 Volatile: Loses data when power off
 Fast: Very quick access
 Sizes: 2GB, 4GB, 8GB, 16GB, 32GB
 Analogy: Like your desk - cleared when you leave
2. ROM (Read-Only Memory)
 Permanent memory
 Stores startup instructions (BIOS)
 Non-volatile: Keeps data when power off
 Read-only: Cannot easily change
RAM vs ROM:
RAM ROM
Temporary Permanent
Volatile Non-volatile
Stores current work Stores startup instructions
Can read and write Mostly read-only

1. Hard Disk Drive (HDD)

WEEK 9: COMPUTER BOOTING AND SHUTDOWN
MEANING OF BOOTING
Booting: Starting up a computer and loading the operating system.
 Computer checks hardware
 Loads OS from hard disk to RAM
 Prepares computer for use
TYPES OF BOOTING
1. Cold Booting (Hard Boot)
38
 Starting from completely powered-off state
 Full startup process (slower)
 Used: First start of day, after power failure
2. Warm Booting (Restart)
 Restarting while computer is on
 Faster than cold boot
 Used: After installing software, fixing problems
BOOTING PROCESS (What Happens)
1. POST (Power-On Self-Test): Checks hardware, beeps if OK
2. BIOS Activation: Locates operating system
3. Load Boot Loader: Small program loads OS
4. Load OS: Windows/macOS loads into RAM
5. Load Startup Programs: Antivirus, etc. start automatically
6. Desktop Appears: Computer ready to use
Time: 30 seconds to 2 minutes
PROPER BOOTING PROCEDURE
1. Check all connections (monitor, keyboard, mouse, power)
2. Turn on monitor
3. Press computer power button once
4. Wait patiently (don't touch anything)
5. Login if required (username/password)
39
6. Wait for desktop to fully load
SHUTTING DOWN A COMPUTER
Why Proper Shutdown Matters:
 Saves work and closes programs properly
 Prevents data loss and file corruption
 Protects hard disk
NEVER:
 Press power button without shutdown process
 Unplug while on
 Turn off at wall socket directly
PROPER SHUTDOWN PROCEDURE (Windows)
1. Save work and close all programs
2. Click Start menu (Windows button)
3. Click Power icon
4. Choose Shut down
5. Wait for complete shutdown (lights off, fans stop)
6. Turn off monitor
7. Turn off wall socket (optional, especially during storms)

WEEK 3: INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY
Meaning of Intellectual Property
Intellectual Property (IP) refers to creations of the human mind that have commercial or artistic
value and are protected by law. These include inventions, artistic works, designs, symbols,
names, and images. IP rights give creators legal ownership and control over their work,
preventing unauthorized use.
Why IP Protection Matters:
 Encourages creativity and innovation
 Protects creators' financial interests
119
 Ensures proper credit to original creators
 Prevents theft of ideas and work
 Supports economic growth
Types of Intellectual Property
1. Copyright
Legal protection for original creative works including books, music, films, software, artwork,
photographs, and digital content.
What Copyright Protects:
 Literary works (books, articles, poems)
 Musical compositions and recordings
 Artistic works (paintings, sculptures, photographs)
 Dramatic works (plays, scripts)
 Films and videos
 Computer programs and software
 Architectural designs
Copyright Duration: Generally lasts for the creator's lifetime plus 50-70 years (varies by
country).
Rights of Copyright Holders:
 Reproduce the work
 Distribute copies
 Create adaptations or derivative works
 Perform or display publicly
 License others to use the work
120
Copyright Symbol: © followed by year and owner's name
2. Trademark
A distinctive sign, symbol, word, phrase, logo, or design that identifies and distinguishes
products or services of one company from others.
Examples:
 Nike's swoosh logo
 McDonald's golden arches
 Apple's apple logo
 Coca-Cola's script font
 Company slogans
Purpose of Trademarks:
 Brand identification
 Consumer protection
 Business reputation
 Market differentiation
Trademark Symbol: ™ (for unregistered) or ® (for registered trademarks)
3. Plagiarism
The act of using someone else's work, ideas, or words without giving proper credit or obtaining
permission, presenting them as your own.
Forms of Plagiarism:
 Direct plagiarism: Copying word-for-word without quotes or citation
 Self-plagiarism: Reusing your own previous work without disclosure
 Mosaic plagiarism: Mixing copied phrases with your own words
121
 Accidental plagiarism: Forgetting to cite or improperly paraphrasing
Consequences of Plagiarism:
 Academic penalties (failing grades, suspension, expulsion)
 Legal action and fines
 Damage to reputation
 Loss of credibility
 Career consequences
How to Avoid Plagiarism:
 Always cite sources properly
 Use quotation marks for direct quotes
 Paraphrase in your own words
 Keep track of sources while researching
 Use plagiarism detection tools
 Ask for clarification when unsure
Respecting Digital Content Ownership
Best Practices:
1. Give Credit: Always acknowledge original creators with proper citations
2. Seek Permission: Ask before using someone's work, especially for commercial purposes
3. Use Legal Alternatives:
o Royalty-free images (Unsplash, Pexels)
o Creative Commons licensed content
o Public domain works
4. Understand Licenses: "Free to view" doesn't mean "free to use"
122
5. Avoid Piracy: Don't illegally download or share copyrighted music, movies, software, or
books
6. Respect Social Media Content: Don't repost others' photos or videos without permission
7. Create Original Work: Develop your own content whenever possible
Creative Commons Licenses: Flexible copyright licenses allowing creators to specify how
others can use their work:
 CC BY: Credit must be given
 CC BY-SA: Credit given, share alike
 CC BY-ND: Credit given, no derivatives
 CC BY-NC: Credit given, non-commercial use only
Digital Citizenship: Being a responsible digital citizen means respecting intellectual property
rights, supporting creators by using legal sources, and promoting ethical online behavior.

WEEK 4: SAFETY AND SECURITY FEATURES ON DIGITAL DEVICES
Antivirus Software
Antivirus software is a program designed to detect, prevent, and remove malicious software
(malware) from computers and devices. It acts as a shield protecting your device from various
threats.
Types of Malware:
 Viruses: Programs that attach to files and spread when files are shared
 Worms: Self-replicating malware that spreads across networks
 Trojans: Disguised as legitimate software but perform harmful actions
125
 Spyware: Secretly monitors and collects user information
 Ransomware: Encrypts files and demands payment for decryption
 Adware: Displays unwanted advertisements
How Antivirus Works:
1. Scanning: Examines files and programs for known malware signatures
2. Real-time Protection: Monitors system activity continuously
3. Quarantine: Isolates suspicious files to prevent damage
4. Removal: Deletes or repairs infected files
5. Updates: Downloads latest threat definitions regularly
Popular Antivirus Programs:
 Windows Defender (built into Windows)
 Norton
 McAfee
 Avast
 Kaspersky
 Bitdefender
Best Practices:
 Keep antivirus software updated
 Run regular full system scans
 Enable real-time protection
 Don't disable antivirus temporarily
 Scan external drives before use
Security Features
126
Screen Locks
Screen locks prevent unauthorized access to devices when not in use.
Types of Screen Locks:
1. Password: Alphanumeric combination
2. PIN: Numeric code (usually 4-6 digits)
3. Pattern: Connecting dots in a specific sequence
4. Fingerprint: Biometric scan of fingerprint
5. Face Recognition: Facial feature identification
6. Iris Scan: Eye pattern recognition
Importance:
 Protects personal data
 Prevents unauthorized access

WEEK 10: COMPUTER WASTE MANAGEMENT
Meaning of E-waste
Definition: Electronic waste (e-waste) refers to discarded electrical or electronic devices and
their components that have reached the end of their useful life.
Common Examples:
 Computing Devices: Computers, laptops, tablets, keyboards, mice
 Mobile Devices: Smartphones, smartwatches
 Display Equipment: Monitors, televisions, projectors
160
 Peripherals: Printers, scanners, hard drives, USB drives
 Home Appliances: Microwaves, refrigerators, washing machines (with electronics)
 Entertainment: Game consoles, DVD players, cameras, speakers
 Batteries and Accessories: Chargers, headphones, adapters
Why E-waste is a Problem
Environmental Concerns
1. Toxic Materials E-waste contains hazardous substances:
 Lead: Damages nervous system (in batteries, monitors)
 Mercury: Affects brain and kidneys (in switches, screens)
 Cadmium: Causes kidney damage (in rechargeable batteries)
 Chromium: Can cause cancer (in metal casings)
 Brominated Flame Retardants: Disrupt hormones (in circuit boards)
2. Soil and Water Contamination
 Toxins leach into soil from landfills
 Contaminate groundwater
 Enter food chain through plants and animals
3. Air Pollution
 Burning e-waste releases toxic fumes
 Health risks for nearby communities
Health Concerns
 Respiratory problems
 Skin diseases and allergies
161
 Neurological damage
 Increased cancer risk
 Particularly dangerous for children
Resource Waste
 E-waste contains valuable materials (gold, silver, copper, platinum)
 Throwing away means losing these resources
 Requires more mining for new materials
Global Scale
 Over 50 million tons generated globally per year
 Fastest-growing waste stream
 Only 17-20% properly recycled
Safe Disposal Methods
1. Return to Manufacturer
How it works: Check manufacturer's website for take-back programs; some offer trade-in
incentives
Examples: Apple Recycling Program, Dell Reconnect, HP, Samsung
Benefits: Ensures proper handling, may receive discount on new purchase
2. Donation
Give working or repairable devices to those who need them.
Where to Donate: Schools, community centers, non-profit organizations, churches, libraries,
charities
162
Before Donating:
 Ensure device works properly
 Delete all personal data
 Include cables and accessories
 Clean the device
Benefits: Extends device lifespan, helps others access technology, reduces e-waste
3. Certified E-waste Recyclers
Specialized facilities that safely process electronic waste.
What They Do:
 Safely dismantle electronics
 Separate hazardous materials
 Recover valuable metals and components
 Follow environmental regulations
How to Find: Search online for certified recyclers with R2 or e-Stewards certifications
4. Government Collection Centers
Features: Free drop-off locations with regular collection hours
Finding Centers: Contact local government environmental office or check municipal websites
5. Retail Store Take-Back Programs
Examples: Best Buy, Staples, Office Depot
Features: Often free for small electronics; can combine with shopping trips
163
Important: Never Do These
 ❌ Don't throw in regular trash

Recycling Practices
The E-waste Recycling Process
Step 1: Collection and Transportation
 E-waste collected and transported to recycling facilities
Step 2: Manual Sorting
 Devices categorized by type
 Working items identified for reuse
 Batteries and hazardous materials removed
164
Step 3: Mechanical Shredding
 Items broken into small pieces
Step 4: Separation
 Magnetic separation removes iron and steel
 Water separation divides plastic from metal
 Screening sorts by size
Step 5: Material Recovery
 Precious metals extracted (gold, silver, platinum)
 Base metals recovered (copper, aluminum)
 Plastics sorted and processed
Step 6: Purification
 Metals refined to pure form
 Materials prepared for reuse in new products
Benefits of E-waste Recycling
Environmental Benefits:
 Reduces landfill waste by 80-90%
 Prevents soil and water pollution
 Conserves natural resources
 Reduces greenhouse gas emissions
Economic Benefits:
 Creates jobs in recycling industry
 Recovers valuable materials worth billions

 Supports circular economy
Resource Conservation:
