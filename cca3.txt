WEEK 1: TYPES AND FEATURES OF NIGERIAN TRADITIONAL ARTS
This lesson focuses on Nigerian Traditional Arts, exploring their types, features, functions,
and importance. Traditional arts form an essential part of Nigeria’s cultural heritage, reflecting
the history, beliefs, values, and creativity of different ethnic groups. Preserving these arts
ensures that future generations appreciate and maintain their cultural identity.
NIGERIAN TRADITIONAL ARTS:
 Nigerian traditional arts include visual arts, performing arts, and decorative arts that
have been developed over centuries.
 They express religious beliefs, social values, historical events, and communal identity.
 Examples of Nigerian traditional arts include:
4
o Sculpture and Carvings: Wooden, ivory, or stone sculptures representing deities,
ancestors, and important events.
o Masks and Costume Arts: Used in festivals, rituals, and ceremonies to convey
messages or entertain.
o Pottery and Weaving: Functional and decorative arts reflecting local
craftsmanship.
o Music and Dance: Integral to storytelling, rituals, and social events.
o Painting and Beadwork: Symbolic and decorative, often used in clothing and
ceremonial items.
Key Features of Nigerian Traditional Arts:
 Cultural Representation: Reflects ethnic identity and social norms.
 Symbolism: Often contains symbolic meaning for religious or social purposes.
 Functional and Aesthetic: Combines beauty with practical use in society.
 Community Participation: Created and performed collectively during festivals,
ceremonies, and rituals.
 Oral Transmission: Skills and knowledge are passed down from one generation to the
next.
FUNCTIONS OF NIGERIAN TRADITIONAL ARTS:
 Religious and Spiritual Functions: Used in rituals, worship, and communication with
ancestors or deities.
 Educational Functions: Teach moral lessons, history, and social values.
 Entertainment: Provide enjoyment during festivals, celebrations, and storytelling
sessions.
5
 Social and Political Functions: Celebrate important events, mark achievements, or
honor leaders.
 Economic Functions: Promote craftsmanship and generate income for artisans.
IMPORTANCE OF PRESERVING ART AS A CULTURAL ELEMENT:
 Maintains Cultural Identity: Helps future generations understand and appreciate their
heritage.
 Promotes National Unity: Traditional arts reflect the diversity of Nigeria while fostering
a sense of shared heritage.
 Encourages Creativity: Inspires new artistic expressions and innovation.
 Tourism and Economy: Attracts tourists and supports local artisans, contributing to
economic growth.
 Education: Serves as a tool for teaching history, values, and social norms.
EVALUATION:
1. Define Nigerian Traditional Arts.
2. List four types of Nigerian Traditional Arts and provide examples.
3. Mention three functions of Nigerian Traditional Arts in society.
4. Explain why it is important to preserve traditional arts as part of culture.
ASSIGNMENT:
1. Write a short essay (200–250 words) on the importance of Nigerian Traditional Arts in
preserving cultural heritage.
6
2. Prepare a chart showing at least five types of Nigerian Traditional Arts and their primary
functions.
3. Discuss in two paragraphs how traditional arts can contribute to national unity and

WEEK 2: CONTEMPORARY NIGERIAN ARTS AND ARTISTS
1. Contemporary Nigerian Artists and their contributions
2. How to generate income through contemporary artworks
INTRODUCTION
 Contemporary Art refers to modern-day art created in Nigeria from the mid-20th
century till today. It reflects current ideas, experiences, and culture using both traditional
and modern techniques.
 Nigerian contemporary artists use painting, sculpture, mixed media, textile design,
ceramics, installation art, photography, and digital arts.
 They promote Nigerian culture globally, create awareness on social issues, and earn
income through their artworks.
8
CONTEMPORARY NIGERIAN ARTISTS AND THEIR CONTRIBUTIONS
A. Notable Contemporary Nigerian Artists
1. Bruce Onobrakpeya – a renowned printmaker and painter; known for experimenting
with plastocast relief prints; trained many younger artists.
2. Ben Enwonwu – famous painter and sculptor; combined African themes with Western
techniques; created the famous Queen Elizabeth II bronze sculpture and Anyanwu statue
at the National Museum, Lagos.
3. Yusuf Grillo – modernist painter; used shades of blue and Yoruba themes in his
paintings; promoted cultural identity in art.
4. Uche Okeke – leader of the Zaria Art Society (“Zaria Rebels”); encouraged “Natural
Synthesis” – blending traditional African art with modern techniques.
5. Lamidi Fakeye – master wood sculptor; combined traditional Yoruba carving with
modern forms.
6. Peju Alatise – contemporary female artist; uses mixed media and installations to
highlight issues of gender, culture, and politics.
7. Nike Davies-Okundaye – textile artist; revived and promoted Adire (tie-and-dye) fabric
design; established art centers to train youths.
8. Jerry Buhari – painter and installation artist who uses his works to address social and
environmental issues.
B. Contributions of Contemporary Nigerian Artists
 Promotion of Nigerian culture and heritage at international exhibitions.
 Creation of employment opportunities for themselves and others.
 Documentation of Nigeria’s history, traditions, and experiences through visual arts.
 Raising awareness on social, political, and environmental issues.
 Training and mentoring of younger artists through workshops and art centers.
9
 Enhancing Nigeria’s global image and attracting tourism.
HOW TO GENERATE INCOME THROUGH CONTEMPORARY ARTWORKS
A. Sources of Income for Contemporary Artists
1. Art Exhibitions and Galleries – selling artworks through local and international art
galleries and exhibitions.
2. Commissions – producing customized artworks for individuals, institutions, or
government projects.
3. Cultural Tourism – artworks attract tourists who buy crafts and paintings.
4. Art Auctions – selling artworks at high-value auctions locally and globally.
5. Online Platforms – selling through websites, social media (Instagram, Facebook), and
digital marketplaces.
6. Training/Workshops – teaching art in schools, workshops, and private classes.
7. Merchandising – reproducing artworks on T-shirts, bags, calendars, and greeting cards.
8. Interior Decoration and Public Sculptures – beautifying homes, offices, and public
spaces.
B. Importance of Art as an Economic Resource
 Provides self-employment and reduces unemployment.
 Encourages creativity and innovation.
 Contributes to national development through cultural exports.
 Improves the standard of living of artists.

WEEK 3: MEANING AND USE OF MOTIFS (PART 1)
Meaning of motif and pattern; uses of motifs
MEANING OF MOTIF
 A motif is a decorative design, symbol, or recurring element used in works of art, textiles,
crafts, and architecture.
 It can be a shape, image, idea, or symbol that is repeated to form a design.
 In visual arts, motifs are used to create patterns, beauty, rhythm, and identity in
artworks.
 Motifs can be natural (from plants, animals, human figures, shells, clouds, etc.) or manmade/
geometric (lines, circles, zigzags, spirals, triangles, etc.).
 In African art, motifs are often symbolic, representing culture, traditions, beliefs, and
values.
12
MEANING OF PATTERN
 A pattern is the regular arrangement or repetition of motifs to decorate a surface.
 When motifs are repeated in a planned and organized way, they form patterns.
 Patterns can be:
o Natural patterns – seen in leaves, flowers, shells, animal skins.
o Geometric patterns – made from shapes like squares, circles, lines, triangles.
o Abstract patterns – based on imagination, with no direct reference to real objects.
USES OF MOTIFS
Motifs are widely used in art, design, and everyday life. Their uses include:
1. Decoration
o Used to beautify textiles, pottery, calabash, walls, floors, and buildings.
o Found in traditional body adornments such as tattoos and scarifications.
2. Symbolism
o Many motifs carry meanings and represent ideas, proverbs, or cultural values.
o For example, Adinkra symbols in Ghana or Uli designs in Igbo culture.
3. Identity and Heritage
o Motifs help identify a people’s cultural background and preserve traditions.
o Certain motifs are peculiar to specific tribes, regions, or societies.
4. Religious and Ritual Use
o Motifs are used in shrines, masks, religious garments, and sacred objects.
13
o They serve as a medium of communication between humans and the spiritual
world.
5. Education and Communication
o Motifs can be used to teach morals, history, and social values through their
symbolic meanings.
6. Commercial Purposes
o Motifs are used in advertising, packaging, fashion design, and branding.
o They add beauty and uniqueness to products.
EVALUATION

2. Differentiate between motif and pattern.
3. Mention four uses of motifs in art and daily life.
4. Give two examples of natural motifs and two examples of geometric motifs.
5. Explain why motifs are important in African traditional art.
ASSIGNMENT

that uses them.
2. Draw and label three simple motifs of your choice (one natural, one geometric, and one
abstract).
3. Explain in detail the differences between motif and pattern, with at least two examples

WEEK 4: MEANING AND USE OF MOTIFS (PART 2)
Creation of motifs from nature and imagination; repeat patterns; transfer of motifs on paper or
fabric
INTRODUCTION TO MOTIFS
 A motif is a decorative design, shape, or figure used in artworks, textiles, and other
creative projects.
 Motifs can be abstract, geometric, or representational, often conveying a theme,
message, or aesthetic appeal.
 Motifs are commonly used in painting, fabric design, sculpture, and architectural
decoration.
15
CREATION OF MOTIFS FROM NATURE AND IMAGINATION
 From Nature:
o Students can draw inspiration from plants, flowers, leaves, animals, insects, or
natural landscapes.
o Example: A motif may be based on a sunflower, a butterfly, or a flowing river
pattern.
o Encourage observation of forms, shapes, and textures in nature to develop
creative motifs.
 From Imagination:
o Students can design motifs from ideas, dreams, symbols, or cultural stories.
o Imaginative motifs may include fantasy creatures, abstract shapes, or symbolic
patterns.
o Imagination allows for unique and original designs not limited by natural forms.
 Techniques:
1. Sketching rough designs in a notebook.
2. Refining motifs with clear lines and shapes.
3. Considering balance, proportion, and visual appeal in the motif.
REPEAT PATTERNS
 Repeat patterns involve reproducing a motif in a regular or symmetrical sequence
across a surface.
 Common types of repeat patterns:
1. Horizontal repeat: Motif repeated from left to right.
2. Vertical repeat: Motif repeated from top to bottom.
16
3. Block repeat: Motif repeated both horizontally and vertically in blocks.
4. Diagonal repeat: Motif repeated along diagonal lines for dynamic effect.
 Importance: Repeat patterns create harmony, rhythm, and visual interest in artworks
and textiles.
TRANSFER OF MOTIFS ON PAPER OR FABRIC
 On Paper:
o Use pencils, pens, or markers to reproduce motifs from sketches.
o Can be done freehand or with stencils for accuracy.
 On Fabric:
o Techniques include:
1. Block printing: Carving a motif on a block and stamping onto fabric.
2. Stenciling: Cutting a motif from a card and applying paint or dye through
it.
3. Batik or Tie-Dye: Using wax or tying techniques to resist dye and create
repeated motifs.
 Considerations:
o Use contrasting colors for clarity.
o Ensure motifs are evenly spaced for aesthetic balance.
o Experiment with different materials and surfaces to achieve desired effects.
IMPORTANCE OF MOTIFS AND REPEAT PATTERNS
1. Enhance the aesthetic appeal of artworks, clothing, and home décor.
17
2. Preserve and promote cultural identity through traditional patterns.
3. Encourage creativity, observation, and design skills among students.
4. Facilitate practical application in fashion, interior design, and art projects.
5. Develop understanding of balance, proportion, and rhythm in visual composition.
EVALUATION
1. Define the term “motif” in art and design.
2. List two sources of inspiration for creating motifs.
3. Explain what repeat patterns are and mention two types.
4. Describe two techniques of transferring motifs onto fabric.
5. Why is it important to use motifs in artworks and textiles?

1. Draw three motifs inspired by nature and three motifs from imagination.

WEEK 6: EXHIBITION AND DISPLAY TECHNIQUES
Preparing artworks; display techniques; categorising; space, screens and lighting
PREPARING ARTWORKS
Exhibition and display of artworks require careful preparation to make them attractive and
presentable. Preparing artworks means getting them ready for public presentation in an art
gallery, museum, school exhibition, or any art show.
Steps in preparing artworks include:
19
1. Selection of artworks – Choose the best works that represent creativity, originality, and
quality.
2. Finishing touches – Repair or retouch damaged areas of artworks to improve neatness.
3. Mounting and framing – Drawings, paintings, and photographs are mounted on
cardboard or framed to give them a professional look.
4. Labelling – Each artwork should be labelled with the artist’s name, title of the work,
medium used (e.g., oil paint, pencil, clay), date of production, and sometimes a short
description.
5. Cleaning – Sculptures, ceramics, and textile works must be dusted and cleaned to
enhance their appearance before display.
Proper preparation ensures that artworks are attractive, protected, and meaningful to the audience.
DISPLAY TECHNIQUES
Display techniques are the different methods or ways by which artworks are presented to the
public for appreciation. The aim is to create an attractive arrangement that will capture the
attention of viewers.
Major display techniques include:
1. Wall display – Hanging paintings, drawings, and photographs on walls.
2. Table display – Putting small artworks like ceramics, crafts, and textiles on tables for
easy viewing.
3. Glass case display – Displaying valuable or fragile artworks inside glass cases for
protection.
4. Panel and screen display – Using movable panels or screens to hang artworks when
wall space is not enough.
5. Floor display – Sculptures and large installations are placed on the floor or pedestals.
20
6. Open-air display – Works can also be exhibited outdoors for wider community
participation.
Effective display techniques make artworks more appealing and communicate the artist’s ideas
better.
CATEGORISING ARTWORKS
Categorising artworks means grouping them according to their type, medium, or theme to create
orderliness and easy understanding during an exhibition.
Ways of categorising include:
1. By medium – Grouping paintings, drawings, sculptures, ceramics, textile works,
photographs, etc. separately.
2. By theme – Grouping works based on subject matter such as nature, culture, religion,
history, or abstract themes.
3. By size – Large works may be displayed in one section while smaller works are arranged
elsewhere.
4. By artist – Works can also be grouped according to the names of the artists for
recognition.
Categorising helps the audience to follow a logical sequence and understand the artworks better.
SPACE, SCREENS AND LIGHTING
Space, screens, and lighting are important in exhibition and display techniques because they
determine how artworks are viewed.
1. Space – Adequate space must be provided between artworks so that each can be viewed
clearly without obstruction. Overcrowding reduces the quality of the exhibition.
21
2. Screens – Screens or partitions are used to divide exhibition areas, create privacy, and
provide extra surfaces for display.
3. Lighting – Proper lighting enhances the beauty of artworks. Bright light is needed for
paintings and textiles, while soft lighting is preferable for delicate artworks like
photographs. Natural and artificial light can be combined to give the best effect.
A good arrangement of space, screens, and lighting ensures comfort for viewers and gives
artworks their full aesthetic value.
SUMMARY
 Preparing artworks involves selecting, retouching, mounting, framing, labelling, and
cleaning.
 Display techniques include wall display, table display, glass case display, panel/screen
display, floor display, and open-air display.
 Categorising artworks may be done by medium, theme, size, or artist.
 Space, screens, and lighting are essential in giving artworks visibility, protection, and
beauty.

WEEK 8: LETTERING
Lettering is a fundamental aspect of visual communication and graphic design. It involves the
art and technique of creating letters for writing, signage, advertisements, artworks, and
decorative purposes. Mastery of lettering enables students to communicate messages clearly
and artistically.
MEANING OF LETTERING:
 Lettering is the art of drawing or designing letters in a visually appealing and readable
manner.
 It is distinct from handwriting as it focuses on precision, style, and proportion.
24
 Lettering is used in various fields such as graphic design, advertising, signage,
calligraphy, and art projects.
TYPES OF LETTERING:
 A. Block Letters:
o Letters are simple, bold, and upright, often used in headlines or labels.
o Characteristics: Easy to read, no decoration, uniform size.
 B. Cursive Letters:
o Letters are joined together in a flowing manner.
o Used for artistic expression or formal writing.
o Emphasizes elegance and continuity.
 C. Roman Letters:
o Based on classical Roman inscriptions.
o Features: Thick and thin strokes, serifs (small lines at the ends), proportional
spacing.
 D. Gothic/Blackletter:
o Angular and ornamental letters, often used in signage, certificates, or artistic
works.
 E. Script and Decorative Letters:
o Artistic or playful letters designed for posters, advertisements, or creative
designs.
LETTERING CONSTRUCTION:
25
 Lettering construction involves drawing each letter systematically, ensuring proportion,
alignment, and consistency.
 Tools: pencils, rulers, compasses, erasers, drawing pens, and grids for guidance.
 Key considerations:
o Height and width of letters
o Spacing between letters (kerning)
o Uniformity in stroke thickness
o Style consistency throughout the text
STAGES IN THE CONSTRUCTION OF LETTERS:
1. Sketching Guidelines: Draw horizontal, vertical, and diagonal lines to maintain
uniform height, width, and alignment.
2. Drawing Basic Shapes: Outline each letter using simple geometric forms (rectangles,
circles, triangles).
3. Adding Details: Refine letters by adding serifs, curves, or stylistic elements depending
on the type of lettering.
4. Finalizing and Inking: Trace the letters with inking pens or markers to enhance clarity
and visual appeal.
5. Reviewing and Correcting: Check for uniformity, spacing, and errors, and make
necessary adjustments.
EVALUATION:
1. Define lettering and explain its importance in art and communication.
2. List and describe four types of lettering.
26
3. Explain the stages involved in the construction of letters.
4. Identify tools commonly used in lettering construction and their purposes.

WEEK 9: CONSTRUCTION AND DESIGN
Meaning of package design; functions of package design; processes in package design
MEANING OF PACKAGE DESIGN
 Package design refers to the creation of the outer wrapping or container of a product. It
involves the artistic and functional design of a package to protect, preserve, and present
goods attractively for sale.
 It is the process of combining form, structure, colour, imagery, typography, and materials
to make a product suitable for marketing and distribution.
 Package design does not only protect the product but also communicates important
information about it to the consumer.
28
FUNCTIONS OF PACKAGE DESIGN
1. Protection of Goods
o Packages protect goods from physical damage (e.g., breakage, leakage, or
spoilage).
o They shield products from environmental factors such as dust, moisture, heat, or
pests.
2. Preservation of Quality
o Packages help to keep food items fresh and uncontaminated.
o Airtight packaging prevents decay and prolongs the shelf-life of perishable goods.
3. Identification of Products
o Packaging helps customers recognize and differentiate products on shelves.
o Use of brand names, logos, colours, and symbols makes products stand out.
4. Information and Communication
o Packages often carry important details such as manufacturer’s name, date of
manufacture, expiry date, ingredients, and instructions for use.
o They serve as a medium of communication between producer and consumer.
5. Marketing and Promotion
o Attractive packaging design can influence consumer choice.
o It serves as a silent salesman by drawing attention to the product.
6. Convenience in Handling
o Packaging makes transportation, storage, and handling easier.
o Items can be grouped, carried, and stored without difficulty.
7. Legal and Safety Functions
29
o Packages may contain safety warnings, government certifications, and barcodes.
o They protect consumers from harmful substances.
PROCESSES IN PACKAGE DESIGN
1. Research and Planning
o Study the product, target market, competitors, and consumer needs.
o Determine the purpose of the package and the message to be communicated.
2. Concept Development
o Generate creative ideas through sketches and brainstorming.
o Select suitable colours, shapes, and themes that match the product.
3. Material Selection
o Choose the right packaging material such as paper, cardboard, plastic, metal, or
glass depending on the product’s nature.
4. Design Creation
o Apply graphic design elements like typography (fonts), logos, colours, and
images.
o Ensure the design is visually appealing and functional.
5. Prototyping
o Create a sample package for testing.
o Check for attractiveness, durability, and suitability for the product.
6. Evaluation and Modification
o Test the prototype with consumers or experts to identify weaknesses.
o Make necessary improvements before mass production.
30
7. Production
o Print and manufacture the package in large quantities.
o Distribute to shops, supermarkets, and markets along with the product.
EVALUATION
1. Define package design.
2. List four functions of package design.
3. State three processes in package design.

5. Explain how package design serves as a means of communication.
ASSIGNMENT

INTRODUCTION
 Marketing of Artworks refers to the processes and strategies through which artists
promote, distribute, and sell their art to the public.
32
 It ensures that art products reach the right audience, generate income, and sustain the art
business.
 Marketing involves funding (capital), outlets (where art is sold), methods of marketing
(how art is promoted), and pricing (value placed on art).
SOURCES OF FUNDING FOR ART BUSINESS
To run an art business successfully, capital is required. Sources of funding include:
1. Personal Savings – money set aside by the artist for starting or expanding an art business.
2. Family and Friends – financial support or loans from relatives and close friends.
3. Loans from Banks and Microfinance Institutions – financial institutions that give
credit facilities to support small-scale art businesses.
4. Government Grants and Subsidies – financial assistance from federal, state, or local
governments to promote art and culture.
5. Non-Governmental Organisations (NGOs) – some NGOs fund art projects, especially
those with social, cultural, or educational value.
6. Art Collectors and Patrons – wealthy individuals or groups who invest in and support
artists.
7. Crowdfunding Platforms – raising money through online contributions from supporters
(e.g., GoFundMe, Kickstarter).
8. Partnerships and Sponsorships – collaboration with companies or individuals who
support art in exchange for recognition or profit-sharing.
OUTLETS FOR MARKETING ART PRODUCTS
These are the places and platforms where art products can be displayed and sold:
1. Art Galleries – established venues for exhibiting and selling artworks.
33
2. Museums – display of artworks for cultural preservation and sale to interested collectors.
3. Art Shops and Boutiques – retail outlets selling craftworks, textiles, and paintings.
4. Cultural Centres and Tourist Attractions – areas where local and foreign tourists buy
artworks.
5. Art Fairs and Exhibitions – special events where artists display and sell their products
to a wide audience.
6. Online Platforms – websites, social media (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok), and ecommerce
platforms.
7. Hotels, Restaurants, and Public Spaces – places where artworks are displayed for
appreciation and purchase.
8. Direct Sales – selling artworks directly to customers or patrons without intermediaries.
WAYS OF MARKETING ART PRODUCTS
Marketing strategies adopted by artists include:
1. Advertising – using newspapers, magazines, radio, TV, and digital media to create
awareness about artworks.
2. Online Marketing – using websites, blogs, and social media pages to showcase artworks
to a global audience.
3. Word of Mouth – personal recommendations from satisfied clients.
4. Art Exhibitions and Competitions – displaying works to attract buyers, collectors, and
critics.
5. Networking – establishing connections with art dealers, galleries, and other artists.
6. Public Relations (PR) – building a strong public image through interviews, art talks, and
workshops.
34
7. Catalogues and Flyers – printed materials showing artworks with prices and
descriptions.
8. Collaborations – working with designers, architects, or fashion brands to promote art
products.
PRICING OF ARTWORKS
Pricing is determining the value of an artwork in money terms. Factors that influence pricing
include:
1. Cost of Production – cost of materials, studio space, and labour involved in making the
artwork.
2. Time and Effort – the length of time and level of skill required in producing the artwork.
3. Size and Medium – larger artworks and those with expensive media (bronze, oil on
canvas, wood sculpture) are priced higher.
4. Artistic Reputation – works by well-known artists attract higher prices.
5. Rarity and Uniqueness – unique, limited-edition, or rare pieces are sold at premium
prices.
6. Market Demand – the level of interest and desire for the artwork among buyers.
7. Cultural and Historical Value – artworks tied to significant events or traditions have
higher value.
8. Economic Conditions – general financial situation of the country or global art market
can affect pricing.

Embroidery designs can come from various natural, cultural, and artificial sources. These include:

METHODS OF TRANSFERRING MOTIFS ON FABRICS
Before stitching begins, the embroidery design (motif) must be transferred to fabric. Common

1. Tracing Method
o Place tracing paper with the design over fabric.

2. Carbon Paper Method
44

WEEK 3: KNITTING
Meaning of knitting; types of knitting; materials/tools; basic knitting stitches
MEANING OF KNITTING
 Knitting is a method of creating fabric by interlocking loops of yarn or thread using
needles or machines.
 It is one of the oldest forms of fabric-making, alongside weaving and crocheting.
 Knitting can be done by hand (using knitting needles) or by machine (industrial knitting).
 It produces soft, elastic, and warm fabrics commonly used for sweaters, socks, caps,
scarves, and blankets.
47
 Unlike weaving, which uses two sets of threads (warp and weft), knitting uses one
continuous yarn looped to form fabric.
TYPES OF KNITTING
1. Hand Knitting
o Involves the use of two or more needles to knit fabric manually.
o It allows for creativity, personal design, and unique patterns.
2. Machine Knitting
o Done with a knitting machine that produces fabric faster and in larger quantities.
o Commonly used in textile industries to make ready-made clothes.
3. Circular Knitting
o Produces a seamless, tubular piece of fabric.
o Often used for making socks, hats, and sleeves.
4. Flat Knitting
o Produces flat pieces of fabric that are later sewn together.
o Used for sweaters, cardigans, and blankets.
MATERIALS/TOOLS FOR KNITTING
 Yarn/Wool – the thread used in knitting, available in different thicknesses, textures, and
colors.
 Knitting Needles – tools used to make loops. They can be:
o Straight needles (for flat knitting)
o Circular needles (for tubular knitting)
48
o Double-pointed needles (for small circular work like socks)
 Measuring Tape – for accurate measurement of knitted items.
 Scissors – for cutting yarn.
 Stitch Markers – for marking points in a pattern.
 Row Counter – for keeping track of the number of rows knitted.
 Yarn Needle (Tapestry Needle) – for sewing pieces together and weaving in yarn ends.
BASIC KNITTING STITCHES
1. Knit Stitch
o The most basic stitch in knitting, forming the foundation of many patterns.
o Produces a smooth surface on one side and bumpy texture on the other.
2. Purl Stitch
o Opposite of the knit stitch.
o Produces a bumpy surface on the front and smooth surface at the back.
3. Stockinette Stitch
o Made by alternating rows of knit and purl stitches.
o Smooth on the right side, bumpy on the wrong side.
4. Garter Stitch
o Made by knitting every row (or purling every row).
o Produces a stretchy, textured fabric.
5. Rib Stitch
o Made by alternating knit and purl stitches in the same row (e.g., knit 2, purl 2).
o Creates stretchy fabric used for cuffs, necklines, and hems.
49

 Provides clothing and warmth.
 Encourages creativity and skill development.
 Serves as a source of income and livelihood (textile industry).
 Preserves cultural and traditional craft techniques.
 Used in household items such as blankets, mats, and cushion covers.

MEANING OF CROCHETING
 Crocheting is a handicraft technique in which yarn or thread is looped and interlocked
using a hooked needle to create fabric, designs, or decorative items.
 It is both a practical and artistic skill used for making clothing, household items, and
accessories.
 Crocheting allows for creative expression, unique patterns, and personalized designs.
 It is different from knitting as it uses one hook instead of two needles.
51
MATERIALS AND TOOLS USED IN CROCHETING
1. Yarn or Thread:
o Comes in various colors, textures, and thicknesses.
o Choice depends on the item to be created (e.g., thick yarn for scarves, thin thread
for lace).
2. Crochet Hooks:
o Metal, plastic, or wooden hooks of different sizes.
o Hook size must match the thickness of the yarn/thread.
3. Scissors:
o Used to cut yarn or thread cleanly.
4. Measuring Tape:
o Helps in measuring the length and width of the crocheted piece.
5. Stitch Markers: (Optional)
o Help mark the beginning or end of a row or pattern repeat.
6. Needle or Tapestry Needle:
o Used for weaving in loose ends and finishing the piece neatly.
HOW TO CROCHET
Basic Steps:
1. Making a Slip Knot:
o The starting point of all crochet work. A loop is created and tightened on the hook.
2. Creating a Foundation Chain:
52
o Series of chain stitches that form the base of the project.
o Determine the length according to the design.
3. Basic Stitches:
o Single Crochet (sc): Insert hook, yarn over, pull through, yarn over again, pull
through two loops.
o Double Crochet (dc): Yarn over, insert hook, yarn over, pull through, yarn over,
pull through two loops, yarn over, pull through remaining loops.
o Half-Double Crochet (hdc): Combination between single and double crochet for
medium height stitches.
4. Following Patterns:
o Work row by row or in the round according to the design or motif.
o Maintain consistent tension for neat and uniform stitches.
5. Finishing the Work:
o Cut the yarn leaving a tail, pull through the last loop, and weave in loose ends.
o Block the piece if necessary to achieve the correct shape.

1. Clothing Items: Sweaters, scarves, hats, gloves, and socks.
2. Home Décor: Tablecloths, doilies, cushion covers, curtains, and wall hangings.

4. Artistic Pieces: Amigurumi (crocheted stuffed toys) and creative patterns.

WEEK 6: BATIK WORK
Meaning of batik; materials/tools for batik work; making a batik project
MEANING OF BATIK
Batik is a traditional method of fabric decoration in which designs are created on cloth by
applying wax to certain areas before dyeing. The wax serves as a resist, preventing dye from
penetrating the covered areas. After dyeing, the wax is removed, leaving behind beautiful
patterns where the dye could not reach.
The word Batik originated from Indonesia, but the practice is widely used in many African
countries, especially Nigeria. In Nigeria, Batik is part of the textile arts and is popular in Yoruba
55
land, Igbo land, and other regions. It is used to produce decorative fabrics for clothing,
household items, and cultural purposes.
Batik is valued because it:
 Encourages creativity and originality.
 Reflects cultural identity and tradition.
 Serves as a source of income for textile artists.
 Produces unique designs since no two Batik works are ever exactly the same.
MATERIALS/TOOLS FOR BATIK WORK
To make a Batik, several materials and tools are needed. They include:
1. Fabric – Cotton fabric or calico is most suitable because it absorbs dye well.
2. Wax – This is usually beeswax or paraffin wax, used to resist the dye. Sometimes a
mixture of both is used.
3. Dyes – These are colour substances dissolved in water for dyeing the fabric. Common
types include vat dye, reactive dye, and indigo dye.
4. Tjanting (or Canting) – A special pen-like tool used to apply hot wax on fabric in fine
lines and patterns.
5. Brushes – For applying wax or dye over larger areas of the fabric.
6. Stamps/Blocks – Wooden, metal, or foam blocks carved with designs, used to stamp wax
onto the fabric.
7. Dyeing containers – Bowls, buckets, or vats used for preparing and holding the dye
solution.
8. Spoons/Stirring sticks – For mixing dye solutions.
9. Heating source – Charcoal stove, gas cooker, or electric heater for melting wax.
56
10. Iron – For removing wax by placing fabric between absorbent papers and pressing with a
hot iron.
11. Protective materials – Gloves, aprons, and newspapers to protect hands, clothes, and
workspace.
MAKING A BATIK PROJECT
The process of making a Batik involves careful steps to achieve the desired patterns and colours.
Step 1: Preparing the fabric
 Wash the fabric to remove starch, oil, or dirt that may prevent dye absorption.
 Dry and iron the fabric before use.
Step 2: Designing the pattern
 Sketch the intended design lightly on the fabric with a pencil or chalk.
 Alternatively, freehand designs can be made directly with wax.
Step 3: Applying the wax
 Melt the wax using a heating source.
 Apply wax on the parts of the fabric where dye should not penetrate using a tjanting tool,
brush, or block stamps.
 Ensure the wax penetrates both sides of the fabric.
Step 4: Dyeing the fabric
 Prepare the dye solution in a container.
 Dip the waxed fabric into the dye solution, stirring gently until it absorbs the desired
colour.
 Remove the fabric and allow it to dry.
Step 5: Repeating the process (for multiple colours)
57
 If more than one colour is desired, reapply wax to other areas and repeat the dyeing
process with a different colour.
 Always start with light colours before applying darker ones.
Step 6: Removing the wax
 After dyeing, remove the wax by boiling the fabric in hot water or ironing it between
newspapers to melt and absorb the wax.
 Rinse and dry the fabric.
Step 7: Finishing
 The completed fabric is ironed and prepared for use.
 The result is a beautifully decorated cloth with colourful and unique Batik designs.
SUMMARY
 Batik is a fabric decoration technique using wax-resist dyeing.
 Common materials include fabric, wax, dyes, tjanting tool, brushes, blocks, dyeing
containers, heating source, and iron.
 Making Batik involves preparing the fabric, applying wax, dyeing, repeating for more
colours, removing wax, and finishing.
EVALUATION
1. Define Batik.
2. Mention four reasons why Batik is important.
3. List six materials/tools used in Batik work.
4. Describe three steps involved in making a Batik.
5. Explain why cotton fabric is the most suitable for Batik.

SOLFA NOTATION
 Definition:
o Solfa notation (also called tonic sol-fa) is a method of representing musical
sounds using syllables (do, re, mi, fa, so, la, ti, do).
o It is used in teaching singing and helps learners to easily identify pitches and
scales in music.
 The Solfa Syllables:
63
o Doh (Do) – the first note of the scale.
o Re – the second note.
o Mi – the third note.
o Fa – the fourth note.
o So (Sol) – the fifth note.
o La – the sixth note.
o Ti (Si) – the seventh note.
o Doh (Do) – the eighth note (repetition at a higher octave).
 Scale in Solfa Notation:
o Ascending scale: do – re – mi – fa – so – la – ti – do.
o Descending scale: do – ti – la – so – fa – mi – re – do.
MUSIC NOTE VALUES
 Definition:
o Note values show the duration or length of a musical sound.
o Each note has a specific time value, usually expressed in beats.
 Types of Note Values:
1. Semibreve ( ) – lasts 4 beats.
2. Minim ( ) – lasts 2 beats.
3. Crotchet ( ) – lasts 1 beat.
4. Quaver ( ) – lasts half a beat.
5. Semiquaver ( ) – lasts a quarter of a beat.
 Corresponding Rests:
64
o Each note has a rest symbol showing silence for the same duration.
o Semibreve rest, minim rest, crotchet rest, quaver rest, and semiquaver rest.
RHYTHM
 Definition:
o Rhythm is the arrangement of sounds and silences (notes and rests) in music
according to time.
o It gives music its movement, flow, and liveliness.
 Importance of Rhythm:
o Helps to organize sounds in a steady pattern.
o Makes music interesting and danceable.
o Helps singers and instrumentalists to keep time.
 Examples of Rhythm Patterns:
o Crotchet – Crotchet – Crotchet – Crotchet (ta – ta – ta – ta).
o Minim – Minim (taa – taa).
o Crotchet – Quaver – Quaver – Crotchet (ta – ti-ti – ta).
CLAPPING A RHYTHM
 Steps in Clapping Rhythm:
1. Identify the time signature (e.g., 2/4, 3/4, 4/4).
2. Look at the note values in the given piece.
3. Clap according to the duration of each note.

 Crotchet ( ): clap once per beat.
 Quaver ( ): clap quickly, half a beat.

o Crotchet – Crotchet – Minim – Crotchet.

WEEK 2: PROCESSES OF CHOREOGRAPHY
1. Meaning of choreographer
2. Processes employed by a choreographer to create a dance
3. Creating a dance pattern
INTRODUCTION
 Dance is one of the most expressive forms of performing art. It combines movements,
gestures, rhythm, and expression to communicate ideas, feelings, or stories.
 Choreography is the art of creating, arranging, and designing dance movements into a
meaningful performance.
76
 A person who creates and directs dance movements is called a choreographer.
 Choreography is essential in cultural dances, drama presentations, musicals, social events,
and professional performances.
MEANING OF CHOREOGRAPHER
 A Choreographer is a person who plans, creates, and arranges dance movements to be
performed by dancers.
 The choreographer’s role is similar to that of a director in drama or a composer in music.
 Functions of a choreographer include:
1. Designing movements and steps to fit music and rhythm.
2. Coordinating dancers and ensuring harmony of movement.
3. Communicating messages, stories, or emotions through dance.
4. Teaching and rehearsing dance sequences with performers.
5. Preserving cultural dances and also creating new dance styles.
PROCESSES EMPLOYED BY A CHOREOGRAPHER TO CREATE A DANCE
The choreographer follows a series of processes to create a dance pattern:
1. Concept Development
o Deciding on the theme, story, or message of the dance.
o Inspiration may come from culture, religion, history, social issues, or personal
expression.
2. Selection of Music and Rhythm
o Music gives dance its mood, pace, and style.
77
o The choreographer selects appropriate songs, instruments, or rhythms.
3. Creation of Movements
o Designing specific steps, gestures, body positions, and facial expressions that
match the rhythm and theme.
4. Arrangement and Sequencing
o Organising the dance steps into a logical order.
o Ensuring smooth transitions between movements.
5. Formation and Space Design
o Planning how dancers will move on stage (lines, circles, diagonals, groups, solos).
o Proper use of space to achieve visual appeal.
6. Rehearsals and Corrections
o Teaching the steps to dancers.
o Repeating until accuracy, timing, and coordination are achieved.
7. Performance and Presentation
o The final stage where the choreographed dance is performed before an audience.
CREATING A DANCE PATTERN
 A dance pattern is the specific arrangement of steps, movements, and formations that
create a dance sequence.
 To create a dance pattern, the following should be considered:
1. Theme or Purpose – Decide if it is a cultural, social, religious, or entertainment dance.
2. Music Selection – Choose a song or drum rhythm that matches the style.
3. Movements – Select steps that are easy to learn but expressive.
4. Formation – Arrange dancers in lines, circles, or groups to enhance performance.
78
5. Coordination – Ensure all dancers move in harmony with the rhythm.
6. Expression – Add gestures, miming, and facial expressions to give meaning.
Example:
 A choreographer wants to create a dance about "Unity".
 Music chosen: a traditional drumbeat.
 Steps: dancers hold hands, move in a circle, and break into smaller groups, then reunite.
 Meaning: despite diversity, people come together in harmony.

3. List five processes a choreographer follows to create a dance.
4. Define dance pattern.
5. Mention three elements to consider when creating a dance pattern.

WEEK 4: UNITY
This lesson focuses on the concept of unity, its importance in personal, school, and societal life,
the causes of disunity, and practical ways to maintain unity. Unity is essential for peaceful
coexistence, social harmony, and collective progress. Understanding unity helps students
appreciate the need for cooperation, tolerance, and mutual respect in different spheres of life.
MEANING OF UNITY:
 Unity refers to the state of being together, working as one, or maintaining harmony
among individuals, groups, or communities.
84
 It involves cooperation, understanding, mutual respect, and the willingness to
resolve differences peacefully.
 Unity is the foundation for strong families, effective schools, and a cohesive society.
Key points:
 Unity promotes peace, progress, and social stability.
 It encourages teamwork, collaboration, and collective problem-solving.
CAUSES OF DISUNITY AT HOME, IN SCHOOL, AND IN THE SOCIETY:
 At Home:
o Favoritism among siblings or family members.
o Misunderstandings or poor communication between parents and children.
o Conflict over resources, responsibilities, or family decisions.
 In School:
o Cliques, rivalry, or bullying among students.
o Disagreement between teachers and students due to strict rules or
miscommunication.
o Competition over leadership positions or academic recognition.
 In the Society:
o Ethnic, religious, or cultural differences leading to prejudice.
o Corruption, inequality, or unfair treatment.
o Political disagreements, rivalry, and lack of social justice.
Key point: Understanding the causes of disunity helps individuals and communities address
issues before they escalate.
85
HOW TO MAINTAIN UNITY AT HOME, IN SCHOOL, AND IN THE SOCIETY:
 At Home:
o Encourage love, respect, and sharing among family members.
o Open and honest communication about issues and responsibilities.
o Fair treatment of all members, avoiding favoritism or discrimination.
 In School:
o Promote teamwork, cooperation, and respect among students and teachers.
o Encourage inclusion and fairness in school activities and leadership roles.
o Resolve conflicts peacefully through dialogue and understanding.
 In Society:
o Promote tolerance, equality, and justice among citizens.
o Respect cultural, ethnic, and religious diversity.
o Engage in community service and collective problem-solving.
DEMONSTRATION OF ACTS OF UNITY:
 Helping a classmate or neighbor in need.
 Participating in group projects or community development activities.
 Attending and respecting cultural, religious, or social events together.
 Resolving disputes peacefully through dialogue and mutual understanding.
 Sharing resources fairly and encouraging teamwork in all settings.
Key point: Acts of unity strengthen relationships and foster cooperation, trust, and social
harmony.
86
