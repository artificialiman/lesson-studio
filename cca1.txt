WEEK 1: INTRODUCTION TO ARTS
Meaning of Arts
Arts can be defined as the expression of human creativity, imagination, and skill to produce
works that are appreciated primarily for their beauty, emotional power, or intellectual content. It
is a form of communication that conveys ideas, feelings, beliefs, and cultural values through
various mediums such as visual forms, performance, music, literature, and craft.
Art is not limited to professional artists; it is a universal aspect of human life. From simple
decorations and traditional crafts to sophisticated paintings, sculptures, and performances, art is a
reflection of human experience and society.
Definitions of Arts
Different scholars and institutions have defined arts in various ways:
 Traditional Definition: Art is a human activity that produces aesthetically pleasing
objects or performances.
 Educational Perspective: Art is a subject that develops creativity, critical thinking, and
problem-solving skills in students.
3
 Cultural Perspective: Art is a means of preserving and transmitting cultural heritage and
societal values.
History and Origin of Arts
The origin of art dates back to prehistoric times when early humans created cave paintings,
carvings, and sculptures. These early artworks served multiple purposes:
 Communication: Conveying stories, events, and messages across generations.
 Religious and Ritualistic Functions: Some art was created to worship deities, honor
ancestors, or perform rituals.
 Decoration and Identity: Art was used to beautify living spaces, objects, and the human
body, reflecting social status and identity.
Over time, art evolved through different historical periods and civilizations:
1. Prehistoric Art – Examples include rock paintings, cave drawings, pottery, and
sculptures.
2. Ancient Civilizations – Egyptians, Greeks, and Romans developed art forms like
architecture, sculpture, and decorative crafts to reflect their religion, culture, and political
systems.
3. Medieval and Renaissance Art – Art became more refined, emphasizing realism,
perspective, and human emotion. Painting, sculpture, and architecture flourished.
4. Modern and Contemporary Art – Art expanded into new forms, including photography,
digital art, cinema, and performance art, reflecting changes in technology and society.
Importance of Arts
Art plays a significant role in society:
 It preserves cultural heritage and teaches historical values.
 It stimulates creativity and imagination, fostering innovation in individuals.
 It enhances emotional expression, helping individuals communicate feelings and
experiences.
4
 It provides aesthetic pleasure, enriching everyday life with beauty.
 It promotes social cohesion by bringing communities together through shared artistic
practices.
 It supports economic development, as art-related industries generate employment and
income.
Summary
Art is a universal form of human expression that has evolved over centuries, reflecting the
creativity, culture, and values of societies. From prehistoric cave paintings to modern digital art,
art continues to serve as a medium of communication, education, emotional expression, and
cultural preservation. Understanding the history and significance of art equips students to
appreciate its value and participate in creative activities responsibly.

How Arts Started in Nigerian Schools, Branches/Types of Arts, Uses of Arts in Society
How Arts Started in Nigerian Schools
5
Art as a formal subject in Nigerian schools began during the colonial period, when Western
education was first introduced. Before then, Nigerians had been practicing traditional forms of
art such as pottery, weaving, body painting, wood carving, metal works, and sculpture. These
traditional arts were taught informally from generation to generation through apprenticeship and
cultural practices.
When missionaries and colonial administrators introduced formal Western education in the 19th
and 20th centuries, art was gradually included as part of the school curriculum. The earliest focus
was on drawing, painting, and handicrafts.
In the 1950s and 1960s, art education became more recognized in secondary schools, teacher
training colleges, and universities. Notable Nigerian artists and educators such as Aina Onabolu
(considered the father of modern Nigerian art) and Ben Enwonwu promoted art in schools and
showed its importance in education and nation building.
Today, art has become an essential subject under the Cultural and Creative Arts (CCA)
curriculum, as approved by the Nigerian Educational Research and Development Council
(NERDC). Students are exposed not only to fine arts but also to performing arts (music, dance,
drama) and applied arts (design, crafts, and technology).
Branches/Types of Arts
Arts is a broad field that is usually divided into three main branches:
6
1. Visual Arts
These are arts that can be seen. They include drawing, painting, sculpture, ceramics,
textiles, photography, printmaking, and crafts. Visual arts use materials such as clay,
paint, charcoal, wood, fabric, and paper.
2. Performing Arts
These are arts performed before an audience. They include music, dance, drama, film,
and theatre. Performing arts use the human body, voice, musical instruments, and stage as
media of expression.
3. Literary Arts
These are arts expressed through written or spoken words. They include poetry, prose,
and drama scripts. Literary arts rely on imagination, storytelling, and creative writing to
pass across meaning and emotion.
In addition to these three main branches, art can also be grouped into applied arts (such as
design, fashion, and architecture), which combine creativity with functionality.
Uses of Arts in Society
Art plays a vital role in the development and expression of human society. Some of its uses
include:
7
 Expression of Ideas and Emotions: Art allows people to express their thoughts, feelings,
beliefs, and cultural values.
 Decoration and Beauty: Works of art beautify our environment—such as paintings in
homes, sculptures in public places, and designs on clothing.
 Cultural Preservation: Art preserves the traditions, history, and identity of a people.
Traditional festivals, dances, masks, and crafts are ways of keeping culture alive.
 Education: Art helps in teaching, learning, and communication. Illustrations, diagrams,
music, and drama make education easier and more interesting.
 Entertainment: Music, drama, dance, and visual arts provide joy, relaxation, and
amusement for people.
 Economic Value: Art provides job opportunities for artists, designers, actors, musicians,
and dancers, thereby contributing to the economy.
 Religious and Spiritual Purposes: Art is used in religious worship, rituals, and
symbolism. Examples are church music, mosque calligraphy, and traditional masks.
 Social Mobilization and Awareness: Art can be used in campaigns, posters, and drama
to pass messages about health, politics, or social issues.

WEEK 3: CRAFTS
Crafts are one of the oldest forms of human creativity. They are works made by hand using
simple tools and locally available materials. Unlike industrial products that are produced by
machines in large quantities, crafts are usually made in smaller quantities and carry the skill,
imagination, and cultural identity of the maker. Crafts are an important aspect of Cultural and
Creative Arts (CCA) because they help us to appreciate creativity, promote culture, and provide
useful materials for everyday life.
Meaning of Crafts
Crafts can be defined as objects that are made skillfully by hand, often for decorative or
functional purposes, using locally available materials. They involve the application of knowledge,
patience, and artistic ability.
For example, basket weaving, pottery, blacksmithing, bead-making, carving, and textile designs
are all crafts.
Types of Crafts
Crafts can be grouped into different categories based on the type of materials used or their
functions.
10
1. Textile Crafts – These are crafts made from fabrics, threads, and dyes. Examples include
tie-dye (adire), weaving, embroidery, knitting, and batik.
2. Ceramic Crafts – These involve shaping and firing clay to produce objects. Examples
include pots, plates, and sculptures.
3. Wood Crafts – Produced by carving or shaping wood into items such as drums, masks,
stools, and furniture.
4. Metal Crafts – Made from metals such as iron, bronze, and gold. Examples include
cutlasses, hoes, jewelry, gongs, and swords.
5. Bead and Jewelry Crafts – Involving the use of beads, cowries, or precious stones to
make necklaces, bracelets, and earrings.
6. Leather Crafts – Using animal skin to produce shoes, bags, belts, drums, and mats.
7. Basketry and Weaving – Using palm fronds, reeds, and grasses to make baskets, fans,
mats, and hats.
Materials Used in Crafts
The materials used in craftwork usually come from the natural environment. Examples include:
 Clay – used for pottery.
 Wood – used for carving and furniture.
 Metal – iron, bronze, or gold for tools and ornaments.
 Leather – hides and skins for shoes and bags.
 Palm fronds and grasses – for weaving baskets and mats.
 Beads, cowries, shells, and stones – for decoration and jewelry.
 Textiles and dyes – for weaving, embroidery, and tie-dye.
11
Tools and Equipment for Crafts
Craft-making requires tools and equipment. Examples are:
 Knives, chisels, and saws for wood carving.
 Looms and needles for weaving and embroidery.
 Hammers, anvils, and tongs for blacksmithing.
 Pottery wheels and kilns for ceramics.
 Awls and cutters for leatherwork.
 Scissors and brushes for textile crafts.
The tools vary depending on the type of craft being produced.
Locations of Local Crafts in Nigeria
Nigeria is blessed with a rich culture of crafts spread across different regions. Some notable
locations include:
 Ile-Ife and Benin – famous for bronze casting and metal works.
 Abeokuta (Ogun State) – known for adire (tie-dye) textile designs.
 Oshogbo (Osun State) – for batik, tie-dye, and bead-making.
 Kano and Zaria (Northern Nigeria) – leather works, weaving, and dyeing.
 Lokoja and Bida (Niger State) – known for glass and bead-making.
 Awka (Anambra State) – blacksmithing and iron works.
 Calabar (Cross River State) – wood carving and mask-making.
Importance of Crafts
 Crafts preserve cultural heritage and traditions.
12
 They provide functional objects such as clothes, shoes, and utensils.
 They create job opportunities and promote self-reliance.
 They serve as a source of income and export for the country.
 They encourage creativity and practical skills among students.
 Crafts promote unity and identity by showcasing Nigeria’s rich cultural diversity.

WEEK 4: CRAFTS (CONT’D) – USES OF CRAFTS IN NIGERIA
Crafts are objects that are produced through skillful use of hands and simple tools. In Nigeria,
crafts are very important because they serve many purposes in different areas of life. From
domestic use to commercial, social, cultural, and religious purposes, crafts contribute to the
survival, identity, and development of Nigerian society.
1. Domestic Uses of Crafts
Crafts are useful in homes and daily living. Traditional woven mats, for example, are used for
sleeping, sitting, and relaxation. Pots are used for cooking, storing water, or preserving grains.
Local baskets are used for carrying goods, while clay stoves and iron pots are used for preparing
meals. These crafts make life easier and provide practical solutions to everyday needs.
2. Economic Uses of Crafts
Crafts serve as sources of income and employment for individuals and communities. For instance,
blacksmiths produce hoes, cutlasses, knives, and farming tools, which are sold in local markets.
Pottery makers, carvers, and weavers sell their works for money. Crafts also attract tourists who
14
buy them as souvenirs, thereby contributing to the nation’s economy and promoting cultural
exchange.
3. Social and Cultural Uses of Crafts
Crafts play important roles in social and cultural life. Masks, costumes, and carved figures are
used during festivals, dances, and cultural ceremonies. Beads, ornaments, and traditional attire
such as Aso-oke or Ankara fabrics are used to beautify individuals during naming ceremonies,
marriages, and chieftaincy titles. These crafts help preserve and project Nigerian culture and
identity.
4. Religious Uses of Crafts
In many traditional religious settings, crafts are used as symbols of worship. For instance, carved
images, shrines, and ritual objects are created for spiritual purposes. In Christianity and Islam,
crafts are used in forms such as church decorations, calligraphy, embroidery, and construction of
places of worship. Crafts help to express devotion and religious values in physical forms.
5. Educational Uses of Crafts
Crafts are also used in schools to teach creativity, skill development, and cultural awareness.
Students learn basket weaving, clay modeling, painting, and carving to develop their creative
ability and to prepare them for possible career opportunities. Crafts, therefore, play a vital role in
education and youth development.
6. Aesthetic Uses of Crafts
Crafts beautify our environment and make it attractive. Carvings, artworks, textiles, and pottery
are used to decorate homes, offices, and public places. Paintings and sculptures found in galleries
and museums not only make places more appealing but also preserve history and heritage for
future generations.

WEEK 6: TIE AND DYE
Tie and Dye is a traditional method of designing fabrics by tying parts of the cloth and then
immersing it in dye to create beautiful patterns. It is one of the oldest textile decoration
techniques, commonly practiced in many African cultures, including Nigeria, where it is known
as “Adire” among the Yoruba people. Tie and Dye involves two main processes: tying and
dyeing.
MEANING OF TIE AND DYE
Tie and Dye refers to a resist-dyeing process where certain sections of a fabric are tied tightly
with strings or rubber bands to prevent dye from penetrating those parts. When the fabric is
immersed in dye, only the untied parts absorb the color, leaving behind distinctive patterns once
the ties are removed.
This technique is widely used to produce colorful fabrics for clothing, household items, and
decorations. It allows creativity in combining colors and patterns to create unique designs.
MATERIALS NEEDED FOR TIE AND DYE
To carry out Tie and Dye successfully, the following materials are required:
17
 Plain cotton fabric (white or light-colored)
 Dyes (chemical or natural dyes)
 Buckets or bowls for dye preparation
 String, rubber bands, or raffia for tying
 Sticks or spoons for stirring the dye
 Gloves and apron for protection
 Water and soap for washing the fabric
METHODS OF TYING IN TIE AND DYE
The method of tying determines the pattern that will appear on the fabric after dyeing. Some
common tying methods include:
1. Spiral Method:
o Spread the fabric flat on a surface.
o Pinch the center and twist it around into a spiral shape.
o Bind the spiral tightly with strings or rubber bands at intervals to hold the shape.
o When dyed, this method produces circular, spiral patterns.
18
2. Marble or Pebble Method:
o Place small stones, pebbles, or seeds in different parts of the fabric.
o Wrap each stone tightly with string or rubber band to create small round shapes.
o This method produces circular or dot-like patterns.
3. Folding Method (Accordion Pleat):
o Fold the fabric repeatedly like a fan, either vertically, horizontally, or diagonally.
o Bind at intervals with string or rubber bands.
o When dyed, the result is a series of parallel or crisscrossed lines.
4. Stitching Method:
o Use needle and thread to stitch patterns into the fabric.
o Pull the thread tightly to gather the cloth.
o After dyeing and removing stitches, intricate patterns are revealed.
5. Knotting Method:
o Simply tie parts of the fabric into knots without using external objects.
o This creates random circular or oval patterns on the fabric.
PROCESS OF TIE AND DYE
1. Preparation: Wash and dry the fabric to remove dirt or finishing chemicals.
2. Tying: Use one or a combination of tying methods to design the cloth.
3. Dyeing: Dissolve dye in water according to instructions, then immerse the tied fabric in
the dye. Leave it for a while to absorb the color.
4. Rinsing: Remove the fabric from the dye, rinse in clean water until water runs clear.
19
5. Drying and Finishing: Untie the strings and spread the cloth to dry. Iron to smoothen
and set the design.
IMPORTANCE OF TIE AND DYE
 Promotes cultural heritage and traditional artistry.
 Provides self-employment opportunities through textile production.
 Enhances creativity and design skills.
 Adds beauty and uniqueness to fabrics used in fashion and home décor.

WEEK 8: PAPER CRAFT
Paper craft is the art of creating decorative or functional items using paper as the primary
material. It is one of the earliest forms of creative expression and is widely used in schools,
homes, and communities for both educational and recreational purposes. Paper craft encourages
creativity, imagination, patience, and fine motor skills. It also provides an opportunity for
students to produce aesthetically pleasing designs from simple materials.
Meaning of Paper Craft
Paper craft refers to the art or skill of making objects, decorations, and artworks using paper. The
process involves cutting, folding, shaping, and sometimes gluing or decorating paper to produce
items such as cards, boxes, ornaments, flowers, and models. Paper craft can be both traditional
and contemporary, and it often combines techniques from origami, quilling, and collage.
Methods of Folding Paper
Folding is a fundamental technique in paper craft that transforms a flat sheet of paper into threedimensional
forms. Various folding methods are used to achieve different designs and effects:
1. Simple Fold: This involves folding the paper in half or into sections to create cards,
envelopes, or basic shapes. It is often the starting point for more complex designs.
21
2. Accordion Fold: The paper is folded back and forth in a zigzag manner, creating a fanlike
effect. This method is used in making decorative fans, pop-up books, and layered
designs.
3. Diagonal Fold: The paper is folded along a diagonal line to produce triangles or diamond
shapes. It is commonly used in origami to create animals, geometric forms, and other
designs.
4. Valley and Mountain Folds: These are basic origami folds where the paper is folded
inward (valley) or outward (mountain). They are essential for creating intricate models
such as flowers, birds, or boxes.
5. Multiple or Layered Folds: Several folds are combined to produce complex shapes and
textures. This method is used in making paper flowers, stars, and decorative objects.
Folding paper correctly requires precision, patience, and careful alignment of edges and corners.
The quality of the final paper craft object often depends on the accuracy of these folds.
Importance of Paper Craft
Paper craft has both educational and practical value:
 It develops creativity, problem-solving, and spatial awareness.
22
 It enhances fine motor skills through precise cutting, folding, and shaping.
 It can be used for decoration, gifts, teaching aids, and cultural displays.
 It promotes appreciation for art and design within students’ environment.

WEEK 9: PAPER CRAFT (CONT’D)
Paper Craft Production; Uses of Paper Crafts
Paper craft refers to the art of creating decorative or useful objects by folding, cutting, gluing, or
assembling paper. It is one of the most common and affordable forms of creative art because
paper is widely available and easy to use. In the previous lesson, we examined the meaning of
paper craft and some basic examples. In this lesson, we shall go deeper into the process of
producing paper crafts and their practical uses in daily life.
Paper Craft Production
Producing paper crafts requires creativity, neatness, patience, and proper handling of tools. The
process usually involves the following steps:
1. Selection of Materials
o Choose the type of paper suitable for the craft. Examples include cardboard,
coloured paper, tissue paper, craft paper, and newsprint.
o Other materials include glue, gum, scissors, staplers, rulers, markers, paints, and
decorative items like beads, ribbons, or glitter.
2. Designing the Craft
o The artist should first plan what to create: e.g., greeting cards, masks, flowers,
boxes, wall decorations, lanterns, or fans.
o A sketch or outline of the design is often made on paper before cutting or folding.
24
3. Cutting and Folding
o Use scissors or craft knives to cut the paper carefully along the outline.
o Folding must be done neatly to give the craft the right shape and balance.
4. Assembling and Joining
o Pieces of paper can be glued, gummed, or stapled together to form the desired
object.
o For example, petals of a flower can be glued around a base to form a complete
flower.
5. Decoration and Finishing
o The final stage involves beautifying the craft with colours, paints, glitter, ribbons,
or other materials.
o Neat finishing is important because it makes the work attractive and valuable.
Examples of paper crafts students can try include:
 Greeting cards
 Paper flowers
 Origami figures (birds, boats, hats)
 Paper fans
 Decorative wall hangings
 Paper masks for drama and festivals
Uses of Paper Crafts
Paper crafts are not just for leisure or decoration; they have real applications in society. Some
uses include:
25
1. Decoration – Paper crafts such as flowers, garlands, and wall hangings are used to
beautify homes, schools, halls, and places of worship.
2. Education – Teachers use paper charts, flash cards, diagrams, and models made from
paper to teach different subjects.
3. Entertainment – Children and adults make paper toys, masks, and origami figures for
play and fun.
4. Cultural and Religious Festivals – Paper lanterns, masks, and decorations are
commonly used during traditional festivals, weddings, and ceremonies.
5. Commercial Purposes – Greeting cards, gift boxes, calendars, and paper bags are
produced and sold as sources of income.
6. Awareness and Information – Posters, banners, and paper crafts are used to pass
information in campaigns, advertisements, and social events.
7. Personal Expression – Artists use paper craft to showcase their creativity, ideas, and
artistic talent.

WEEK 1: COLLAGE
Meaning of Collage
A collage is an art technique that involves assembling different materials such as paper, fabric,
photographs, or other objects onto a surface to create a new, unified composition. The word
“collage” comes from the French word coller, which means “to glue.” Collage allows artists to
combine various textures, colors, shapes, and images to produce a visually interesting and
expressive artwork.
Collage can be used for decoration, storytelling, creative expression, or educational purposes. It
encourages creativity, imagination, and resourcefulness, as students learn to reuse materials and
combine them in new ways. Collage is both a practical and experimental art form, making it an
ideal activity for beginners and advanced artists alike.
Materials and Tools for Collage
To create a collage, a variety of materials and tools can be used. These include:
Materials:
28
 Paper – colored paper, newspapers, magazines, or cardboards.
 Fabric – pieces of cloth, lace, or textile scraps.
 Photographs – cut-outs from magazines, personal photographs, or printed images.
 Natural Materials – leaves, flowers, sand, or small pebbles.
 Other Decorative Items – beads, buttons, ribbons, and sequins.
Tools:
 Scissors – for cutting paper, fabric, or photographs.
 Glue or Adhesives – for sticking materials onto the base surface.
 Brushes – sometimes used to apply glue evenly.
 Tweezers – to handle small or delicate materials.
 Base Surface – such as cardboard, thick paper, or canvas to hold the collage.
Steps in Making a Collage
1. Select the theme or subject for the collage.
2. Collect the materials and tools needed.
3. Arrange the materials on the base surface without gluing first, experimenting with
placement.
4. Once satisfied with the arrangement, stick the items using glue or other adhesives.
5. Allow the collage to dry completely before handling or displaying it.
6. Optionally, add details or embellishments using paint, markers, or other decorative
elements.
Importance of Collage in Cultural and Creative Arts
 Collage promotes creativity and imagination by encouraging students to combine
materials in unique ways.
29
 It helps develop fine motor skills through cutting, arranging, and gluing materials.
 Collage fosters problem-solving and planning skills, as students decide how to arrange
elements for balance and effect.
 It allows for recycling and resourcefulness, using discarded materials to create art.
 Collage is a versatile medium for storytelling, decoration, and cultural expression,
reflecting personal or communal ideas.
Summary
Collage is a creative art technique that combines various materials to form a cohesive artwork.
By understanding the materials, tools, and methods used in collage, students can explore their
creativity, develop practical skills, and express ideas visually. Collage not only enhances artistic
skills but also promotes imagination, planning, and cultural awareness.

WEEK 2: COLLAGE (CONT’D): PRODUCTION OF COLLAGE, USES OF COLLAGE
Production of Collage
A collage is a work of art created by assembling different materials such as paper, cloth,
photographs, wood shavings, leaves, sand, beads, or other found objects and pasting them on a
flat surface, usually cardboard, paper, plywood, or canvas. The word collage comes from the
French word coller which means "to glue."
The production of collage requires creativity, careful selection of materials, and neat
arrangement. To produce a collage, the following steps are involved:
1. Gather Materials: Collect suitable materials such as newspapers, magazines, pieces of
cloth, cardboard, beads, photographs, dried leaves, buttons, and other small objects. Also
prepare tools such as scissors, glue, gum, and brushes.
2. Select a Theme or Idea: Decide on what you want your collage to represent. For

WEEK 6: MODELLING WITH PAPIER-MÂCHÉ (CONTINUED)
Papier-mâché is a craft technique that involves creating three-dimensional objects by molding
layers of paper soaked in a glue-like substance over a surface or structure. It is a versatile art
form that allows artists to produce decorative and functional items such as masks, bowls, toys,
and sculptures. This lesson focuses on the preparation and making of papier-mâché,

PREPARATION AND MAKING OF PAPIER-MÂCHÉ
To produce a papier-mâché object, certain materials must be prepared, and specific steps
followed to ensure a good outcome.
Materials Needed:
 Old newspapers, cardboard, or tissue paper
 Adhesive (flour paste, starch paste, or white glue mixed with water)
 Water
41
 Mixing bowl or container
 Brush or spatula for application
 Base mold or structure (balloon, cardboard shape, clay form, etc.)
 Paints and brushes for finishing
Steps in Preparation and Making:
1. Preparing the Adhesive (Paste):
o Mix flour with water to form a smooth paste, ensuring there are no lumps.
Alternatively, dissolve white glue in a small amount of water.
o Stir thoroughly until it becomes consistent.
2. Tearing the Paper:
o Tear old newspapers or tissue paper into small strips. Do not cut with scissors, as
rough edges stick better.
3. Creating the Base Structure (Armature):
o Decide on the object to make and create a supporting structure if needed.
o For example, blow up a balloon for a round object or build a shape from
cardboard.
4. Applying the Papier-Mâché:
o Dip the torn paper strips into the adhesive.
o Place the strips layer by layer over the base structure, smoothing them down to
remove air bubbles.
o Repeat this process until the desired thickness is achieved.
5. Drying:
o Leave the object to dry completely in a well-ventilated area. This may take 24–48
hours depending on the thickness.
42
6. Finishing:
o Once dry, pop the balloon (if used as a mold) and remove it.
o Smoothen rough surfaces by trimming or sanding gently.
o Paint and decorate the object as desired.
MODELLING TECHNIQUES IN PAPIER-MÂCHÉ
1. Layering Technique: Applying several layers of paper strips over a mold or structure to
build up strength and shape.
2. Pulp Method: Making a pulp from soaked paper blended with water and adhesive, then
molding it by hand into shapes like clay.
3. Combination Method: Using both layering and pulp techniques in one project, such as
layering for the base and pulp for details.
4. Molding Technique: Pressing paper pulp into molds to form specific patterns or designs.

 Keep sharp objects like scissors or knives out of reach when not in use and handle with

 Ensure proper disposal of waste materials to maintain cleanliness and prevent accidents.
 Avoid leaving wet projects in damp places to prevent mold growth.

WEEK 1: MUSIC THEORY
Introduction to Music Theory
Music theory is the study of the fundamental elements of music and the principles that govern
the creation, performance, and understanding of music. It helps learners understand how music is
structured, enabling them to read, write, and interpret musical compositions effectively. Music
theory forms the foundation for practical music-making, whether on the keyboard, percussion, or
other instruments.
Fundamentals of Music
The fundamentals of music include several essential concepts:
1. Sound and Tone
o Sound is produced by vibrations and is perceived by the ear.
o Tone refers to the quality of a musical sound, which can be high or low (pitch),
loud or soft (dynamics), and long or short in duration.
52
2. Pitch
o Pitch is the perceived highness or lowness of a sound.
o Notes are assigned different pitches, allowing musicians to create melodies and
harmonies.
3. Rhythm
o Rhythm refers to the pattern of beats or timing in music.
o It organizes music in terms of duration, tempo, and meter, making it possible to
perform compositions consistently.
4. Melody
o Melody is a sequence of notes that are perceived as a single, coherent musical
idea.
o It is often the most memorable part of a piece of music.
5. Harmony
o Harmony occurs when two or more notes are played simultaneously to produce a
pleasing sound.
o It adds depth and richness to music, supporting the melody.
6. Dynamics and Expression
o Dynamics indicate the volume of music, such as soft (piano) or loud (forte).
o Expression involves articulation, phrasing, and emotion conveyed through
performance.
Notes on the Keyboard
The keyboard, such as a piano or electronic keyboard, is a common instrument used to apply
music theory in practice. Understanding the notes on the keyboard is crucial for playing melodies,
harmonies, and chords.
53
1. Keys
o A keyboard consists of white keys and black keys arranged in a repeating pattern.
o White keys represent the natural notes: C, D, E, F, G, A, B.
o Black keys represent the sharps (#) and flats (b) between certain white keys.
2. Octaves
o An octave is the interval between one note and another with double or half its
frequency.
o The keyboard is divided into multiple octaves, allowing for a wide range of
pitches.
3. Identifying Notes
o The white key immediately to the left of a group of two black keys is C.
o Moving right from C in sequence, the notes are D, E, F, G, A, B, then C again in
the next octave.
o Black keys are named according to the white keys they are closest to, either as
sharps or flats. For example, the black key between C and D is C# (sharp) or Db
(flat).
4. Using Notes to Play Music
o Combining knowledge of notes, rhythm, and melody, students can play simple
tunes and practice scales.
o This lays the foundation for reading musical notation, understanding chords, and
composing music.
Importance of Music Theory
 Music theory provides the knowledge and tools to understand, read, and create music
accurately.

Rudiments of Harmony
Harmony in music refers to the simultaneous combination of two or more musical notes or tones
that are sung or played together to produce a pleasant sound. While melody is the tune we sing or
hear, harmony supports the melody and makes the music richer and fuller.
The rudiments of harmony are the basic principles or foundations upon which harmonious
music is built. They include:
1. Scales and Keys: Understanding scales (major and minor) is important because harmony
is created by combining notes that belong to the same scale.
2. Chords: A chord is the simultaneous sounding of three or more notes. For example, the
C major chord consists of C–E–G. Chords are the building blocks of harmony.
56
3. Intervals: An interval is the distance between two notes. Consonant intervals (like a third,
fifth, or octave) produce pleasant sounds, while dissonant intervals create tension that
requires resolution.
4. Chord Progressions: Harmony progresses when one chord moves to another in an
orderly manner. For example, a common progression is I–IV–V–I.
5. Consonance and Dissonance: Harmony is created by balancing consonance (pleasant
sounds) with dissonance (clashing sounds) to create musical interest.
6. Voice Leading: This refers to the smooth movement of individual musical lines or voices
from one note to the next in a chord progression.
In simple terms, harmony is what makes a song more colorful and emotional by blending
different notes and voices together.
Uses of Ear Training
Ear training is the practice of developing the ability to recognize, identify, and reproduce
different musical sounds, notes, chords, rhythms, and melodies by listening carefully. It sharpens
a musician’s sense of hearing and improves overall musical ability.
The uses of ear training include:
 Recognition of Musical Notes: Ear training helps students identify notes by listening,
even without looking at a musical instrument or score.
 Improved Singing Accuracy: It enables singers to sing on key, match pitches, and
maintain correct intonation.
 Better Instrument Playing: Instrumentalists can play by ear, improvise, and accompany
others more effectively.
 Understanding Harmony: Ear training helps musicians hear and identify chords, chord
progressions, and harmonic structures.
57
 Improvisation Skills: Musicians can create music spontaneously when they have trained
ears.
 Aural Dictation: In music education, students with ear training can listen to a melody or
rhythm and write it down correctly in musical notation.
 Ensemble Performance: In choirs, bands, or orchestras, ear training helps members
blend their parts with others to produce balanced harmony.
 Creativity in Composition: Composers use ear training to imagine sounds, experiment
with harmonies, and create new music.

Musical Alphabets
Music, like language, has its own alphabet. The musical alphabet consists of seven letters
arranged in sequence: A, B, C, D, E, F, G. After G, the sequence repeats itself again from A.
These alphabets are the foundation of musical sounds and are used to name notes on instruments
and in written music. For example, the white keys on a piano are named after these alphabets.
The order of the alphabets helps musicians identify and perform melodies and harmonies
correctly.
The Staff (Stave)
The staff, also called the stave, is the set of five parallel horizontal lines and four spaces upon
which musical notes and symbols are written. Each line and space represents a definite musical
pitch or sound. Notes are placed on the staff to show how high or low they should be sung or
played. The staff is the basic framework of music writing.
There are two types of staff:
62
1. The Single Staff – which contains five lines and four spaces.
2. The Grand Staff – which combines two staves, usually connected by a brace, often used
in piano music (treble and bass staves).
The Clefs
A clef is a musical sign placed at the beginning of the staff to indicate the pitch of the notes
written on it. It acts as a guide for reading music. The two most common clefs are:
1. The Treble Clef (G-Clef): It is used for higher-pitched sounds and instruments such as
the violin, trumpet, flute, and the right hand of the piano. The symbol circles around the
second line of the staff, which is designated as G.
2. The Bass Clef (F-Clef): It is used for lower-pitched sounds and instruments such as the
bass guitar, tuba, trombone, and the left hand of the piano. The two dots of the clef
surround the fourth line of the staff, which is designated as F.
The use of clefs ensures that notes are properly read and interpreted, preventing confusion in
pitch.
Notes and Their Relative Values
Notes are symbols written on the staff to represent musical sounds and their duration (length of
time). Each note has a specific value that determines how long it is to be held.
The common notes and their relative values include:
 Semibreve (Whole Note): An open oval note without a stem. It lasts for 4 beats.
 Minim (Half Note): An open oval note with a stem. It lasts for 2 beats.
 Crotchet (Quarter Note): A filled-in oval note with a stem. It lasts for 1 beat.
 Quaver (Eighth Note): A filled-in oval note with a stem and one tail (or flag). It lasts for
half a beat.
63
 Semiquaver (Sixteenth Note): A filled-in oval note with a stem and two tails. It lasts for
a quarter of a beat.
These notes can be combined to make rhythms and melodies. Their arrangement is what gives
music its timing and flow.

Music is an essential part of human life and culture. It is expressed in various forms, including

MAJOR SCALES IN MUSIC
A scale in music is a series of notes arranged in ascending or descending order of pitch. The
major scale is one of the most important and widely used scales in music. It is made up of eight
notes that follow a specific pattern of tones (whole steps) and semitones (half steps):
Pattern of a Major Scale:
 Tone – Tone – Semitone – Tone – Tone – Tone – Semitone
65
For example, the C major scale consists of the notes: C – D – E – F – G – A – B – C.
Each note is called a degree of the scale, and they are often sung using solfa syllables: Do – Re
– Mi – Fa – So – La – Ti – Do.
Major scales are the foundation of most Western and African music, as they provide the basis for
melodies and harmonies.
PLAYING THE RECORDER
The recorder is a simple woodwind musical instrument played by blowing air into a mouthpiece
while covering and uncovering finger holes to produce different notes. It is commonly used in
schools to teach music because it is easy to learn and inexpensive.
Parts of the Recorder:
 Mouthpiece: Where the player blows air into the instrument.
 Body: Contains finger holes that are covered to produce different notes.
 Foot Joint: The lower end of the recorder.
Steps in Playing the Recorder:
1. Holding the Recorder Properly:
o Sit or stand upright.
o Use the left hand for the upper holes and the right hand for the lower holes.
2. Blowing Technique:
o Blow gently into the mouthpiece. Blowing too hard produces harsh sounds.
3. Finger Placement:
o Cover the holes completely with fingertips to avoid air leakage.
4. Playing Notes:
66
o Start with simple notes like B, A, and G, then progress to full scales such as the C
major scale.
5. Practice Regularly:
o Consistent practice improves tone quality, speed, and accuracy.

2. Education: Music is used to teach values, history, language, and culture through songs

3. Communication: Traditional societies use music to send messages, announce events, or

4. Religious Worship: Music forms an integral part of religious practices, praise, and

5. Cultural Identity: It reflects the traditions and heritage of a people, preserving customs

6. Healing and Therapy: Music is used in hospitals and therapy sessions to calm patients

7. Nationalism and Unity: National anthems and patriotic songs foster pride and unity

WEEK 8: DRAMA & THEATRE
Drama and theatre are significant components of cultural and creative arts, reflecting human
experiences, emotions, and social values through performance. They serve as a medium for
storytelling, education, entertainment, and cultural preservation. Understanding drama and
theatre equips students with knowledge of their historical development, key participants, and
functional roles in society.
Definition of Drama and Theatre
Drama refers to a literary form written for performance, where characters enact a story through
dialogue and action. It explores themes such as conflict, morality, human behavior, and societal
issues. Theatre, on the other hand, is the art of performing drama before an audience. It combines
69
elements of performance, stagecraft, costume, music, and speech to bring the story to life.
Together, drama and theatre provide both creative expression and communal engagement.
Origin of Drama
Drama has its origins in ancient civilizations. Early forms of drama were closely linked to
religious ceremonies and rituals, where performances were used to honor gods, tell myths, or
celebrate cultural traditions. In ancient Greece, drama developed as a structured form of
storytelling performed in amphitheatres, leading to the creation of tragedy, comedy, and satirical
plays. Drama evolved over centuries to incorporate diverse cultural narratives, styles, and
theatrical techniques around the world.
People in Drama and Theatre
Several key participants contribute to the creation and performance of drama and theatre:
 Playwright: The person who writes the script or story for the performance.
 Director: The individual responsible for guiding actors, interpreting the script, and
coordinating all aspects of the production.
 Actors/Performers: Individuals who portray characters in the drama, bringing life to the
story through speech, movement, and expression.
 Stage Manager: Oversees the organization of the performance, manages props, and
ensures smooth execution.
 Costume and Set Designers: Create appropriate costumes, scenery, and props to support
the theme and setting of the drama.
 Audience: Although not performers, the audience plays a critical role, as their reactions
and engagement shape the impact of the performance.
Functions of Drama and Theatre
Drama and theatre serve multiple social, educational, and cultural functions:
 Entertainment: Provides amusement and enjoyment through storytelling, humor, and
spectacle.
 Education: Teaches moral lessons, social values, historical events, and cultural practices.
70
 Cultural Preservation: Reflects and sustains traditions, languages, and customs through
performance.
 Social Commentary: Critiques societal issues, politics, and human behavior, prompting
reflection and dialogue.
 Emotional Expression: Offers actors and audiences a platform to explore and express
emotions, empathy, and human experiences.
In summary, drama and theatre are both artistic and educational tools that combine literature,
performance, and cultural knowledge to enrich society. They encourage creativity, collaboration,
critical thinking, and appreciation of cultural heritage.

WEEK 9: ELEMENTS AND PLAYWRITING
Elements and Forms of Drama; Playwriting and Dramatists
Drama is one of the major branches of Creative Arts that deals with acting, dialogue, and
performance before an audience. It tells a story through the actions and speeches of characters.
Drama has been part of human life for centuries, beginning from simple rituals, folk
performances, and storytelling, to modern stage plays and films. To understand drama, we must
first examine its elements and forms, then consider the process of playwriting and the role of
dramatists.
Elements of Drama
The elements of drama are the basic components that make a play or performance complete and
meaningful. They include:
1. Plot – This is the storyline or sequence of events in the drama. It shows how the story
begins, develops, and ends. A good plot usually has a beginning, a middle, and an end.
2. Character – Characters are the people or figures in the drama who carry out the actions
and deliver the dialogues. They may be main characters (protagonists), supporting
characters, or antagonists (those opposing the main character).
3. Dialogue – Drama is expressed through speech. Dialogue refers to the spoken words
between characters, which reveal their thoughts, emotions, and intentions.
4. Theme – The theme is the central idea or message that the play conveys. For example,
themes can be love, honesty, corruption, justice, greed, or sacrifice.
5. Setting – This refers to the time and place where the play is set. It could be a palace, a
village square, a school, or any environment. Setting also includes the period (past,
present, or future).
6. Conflict – Conflict is the struggle or problem that arises between characters in the play. It
drives the plot and keeps the audience interested. Conflict may be internal (within a
character) or external (between characters, groups, or forces).
72
7. Music, Sound, and Dance – These enrich drama, making it lively and expressive.
Traditional plays often use drums, songs, or dances to convey emotion and cultural
identity.
8. Spectacle – This refers to the visual elements of a performance, such as costumes, props,
lighting, and stage design. It is what the audience sees and experiences during the
performance.
Forms of Drama
Drama comes in different forms, depending on its content, purpose, and mood. Some major
forms are:
1. Tragedy – A serious play that usually ends sadly, often with the downfall or death of the
main character. Example: Shakespeare’s Macbeth.
2. Comedy – A humorous play that entertains the audience and often ends happily. It
focuses on human weaknesses in a light-hearted way.
3. Tragicomedy – A blend of tragedy and comedy, where serious issues are treated with
humour, and the play may end on a mixed note.
4. Farce – A play full of exaggerated actions, foolishness, and absurd situations, mainly to
provoke laughter.
5. Historical Drama – Plays that are based on true historical events and figures, teaching
lessons from the past.
6. Satire – A play that ridicules human vices, corruption, or societal problems, aiming to
correct and reform society.
7. Ritual or Traditional Drama – Found in African societies, these are performances
linked with festivals, rituals, and community celebrations.
73
Playwriting
Playwriting is the process of writing scripts for drama. A script is the written text of a play that
contains the dialogue, stage directions, and actions of characters.
The process of playwriting involves:
 Choosing a theme or central idea that the play will be built around.
 Creating characters with distinct roles, personalities, and relationships.
 Developing a plot with clear introduction, rising action, climax, falling action, and
resolution.
 Writing dialogue that sounds natural, reveals emotions, and fits the character.
 Adding stage directions to guide actors and directors on how the play should be
performed.
74
 Revising and editing the script to make it smooth, engaging, and effective for
performance.
A good play must be clear, relevant, and interesting to its audience.
Dramatists
A dramatist is a person who writes plays. Dramatists are also called playwrights. They are the
creative minds behind stage plays, scripts for films, or traditional performances.
Examples of famous dramatists include:
 William Shakespeare (England) – wrote plays such as Romeo and Juliet, Othello, and
Hamlet.
 Wole Soyinka (Nigeria) – a Nobel Prize-winning playwright who wrote The Lion and
the Jewel and Death and the King’s Horseman.
 Ola Rotimi (Nigeria) – wrote The Gods Are Not to Blame, a powerful play based on
Yoruba culture.
 Sophocles (Greece) – wrote Oedipus Rex, a classic tragic play.
Dramatists play an important role in shaping culture, preserving traditions, and educating society
through drama.

WEEK 10: THE DIRECTOR
Meaning, Duties, and Functions of a Director
The Director is one of the most important figures in the production of drama, film, or any form
of stage performance. The director is the person responsible for overseeing, guiding, and
controlling all aspects of a production to ensure that the writer’s ideas are properly interpreted
and communicated to the audience. In drama and theatre, the director works as the leader who
unites the actors, designers, stage managers, and technical crew to achieve a successful
performance. The director interprets the script, decides on the mood and style of the play,
supervises rehearsals, and ensures that the production is well-coordinated. Without a director, a
performance may lack organization, artistic direction, and clarity.
Duties of a Director
The duties of a director involve the responsibilities that he or she performs to ensure a successful
production. These include:
77
 Selecting or approving the play or script to be produced.
 Interpreting the script to bring out its intended meaning.
 Casting the right actors and assigning them suitable roles.
 Guiding and instructing actors on how to act their roles.
 Organizing rehearsals and ensuring punctuality and discipline.
 Supervising the work of the costume, makeup, props, sound, and lighting crew.
 Coordinating all the different aspects of the production into one harmonious performance.
 Motivating the cast and crew to give their best during rehearsals and performance.
Functions of a Director
The functions of a director are the general roles that define the importance of the director in a
performance. They include:
 Artistic Function: Ensuring the performance is entertaining, creative, and of high artistic
quality.
 Educational Function: Helping the audience to learn lessons, morals, and values from
the performance.
 Social Function: Using drama and performance to address societal problems and
promote positive behaviour.
 Managerial Function: Overseeing the whole production process, from rehearsals to final
performance.
 Unifying Function: Bringing together all the elements of the play – actors, stage,
costumes, music, and lighting – into a single well-organized production.
In summary, the director is both a leader and a teacher who ensures that a play or performance is
meaningful, entertaining, educative, and properly delivered to the audience.
