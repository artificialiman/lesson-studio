NAMING COMPOUNDS — DIAGRAM EDITION

80 questions. Every question is built on a diagram: structural formulas, reaction schemes, isomer sets, ring drawings, ion and electron-shell pictures. Organic naming, isomers and reaction products come first; mineral and inorganic naming closes the paper.

How to read the diagrams

  • Every line is one shared pair of electrons. Hydrogens are drawn in full, except where a group is written as CH₃ or CH₂ to keep a branch tidy.
  • A dashed box with ? is the product you must name. I, II, III label structures to compare. In "which structure" questions the options are the drawings A–D.
  • Inorganic pictures: grey circle = positive ion, white circle = negative ion, small circle = water molecule, p = protons, dots = electrons.

Naming an organic compound in four moves

  1. Parent chain. Find the longest carbon chain that contains the principal functional group: meth- 1, eth- 2, prop- 3, but- 4, pent- 5, hex- 6.
  2. Number it so the principal group gets the lowest number. If there is no principal group, give the branches the lowest numbers.
  3. Name the branches (methyl, ethyl, chloro, bromo), use di-, tri- for repeats, and list them in alphabetical order (e before m).
  4. Ending, by priority: acid (-oic acid) > ester (-oate) > aldehyde (-al) > ketone (-one) > alcohol (-ol) > amine (-amine) > C=C (-ene), C≡C (-yne). Lower-priority groups become prefixes (hydroxy-, amino-).

Part A — Alkanes and branched chains (Q1–10)

1.NC-001 · A01 · E

The alkane whose structure is shown is named

A. pentene
B. butane
C. pentane
D. hexane
Answer: C — pentane. Count the carbons along the chain: five, all joined by single bonds. Five carbons = pent-, single bonds = -ane: pentane, C₅H₁2.Trap: Pentene would need a C=C double bond. There is none.
2.NC-002 · A02 · M

Name the hydrocarbon shown. (CH₃ = methyl end group, CH₂ = chain carbon.)

A. 2,2-dimethylpropane
B. 3-methylbutane
C. 2-methylbutane
D. pentane
Answer: D — pentane. Follow the bonds, not the shape on the page: CH₃-CH₂-CH₂-CH₂-CH₃. It is one unbranched chain of five carbons that happens to bend. Name: pentane.Trap: A bend in the drawing is not a branch. Reading the bend as a methyl group gives 2-methylbutane.
3.NC-003 · A03 · M

The IUPAC name of the compound shown is

A. 3-methylbutane
B. 2-methylbutane
C. 2-ethylpropane
D. pentane
Answer: B — 2-methylbutane. Longest chain = 4 carbons (butane). Number from the end nearer the branch, which is the right-hand end. The methyl group is then on C₂: 2-methylbutane.Trap: Numbering from the left puts the methyl on C₃. The lowest-locant rule forbids that.
4.NC-004 · A04 · M

Name the compound shown (formula C₅H₁2).

A. 2,2-dimethylpropane
B. 2-methylbutane
C. pentane
D. 1,1-dimethylpropane
Answer: A — 2,2-dimethylpropane. The longest chain is only three carbons (CH₃-C-CH₃), so the parent is propane. Two more CH₃ groups hang on the middle carbon: 2,2-dimethylpropane.Trap: 1,1-dimethylpropane is not a valid name: it breaks the longest-chain rule (it is really 2-methylbutane).
5.NC-005 · A05 · M

The IUPAC name of the compound shown is

A. 2,3-dimethylbutane
B. 2,2-dimethylbutane
C. 3,4-dimethylbutane
D. hexane
Answer: A — 2,3-dimethylbutane. Longest chain = 4 carbons (butane). One methyl on C₂ and one on C₃: 2,3-dimethylbutane. Two groups = di- before methyl, each with its own locant.Trap: The two methyls sit on different carbons, so 2,2- is wrong. 3,4- would number past the middle of the chain.
6.NC-006 · A06 · E

Name the compound shown.

A. 2-methylpentane
B. 2-ethylbutane
C. 3-methylhexane
D. 3-methylpentane
Answer: D — 3-methylpentane. Five-carbon chain (pentane) with a methyl on the middle carbon, which is C₃ from either end: 3-methylpentane (C₆H₁4).Trap: Count carbons first: 3-methylhexane would have 7 carbons. The drawing has 6.
7.NC-007 · A07 · H

The IUPAC name of the compound shown is (CH₃ = methyl, CH₂ = chain carbon)

A. 2-methyl-3-ethylpentane
B. 3-ethyl-4-methylpentane
C. 3-ethyl-2-methylpentane
D. 2,3-dimethylhexane
Answer: C — 3-ethyl-2-methylpentane. Longest chain = 5 carbons. Number from the left so the branches get the lowest locants {2,3}: methyl on C₂, ethyl on C₃. Prefixes go in alphabetical order, e before m: 3-ethyl-2-methylpentane.Trap: Alphabetical order beats numerical order when writing the name. 2,3-dimethylhexane is an isomer (also C₈H₁8) with a six-carbon chain.
8.NC-008 · A08 · E

Name the compound shown. (Each corner of the ring is a CH₂ group.)

A. hexane
B. cyclohexane
C. cyclohexene
D. benzene
Answer: B — cyclohexane. A ring of six carbons joined by single bonds: cyclohexane, C₆H₁2.Trap: Benzene is drawn with a circle (or alternate double bonds) and has only 6 hydrogens. Hexane is an open chain.
9.NC-009 · A09 · M

Name the compound shown (CH₃ = methyl, CH₂ = chain carbon).

A. 3-ethylpentane
B. 2-ethylpentane
C. heptane
D. 3-methylhexane
Answer: A — 3-ethylpentane. Trace every possible path: the longest chain is 5 carbons (through the ethyl group you also get only 5). Parent = pentane, with an ethyl group on C₃: 3-ethylpentane (C₇H₁6).Trap: 3-methylhexane is an isomer with a six-carbon chain. Here no chain longer than five exists.
10.NC-010 · A10 · E

Structures I, II and III belong, in that order, to the homologous series of

A. alkanes, alkynes, alkenes
B. alkanes, alkenes, alkynes
C. alkenes, alkanes, alkynes
D. alkynes, alkenes, alkanes
Answer: B — alkanes, alkenes, alkynes. I has only single bonds (alkane, propane). II has one C=C (alkene, propene). III has one C≡C (alkyne, propyne).Trap: Match bond to ending: single = -ane, double = -ene, triple = -yne.

B. butanoic acid

D. propanoic acid
Answer: D — propanoic acid. Count all the carbons, including the carboxyl carbon: three. Name: propanoic acid.Trap: Leaving out the carboxyl carbon gives a count of two, which is ethanoic acid.

Name the compound shown.

A. propanoic acid
B. methyl ethanoate
C. ethyl methanoate
D. methyl propanoate
Answer: B — methyl ethanoate. In an ester, the alkyl group on the single-bonded oxygen is named first (CH₃: methyl). The acid part, CH₃-C=O (two carbons), is named second: ethanoate. Name: methyl ethanoate.Trap: Reversing the two parts gives ethyl methanoate, a different compound with the same formula, C₃H₆O₂.

Part E — Isomers (Q39–50)

39.NC-039 · E01 · M

Structures I and II both have the formula C₄H₁0. They are

A. chain isomers
B. functional group isomers
C. position isomers
D. geometric isomers
Answer: A — chain isomers. Same molecular formula. The carbon skeleton is straight in I and branched in II (butane and 2-methylpropane): chain isomers.Trap: Position isomers keep the same skeleton and move the functional group. Nothing has moved here: the skeleton changed.
40.NC-040 · E02 · M

Structures I and II both have the formula C₃H₈O. They are

A. geometric isomers
B. chain isomers
C. position isomers
D. functional group isomers
Answer: C — position isomers. Both are alcohols on a three-carbon chain. The -OH is on C₁ in I and on C₂ in II: propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol, position isomers.Trap: The skeleton and the functional group are the same in both; only the position of -OH differs.
41.NC-041 · E03 · M

Structures I and II both have the formula C₂H₆O. They are

A. chain isomers
B. position isomers
C. geometric isomers
D. functional group isomers
Answer: D — functional group isomers. I is an alcohol (ethanol, -OH). II is an ether (methoxymethane, C-O-C). Same formula, different functional group: functional group isomers.Trap: This pair is the classic trap: ethanol boils at 78 °C, methoxymethane at -24 °C, although the formulas match.
42.NC-042 · E04 · M

Which TWO of the structures I, II and III are isomers of each other?

A. I and II
B. I, II and III
C. II and III
D. I and III
Answer: A — I and II. Work out each formula. I is propanone, C₃H₆O. II is propanal, C₃H₆O. III is propan-2-ol, C₃H₈O. Only I and II share a formula: functional group isomers (ketone and aldehyde).Trap: Similar-looking drawings can still have different formulas. Count the H atoms.
43.NC-043 · E05 · H

Two structural isomers of C₅H₁2 are shown. Box III stands for the others. How many structural isomers does C₅H₁2 have altogether?

A. 4
B. 3
C. 2
D. 5
Answer: B — 3. C₅H₁2: pentane (straight), 2-methylbutane (one methyl branch) and 2,2-dimethylpropane (two methyls on one carbon). That is 3.Trap: Isomer counts: C₄H₁0 has 2, C₅H₁2 has 3, C₆H₁4 has 5.
44.NC-044 · E06 · M

I and II both have the formula C₄H₈. They are

A. functional group isomers
B. position isomers
C. geometric (cis-trans) isomers
D. chain isomers
Answer: C — geometric (cis-trans) isomers. Same atoms joined in the same order. The CH₃ groups are on the same side of the C=C in I (cis) and on opposite sides in II (trans): geometric isomers.Trap: Rotation about a C=C is blocked, so the two arrangements cannot interconvert.
45.NC-045 · E07 · H

Which of the structures I, II and III can show cis-trans isomerism?

A. I only
B. III only
C. I and II
D. II only
Answer: D — II only. Cis-trans isomerism needs two DIFFERENT groups on each carbon of the C=C. Only II (but-2-ene) has H and CH₃ on each. I and III have a CH₂ end with two identical H atoms.Trap: Having a double bond is not enough. Look at what is attached to each end of it.
46.NC-046 · E08 · M

Structures I, II and III all have the formula C₃H₈O. Which pair are POSITION isomers?

A. none of the pairs
B. I and II
C. I and III
D. II and III
Answer: B — I and II. I and II are both alcohols with the same skeleton and the -OH in different places. III is an ether, so its relation to I and to II is functional group isomerism.Trap: Position isomerism needs the same functional group. III has a different one.
47.NC-047 · E09 · M

I and II both have the formula C₄H₈O₂. They are

A. functional group isomers
B. position isomers
C. chain isomers
D. geometric isomers
Answer: A — functional group isomers. I is butanoic acid (-COOH). II is ethyl ethanoate (-COO- ester link). Same formula, different functional group.Trap: Carboxylic acids and esters with the same number of carbons are always isomers of each other.
48.NC-048 · E10 · H

I and II both have the formula C₆H₁2. They are

A. members of the same homologous series
B. geometric isomers
C. structural isomers
D. the same compound
Answer: C — structural isomers. I is hex-1-ene (open chain, one C=C). II is cyclohexane (ring, no C=C). Same formula, different structure: structural (ring-chain) isomers.Trap: They are in different homologous series (alkene and cycloalkane), which is exactly why they cannot be the same compound.
49.NC-049 · E11 · M

Four structures are shown. Which one is NOT an isomer of the other three?

A. III
B. IV
C. II
D. I
Answer: A — III. I butan-1-ol, II butan-2-ol and IV ethoxyethane are all C₄H₁0O. III is butanal, C₄H₈O (two fewer H).Trap: The C=O in III costs two hydrogens. Always compare formulas before comparing shapes.
50.NC-050 · E12 · M

Which of the rings A to D is 1,3-dimethylbenzene?

A. Structure A
B. Structure B
C. Structure C
D. Structure D
Answer: D — Structure D. 1,3- means the two methyl groups are on carbons separated by one ring carbon (meta). Counting round the ring: 1 and 3.Trap: Adjacent = 1,2 (ortho). Opposite = 1,4 (para). The three-methyl ring is 1,3,5-trimethylbenzene.

Part F — Products of basic organic reactions (Q51–64)

51.NC-051 · F01 · E

What is the organic product of the reaction shown?

A. ethanol
B. ethane
C. chloroethane
D. ethyne
Answer: B — ethane. Hydrogenation: H₂ adds across the C=C, so CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ gives CH₃-CH₃, ethane. No double bond is left, so the ending is -ane.Trap: ethanol would need water (steam), not hydrogen.
52.NC-052 · F02 · E

What is the organic product of the reaction shown? (The orange bromine is decolourised.)

A. 1,1-dibromoethane
B. ethanol
C. 1,2-dibromoethane
D. bromoethane
Answer: C — 1,2-dibromoethane. Br₂ adds across the C=C, one Br on each carbon: 1,2-dibromoethane. This is the standard test for unsaturation.Trap: Bromoethane would be a substitution product of ethane and needs UV light.
53.NC-053 · F03 · H

What is the MAJOR organic product of the reaction shown?

A. 1-bromopropane
B. 1,2-dibromopropane
C. propane
D. 2-bromopropane
Answer: D — 2-bromopropane. HBr adds across C=C. The H goes to the carbon that already has more hydrogens (Markovnikov rule), so Br ends up on the middle carbon: 2-bromopropane.Trap: 1-bromopropane is the minor product. 1,2-dibromopropane would come from Br₂, not HBr.
54.NC-054 · F04 · M

What is the organic product of the reaction shown?

A. ethanal
B. ethane
C. ethanol
D. ethanoic acid
Answer: C — ethanol. Hydration: H-OH adds across the C=C, giving CH₃CH₂OH, ethanol (the industrial method).Trap: Ethanal and ethanoic acid are oxidation products and need an oxidising agent.
55.NC-055 · F05 · M

What is the organic product of the reaction shown when H₂ is in excess?

A. ethene
B. ethane
C. ethyne
D. ethanol
Answer: B — ethane. Excess H₂ saturates the triple bond completely: C≡C takes two H₂ molecules, first becoming C=C (ethene) and then C-C. Final product: ethane.Trap: With only one mole of H₂ the reaction stops at ethene.
56.NC-056 · F06 · M

What is the main organic product of the reaction shown?

A. chloroethane
B. ethanol
C. 1,2-dichloroethane
D. chloroethene
Answer: A — chloroethane. Alkanes cannot add anything. With UV light, a free-radical substitution replaces one H with Cl: CH₃CH₂Cl, chloroethane (plus HCl).Trap: chloroethene would have a C=C, which this reaction does not create.
57.NC-057 · F07 · M

What is the organic product when ethanol is heated under reflux with excess oxidising agent?

A. ethyl ethanoate
B. ethanal
C. ethene
D. ethanoic acid
Answer: D — ethanoic acid. A primary alcohol is oxidised first to an aldehyde and then to a carboxylic acid. Excess oxidant and reflux carry it all the way: ethanoic acid.Trap: If the ethanal is distilled off as it forms, the product is ethanal. Heating under reflux does not allow that.
58.NC-058 · F08 · M

What is the organic product of the reaction shown?

A. propanoic acid
B. propene
C. propanone
D. propanal
Answer: C — propanone. A secondary alcohol is oxidised to a ketone and stops there: the carbon with the -OH has no second H to lose. Product: propanone.Trap: propanal and propanoic acid come from propan-1-ol, the primary alcohol.
59.NC-059 · F09 · M

What is the organic product of the reaction shown?

A. methyl ethanoate
B. ethyl ethanoate
C. ethoxyethane
D. ethyl methanoate
Answer: B — ethyl ethanoate. Esterification: acid + alcohol gives ester + water. The alcohol part is ethyl (from ethanol) and the acid part is ethanoate: ethyl ethanoate.Trap: methyl ethanoate would need methanol, and ethoxyethane is an ether made from two alcohols.
60.NC-060 · F10 · M

What is the organic product of the reaction shown?

A. ethene
B. ethanal
C. ethane
D. ethoxyethane
Answer: A — ethene. Conc. H₂SO₄ at 170 °C removes H₂O from one ethanol molecule, forming the C=C: ethene.Trap: At about 140 °C with excess ethanol, two molecules join to give the ether, ethoxyethane. The temperature decides.
61.NC-061 · F11 · M

What is the organic product of the reaction shown?

B. ethane

D. ethanal

62.NC-062 · F12 · H

What is the organic product of the reaction shown?

A. ethanol
B. ethene
C. ethane
D. ethoxyethane
Answer: B — ethene. Alcoholic KOH removes H and Br from neighbouring carbons (elimination): CH₃CH₂Br becomes CH₂=CH₂, ethene.Trap: Same starting material as the previous question; the solvent decides between substitution (water) and elimination (ethanol).
63.NC-063 · F13 · H

Ethyl ethanoate is heated with aqueous NaOH. Name the sodium salt formed (box).

A. sodium ethoxide
B. ethanoic acid
C. sodium ethanoate
D. sodium methanoate
Answer: C — sodium ethanoate. Alkaline hydrolysis (saponification) splits the ester: the acid part becomes its sodium salt and the alcohol part is released as ethanol. Acid part = ethanoate: sodium ethanoate.Trap: ethanoic acid is only produced by acid hydrolysis. With NaOH the acid part ends up as its salt.
64.NC-064 · F14 · M

Name the polymer formed (box) when chloroethene polymerises as shown.

A. poly(chloroethane)
B. poly(ethene)
C. poly(propene)
D. poly(chloroethene)
Answer: D — poly(chloroethene). Addition polymerisation: the C=C of each monomer opens and the molecules join in a chain. The polymer is named after its monomer, chloroethene: poly(chloroethene), PVC.Trap: poly(chloroethane) names the wrong monomer: a saturated molecule cannot polymerise by addition.

Grey circle = positive ion (cation). White circle = negative ion (anion). Small circle = water molecule. Boxes show an element and its charge; ? means work it out from the fact that the compound is neutral.

71.NC-071 · H01 · M

The formula unit of a compound is shown (grey = positive ion, white = negative ion). Its name is

A. sodium sulphite
B. sodium thiosulphate
C. sodium sulphate
D. sodium sulphide
Answer: C — sodium sulphate. The anion SO₄²⁻ is sulphate (SO₃²⁻ is sulphite, S²⁻ is sulphide). Two Na+ balance one 2- ion: sodium sulphate. Spellings follow WAEC/JAMB (sulphate, sulphide); IUPAC writes sulfate, sulfide.Trap: Do not put "di" in front of sodium: the ratio is shown by the formula, never in the name of an ionic compound.
72.NC-072 · H02 · M

Name the compound whose formula unit is shown.

A. iron(III) chloride
B. iron(II) chloride
C. iron(III) chlorine
D. iron trichloride
Answer: A — iron(III) chloride. Iron forms more than one ion, so the charge goes in Roman numerals. The ion drawn is Fe³⁺: iron(III). The anion of chlorine is chloride: iron(III) chloride.Trap: iron(II) chloride is FeCl₂. The numeral is the charge on the metal ion, not the number of chlorine atoms.
73.NC-073 · H03 · H

In this oxide the compound is neutral overall. What is its name?

A. copper(II) peroxide
B. copper(III) oxide
C. copper(II) oxide
D. copper(I) oxide
Answer: D — copper(I) oxide. Two copper atoms and one O²⁻. Total must be zero: 2x + (-2) = 0, so x = +1 for each copper. Name: copper(I) oxide.Trap: copper(II) oxide is CuO (one Cu per O). The two copper atoms share the charge.
74.NC-074 · H04 · M

The diagram shows the electron arrangements of the two ions in a compound (p = protons). Name the compound.

A. sodium oxide
B. magnesium peroxide
C. magnesium oxide
D. magnesium fluoride
Answer: C — magnesium oxide. Atomic number 12 is magnesium and 8 is oxygen. Mg²⁺ has lost 2 electrons (10 left, 2.8) and O²⁻ has gained 2 (10, 2.8). Compound: magnesium oxide, MgO.Trap: Fluoride (9p) and sodium (11p) also have 2.8 ions, so the proton numbers matter: read 12p and 8p.
75.NC-075 · H05 · H

The diagram shows the three ions in one formula unit of a mineral (fluorspar). Name the compound.

A. calcium fluoride
B. calcium fluorite
C. calcium chloride
D. calcium oxide
Answer: A — calcium fluoride. Atomic number 20 is calcium (Ca²⁺, 2.8.8) and 9 is fluorine (F⁻, 2.8). Two F⁻ balance one Ca²⁺: calcium fluoride, CaF₂, the mineral fluorite.Trap: Chloride would be 17p with 2.8.8. The mineral is often called fluorite, but the compound is named calcium fluoride.
76.NC-076 · H06 · M

The neutralisation reaction shown gives a salt (box). Name it.

A. sodium phosphite
B. sodium phosphate
C. sodium hydrogenphosphate
D. sodium phosphide
Answer: B — sodium phosphate. The salt takes its metal from the base (sodium) and its anion from the acid. H₃PO₄ loses all three H+, leaving PO₄³⁻: sodium phosphate, Na₃PO₄.Trap: All 3 H are used up (3NaOH), so there is no hydrogen left in the anion. Hydrogenphosphate would keep one.
77.NC-077 · H07 · M

A blue crystal contains the particles shown (large circles = ions, small circles = water molecules). Name the compound.

A. copper(I) sulphate pentahydrate
B. copper(II) sulphide pentahydrate
C. copper(II) sulphate pentahydrate
D. copper(II) sulphite pentahydrate
Answer: C — copper(II) sulphate pentahydrate. Cu²⁺ gives copper(II); SO₄²⁻ gives sulphate; five water molecules in the crystal give "penta-hydrate": copper(II) sulphate pentahydrate, CuSO₄·5H₂O.Trap: The Greek prefix is used for the water only. Charge numbers (Roman) go on the metal.
78.NC-078 · H08 · M

The ore galena has the composition shown. Its chemical name is

A. lead(II) sulphide
B. lead(II) sulphate
C. lead(II) sulphite
D. lead(IV) sulphide
Answer: A — lead(II) sulphide. Pb²⁺ is lead(II) and S²⁻ is sulphide: lead(II) sulphide, PbS. Galena is the main ore of lead.Trap: Sulphide is S²⁻ on its own. Sulphate is SO₄²⁻ and sulphite is SO₃²⁻.
79.NC-079 · H09 · H

The copper ore malachite contains the ions shown. Its chemical name is

A. copper(I) carbonate hydroxide
B. copper(II) carbonate hydroxide
C. copper(II) carbonate
D. copper(II) hydrogencarbonate
Answer: B — copper(II) carbonate hydroxide. Two different anions are present: carbonate and hydroxide.
