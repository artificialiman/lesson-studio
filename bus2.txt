WEEK 1: EFFECTIVE OFFICE PRACTICE
Meaning of an Office
An office is a place, room, or building where the clerical, administrative, and managerial work of
an organization is carried out. It serves as the centre of coordination for business activities such
as receiving, processing, storing, and communicating information.
The office is not limited to physical space only. In modern times, with the use of computers and
the internet, many people operate virtual offices from their homes or remote locations.
Types of Offices
3
1. Small Office
o Usually run by a small business or sole proprietorship.
o Few staff members.
o Simple structure and functions.
o Examples: a lawyer’s office, tailoring shop, local mechanic’s office.
2. Large Office
o Found in big organizations, companies, or government agencies.
o Many departments and a large number of staff.
o Specialized division of labour and modern office equipment.
o Examples: Central Bank of Nigeria, large manufacturing companies, ministries.
3. Open Office
o A large hall where many clerks or workers perform their duties together without
partitions.
o Advantages: easy supervision, better communication, less expensive.
o Disadvantages: noisy environment, lack of privacy, easy spread of gossip.
4. Private Office
o Small enclosed office, usually assigned to one person (manager or senior officer).
o Advantages: privacy, reduced noise, increased concentration.
o Disadvantages: more expensive to maintain, difficult supervision of workers.
Importance of an Office
1. Information Management
o Receives, records, stores, and supplies information for decision-making.
4
2. Communication
o Acts as a link between the organization and outsiders (customers, suppliers,
government).
3. Decision-Making
o Provides necessary information and reports that help managers make informed
decisions.
4. Coordination
o Ensures that all departments work together smoothly by providing accurate data
and support.
5. Safekeeping of Records
o Stores important documents like contracts, receipts, invoices, financial statements,
and correspondence.
6. Image of the Organization
o A well-organized office creates a good impression of efficiency and orderliness.
7. Support Services
o Provides clerical and administrative support such as typing, filing, scheduling,
and data processing.
Qualities of an Effective Office
An effective office should:
 Be located in a convenient and accessible place.
 Be well-organized with clear arrangements of departments.
 Have qualified and competent staff.
 Possess adequate modern office equipment and facilities.
5
 Ensure confidentiality of information.
 Promote efficiency, productivity, and good customer service.
EVALUATION

5. List three qualities of an effective office.

WEEK 2: THE RECEPTION OFFICE
MEANING OF A RECEPTION OFFICE
 The reception office is the section of an organization, business, or establishment where
visitors, clients, or customers are first received, attended to, and directed to the right
offices.
 It is usually located at the entrance or front area of a company.
 It serves as the first point of contact between the public and the organization.
 It is sometimes referred to as the front office.
7
MEANING OF A RECEPTIONIST
 A receptionist is the officer or staff member responsible for manning the reception office.
 He or she receives visitors, provides information, answers telephone calls, directs people
to the appropriate offices, and performs other clerical duties.
 A receptionist acts as the link between the public and the organization.
 The receptionist is often the first impression of the organization, so he or she must be
polite, neat, and professional.
QUALITIES OF A GOOD RECEPTIONIST
A good receptionist should possess the following qualities:
 Neat appearance and good grooming.
 Polite, friendly, and welcoming attitude.
 Ability to communicate clearly (both orally and in writing).
 Knowledge of the organization and its activities.
 Ability to keep records properly.
 Tactfulness and patience in dealing with different people.
 Ability to handle office machines (telephone, intercom, computer, photocopier, etc.).
 Punctuality and reliability.
IMPORTANCE OF A RECEPTIONIST
The receptionist plays an important role in any organization:
 Creates first impressions: The receptionist’s attitude and appearance create the first
impression of the organization.
8
 Attends to visitors: Provides information, assistance, and directions to visitors and
customers.
 Handles communication: Answers and makes telephone calls, takes messages, and
relays information to staff.
 Maintains records: Keeps visitors’ books, schedules appointments, and manages
incoming and outgoing correspondence.
 Controls access: Ensures visitors go to the right offices and prevents unnecessary
disturbances to management.
 Improves organizational efficiency: By directing visitors properly, time and resources
are saved.
 Supports security: The receptionist monitors movement of visitors, helping in the
security of the organization.
 Promotes customer relations: Friendly reception encourages customers to continue
doing business with the organization.
IMPORTANCE OF A RECEPTION OFFICE
 Serves as the organization’s first contact point with outsiders.
 Enhances the image and reputation of the organization.
 Provides a comfortable waiting area for visitors.
 Promotes effective communication between the organization and the public.
 Assists in smooth coordination of visitors and staff interactions.
 Prevents unnecessary interruptions to official work.
EVALUATION

WEEK 3: DUTIES OF A RECEPTIONIST
Qualities of a Good Receptionist; How to Receive and Treat Visitors
INTRODUCTION
In any business organization, the receptionist plays a very important role. The receptionist is
usually the first person visitors, customers, and even staff meet when they come into an office.
Because first impressions often determine how outsiders perceive an organization, the
receptionist acts as the face and representative of the business. Therefore, the duties of a
receptionist go beyond merely answering the telephone or welcoming visitors; they involve
creating a positive image for the organization.
11
QUALITIES OF A GOOD RECEPTIONIST
To perform effectively, a receptionist must possess certain qualities. These include:
1. Politeness and Courtesy: A receptionist must always use polite language and treat
everyone with respect, no matter their status.
2. Good Communication Skills: Since the job involves constant interaction with visitors,
the receptionist should speak clearly, confidently, and with understanding.
3. Neat Appearance: Because appearance creates a lasting impression, a receptionist
should dress neatly and maintain proper grooming.
4. Patience: A receptionist must deal calmly with difficult or impatient visitors without
becoming rude.
5. Alertness and Attentiveness: Receptionists must remain focused to respond quickly to
enquiries and situations.
6. Confidentiality: Sensitive information must be handled discreetly without gossip or
disclosure.
7. Efficiency and Organization: A receptionist should be able to handle calls, record
appointments, and manage visitors without confusion.
8. Friendliness and Warmth: A good smile and approachable attitude put visitors at ease.
HOW TO RECEIVE AND TREAT VISITORS
The way visitors are handled in an office often determines whether they will return or not. The
receptionist must therefore ensure that every visitor is received warmly and directed
appropriately. The following steps are important in receiving and treating visitors:
1. Prompt Attention: Visitors should be acknowledged as soon as they arrive, even if the
receptionist is busy.
12
2. Polite Greeting: The receptionist should greet visitors with courtesy (e.g., “Good
morning, sir/ma, how may I help you?”).
3. Enquiry and Assistance: Politely find out the visitor’s purpose for coming and provide
guidance.
4. Visitor’s Record: Record the visitor’s details (name, whom they wish to see, and time of
arrival) in a visitor’s book if required.
5. Notification: Contact the officer or staff member the visitor has come to see and inform
them of the arrival.
6. Proper Waiting Arrangement: If the person to be visited is not immediately available,
the receptionist should politely direct the visitor to the waiting area and ensure they are
comfortable.
7. Offering Assistance: Depending on the office, the receptionist may offer water or
reading material while the visitor waits.
8. Directing or Escorting: When the staff member is ready, the receptionist should direct
or escort the visitor to the appropriate office.
9. Polite Farewell: After the visit, the receptionist should bid the visitor goodbye
courteously to leave a lasting positive impression.
IMPORTANCE OF A RECEPTIONIST IN AN ORGANIZATION
 Creates the first impression of the organization.
 Ensures orderliness and proper record keeping of visitors.
 Acts as a link between outsiders and staff of the organization.
 Enhances the professional image of the company.
 Reduces time wasted by guiding visitors promptly.
13

Meaning of Office Etiquette
 Office etiquette refers to the acceptable manners, behaviors, and professional conduct
expected of workers in an office environment.
15
 It includes dress, communication, time management, respect, teamwork, and general
workplace behavior.
Appropriate Office Dress Code
 Workers are expected to appear neat, clean, and professional at all times.
 For men:
o Suit, shirt, tie, neat shoes, well–kept hair.
o Avoid slippers, torn jeans, or flashy outfits.
 For women:
o Decent dresses, suits, skirts, or blouses.
o Avoid excessively tight, short, or revealing clothes.
o Minimal, professional make-up and accessories.
 General Rules:
o Clothes must be ironed and neat.
o Shoes polished.
o Identification card (if required) should be worn.
o Uniform (in some offices like banks, airlines, or security firms).
 Dressing well creates a good impression, enhances confidence, and reflects positively on
the organization.
Documents Handled by the Receptionist
A receptionist is the first point of contact in the office. He/She handles important documents
such as:
16
1. Visitors’ Book/Log Book – records names, addresses, time of arrival and departure of
visitors.
2. Incoming Mail – letters, parcels, and other correspondence addressed to the organization.
3. Outgoing Mail – letters and packages sent out from the office.
4. Telephone Messages/Call Slips – notes and messages taken on behalf of staff.
5. Appointment Register – records staff or official appointments.
6. Internal Memos and Circulars – short office communication passed to different
departments.
7. Delivery Notes/Receipts – acknowledgments for parcels or items delivered.
Importance of Office Etiquette
 Promotes discipline and order in the office.
 Enhances the image of the organization.
 Encourages good working relationships among staff.
 Increases productivity and efficiency.
 Builds trust with clients and customers.

WEEK 6: OFFICE CORRESPONDENCE (PART I)
 Meaning of Office Correspondence
 Types of Correspondence
INTRODUCTION
Every organization, whether big or small, depends on communication for its smooth running. In
the office, communication is usually done in writing for accuracy, reference, and record-keeping.
Such written communications are called office correspondence. They are essential for day-today
administration, decision-making, and maintenance of proper records in business transactions.
In modern business, correspondence is not only done within the office but also between the
organization and external parties such as customers, suppliers, government agencies, and other
stakeholders. Therefore, understanding office correspondence is important for students who wish
to develop administrative and business communication skills.
18
MEANING OF OFFICE CORRESPONDENCE
Office correspondence refers to all forms of written communication exchanged within an office
or between an office and other organizations or individuals. It includes letters, memos, circulars,
reports, notices, emails, and other written messages.
It is a permanent means of communication in business because it provides evidence, can be
referred to in the future, and ensures accuracy.
Importance of Office Correspondence
1. Record Keeping – It provides permanent records of transactions.
2. Evidence in Case of Dispute – Written correspondence can be used as proof in legal
matters.
3. Reference for Future Actions – It serves as a guide for decision-making.
19
4. Effective Communication – It ensures clear and precise exchange of information.
5. Facilitates Smooth Running of Business – It helps to give instructions, seek
clarifications, and confirm agreements.
6. Maintains Relationship – Correspondence helps in maintaining good relations with
customers, suppliers, and other stakeholders.
TYPES OF OFFICE CORRESPONDENCE
There are different types of correspondence used in business and office administration. They can
be grouped into:
1. Internal Correspondence
 This is correspondence exchanged within the same organization.
 Examples: memos between departments, notices to staff, circulars, internal reports.
 Purpose: to facilitate communication among workers and management.
2. External Correspondence
 This is correspondence exchanged between an organization and outside individuals or
bodies.
 Examples: letters to customers, suppliers, government agencies, or other companies.
 Purpose: to maintain business relationships, seek information, or conduct transactions.
3. Personal Correspondence
 This is correspondence between individuals within or outside the office.
 Examples: letters of application, leave letters, appreciation notes.
 Purpose: usually for personal communication within a professional setting.
4. Official Correspondence
20
 This involves communication between government offices or between government and
private organizations.
 Examples: official circulars, government notices, directives.
 Purpose: to ensure smooth running of government activities and communication with the
public.
5. Business Correspondence
 This refers to written communication between two or more business organizations, or
between a business and its clients.
 Examples: inquiry letters, order letters, complaint letters, sales letters, acknowledgement
letters.
 Purpose: to promote trade, negotiate deals, and solve business-related issues.
MORAL AND PRACTICAL LESSONS
1. Good correspondence promotes order and efficiency in offices.
2. Students should learn to write clearly and correctly to prepare for future careers.
3. Honesty and accuracy are important when handling correspondence.
4. Business communication requires courtesy, conciseness, and correctness.
EVALUATION

4. List and explain four types of correspondence with examples.
5. Why is correspondence considered a permanent form of communication in business?

USES OF OFFICE CORRESPONDENCE
23

1. Communication of Information

2. Record Keeping

WEEK 9: HANDLING OF MAIL
 Inward mail and outward mail
 Mail handling procedures
INTRODUCTION
Every organization, whether public or private, receives and sends mail on a daily basis. Mail is
an important means of official communication within and outside the organization. Proper
handling of mail ensures smooth communication, efficiency in operations, and good record
keeping.
27
MEANING OF MAIL
 Mail refers to all letters, parcels, documents, and messages received or sent out of an
organization through postal services, couriers, hand delivery, or electronic means.
 It can be divided into:
1. Inward mail – all letters and parcels received into an organization from outside.
2. Outward mail – all letters and parcels sent out from an organization to other
people or organizations.
28
INWARD MAIL
 Meaning: Mail coming into an organization from customers, clients, business partners,
government, or the public.
 Examples: Orders from customers, official circulars, inquiries, complaints, bank
statements, invoices, receipts, contracts.
 Procedure for handling inward mail:
o Collection of mail from post office, courier, or delivery source.
o Opening of mail by authorized officer or secretary.
o Date stamping of all letters to mark receipt.
o Sorting of letters according to departments or officers concerned.
o Recording of all letters received in an inward mail register.
o Distribution of mail to relevant offices or staff.
OUTWARD MAIL
 Meaning: Mail sent out from an organization to customers, suppliers, partners, or other
institutions.
 Examples: Replies to inquiries, invoices, quotations, orders, receipts, appointment letters,
circulars, reminders, newsletters.
 Procedure for handling outward mail:
o Drafting and typing of outgoing letters.
o Checking and correction (proofreading) for accuracy and clarity.
o Signing of the letter by the authorized officer or manager.
o Recording in an outward mail register.
29
o Insertion of letters into envelopes, sealing, and addressing.
o Stamping or franking with postage stamps/machine.
o Dispatching mail through post office, courier, or electronic means.
MAIL HANDLING PROCEDURES (GENERAL STEPS)
1. Collection of mail:
o Inward mail is collected from post office, courier service, or reception desk.
o Outward mail is collected from departments after approval.
2. Opening and sorting:
o Letters are opened (except confidential ones) by the mailing officer.
o Sorted according to urgency, department, or subject matter.
3. Recording:
o All inward and outward mails must be entered into registers for accountability.
4. Distribution:
o Mails are delivered promptly to the officers or departments concerned.
5. Filing:
o After action has been taken, copies of mails are filed for reference and record
keeping.
IMPORTANCE OF PROPER MAIL HANDLING
 Ensures smooth communication in an organization.
 Prevents loss or misplacement of important documents.
 Enhances efficiency and quick decision-making.
30
 Promotes accountability and good record keeping.
 Projects the organization’s image positively to outsiders.
EVALUATION
1. Define the term “mail.”
2. Differentiate between inward mail and outward mail.
3. List and explain four procedures involved in handling inward mail.
4. State three procedures involved in dispatching outward mail.
5. Mention three reasons why proper mail handling is important in an organization.

WEEK 10: OFFICE DOCUMENTS
 Meaning of Office Documents
 Types of Office Documents
 Preparation and Uses of Sales and Purchase Documents
INTRODUCTION
In every office or business organization, different forms of documents are prepared and
exchanged in the course of carrying out daily operations. These documents serve as evidence of
transactions, tools for record keeping, and references for future actions. Without office
documents, businesses would face confusion, disputes, and lack of accountability.
Office documents are therefore very essential because they show how goods are bought and sold,
how services are rendered, and how financial activities are recorded. They are used for
communication, transaction recording, and legal purposes in both private and government
organizations.
MEANING OF OFFICE DOCUMENTS
Office documents are written records prepared and used in offices to facilitate business activities,
confirm agreements, and provide evidence of transactions between parties. They are prepared
either internally (within the office) or externally (between different organizations).
They can take the form of letters, vouchers, receipts, invoices, memos, and reports depending on
their use.
TYPES OF OFFICE DOCUMENTS
Office documents can be broadly classified into the following groups:
1. Administrative Documents
32
o These include memos, reports, circulars, and internal notes used within the office
to communicate and keep records.
o Example: a memo from the principal to teachers, a report on a school project.
2. Financial Documents
o These relate to money transactions and financial dealings.
o Examples: receipts, vouchers, payment slips, payrolls.
3. Commercial Documents
o These are documents used in buying and selling of goods and services.
o Examples: invoices, proforma invoices, debit notes, credit notes, delivery notes,
purchase orders, waybills.
4. Legal Documents
o These are documents that have legal backing and can be used in court to settle
disputes.
o Examples: contracts, agreements, deeds, certificates.
5. Personnel Documents
o These are records relating to employees in an organization.
o Examples: staff files, appointment letters, leave applications.
SALES AND PURCHASE DOCUMENTS
The most important office documents in business are sales and purchase documents because they
deal with day-to-day trade transactions.
1. Purchase Order
 A document prepared by the buyer and sent to the seller, showing items, quantities, and
terms of goods to be bought.
33
 Purpose: It serves as a formal request for goods.
2. Invoice
 A document sent by the seller to the buyer showing the description, quantity, unit price,
total cost of goods, and terms of payment.
 Purpose: It serves as a bill for goods supplied and a record of sales.
3. Proforma Invoice
 A document sent before goods are supplied, giving an estimated cost.
 Purpose: It is usually used when the buyer requests the price quotation before ordering.
4. Delivery Note
 A document sent with the goods delivered, listing items supplied.
 Purpose: To confirm that the right goods have been delivered.
5. Receipt
 A document issued by the seller to acknowledge payment for goods purchased.
 Purpose: It serves as evidence of payment.
6. Debit Note
 A document sent by the buyer to the seller to indicate that goods supplied were
overcharged or not up to standard.
 Purpose: To request correction of an overcharge.
7. Credit Note
 A document sent by the seller to the buyer acknowledging a reduction in the buyer’s
account due to return of goods or overcharge.
 Purpose: To correct errors or acknowledge returned goods.
34
PREPARATION OF SALES AND PURCHASE DOCUMENTS
 Purchase Order: Prepared by the buyer, containing buyer’s name, address, items
ordered, quantity, and agreed terms.
 Invoice: Prepared by the seller, showing goods supplied, cost, VAT (if any), and
payment terms.
 Delivery Note: Prepared by the seller, signed by the buyer upon receipt of goods.
 Receipt: Prepared by the seller as acknowledgment of money received.
 Credit and Debit Notes: Prepared by seller or buyer as adjustments to invoices.
USES OF SALES AND PURCHASE DOCUMENTS
1. They serve as evidence of sales and purchase transactions.
2. They prevent disputes between buyers and sellers.
3. They act as records for accounting and auditing.
4. They enable buyers and sellers to know the details of the goods supplied and payments
made.
5. They are useful for calculating profit and loss.
EVALUATION

WEEK 1: RIGHT ATTITUDE TO WORK
Meaning of Right Attitude to Work
The right attitude to work refers to a positive and responsible approach towards one’s duties
and responsibilities in a workplace, school, or community. It is the habit of doing the right thing
at the right time, with honesty, commitment, and dedication.
Having the right attitude to work shows seriousness, reliability, and a willingness to contribute to
the growth of an organization or society. It is the opposite of laziness, lateness, negligence,
dishonesty, and irresponsibility.
38
Characteristics of a Person with the Right Attitude to Work
 Punctuality and regular attendance.
 Willingness to follow instructions.
 Dedication and commitment to duties.
 Honesty and trustworthiness.
 Hard work and perseverance.
 Respect for authority and rules.
 Desire to improve and learn new skills.

WEEK 3: COMMERCE – THE HEART OF BUSINESS
Meaning and Importance of Trade; Forms of Trade
INTRODUCTION
Business involves activities carried out to provide goods and services to satisfy human needs and
wants. At the center of all business activities is commerce, which serves as the “heart of
business.” Commerce is concerned with trade and all the services that make trade possible.
Without commerce, production would be meaningless because goods and services would not be
able to reach consumers. In essence, commerce connects the producer to the consumer, ensuring
that goods and services move smoothly within the economy.
MEANING OF TRADE
47
Trade is the buying and selling of goods and services. It is one of the major aspects of
commerce. Trade provides a channel for producers to exchange their products for money and for
consumers to obtain what they need.
Importance of Trade
1. Exchange of Goods and Services: It enables producers and consumers to meet each
other’s needs.
2. Distribution of Goods: Trade helps in moving goods from areas of surplus to areas of
scarcity.
3. Encourages Specialization: Producers focus on producing certain goods because they
know they can sell them through trade.
4. Facilitates Economic Growth: Trade generates revenue, employment, and development
in society.
5. Improves Standard of Living: Consumers gain access to a wide range of products that
improve comfort and lifestyle.
6. International Relationship: International trade promotes peace and cooperation among
nations.
FORMS OF TRADE
Trade exists in two major forms: Home (or Internal) Trade and Foreign (or International)
Trade.
1. Home Trade (Internal Trade):
o This is trade carried out within a country’s borders.
o It involves buying and selling goods and services locally.
o Examples: A farmer in Kano selling tomatoes to a trader in Lagos; a boutique in
Abuja selling clothes to customers.
o It is divided into:
48
 Wholesale Trade: Buying in large quantities from producers and selling

 Retail Trade: Buying in small quantities from wholesalers and selling

2. Foreign Trade (International Trade):
o This is trade that takes place between one country and another.
o It allows nations to obtain goods they cannot produce or those they can produce
but in limited quantity.
o Examples: Nigeria exporting crude oil to other countries or importing cars,
electronics, and machinery.
o It is divided into:
 Import Trade: Buying goods and services from other countries into one’s
country.
 Export Trade: Selling goods and services produced in one’s country to
other nations.
 Entrepôt Trade (Re-export): Importing goods from one country and reexporting
them to another country without major changes.
WHY COMMERCE IS THE HEART OF BUSINESS
 It provides the avenue through which goods move from producers to final consumers.
 Commerce involves both trade and aids to trade (such as banking, insurance,
communication, advertising, and transport). These services make buying and selling

 Without commerce, production would end in waste since goods would not reach those
who need them.

WEEK 6: THE MARKET
 Meaning of a Market
 Features of a Market
 Commodity Market
 Money Market
 Capital Market
INTRODUCTION
The word market is commonly used to describe a physical place where buyers and sellers meet to
exchange goods and services, such as Balogun Market in Lagos or Ariaria Market in Aba.
However, in Business Studies, the term market goes beyond just a physical location. A market
exists anywhere buyers and sellers come into contact—physically or through other means like
telephone, internet, or banking systems—for the purpose of exchanging goods and services.
Markets are essential in every economy because they create opportunities for trade, ensure goods
and services are distributed, and allow money to circulate within the system.
55
MEANING OF A MARKET
A market is a system, structure, or arrangement that brings buyers and sellers of goods and
services together for the purpose of exchange. It is not limited to physical space, but can also be
virtual (like online markets), institutional (like the stock exchange), or informal (like roadside
trading).
In summary, a market can be defined as:
1. A place where buyers and sellers meet to exchange goods and services.
2. Any arrangement that facilitates the exchange of goods and services between buyers and
sellers.
FEATURES OF A MARKET
For a market to exist, the following features must be present:
1. Presence of Buyers and Sellers – Buyers demand goods and services while sellers
provide them.
2. Exchange of Goods and Services – The primary purpose of a market is the exchange of
goods and services for money or other goods.
3. Medium of Exchange – In most markets, money serves as the medium of exchange, but
in some cases barter (exchange of goods for goods) may occur.
4. Communication and Contact – There must be a form of communication between
buyers and sellers, either face-to-face or through technology.
5. Competition – Different sellers compete for buyers by offering better quality goods,
lower prices, or better services.
6. Demand and Supply – The market is regulated by the forces of demand and supply.
Prices are often determined by these forces.
7. Variety of Goods and Services – A market offers different kinds of goods and services
to meet the diverse needs of consumers.
56
TYPES OF MARKETS
Markets can be classified based on the nature of the goods or services traded and the methods of
operation. For JSS 2, we will focus on three important types:
1. Commodity Market
 This is a market where raw materials and agricultural produce are bought and sold.
 Examples: Cocoa market, Cotton market, Palm oil market, Grains market, Oil and gas
market.
 Features of Commodity Market:
o Involves buying and selling of physical goods (raw materials and farm produce).
o Prices are usually influenced by demand and supply.
o May be local (within Nigeria) or international (global trading of crude oil, gold,
etc.).
 Importance: Provides raw materials for industries and food for the population.
2. Money Market
 This is a financial market where short-term funds and financial instruments are bought
and sold (short-term means less than one year).
 Examples: Treasury bills, Bank loans, Commercial papers.
 Institutions involved: Commercial banks, Central bank, Finance houses.
 Features of Money Market:
o Deals with short-term borrowing and lending.
o Provides businesses with working capital.
o Ensures liquidity in the economy.
57
 Importance: Helps businesses meet urgent financial needs and supports government
short-term borrowing.
3. Capital Market
 This is a financial market where long-term funds and securities are traded (more than one
year).
 Examples: Shares, Bonds, Debentures.
 Institutions involved: Nigerian Stock Exchange, Securities and Exchange Commission
(SEC), Development banks.
 Features of Capital Market:
o Provides long-term financing for industries and government projects.
o Encourages investment by individuals and institutions.
o Helps companies raise money by selling shares.
 Importance: Promotes economic development by financing large projects like roads,
industries, and housing.

WEEK 10: PROFIT AND LOSS
 Calculation of profit and loss
 Importance of profit to business
INTRODUCTION
Every business exists to make profit. Profit and loss are financial results of trading activities. If
the selling price of goods is greater than the cost price, profit is made. If the selling price is less
than the cost price, a loss occurs. Understanding profit and loss helps entrepreneurs measure the
success or failure of their business.
CALCULATION OF PROFIT AND LOSS
 Key Terms
o Cost Price (C.P): The price at which goods are bought or produced.
70
o Selling Price (S.P): The price at which goods are sold.
o Profit: When Selling Price > Cost Price.
o Loss: When Cost Price > Selling Price.
 Formulas
o Profit = S.P – C.P
o Loss = C.P – S.P
o Profit % = (Profit ÷ C.P) × 100
o Loss % = (Loss ÷ C.P) × 100
 Worked Examples
1. A trader bought goods for ₦5,000 and sold them for ₦6,000.
 Profit = 6,000 – 5,000 = ₦1,000
 Profit % = (1,000 ÷ 5,000) × 100 = 20%
2. A radio was bought for ₦8,000 but sold for ₦7,000.
 Loss = 8,000 – 7,000 = ₦1,000
 Loss % = (1,000 ÷ 8,000) × 100 = 12.5%
IMPORTANCE OF PROFIT TO BUSINESS
 Sustains the Business: Provides funds for daily operations.
 Expansion: Enables the business to grow and open new branches.
 Reward for Risk-Taking: Entrepreneurs are encouraged to continue business despite
uncertainties.
 Improves Standard of Living: Higher profit allows business owners to live better and
provide for family needs.
71
 Employment Creation: Profitable businesses expand and employ more workers.
 Reinvestment: Profit can be reinvested into research, new technology, and equipment.
 Government Revenue: Profitable businesses pay taxes, which increase national
development.
 Motivation: Profit encourages innovation, hard work, and efficiency.
DIFFERENCES BETWEEN PROFIT AND LOSS
Profit Loss
Occurs when S.P > C.P Occurs when C.P > S.P
Brings financial gain to the business Brings financial setback
Encourages business expansion May cause business closure
Motivates the entrepreneur Discourages investment if persistent

WEEK 1: DISTRIBUTION
Meaning of Distribution
Distribution refers to the process of making goods and services available from the point of
production (the manufacturer or producer) to the final consumer. It involves all the activities,
people, and channels that ensure that goods produced in farms, factories, or workshops reach
those who need or want them.
In other words, distribution is the link between producers and consumers. Without distribution,
goods would remain in the place of production and would not serve the needs of people in
different locations.
Channels of Distribution
75
Channels of distribution are the paths or routes through which goods and services move from the
producers to the final consumers. They involve middlemen such as wholesalers and retailers, as
well as institutions that help in the movement of goods.
The main channels of distribution are:
1. Producer → Consumer
o This is a direct channel where the producer sells directly to the final consumer.
o Example: A farmer selling vegetables directly to families in a local market.
2. Producer → Retailer → Consumer
o Here, the producer sells goods directly to the retailer, who then sells to the final
consumer.
o Example: Shoe manufacturers selling directly to shoe shops that sell to customers.
3. Producer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer
o In this channel, the producer sells in bulk to wholesalers. The wholesaler then
sells to retailers, and the retailers sell to the consumers.
o Example: Beverage companies (Coca-Cola) → major distributors (wholesalers)
→ supermarkets/shops (retailers) → final consumers.
4. Producer → Agent → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer
o An agent serves as a representative of the producer, helping to sell goods to
wholesalers. The goods later pass through retailers to consumers.
o Example: Exporting companies using shipping agents to reach foreign
wholesalers before goods get to retailers.
Functions of Wholesalers
Wholesalers are middlemen who buy goods in large quantities from producers and sell in smaller
quantities to retailers.
76
Functions of wholesalers include:
 Bulk breaking: Buying goods in large quantities from producers and selling in smaller
quantities to retailers.
 Storage: Providing storage facilities to keep goods until they are needed.
 Financing: Offering credit facilities to retailers and sometimes to producers.
 Risk bearing: Taking responsibility for risks such as damage, spoilage, or unsold stock.
 Transportation: Helping in the movement of goods from producers to retailers.
 Market information: Providing valuable feedback to producers about consumer needs,
demand, and market trends.
 Advertising support: Assisting in promoting goods through displays and advertisements.
Functions of Retailers
Retailers are middlemen who buy goods in smaller quantities from wholesalers or producers and
sell directly to the final consumers.
Functions of retailers include:
 Selling in small units: Breaking bulk and selling in the quantities needed by consumers.
 Providing variety: Offering consumers different brands, sizes, and qualities of goods to
choose from.
 Information supply: Giving producers feedback about customers’ needs, preferences,
and complaints.
 Credit facilities: Sometimes granting credit to regular customers.
 After-sales services: Offering services such as delivery, installation, and repairs (e.g.,
electronics dealers).
 Advertising and promotion: Displaying and promoting goods to increase sales.
77
 Convenience: Bringing goods closer to consumers in their localities.
Importance of Distribution
1. Makes goods available to consumers at the right place and time.
2. Helps producers to sell their goods and make profit.
3. Encourages mass production since producers are sure of distribution outlets.
4. Provides employment opportunities (wholesalers, retailers, transporters, agents).
5. Facilitates the flow of market information between consumers and producers.
6. Enhances economic growth by ensuring steady flow of goods and services.

WEEK 2: LICENSED CHEMICAL VENDORS
MEANING OF LICENSED CHEMICAL VENDOR
 A licensed chemical vendor is an individual or business enterprise that is legally
permitted by the government through regulatory agencies (such as Pharmacists
Council of Nigeria – PCN, or NAFDAC) to sell and distribute certain categories of drugs
and chemicals to the public.
 They serve as community-based drug vendors especially in rural areas where access to
standard pharmacies may be limited.
 Licensing ensures that only trained and approved vendors are allowed to handle, stock,
and sell chemicals or drugs.
79
HANDLING AND DISTRIBUTION OF CHEMICALS
Licensed chemical vendors must handle and distribute chemicals carefully because of their
sensitive nature. Proper handling prevents accidents, poisoning, or misuse.
Ways of handling chemicals:
1. Proper storage – Chemicals should be kept in well-labeled containers, away from
children, fire, or direct sunlight.
2. Accurate measurement – Dosages must be carefully measured to avoid overdose or
underdose.
3. Good record-keeping – Details of chemicals purchased, sold, or dispensed should be
recorded.
4. Observing expiry dates – Expired chemicals must never be sold or used.
5. Hygiene and safety practices – Clean environment, use of gloves, protective equipment,
and washing of hands after handling.
6. Knowledge of usage – Vendors should know what each chemical is used for and how to
advise customers correctly.
7. Avoiding self-prescription – Vendors must not prescribe drugs beyond their scope but
should refer serious cases to hospitals.
Distribution of chemicals:
1. Through registered outlets only – Chemicals must be sold at recognized and approved
shops, not in markets or on the streets.
2. Following government regulations – Only chemicals/drugs permitted under the license
should be distributed.
3. Supplying in small quantities – Chemicals should not be distributed in bulk to untrained
persons.
4. Safe transportation – Chemicals must be moved using safe containers and vehicles, not
exposed to contamination.
80
5. Community accessibility – Vendors serve as the nearest points of access for basic
medications in rural and semi-urban areas.
EFFECTS OF WRONG HANDLING OF CHEMICALS
If chemicals are mishandled, several negative consequences can occur:
(a) On Health
 Poisoning from accidental ingestion.
 Drug resistance from misuse of antibiotics.
 Death from overdose or toxic exposure.
 Spread of diseases if expired or contaminated drugs are sold.
(b) On Society
 Increase in fake/illegal drug circulation.
 Loss of trust in health care systems.
 Drug abuse and addiction if chemicals like sedatives or narcotics are mishandled.
(c) On Business/Economy
 Revocation of license of careless vendors.
 Legal penalties such as fines, imprisonment, or shop closure.
 Financial loss due to spoilage, wastage, or confiscation of goods.
 Loss of customers as people avoid vendors known for wrong practices.
IMPORTANCE OF LICENSING CHEMICAL VENDORS
1. Ensures safety of the public by controlling who handles drugs/chemicals.
2. Prevents circulation of fake or harmful drugs.
81
3. Promotes accountability and professionalism.
4. Brings chemicals closer to rural communities.
5. Protects lives and property through safe handling guidelines.

INTRODUCTION
Financial institutions are establishments that deal with the management of money, including
accepting deposits, giving loans, and offering other financial services. Among all financial
institutions, banks are the most popular and widely used. A bank is a licensed financial
institution that receives deposits from individuals, organizations, and governments, and makes
these funds available to those who need them. Banks also provide various services that aid
commerce and general economic growth.
83
COMMERCIAL BANKS
Commercial banks are financial institutions that accept deposits from the public, provide loans,
and offer other financial services for profit. They operate under government regulations and play
a vital role in the economy by keeping money safe and ensuring that funds circulate productively.
Functions of Commercial Banks include:
1. Accepting deposits from customers.
2. Giving loans and overdrafts to individuals and businesses.
3. Providing safe-keeping for valuables like documents and jewellery.
4. Issuing debit and credit cards for transactions.
5. Facilitating payments through cheques, drafts, and electronic transfers.
6. Serving as agents for customers in the purchase of shares, bonds, and other investments.
7. Providing foreign exchange services for international trade.
BANK SERVICES
Banks provide a wide range of services to individuals, businesses, and governments. These
include:
1. Deposit Services: Customers can open savings, current, and fixed deposit accounts.
2. Loan and Credit Facilities: Banks grant personal loans, business loans, overdrafts, and
mortgages.
3. Payment Services: Banks enable customers to make payments using cheques, ATM
cards, mobile apps, and online banking platforms.
4. Money Transfer Services: Local and international money transfers can be carried out
through banks.
5. Advisory Services: Banks advise customers on investment opportunities and financial
management.
84
6. Foreign Exchange Services: Banks provide currency exchange for international
business and travel.
7. Agency Services: Banks can act on behalf of customers in buying and selling shares,
collecting dividends, and paying bills.
8. Safekeeping Services: Banks provide lockers and vaults for securing valuables.
ETHICAL ISSUES IN BANKING
Since banks handle people’s money and sensitive information, ethical conduct is very important.
Banking ethics refers to the moral principles that guide how bankers relate with customers,
colleagues, and the public.
Some ethical issues in banking include:
1. Honesty and Integrity: Bank officials must be truthful and trustworthy in dealing with
customers’ funds.
2. Confidentiality: Banks must keep customers’ financial details private and not disclose
them to unauthorized persons.
3. Fairness: Customers should be treated fairly without discrimination based on wealth,
race, gender, or status.
4. Avoiding Fraud and Corruption: Bank staff must not misuse customers’ money or
engage in fraudulent practices.
5. Transparency: Charges, interest rates, and other conditions must be clearly explained to
customers.
6. Professionalism: Bank workers should maintain good conduct, courtesy, and efficiency
in service delivery.
IMPORTANCE OF BANKS IN BUSINESS AND SOCIETY

 Provide safe custody for money and valuables.

 Provide loans that help businesses grow.
 Facilitate smooth payment systems for trade and commerce.

INTRODUCTION
Every individual is a consumer because we all buy and use goods and services daily. A
consumer is any person who purchases or uses goods and services to satisfy personal, family, or
community needs. Because consumers often deal with producers, sellers, and service providers,
there must be certain rights that protect them from exploitation. Alongside these rights,
consumers also have responsibilities to ensure fairness and balance in the marketplace.
99
MEANING OF CONSUMER RIGHTS
 Consumer rights are the legal and moral entitlements or protections that consumers have
when purchasing goods or services.
 They are designed to protect buyers against unfair practices by sellers, producers, or
service providers.
 They ensure that consumers get value for money, safety, and truthful information about
what they buy.
MEANING OF CONSUMERISM
 Consumerism refers to the social and economic movement aimed at protecting the rights
of consumers against exploitation and ensuring fair treatment in the marketplace.
 It involves:
o Advocating for laws and policies that safeguard consumers.
o Promoting awareness of consumer rights.
o Educating consumers on their responsibilities.
 Consumerism helps balance the power between producers and consumers.
THE EIGHT UNIVERSAL RIGHTS OF A CONSUMER
According to the United Nations guidelines, the following are the eight fundamental rights of
consumers:
1. Right to Safety: Protection against harmful or dangerous products and services.
2. Right to Information: Access to accurate details about goods and services to make
informed choices.
3. Right to Choose: Freedom to select from a variety of products and services at
competitive prices.
100
4. Right to Be Heard: Consumers’ complaints and opinions must be considered in policymaking
and business practices.
5. Right to Redress: Consumers should receive fair compensation, refund, or replacement
when cheated or sold faulty goods.
6. Right to Consumer Education: Consumers should be taught about their rights,
responsibilities, and how to make wise decisions.
7. Right to a Healthy Environment: Consumers are entitled to live and work in an
environment that is safe and non-threatening to health.
8. Right to Satisfaction of Basic Needs: Access to essential goods and services like food,
shelter, clothing, education, and healthcare.
RESPONSIBILITIES OF CONSUMERS
For consumer rights to be effective, consumers must also perform their responsibilities. These
include:
 Awareness: Be informed about goods and services before purchase.
 Critical Mind: Read product labels, instructions, and expiry dates carefully.
 Action: Speak up when dissatisfied with a product or service.
 Social Concern: Avoid products that harm the environment or society.
 Environmental Responsibility: Dispose of waste properly and support eco-friendly
goods.
 Honesty: Do not cheat sellers by returning used items falsely or failing to pay debts.
 Sensible Buying: Compare prices, quality, and usefulness before purchase.
 Respect for Law: Follow the rules and regulations guiding the market.

WEEK 10: SHOPPING AND AFTER-SALES SERVICES

INTRODUCTION
Shopping is the process of selecting and purchasing goods and services to satisfy our needs and
wants. Every consumer shops, whether for food, clothing, household items, or even luxury goods.
However, shopping wisely is very important because it helps individuals and families manage
limited resources (money, time, and energy).
103
Many people often confuse needs with wants, or fall victim to impulse buying, thereby wasting
money on unnecessary items. Furthermore, after buying goods, consumers are entitled to aftersales
services, which ensure the safe use, maintenance, and enjoyment of purchased products.
This lesson explains needs vs wants, decision-making in shopping, impulse buying and its
dangers, as well as after-sales services.
NEEDS VS WANTS
 Needs are basic necessities of life that are essential for survival and daily living.
o Without needs, human existence will be difficult or impossible.
o Examples:
 Food (to provide energy and growth).
 Water (for survival).
 Shelter (protection against weather).
 Clothing (covering and protection).
 Healthcare and education (for well-being and development).
 Wants are goods and services that are not essential for survival but are desired for
comfort, enjoyment, or prestige.
o Wants are unlimited, and they usually depend on personal taste, fashion, or
income level.
o Examples:
 Designer clothes and shoes.
 Luxury cars.
 Smartphones and tablets.
 Jewelry.
104
 Video games.
Key Differences between Needs and Wants:
 Needs are essential; wants are optional.
 Needs are limited; wants are unlimited.
 Needs must be satisfied first before wants.
Example: Buying rice for food is a need; buying ice cream is a want.
DECISION MAKING IN SHOPPING
Good decision making in shopping means making the right choices before and during buying.
This helps the consumer get value for money and avoid waste.
Steps/Factors to Consider in Wise Shopping:
 Plan a budget: Decide how much money you have and how to spend it.
 Make a shopping list: Write down what you need to avoid forgetting essentials.
 Check for quality: Ensure that goods are durable, safe, and standard.
 Compare prices: Visit different shops or markets before buying to get the best price.
 Buy within your income: Do not buy items beyond your financial strength.
 Check labels and expiry dates: Especially on drugs, food, and cosmetics.
 Evaluate need vs want: Ask yourself if the item is truly necessary.
 Consider alternatives: Buy substitutes when the preferred brand is too costly.
IMPULSE BUYING AND ITS EFFECTS
 Impulse Buying: This is the act of purchasing items suddenly without prior planning or
consideration. It is influenced by emotions, advertisements, discounts, or peer pressure.
105
Examples of impulse buying:
 Buying snacks while passing a roadside seller, even though you don’t need them.
 Purchasing clothes because they are “on sale,” not because you need them.
Causes of Impulse Buying:
 Lack of proper planning.
 Attractive promotions or discounts (“Buy one, get one free”).
 Peer pressure or influence.
 Emotional buying (e.g., shopping when sad or excited).
Effects of Impulse Buying:
 Waste of money on unnecessary goods.
 Neglect of essential needs.
 Regret or dissatisfaction after purchase.
 Financial difficulties due to overspending.
 Accumulation of debt if items are bought on credit.
AFTER-SALES SERVICES
After-sales services are additional benefits or support that a seller or manufacturer provides to
the buyer after purchase. They are usually offered on expensive or technical items such as cars,
electronics, machines, and gadgets.
Examples of After-Sales Services:
 Free installation (e.g., air conditioner, TV, fridge).
