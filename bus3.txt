WEEK 1: EFFECTIVE OFFICE PRACTICE
Effective office practice is essential for the smooth operation of any organization. It involves
the implementation of well-structured office procedures, systems, and routines that ensure
work is carried out efficiently and professionally. Understanding office procedures helps students
appreciate the importance of organization, discipline, and communication in business
environments.
MEANING OF OFFICE PROCEDURE:
 Office procedure refers to the established methods and steps used to handle office tasks
and activities in an organized manner.
3
 It includes record-keeping, correspondence handling, filing, communication, and
coordination of office functions.
 Office procedures are guided by rules, regulations, and standards that promote
efficiency and accountability.
Key points:
 Procedures ensure that tasks are completed in the correct sequence.
 They provide a reference framework for new employees and minimize errors.
 Office procedures can be formal (documented) or informal (practices followed routinely).
IMPORTANCE OF OFFICE PROCEDURE:
Proper office procedures are vital for the following reasons:
 Efficiency and Productivity:
o Ensures that work is done systematically and promptly, reducing wastage of
time and resources.
 Consistency:
o Standardized procedures ensure uniformity in operations, allowing predictable
and reliable results.
 Accountability:
o Clear procedures help in tracking responsibilities, making it easier to identify
errors or lapses.
 Improved Communication:
o Structured office procedures facilitate effective flow of information within and
outside the organization.
 Professionalism:
o Demonstrates discipline, orderliness, and reliability, which enhance the
organization’s reputation.
 Record Keeping and Documentation:
4
o Proper filing, documentation, and reporting ensure that important information is
accessible for future reference.
 Decision-Making:
o Organized procedures provide accurate information, which aids management in
making informed decisions.
EVALUATION:
1. Define office procedure in your own words.
2. List four reasons why office procedures are important in an organization.
3. Explain how office procedures improve communication within a business.
4. State two ways office procedures promote accountability.
ASSIGNMENT:

smooth running of a business.
2. Prepare a chart showing at least five common office procedures and their purposes.
3. Describe in two paragraphs how consistency in office procedures can enhance
professionalism and efficiency in the workplace.

WEEK 2: OFFICE PROCEDURE (PART 1)
Procedures for making payments; preparation of sales and purchases documents
INTRODUCTION
 An office procedure is a step-by-step method adopted in business organizations to carry
out activities in an orderly and efficient manner.
 Proper office procedures are important in ensuring accuracy, accountability, and
transparency in business transactions.
 Two important aspects of office procedure are:
1. Procedures for making payments.
6
2. Preparation of sales and purchases documents.
PROCEDURES FOR MAKING PAYMENTS
Payments in business are made either in cash or through non-cash methods. The procedure
ensures that money paid is recorded properly and acknowledged.
A. CASH PAYMENTS
 Steps in cash payments:
1. The cashier prepares a payment voucher stating amount, purpose, and payee.
2. The voucher is authorized by a superior officer or manager.
3. Cash is paid to the beneficiary.
4. The payee signs the voucher or receipt to acknowledge payment.
5. The transaction is recorded in the cashbook.
B. NON-CASH PAYMENTS
1. By Cheque – A cheque is written and signed by the account holder to authorize payment.
o Steps: writing the cheque → signing → presenting to bank → beneficiary cashes
or deposits it.
2. Bank Draft / Bank Transfer – Payment is made through the bank directly.
3. Electronic Payments – Using POS, online transfer, mobile banking, etc.
Importance of Proper Payment Procedures:
 Prevents fraud and financial mismanagement.
 Ensures accountability and proper record keeping.
 Helps in auditing and financial control.
 Builds trust between buyer and seller.
7
PREPARATION OF SALES AND PURCHASES DOCUMENTS
In business, when goods are sold or bought, certain documents are prepared to record the
transaction. These documents ensure proof, accountability, and accuracy.
A. SALES DOCUMENTS
1. Invoice – Sent by the seller to the buyer showing details of goods sold, quantity, unit
price, total cost, discounts, etc.
2. Receipt – Issued by the seller to the buyer as evidence that payment has been made.
3. Delivery Note – Accompanies goods delivered, showing details of items delivered.
4. Proforma Invoice – A quotation given to the buyer before goods are supplied.
5. Credit Note – Issued by the seller when goods are returned or an overcharge is corrected.
6. Debit Note – Issued to notify a buyer of undercharge or additional payment required.
B. PURCHASE DOCUMENTS
1. Purchase Order – Sent by the buyer to the seller, requesting for goods.
2. Goods Received Note (GRN) – Prepared by the buyer to confirm receipt of goods.
3. Payment Voucher – Used when payment is being made.
4. Statement of Account – A summary of transactions sent by the seller to the buyer over a
period.
STEPS IN PREPARING SALES AND PURCHASE DOCUMENTS:
 Ensure correct details of buyer and seller (names, addresses).
 Indicate date of transaction.
 Record correct description of goods (quantity, unit price, total).
 Ensure documents are signed by authorized persons.
8
 File copies systematically for future reference.
IMPORTANCE OF SALES AND PURCHASE DOCUMENTS
 Serve as legal evidence of transactions.
 Help in keeping accurate financial records.
 Facilitate auditing and accountability.
 Prevent disputes between buyer and seller.
 Ensure transparency in business transactions.
EVALUATION

4. Mention four examples of sales documents and four examples of purchase documents.

WEEK 3: OFFICE PROCEDURE (PART 2)
Imprest account – meaning, features, advantages
MEANING OF IMPREST ACCOUNT
 An Imprest Account is a type of petty cash system used in offices to take care of small,
day-to-day expenses.
 It is a method where a fixed sum of money (called the float) is given to the petty cashier
at the beginning of a period (weekly, monthly, etc.).
 The petty cashier spends from this amount on minor expenses like buying stamps, tea,
pencils, transport fares, cleaning materials, etc.
10
 At the end of the period, the cashier is reimbursed (refunded) the exact amount spent so
that the account returns to the original fixed balance.
Example:
If the imprest is ₦20,000 and the petty cashier spends ₦5,000 on transport and office supplies,
he/she will be refunded ₦5,000 so that the balance returns to ₦20,000.
This system ensures accountability, control, and proper management of petty cash in an office.
FEATURES OF AN IMPREST ACCOUNT
1. Fixed Sum (Float): A definite amount of money is set aside at the beginning of the
period.
2. Petty Cashier: A petty cashier is appointed to manage and record petty cash expenses.
3. Small/Minor Payments: It is used only for small, everyday expenses, not major
payments.
4. Reimbursement: At the end of the period, the petty cashier is reimbursed for the exact
amount spent.
5. Petty Cash Book: All transactions are recorded in a petty cash book for accountability.
6. Controlled by Main Cashier/Accountant: The main cashier supervises the petty cashier
to avoid misuse.
7. Return to Original Balance: After reimbursement, the balance always returns to the
original imprest amount.
8. Use of Vouchers: Petty cash vouchers are used as evidence for every expense made.
ADVANTAGES OF AN IMPREST ACCOUNT
1. Easy Control of Cash – Since the amount is fixed and small, it is easy to monitor and
control.
11
2. Reduces Workload of Main Cashier – The petty cashier handles minor payments,
leaving the main cashier to focus on bigger transactions.
3. Promotes Accountability – Every expense must be supported by vouchers and receipts,
making it difficult to misuse funds.
4. Saves Time – Minor payments are quickly made without disturbing the main cashier.
5. Prevents Excessive Spending – Since the float is fixed, it prevents careless or excessive
spending.
6. Simple to Operate – The system is straightforward and easy for petty cashiers to manage.
7. Helps in Budgeting – Records from the petty cash book provide information for
planning future expenses.
8. Transparency – It ensures openness, as all petty expenses are properly documented.
EVALUATION
1. What is an imprest account?
2. List five features of an imprest account.
3. State four advantages of using an imprest account in an office.
4. Who is a petty cashier, and what is his/her role in an imprest system?
5. Explain why reimbursement is necessary in an imprest system.
ASSIGNMENT
1. Define an imprest account and explain, with an example, how it operates.
2. Identify and explain six features of an imprest account.
3. Discuss five advantages of using an imprest system in an office.

WEEK 4: STORE RECORDS
Meaning of store records; delivery note; gate pass
MEANING OF STORE RECORDS
 Store records are written or electronic documents used to record details of all goods and
materials received, issued, or kept in a store.
 They provide accurate information about the quantity, type, value, and movement of
goods in and out of a store.
 Store records help management monitor stock levels, prevent shortages, losses, or theft,
and ensure accountability.
13
 They are maintained by the storekeeper or store manager in every business
organization.
 Examples of store records include stock cards, bin cards, goods received notes, delivery
notes, and gate passes.
IMPORTANCE OF STORE RECORDS
1. Stock Control: Helps track and control inventory levels to avoid overstocking or
stockouts.
2. Accountability: Provides evidence of transactions and makes staff responsible for items
in their care.
3. Prevention of Fraud and Theft: Reduces pilfering through accurate documentation.
4. Decision-Making: Helps management plan purchases, production, and sales.
5. Audit and Verification: Makes auditing easy as every stock movement is recorded.
6. Cost Control: Helps organizations know the cost of stock, manage resources efficiently,
and avoid wastage.
7. Efficient Operations: Ensures smooth running of the store and timely supply of goods.
DELIVERY NOTE
 A delivery note is a document sent by a supplier to a buyer that lists the goods being
delivered.
 It is used to confirm that items delivered match the purchase order.
 Delivery notes do not include prices but only quantities and descriptions.
 The buyer checks and signs the delivery note as proof that goods were received in good
condition.
Features of a Delivery Note:
1. Name and address of the supplier and buyer.
14
2. Date of delivery.
3. Description of goods supplied.
4. Quantity of goods supplied.
5. Signature of the recipient to confirm receipt.
6. Delivery note number for easy tracking.
Uses of a Delivery Note:
 Serves as proof of delivery.
 Helps update store records.
 Aids in reconciling orders with supplies.
 Acts as evidence in case of disputes.
GATE PASS
 A gate pass is a written authorization for goods to be moved out of or into a business
premises.
 It ensures that only authorized goods enter or leave an organization’s premises, thereby
preventing theft.
 Gate passes are usually issued by the storekeeper or security department and signed by a
superior officer.
Types of Gate Passes:
1. Goods Outward Gate Pass: Issued when goods are leaving the premises, e.g., for sales
or repairs.
2. Goods Inward Gate Pass: Issued when goods are entering the premises, e.g., deliveries
from suppliers.
Contents of a Gate Pass:
15
 Date and time of movement.
 Description of goods.
 Quantity of goods.
 Name of person moving the goods.
 Signature of authorized personnel.
Uses of a Gate Pass:
 Monitors movement of goods.
 Prevents theft and unauthorized removal of stock.
 Assists in maintaining accurate store records.
 Acts as evidence during auditing and investigations.
RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN STORE RECORDS, DELIVERY NOTE, AND GATE
PASS
 Store records track all stock movement while delivery notes confirm items supplied by
vendors.
 Gate passes provide security control and authorization for goods moving in or out.
 Together, they ensure efficient store management, accountability, and security.

WEEK 6: STOCKTAKING
Meaning of stocktaking; importance of stocktaking in business
MEANING OF STOCKTAKING
Stocktaking is the process of physically counting, recording, and valuing all the goods or items a
business has in its store, shop, or warehouse at a particular time. It involves checking the actual
quantity of goods in stock against the records in the books of account or stock register.
It is usually carried out periodically—either daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, or annually—
depending on the size of the business. In many businesses, stocktaking is done at the end of the
financial year to prepare final accounts.
18
Stocktaking is not just about counting; it is also about verifying the condition, quality, and value
of the goods available in the store.
IMPORTANCE OF STOCKTAKING IN BUSINESS
Stocktaking is very important to the growth and survival of any business. The major importance
includes the following:
1. Helps to know the actual quantity of goods in stock
Stocktaking enables the business owner or manager to know the exact amount of goods
remaining in the store. This helps in planning for replenishment and prevents
overstocking or understocking.
2. Prevents theft and fraud
Regular stocktaking discourages dishonest workers from stealing goods or engaging in
fraudulent practices since they know that stock records are checked against physical
counts.
3. Helps in detecting damaged or expired goods
During stocktaking, the business can identify goods that are damaged, expired, or
obsolete. This prevents losses and helps in replacing them with new stock.
4. Ensures accurate records
It helps to compare the physical stock with stock records in the books of account. Any
differences can be corrected immediately.
5. Assists in preparing financial statements
The closing stock value obtained from stocktaking is used in preparing the trading
account and balance sheet at the end of the financial year.
6. Helps in making decisions
Business owners can make better decisions regarding re-ordering, sales strategies, and
pricing when they have accurate stock information.
19
7. Improves customer satisfaction
By ensuring that goods are always available and not out of stock, customers are better
served, and the business maintains a good reputation.
8. Prevents overstocking and wastage
Excess stock may tie down capital or even spoil, but stocktaking ensures that the right
amount of goods is maintained in the store.
9. Helps to detect errors
Errors in recording, issuing, or receiving stock can be easily detected and corrected
through stocktaking.
SUMMARY
Stocktaking is the process of counting and recording the goods available in the store at a
particular time. It is very important in business because it ensures accuracy of records, prevents
theft and fraud, detects damaged or expired goods, and assists in decision-making and financial
reporting.
EVALUATION

2. State three reasons why stocktaking is carried out in business.
3. Mention three consequences of not carrying out stocktaking.
4. Explain how stocktaking helps in detecting fraud.
5. Why is stocktaking important in preparing financial statements?
ASSIGNMENT
1. List and explain five importance of stocktaking to a business enterprise.
20

WEEK 8: OFFICE EQUIPMENT (PART 1)
Office equipment refers to the tools, machines, and devices used in an office to facilitate the
efficient performance of tasks. They help employees carry out duties such as communication,
record-keeping, data processing, and administrative work. The use of appropriate office
equipment enhances productivity, accuracy, and organization in any office.
MEANING OF OFFICE EQUIPMENT:
 Office equipment can be defined as all physical tools and machines used in offices to
perform specific tasks more efficiently.
22
 These are different from office furniture (like chairs and desks), as equipment is directly
involved in completing office activities.
 Examples include typewriters, computers, fax machines, calculators, telephones,
photocopiers, and shredders.
TYPES OF OFFICE EQUIPMENT:
 A. Office Machines:
Machines used to process information or perform tasks faster than manual methods.
o Typewriters: For preparing written documents.
o Computers: For word processing, data storage, calculations, and communication.
o Photocopiers: To make copies of documents efficiently.
o Fax Machines: For transmitting documents over a telephone line.
o Calculators: For arithmetic operations and quick calculations.
 B. Communication Equipment:
Tools used to facilitate communication within and outside the office.
o Telephones: For voice communication.
o Intercoms: For internal office communication.
o Email Systems and Internet-enabled Devices: For electronic communication
and information sharing.
 C. Recording and Filing Equipment:
Devices used to store, retrieve, and organize office information.
o Filing Cabinets: For safe storage of files and documents.
o Shredders: For securely destroying confidential documents.
o Binders and Filing Trays: For organizing papers systematically.
23
 D. Miscellaneous Office Equipment:
Equipment that supports office work but does not fall strictly under the above categories.
o Projectors: For presentations and meetings.
o Laminators: For protecting important documents.
o Paper Cutters: For trimming papers to size efficiently.
IMPORTANCE OF OFFICE EQUIPMENT:
 Increases Efficiency: Tasks are completed faster and with less effort.
 Enhances Accuracy: Machines such as computers and calculators reduce human error.
 Improves Communication: Equipment like telephones, emails, and fax machines
connect offices locally and internationally.
 Supports Record Keeping: Filing equipment ensures documents are organized and
easily retrievable.
 Promotes Professionalism: Use of appropriate equipment presents the office as
organized and efficient.

WEEK 9: OFFICE EQUIPMENT (PART 2)
Care of office equipment; advantages and disadvantages
CARE OF OFFICE EQUIPMENT
Proper care and maintenance of office equipment is important to ensure efficiency, long life, and
reduction in cost of repairs or replacement. Some methods of care include:
1. Regular Cleaning
o Dust and dirt should be removed daily from equipment such as computers,
typewriters, photocopiers, and calculators.
o Use of soft, dry cloths or recommended cleaning agents prevents damage.
26
2. Proper Handling
o Operators should be careful in using delicate machines like printers and
computers.
o Avoid rough handling, dropping, or exposing equipment to harsh conditions.
3. Lubrication of Mechanical Equipment
o Machines with moving parts (e.g., typewriters, duplicating machines) should be
oiled or greased regularly to prevent rust and wear.
4. Protection Against Power Fluctuations
o Computers, photocopiers, and other electronic gadgets should be connected to
stabilizers, surge protectors, or UPS to avoid damage.
5. Safe Storage
o Equipment not in use should be properly covered or stored in safe conditions to
protect from dust, moisture, or theft.
6. Regular Servicing and Repairs
o Professional technicians should service machines periodically.
o Faulty equipment must be repaired immediately to prevent further damage.
7. Proper Training of Staff
o Only trained personnel should handle complex machines like computers,
projectors, and photocopiers.
8. Following Manufacturer’s Instructions
o Equipment manuals should be read and instructions followed for correct usage.
ADVANTAGES OF OFFICE EQUIPMENT
1. Increases Efficiency – office machines speed up work and reduce fatigue.
27
2. Saves Time – tasks like typing, photocopying, and calculations are completed faster.
3. Accuracy – calculators and computers reduce human errors.
4. Reduces Cost of Labour – fewer workers can perform more tasks with machines.
5. Improves Record Keeping – data can be stored safely and retrieved when needed.
6. Enhances Communication – telephones, fax machines, and emails make
communication easier.
7. Standardization of Work – documents produced by machines have uniform quality.
8. Motivates Workers – availability of modern equipment reduces stress and increases job
satisfaction.
DISADVANTAGES OF OFFICE EQUIPMENT
1. High Cost of Purchase – modern machines like computers and photocopiers are
expensive.
2. High Cost of Maintenance – regular servicing and repairs require money.
3. Unemployment – excessive use of machines may reduce the need for manual labour.
4. Dependency on Machines – workers may become too reliant on machines and less
skillful without them.
5. Breakdown of Equipment – when machines fail, work is delayed or stopped.
6. Risk of Obsolescence – modern technology becomes outdated quickly, requiring
frequent upgrades.
7. Need for Skilled Operators – lack of training may limit effective usage.
8. Electricity Dependence – electronic machines cannot function without stable power
supply.
28

WEEK 10: COMMERCE – THE HEART OF BUSINESS
Commerce is often described as the heart of business because it is the central activity around
which all other business operations revolve. While business involves production, finance,
marketing, and management, commerce links these activities by ensuring goods and services
are made available to consumers efficiently.
MEANING OF COMMERCE:
 Commerce refers to all activities involved in the distribution and exchange of goods
and services from producers to consumers.
 It includes buying, selling, transportation, storage, insurance, and banking activities that
facilitate trade.
30
 Commerce does not produce goods but enables their movement from production to
consumption.
NATURE OF COMMERCE AS THE CENTER OF BUSINESS ACTIVITIES:
 Central Role in Trade:
Commerce connects producers and consumers, making it the central link in the
business chain. Without commerce, products cannot reach the market.
 Facilitates Exchange of Goods and Services:
Commerce involves buying and selling, ensuring that goods produced are sold and that
consumers can access what they need.
 Involves Auxiliary Services:
To function effectively, commerce relies on transportation, warehousing, banking,
insurance, and advertising, which support the main trade activities.
 Encourages Specialization and Division of Labour:
By enabling goods to reach different markets, commerce allows producers to focus on
production, while traders specialize in distribution.
 Promotes Economic Growth:
Efficient commerce stimulates production, generates employment, increases revenue, and
contributes to the overall development of the economy.
 Supports Consumer Satisfaction:
Commerce ensures timely availability of goods and services, variety, and quality,
thereby enhancing consumer welfare.
IMPORTANCE OF COMMERCE IN BUSINESS:
 Bridges the gap between producers and consumers.
 Reduces wastage of goods through effective storage and transportation.
31
 Stimulates industrial and agricultural production.
 Generates employment and income.
 Encourages innovation and competitive pricing.
EVALUATION:
1. Define commerce and explain why it is called the heart of business.
2. List three auxiliary services that support commerce.
3. How does commerce promote economic growth?
4. Explain the relationship between commerce and consumer satisfaction.
ASSIGNMENT:
1. Prepare a table showing main activities of commerce, examples, and their functions.
2. Write a short essay (200 words) explaining how commerce links production and
consumption in business.
3. Draw a diagram showing the flow of goods from producers to consumers, including the
role of commerce.

WEEK 1: ADVERTISING (PART 1)
Advertising is a key aspect of business communication and marketing. It involves informing,
persuading, and reminding potential customers about products, services, or ideas to
encourage purchase or adoption. Understanding advertising helps students appreciate how
businesses promote products, influence consumer behavior, and compete in the market.
MEANING OF ADVERTISING:
 Advertising is the deliberate and paid form of communication used by businesses to
inform the public about goods, services, or ideas.
 It is aimed at creating awareness, influencing consumer choices, and increasing sales.
34
 Advertising can target existing customers, potential customers, or the general public
depending on the business strategy.
Key points:
 Advertising is different from personal selling, as it does not involve direct interaction
with the customer.
 It is paid for, meaning businesses bear the cost of producing and distributing the
advertisement.
 Advertising can be informative, persuasive, or reminder-oriented depending on its
purpose.

locations.

 Digital Advertising:

 Transit Advertising:

 Point-of-Sale Advertising:
o Displays, posters, and promotions at shops and supermarkets to influence
buyers at the point of purchase.
 Sponsorship and Publicity:
o Businesses sponsor events or activities to promote brand visibility and goodwill.

WEEK 2: ADVERTISING (PART 2)
Functions and methods of advertising
INTRODUCTION
 Advertising is the process of creating awareness about a product, service, or idea
through various media channels with the aim of influencing people’s decisions.
 It is an important element of marketing because it connects producers with consumers.
 In this lesson, we will focus on:
1. Functions of advertising.
2. Methods of advertising.
\FUNCTIONS OF ADVERTISING
Advertising performs several functions in business and society:
37
1. Creates Awareness – Makes people know about the existence of a product or service.

WEEK 6: COMMUNICATION (PART 1)
Meaning of communication; process of effective communication; hindrances to communication
MEANING OF COMMUNICATION
Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, feelings, or instructions
between two or more people in a way that the message is understood. It involves sending and
receiving messages through verbal means (spoken or written words), non-verbal means (gestures,
body language, facial expressions), or through electronic channels (telephone, email, radio,
television, etc.).
In business, communication is very important because it helps to maintain good relationships,
coordinate activities, give instructions, and achieve organizational goals. Without
communication, no business or society can function properly.
Characteristics of communication:
1. It involves at least two parties: the sender and the receiver.
2. It is a two-way process involving sending and feedback.
3. It can be verbal, non-verbal, or written.
51
4. It requires understanding between both parties.
5. It may be formal (official) or informal (casual).
PROCESS OF EFFECTIVE COMMUNICATION
For communication to be effective, it must follow a clear process. This process is often referred
to as the communication cycle:
1. Sender/Communicator – The person who has an idea or message to pass across.
2. Message – The information, instruction, idea, or feeling to be communicated.
3. Encoding – The process of putting the message into a form that can be transmitted (e.g.,
speech, writing, signs, pictures, or gestures).
4. Channel/Medium – The means by which the message is sent (e.g., face-to-face, phone
call, letter, email, social media).
5. Receiver – The person or group who receives the message.
6. Decoding – The process of interpreting or understanding the message sent.
7. Feedback – The response from the receiver to the sender, showing that the message has
been understood or needs clarification.
Effective communication only takes place when the receiver fully understands the message and
provides the appropriate feedback.
HINDRANCES TO COMMUNICATION
Despite its importance, communication can be hindered by several barriers which prevent the
accurate exchange of ideas. These barriers include:
1. Language differences – When the sender and receiver do not share a common language
or use complex/technical terms that the receiver cannot understand.
52
2. Poor listening – If the receiver does not pay attention, the message may be
misunderstood.
3. Noise – Any external distraction (e.g., loud sounds, interruptions, or background
disturbances) that prevents clear communication.
4. Distortion of message – When a message is changed or misinterpreted as it passes
through different channels.
5. Prejudice and bias – If the receiver has a negative attitude toward the sender, they may
reject the message without proper consideration.
6. Lack of clarity – When the message is not clearly expressed or organized.
7. Emotional interference – Anger, stress, or excitement can prevent proper understanding
of a message.
8. Poor communication channels – Using the wrong medium, such as sending a long
report verbally instead of in writing.
9. Cultural differences – Certain gestures, words, or behaviors may mean different things
in different cultures, leading to misunderstanding.
10. Information overload – When too much information is given at once, making it difficult
for the receiver to process and understand.
SUMMARY
Communication is the process of exchanging information between people in a way that is
understood. The communication process involves the sender, message, encoding, channel,
receiver, decoding, and feedback. Effective communication can be hindered by language barriers,
noise, poor listening, prejudice, emotional factors, and the use of wrong communication channels.

WEEK 6: CONSUMER PROTECTION (PART 3)
Complaints – meaning; justified vs unjustified complaints; steps in lodging complaints; writing a

MEANING OF COMPLAINTS
A complaint is an expression of dissatisfaction or unhappiness made by a consumer when the
goods or services received are not up to the expected standard or when the consumer’s rights are
violated. Complaints may be directed to the seller, producer, service provider, or even consumer
protection agencies.
Complaints are an important part of consumer protection because they alert businesses to
problems with their goods or services and help ensure that consumers receive fair treatment.
86
JUSTIFIED VS UNJUSTIFIED COMPLAINTS
1. Justified Complaints
These are genuine complaints made by consumers when they do not get the value for
their money. Such complaints are valid and should be addressed.
Examples:
 Buying an expired product.
 Purchasing a defective item (e.g., a phone that does not switch on).
 Being overcharged for a product or service.
 Receiving a service that is below the promised standard (e.g., poor hotel accommodation
despite paying full price).
2. Unjustified Complaints
These are complaints made by consumers that are not valid, often due to ignorance,
misuse of products, or unrealistic expectations.
Examples:
 Complaining that a product did not last forever when it was designed for limited use.
 Complaining about a service without reading or understanding the terms and conditions.
 Damaging a product through careless use and blaming the seller.
STEPS IN LODGING COMPLAINTS
When a consumer has a genuine complaint, the following steps should be taken to ensure it is
properly addressed:
1. Identify the problem – The consumer should be clear about the defect or issue with the
product or service.
87
2. Gather evidence – Keep receipts, warranty cards, packaging, or any other proof of
purchase.
3. Approach the seller – Start by reporting the problem politely to the seller or service
provider.
4. Be clear and firm – State exactly what went wrong and what remedy is expected (e.g.,
refund, repair, or replacement).
5. Escalate the complaint – If the seller does not respond, report to higher authorities such
as the Consumer Protection Council, Standards Organisation of Nigeria (SON), or other
regulatory agencies.
6. Write a formal complaint – If verbal complaints fail, write a letter of complaint stating
all necessary details.
7. Seek legal action as a last resort – If all else fails, the consumer may seek redress in a
law court.
WRITING A LETTER OF COMPLAINT
A letter of complaint is a formal letter written by a consumer to a seller, producer, or consumer
protection agency to report a problem with goods or services purchased. It must be clear, polite,
and provide all necessary information.
Features of a letter of complaint:
1. Consumer’s address and date.
2. Recipient’s address.
3. Salutation (Dear Sir/Madam).
4. Heading or subject line (e.g., Complaint about Defective Refrigerator).
5. Introduction (state the purpose of writing).
88
6. Body (describe the product/service, date of purchase, nature of the complaint, and
evidence).
7. Request for remedy (refund, replacement, or repair).
8. Conclusion (polite closing remark).
9. Signature and full name.

ABC Electronics Ltd,

Complaint about Defective Television Set

Upon installation, I discovered that the television does not come on despite several attempts. The

I therefore request that the television be replaced with a new one or a full refund made to me
within seven (7) days.

SUMMARY
A complaint is a way for consumers to express dissatisfaction with goods or services.
Complaints can be justified (valid) or unjustified (invalid). To lodge a complaint, consumers
must identify the problem, keep evidence, report politely to the seller, escalate when necessary,
and write a letter of complaint if verbal resolution fails.

100

TRADING ACCOUNT
 Meaning:
o The trading account shows the results of buying and selling goods over a period.
o It is prepared to calculate gross profit or gross loss.
 Purpose:
1. To determine the profitability of trading activities.
2. To ascertain gross profit or loss for a period.
3. To provide information for management decisions.
 Preparation:
o List sales revenue on the credit side.
o List cost of goods sold (opening stock + purchases – closing stock) on the debit

 Meaning:

 Purpose:
101
1. To determine financial performance over a period.
2. To provide information for taxation and investment decisions.
 Preparation:
0. Bring down gross profit from the trading account.
1. List all operating expenses (rent, salaries, utilities) on the debit side.
2. List all other incomes (interest received, commission earned) on the credit side.
