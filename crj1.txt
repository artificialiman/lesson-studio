WEEK 1: GOD AND HIS CREATION
Introduction to God and His Creation
Christian Religious Studies teaches that God is the supreme, eternal, and all-powerful Creator of
the universe. The Bible presents God as the origin of all life, the sustainer of the world, and the
one worthy of worship and obedience. Understanding who God is helps believers appreciate His
nature, His works, and their relationship with Him.
Who is God?
 Definition and Nature of God:
God is an eternal, infinite, and all-powerful being who exists outside the limitations of
3
time and space. He is the Creator, Preserver, and Judge of all creation. God is also loving,
just, merciful, and holy.
 Biblical References:
o Genesis 1:1 – “In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.” This
verse establishes God as the origin of all things, highlighting His power and
sovereignty.
o Jeremiah 10:12 – “He has made the earth by His power, He has established the
world by His wisdom, and has stretched out the heavens by His understanding.”
This verse emphasizes God’s wisdom, power, and authority over creation.
 Attributes of God:
o Omnipotent: God is all-powerful and capable of doing anything that is consistent
with His nature.
o Omniscient: God is all-knowing and understands all things, past, present, and
future.
o Omnipresent: God is present everywhere at all times.
o Loving and Merciful: God shows care, compassion, and forgiveness to His
creation.
o Just and Righteous: God’s actions are always fair, morally right, and perfect.
God as Creator
 God created the universe and everything within it, including the earth, sky, seas, plants,
animals, and human beings.
 Creation is a reflection of God’s power, wisdom, and creativity.
 The act of creation demonstrates that God is purposeful and intentional; nothing in the
universe exists by chance.
4
 Human beings, created in God’s image (Genesis 1:27), are unique and have the
responsibility to care for the rest of creation.
Importance of Knowing God as Creator
1. Develops Worship and Reverence: Understanding God’s role as Creator inspires praise,
worship, and obedience.
2. Encourages Responsibility: Humans are called to care for God’s creation, promoting
environmental stewardship.
3. Strengthens Faith: Knowledge of God’s power and wisdom builds trust in His guidance
and protection.
4. Provides Moral Direction: Recognizing God as Creator helps believers understand the
purpose of life and live righteously.
Summary
God is the eternal, all-powerful, and all-wise Creator of the universe. Through scripture,
particularly Genesis 1:1 and Jeremiah 10:12, believers learn about God’s nature, His attributes,
5
and His relationship with creation. Understanding God as Creator helps Christians develop
worship, reverence, responsibility, and faith in His guidance.

4. Explain why humans are considered unique in God’s creation.

WEEK 2: THE CREATION STORY
Biblical Account of Creation in Genesis 1
The creation story is found in the book of Genesis chapter 1. Genesis is the first book of the
Bible, and it begins by explaining how God created the world and everything in it. The word
Genesis means “beginning,” and it records the beginning of the world, the beginning of human
life, and the beginning of God’s relationship with man.
The creation story shows that God is the Almighty Creator who made the heavens and the earth
out of nothing by the power of His word. The story is divided into six days of creative work
and one day of rest.
The Biblical Account of Creation (Genesis 1:1–31, 2:1–3)
 Day 1: God created light. He separated the light from darkness and called the light Day
and the darkness Night.
 Day 2: God created the firmament (the sky or heaven) to separate the waters above from
the waters below.
 Day 3: God gathered the waters together to form seas and allowed dry land to appear,
which He called Earth. He also created vegetation — plants, grass, and fruit-bearing trees.
 Day 4: God created the heavenly bodies: the sun to rule the day, the moon to rule the
night, and the stars to shine.
 Day 5: God created living creatures in the waters (fishes and sea animals) and birds to fly
in the sky.
 Day 6: God created animals that live on land (domestic and wild animals). Finally, God
created man and woman in His own image and likeness. He blessed them, gave them
authority to dominate the earth, and commanded them to be fruitful and multiply.
 Day 7: God finished His work and rested. He blessed the seventh day and made it holy.
7
Lessons from the Creation Story
1. God is the Supreme Creator of everything in the universe.
2. God created everything in an orderly manner and not by accident.
3. Man is the most special of God’s creation, made in His image and likeness.
4. The creation story shows God’s love, as He provided everything man would need before
creating man.
5. God gave man responsibility to take care of the earth and its creatures.
6. The Sabbath day (seventh day) is a day of rest and should be kept holy.
8
Difference between the Creation of Man and Other Creatures
 Man was created in God’s image and likeness, unlike other creatures.
 Man was given authority to rule over the earth, while other creatures were created to
serve man.
 God personally formed man and breathed into him the breath of life, while other
creatures came into being through spoken words.
 Man was given wisdom, intelligence, and the ability to distinguish between right and
wrong.
Evaluation

3. State three things God created on the sixth day.

5. What did God do on the seventh day, and why is it important?

WEEK 3: THE CREATION OF MAN AND WOMAN
(Genesis 2:4–24)
The creation of man and woman is one of the most important events recorded in the Bible.
According to Genesis 2:4–24, God created man and woman in a special way, different from the
rest of creation. This passage helps us to understand the uniqueness of human beings, their
relationship with God, and their roles in the world.
Account of the Creation of Man (Genesis 2:7)
The Bible records that after God had created the heavens, the earth, plants, and animals, He
formed the first man, Adam, out of the dust of the ground. Then, God breathed into his nostrils
the breath of life, and Adam became a living being. This shows that man is both physical (from
dust) and spiritual (from God’s breath).
10
God placed Adam in the Garden of Eden, a beautiful garden with rivers, trees, and fruits, to live
in and take care of. He was given authority over the animals and all of God’s creation, showing
man’s responsibility as a caretaker and ruler under God’s authority.
Account of the Creation of Woman (Genesis 2:18–22)
While Adam was in the garden, God saw that it was not good for man to be alone. He needed a
companion and helper. God first brought all the animals to Adam to name them, but none was
found suitable to be his partner.
So, God caused Adam to fall into a deep sleep. While Adam slept, God took one of his ribs and
closed up the place with flesh. From the rib, God made the first woman, Eve, and brought her to
Adam.
When Adam saw her, he said:
"This is now bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called Woman, because she
was taken out of man." (Genesis 2:23)
This shows the closeness and unity between man and woman.
The Institution of Marriage (Genesis 2:24)
The passage concludes with the origin of marriage. The Bible says:
"Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they
shall become one flesh."
This means that marriage was instituted by God for companionship, unity, love, and continuity
of the human race.
Lessons from the Creation of Man and Woman
Lesson Explanation
Man is special in creation God personally formed man and breathed life into him.
11
God provides for man The Garden of Eden shows God’s care in giving man food, shelter,
and beauty.
Man has responsibilities Adam was placed in the garden to work and take care of it.
Companionship is God’s
plan
Woman was created to be a helper and companion for man.
Unity in marriage Marriage unites man and woman as “one flesh.”
Dependence on God Human beings depend on God for life, purpose, and survival.
Importance of the Creation Story
 It teaches that God is the Creator of all life.
 It shows that human beings are the highest of God’s creation.
 It explains the origin of man, woman, and marriage.
 It reveals the close relationship between man and woman.
 It emphasizes that man has work to do and must be responsible.
 It teaches that companionship and love are part of God’s plan for humanity.
Evaluation
1. From which material did God create man?
2. How did God give life to Adam?
3. Why did God decide to create a woman?
4. From what part of man was the woman created?
5. State three lessons from the creation of man and woman.

give Him glory through their lives.
6. To Enjoy God’s Blessings – God created humans to share in His goodness, love, and
abundant creation.
7. To Live in Harmony – God created man and woman to live together in love, unity, and
peace.

WEEK 6: MARRIAGE
Marriage is one of the most important institutions established by God for humanity. It is the
foundation of family life and is highly valued in both Christian teachings and society. In this
lesson, we will explore the meaning of marriage according to the Bible and the functions of
marriage as explained in the scriptures.
MEANING OF MARRIAGE (GENESIS 2:22–25)
Marriage, according to the Bible, is a sacred union between a man and a woman as ordained by
God. In Genesis 2:22–25, we learn that God created woman from the rib of man to be his
companion. After creating Eve, God brought her to Adam, and Adam recognized her as “bone of
my bones and flesh of my flesh.”
17
This passage teaches that marriage:
 Is instituted by God: It was God’s idea for man and woman to live together in love and
unity.
 Is a union of companionship: God said, “It is not good that the man should be alone,”
so He made a helper suitable for him.
 Creates a lifelong bond: Verse 24 states, “Therefore a man shall leave his father and his
mother and hold fast to his wife, and they shall become one flesh.” This shows that
marriage establishes a permanent, intimate relationship between husband and wife.
 Is based on love, trust, and mutual support: The couple is expected to live together in
harmony and faithfulness.
Thus, marriage is not just a social contract but a divine covenant blessed by God.
FUNCTIONS OF MARRIAGE (EPHESIANS 5:21–31)
In Ephesians 5:21–31, Apostle Paul explains the responsibilities and purposes of marriage for
Christians. These include:
1. Companionship and Love:
Marriage provides a partner with whom one shares life’s joys, sorrows, and challenges.
Paul teaches that husbands should love their wives as Christ loved the Church, and wives
should respect their husbands. This mutual love strengthens the bond between spouses.
2. Procreation and Continuation of Human Race:
One of the primary purposes of marriage is to bring children into the world and raise
them in a loving and godly environment.
3. Mutual Support and Care:
Spouses are to support one another physically, emotionally, and spiritually. They are to
encourage each other in faith and daily living.
18
4. Moral and Sexual Purity:
Marriage provides a proper and godly setting for sexual relations, helping to prevent
immorality and unfaithfulness.
5. Symbol of Christ’s Relationship with the Church:
Paul compares marriage to the relationship between Christ and the Church. Just as Christ
is faithful to the Church, so should a husband be faithful to his wife, and a wife to her
husband.
IMPORTANCE OF MARRIAGE TO CHRISTIANS
 It fulfills God’s plan for humanity.
 It builds strong families and societies.
 It helps individuals grow in love, patience, and responsibility.
 It reflects the love of God in human relationships.
EVALUATION:
1. What is the meaning of marriage according to Genesis 2:22–25?
2. Explain two reasons why God instituted marriage.
3. Mention three functions of marriage as taught in Ephesians 5:21–31.
4. How is Christian marriage similar to Christ’s relationship with the Church?
5. Why is marriage important in society today?

WEEK 8: MARRIAGE II
Marriage is a fundamental institution in Christian teaching, regarded as a sacred union between a
man and a woman. It serves as a foundation for family life, social stability, and spiritual growth.
Beyond its spiritual significance, marriage has social, emotional, and economic implications, and
understanding its types and importance helps students appreciate its role in both personal and
societal development.
Types of Marriage
Marriage can take various forms, reflecting cultural, religious, and legal perspectives:
1. Monogamy
Monogamy is the union of one man and one woman. It is the most widely recognized
form of marriage in Christian teachings. Monogamous marriages emphasize fidelity,
mutual respect, and shared responsibilities, fostering a stable environment for raising
children and building strong family relationships.
2. Polygamy
Polygamy involves one man marrying multiple wives. While it exists in some cultures, it
is generally not encouraged in Christian doctrine, which promotes monogamy as the ideal.
Polygamous marriages require careful management to ensure fairness, harmony, and
equitable treatment of all partners.
3. Civil Marriage
Civil marriage is conducted and recognized by the government under statutory laws. It
involves legal registration and provides legal rights and obligations for both spouses,
including inheritance, property ownership, and child custody.
4. Religious Marriage
Religious marriage is solemnized according to religious rites and ceremonies. In
Christianity, this involves vows before God and witnesses, signifying a covenantal
relationship that is spiritually binding and morally significant.
Importance of Marriage
Marriage serves several essential purposes in individual lives and society:
20
 Spiritual Significance: Marriage is a sacred covenant reflecting God’s design for
companionship, love, and mutual support. It mirrors the relationship between Christ and
the Church.
 Companionship and Emotional Support: It provides partners with emotional intimacy,
friendship, and mutual encouragement.
 Procreation and Family Life: Marriage creates a stable environment for raising children
and ensuring the continuity of society.
 Social Stability: By regulating relationships and responsibilities, marriage contributes to
order, social cohesion, and moral discipline.
 Economic Cooperation: Spouses often share resources, work together for family
sustenance, and support each other in personal and professional endeavors.
In summary, marriage is both a spiritual and social institution that requires commitment, mutual
respect, and understanding. Recognizing its types and importance equips students with
knowledge for future life, personal relationships, and responsible citizenship.

4. How does civil marriage differ from religious marriage?
5. Explain how marriage contributes to family stability.

WEEK 9: DISOBEDIENCE I
Meaning of Disobedience; Adam and Eve’s Disobedience (Genesis 3:1–19)
Meaning of Disobedience
Disobedience means the refusal to obey instructions, rules, laws, or commands given by
someone in authority. It is going against what is expected of us by God, parents, teachers, elders,
or leaders. Disobedience shows lack of respect, self-control, and regard for authority.
In the Bible, disobedience is often regarded as sin because it is a direct violation of God’s
commandments. To disobey is to choose one’s own will instead of following the will of God or
the guidance of rightful authority.
Examples of disobedience include:
 A child refusing to do what his or her parents instructed.
23
 A student refusing to follow school rules.
 A person telling lies instead of the truth.
 Adam and Eve disobeying God in the Garden of Eden.
Adam and Eve’s Disobedience (Genesis 3:1–19)
Adam and Eve were the first human beings created by God. They lived in the Garden of Eden, a
beautiful and peaceful place where they had everything they needed. God gave them the freedom
to eat from any tree in the garden except the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. God warned
them that the day they ate from that tree, they would surely die.
However, the serpent (Satan) deceived Eve. He told her that if she ate the fruit, she would not die
but would become like God, knowing good and evil. Eve listened to the serpent and took the
fruit. She ate it and gave some to Adam, and he also ate.
The moment they disobeyed God’s command, their eyes were opened, and they realized they
were naked. They felt ashamed and tried to cover themselves with leaves. When God came to the
garden, Adam and Eve hid themselves. God asked Adam why he was hiding, and Adam said it
was because he was naked. When God questioned him further, Adam blamed Eve, and Eve
blamed the serpent.
Because of their disobedience, God pronounced punishment on them:
 The serpent was cursed to crawl on its belly and eat dust.
 Enmity was placed between the serpent and the woman’s offspring.
 Eve was punished with pain in childbirth and submission to her husband.
 Adam was punished with hard labor; he would struggle to produce food from the ground.
 Finally, they were driven out of the Garden of Eden and denied access to the tree of life.
This was the beginning of human suffering, sin, and separation from God.
24
Lessons from the Disobedience of Adam and Eve
1. Disobedience brings consequences and suffering.
2. We must resist temptation no matter how attractive it looks.
3. Sin separates man from God.
4. We should take responsibility for our actions instead of blaming others.
5. God loves us but punishes sin because He is just.

3. Summarize the story of Adam and Eve’s disobedience.
4. State three punishments that came upon Adam and Eve because of their disobedience.
5. Give two lessons Christians should learn from Adam and Eve’s disobedience.

WEEK 10: DISOBEDIENCE II
The story of Adam and Eve in the book of Genesis provides the foundation for understanding
disobedience to God and its consequences. After God created Adam and Eve and placed them in
the Garden of Eden, He gave them freedom to eat from every tree except the tree of the
knowledge of good and evil. Unfortunately, Adam and Eve disobeyed God by eating the
forbidden fruit.
The consequences of Adam and Eve’s disobedience were both immediate and long-lasting.
Immediately, they became aware of their nakedness and felt shame and guilt. They lost their
innocence and could no longer enjoy perfect fellowship with God. They were driven out of the
Garden of Eden, and humanity became subject to suffering, hardship, pain, and death. Eve was
told that she would experience pain in childbirth, while Adam was condemned to labor hard to
cultivate the land for survival. Above all, their sin brought separation between humanity and God,
introducing sin into the world.
26
In addition to Adam and Eve’s disobedience, human disobedience in general continues to have
serious effects. When individuals choose to disobey God, they often face personal consequences
such as guilt, shame, lack of peace, suffering, or punishment. Disobedience also brings about a
broken relationship with God, leading to spiritual emptiness. For example, disobeying God’s
commandments by lying, stealing, or engaging in immoral behavior often results in loss of trust,
conflict, and negative outcomes in society.
On a larger scale, there is also national disobedience. This occurs when an entire nation turns
away from God’s laws and instructions. In the Bible, the people of Israel often suffered defeat,
famine, and exile because of their collective disobedience to God. In the same way, when a
country embraces corruption, injustice, immorality, and neglect of God’s will, it experiences
national problems such as poverty, insecurity, underdevelopment, war, and natural disasters.
National disobedience brings about the withdrawal of God’s blessings, leaving the nation
vulnerable to calamity and destruction.
In summary, disobedience—whether personal or collective—always leads to negative
consequences. God desires obedience because it brings blessings, peace, prosperity, and
fellowship with Him. Disobedience, on the other hand, brings suffering, punishment, and
separation from God.
Evaluation:

4. Mention two results of national disobedience.

WEEK 1: CALL TO REPENTANCE I
Introduction to Repentance
Repentance is one of the central themes in the Christian faith. It means turning away from sin
and sincerely returning to God. When a person repents, he or she feels sorrow for doing wrong,
confesses the sin before God, and makes a firm decision not to repeat it. Repentance brings
forgiveness, restores a broken relationship with God, and gives peace of mind.
The Bible constantly calls men and women to repentance, showing that sin separates people from
God, but repentance leads to reconciliation with Him. To repent is not just to feel sorry, but to
experience a complete change of heart, attitude, and behavior.
Meaning of Repentance
Repentance can be defined as the act of feeling genuine sorrow for one’s sins, confessing them,
seeking forgiveness, and turning away from them to live a new life that pleases God. It is both an
inward change of the heart and an outward change of conduct.
Repentance involves three main steps:
29
1. Realization of Sin: A person must recognize that he or she has done wrong and fallen
short of God’s standards.
2. Confession of Sin: The person openly admits the sin to God and, where necessary, to
others wronged.
3. Turning Away from Sin: Repentance is complete only when the individual forsakes
sinful habits and chooses to live in obedience to God.
Repentance is important because it leads to forgiveness, cleanses the conscience, restores peace
with God, and helps one to grow in holiness.
King David’s Repentance (2 Samuel 12:7–13; Psalm 51)
King David was a man after God’s heart, but he sinned greatly when he committed adultery with
Bathsheba and arranged for her husband, Uriah, to be killed in battle (2 Samuel 11). This act
displeased God, and He sent the prophet Nathan to rebuke David.
 Nathan’s Message (2 Sam. 12:7–13): Nathan told David a parable that revealed his sin,
and when David realized the gravity of his offense, he admitted his guilt. David said, “I
have sinned against the Lord.” This was his moment of repentance. God forgave David,
although he still faced consequences for his actions.
 David’s Prayer of Repentance (Psalm 51): In this psalm, David poured out his heart to
God, asking for mercy and forgiveness. He acknowledged his sins, expressed deep
sorrow, and begged God to cleanse him, create a pure heart within him, and restore the
joy of salvation. This prayer is a classic example of true repentance.
David’s repentance teaches that:
 No matter how great a sin is, God forgives when one repents sincerely.
 Repentance must come from the heart, not just from fear of punishment.
 A repentant person should desire renewal and transformation of life, not just forgiveness.
30
Lessons from David’s Repentance
1. Everyone is capable of sin, no matter how close they are to God.
2. God is merciful and ready to forgive anyone who repents genuinely.
3. Repentance requires humility and honesty before God.
4. Sin has consequences, but repentance brings restoration and peace.
5. A true repentant heart desires to live righteously and avoid repeating past mistakes.
Summary
Repentance means turning away from sin and returning to God with a sincere heart. King
David’s story shows that even great leaders can fall into sin, but God’s mercy is available when
they repent. David’s prayer in Psalm 51 remains a model for Christians today, reminding us that
genuine repentance involves confession, sorrow for sin, and a desire for renewal.

WEEK 2: CALL TO REPENTANCE II
The People of Nineveh Repent (Jonah 3:1–10)
The call to repentance is an important theme in the Bible. It shows that although God hates sin,
He is merciful to forgive anyone who turns away from their evil deeds. One of the best examples
of repentance is found in the story of Jonah and the people of Nineveh.
Background of the Story:
Nineveh was the capital city of Assyria, a very powerful empire in Old Testament times. The
people of Nineveh were known for their wickedness, cruelty, violence, and idolatry. Their sins
were so great that they attracted God’s judgment. God wanted to destroy Nineveh, but in His
mercy, He decided to first give them a chance to repent.
God commanded Jonah, a Hebrew prophet, to go to Nineveh and warn the people of their
coming destruction. At first, Jonah disobeyed and tried to run away to Tarshish. But after God
disciplined him by allowing him to be swallowed by a great fish for three days and nights, Jonah
prayed and repented. The fish vomited him out, and he finally obeyed God’s command to go to
Nineveh.
33
Jonah’s Preaching in Nineveh:
When Jonah entered the great city of Nineveh, he began to preach God’s message, walking
around the city and crying out:
“Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!”
This was a simple but powerful warning. It meant that within forty days, if they did not change
their ways, Nineveh would be destroyed by God.
The Response of the People of Nineveh:
The people of Nineveh responded in a surprising and sincere way:
 They immediately believed the message of God through Jonah.
 They declared a fast, refusing to eat or drink as a sign of humility and sorrow for their
sins.
 They put on sackcloth, which was a rough and uncomfortable garment worn to show
repentance.
 Everyone, from the greatest to the least, participated in this act of repentance.
The King’s Example:
The king of Nineveh set the best example. When he heard Jonah’s message:
 He rose from his throne and removed his royal robe, showing humility before God.
 He put on sackcloth and sat in ashes, a symbol of sorrow and repentance.
 He proclaimed a decree that no person or animal should eat or drink until they had cried
out to God.
 He commanded everyone to turn away from their evil ways and violence, hoping that
God would have mercy on them.
God’s Response to Nineveh:
God saw the sincerity of their repentance. He noticed that they were not only fasting and praying
but also turning away from their wicked behavior. Because of this, God showed mercy to them.
He did not destroy Nineveh as He had threatened.
34
This shows us the nature of God. He is holy and just, but He is also loving and merciful. He
punishes sin, but He forgives those who repent sincerely.
Lessons from the Repentance of the Ninevites
1. God gives warnings before judgment. He does not punish immediately but gives
sinners an opportunity to repent.
2. Repentance must be genuine. It involves not only saying “sorry” but also forsaking
sinful actions and changing one’s behavior.
3. Leadership matters. The king of Nineveh humbled himself and led his people by
example, showing that leaders must guide their subjects in righteousness.
4. Prayer and fasting are powerful tools. When combined with a sincere heart, they can
bring forgiveness and deliverance.
5. God’s mercy is for everyone. It does not matter how sinful or wicked people have been;
if they repent, God is willing to forgive.
6. Judgment can be delayed or cancelled. God postponed the destruction of Nineveh
because of their repentance, proving that our actions can influence God’s decision.
7. Repentance brings peace and restoration. Nineveh was saved from destruction and
given another chance because they humbled themselves before God.
Differences Between Remorse and Repentance
 Remorse means feeling sorry or guilty after committing a sin, but without necessarily
changing one’s behavior.
 Repentance means feeling sorry for sin and deciding to completely turn away from it,
never to return.
The Ninevites did not only feel remorse; they repented by abandoning their evil ways.

WEEK 6: CALL TO OBEDIENCE I
The call to obedience is one of the most important themes in the Bible. God requires His children
to listen to His voice and carry out His instructions completely and faithfully. Without obedience,
it is impossible to walk with God. Disobedience separates man from God, while obedience
attracts His blessings, protection, and favor. In this lesson, we will study the meaning of
obedience and learn from the example of Abraham, who obeyed God’s call in Genesis 12:1–9.
MEANING OF OBEDIENCE
Obedience means doing what one is told to do, following rules, laws, or instructions given by
someone in authority. In the Christian sense, obedience is hearing God’s word and acting on it
without argument or hesitation. It is not partial compliance but total submission to the will of
God.
46
For example, when God gives a command in the Bible, the believer is expected to obey promptly
and without excuses. Failure to obey leads to sin, disappointment, and sometimes punishment.
On the other hand, obedience brings blessings, promotion, and a closer relationship with God.
Obedience to God therefore involves three major elements:
1. Listening: Paying attention to God’s word through the Bible, prayers, and the teachings
of spiritual leaders.
2. Trusting: Believing that God’s instructions are for our good, even when they seem
difficult or do not make sense immediately.
3. Acting: Taking practical steps to carry out what God has commanded fully, willingly,
and faithfully.
Disobedience often arises from pride, fear, doubt, or lack of faith. However, true obedience is
proof of love for God, as Jesus Himself said, “If you love me, keep my commandments” (John
14:15).
ABRAHAM OBEYS GOD’S CALL (GENESIS 12:1–9)
Abraham, then known as Abram, lived in Haran with his family. God appeared to him and gave
him a special instruction that would change his life forever.
1. The Call:
God commanded Abraham to leave his country, his relatives, and his father’s house and go to a
land that He would show him. The instruction was not an easy one because it meant leaving
behind his comfort zone, his culture, his people, and the things he was familiar with.
2. The Promise:
God gave Abraham several promises if he obeyed. These included:
 God would make him into a great nation.
 God would bless him and make his name great.
 God would make him a blessing to others.
47
 Whoever blessed Abraham would be blessed, and whoever cursed him would be cursed.
 Through him, all the families of the earth would be blessed.
These promises showed that obedience to God always comes with reward.
3. The Response:
Abraham did not argue with God. He did not delay or complain. He trusted God’s words and
immediately obeyed. He took his wife Sarai, his nephew Lot, and all their possessions and left
Haran to begin the journey to the land of Canaan.
4. Worship:
When Abraham reached the land of Canaan, he built altars to the Lord and called on His name.
This act showed his faith and gratitude to God. It also revealed that Abraham was not only
physically obedient in moving to a new land but also spiritually devoted to God in worship.
IMPORTANCE AND LESSONS FROM ABRAHAM’S OBEDIENCE
1. Faith in God: Abraham’s obedience shows that faith and obedience go hand in hand.
True faith in God is demonstrated by obedience to His instructions.
2. Sacrifice is sometimes required: Abraham had to leave his comfort zone, family ties,
and security to follow God’s command. Obedience often demands sacrifice.
3. Obedience brings blessings: Abraham’s life proves that when we obey God, He rewards
us with blessings beyond our imagination. Abraham later became the father of many
nations, just as God had promised.
4. God’s promises are sure: Even though Abraham did not see immediate results, God’s
promises to him were fulfilled. This teaches that God always keeps His word.
5. Obedience influences others positively: Through Abraham, all the nations of the earth
were blessed. Our obedience can have a positive impact on our family, community, and
even generations after us.
6. Obedience strengthens relationship with God: Each act of obedience deepens our trust
in God and strengthens our walk with Him.

 Christians must learn to obey God even when it is not convenient, because God knows

WEEK 8: CALL TO OBEDIENCE II
Obedience is a key virtue in Christian life. It means total submission to God’s word, commands,
and instructions without resistance or questioning. Obedience requires trust, humility, and faith.
The life of Abraham, known as the "Father of Faith," shows that obedience to God, even when
it is difficult, brings blessings, covenant relationships, and divine favor. In this lesson, we will
study Abraham’s blessings and his sacrifice of Isaac, two remarkable events that teach us that
obeying God is always rewarding, even if it involves sacrifice or delay.
Abraham’s Blessings (Genesis 17:1–21)
When Abraham was ninety-nine years old, God appeared to him and renewed His covenant. God
commanded Abraham to “walk before Me and be blameless,” meaning he should live a life of
righteousness and total obedience. In return, God made promises that are known today as
Abraham’s blessings.
50
1. Covenant of Nations: God changed Abram’s name to Abraham, meaning "father of
many nations," to signify the fulfillment of His promise. This shows that Abraham’s
descendants would become great nations, spiritually and physically.
2. Blessing of Descendants: God promised that kings and nations would come from
Abraham’s lineage. Despite Abraham and Sarah’s old age, God promised Sarah would
give birth to a son, Isaac, through whom the covenant would continue.
3. Everlasting Covenant: The agreement was not just for Abraham, but for his descendants
forever. This was a covenant of relationship, protection, and divine favor.
4. The Land of Canaan: God promised to give Abraham and his descendants the land of
Canaan as an everlasting possession.
5. The Sign of Circumcision: God instructed Abraham that every male among his
descendants must be circumcised as a permanent sign of the covenant. Circumcision
symbolized separation, holiness, and commitment to God’s command.
Through these blessings, we see that obedience to God brings both spiritual and physical rewards
that extend to generations yet unborn. Abraham’s life teaches that God fulfills His promises at
His appointed time, and our responsibility is to remain faithful and obedient.
Abraham’s Sacrifice of Isaac (Genesis 21:1–8; 22:1–19)
The greatest test of Abraham’s obedience came when God asked him to sacrifice Isaac, the child
of promise. This event teaches deep lessons about faith, obedience, and God’s provision.
1. The Birth of Isaac (Genesis 21:1–8):
God kept His word, and Sarah gave birth to Isaac in her old age. Isaac’s birth was a
miracle and a sign that God’s promises never fail, no matter how long it takes. Isaac was
dearly loved by Abraham and Sarah because he was the son of promise.
2. The Test of Faith (Genesis 22:1–10):
God commanded Abraham to sacrifice Isaac on Mount Moriah. This command tested
Abraham’s loyalty. Despite the pain and difficulty, Abraham obeyed without hesitation
51
because he trusted God completely. His willingness to give up his only son shows that
true obedience may demand sacrifice.
3. Divine Intervention (Genesis 22:11–14):
As Abraham was about to sacrifice Isaac, God stopped him and provided a ram as a
substitute offering. This act shows God’s mercy and provision. Abraham named the place
Jehovah Jireh, meaning “The Lord will provide.”
4. Renewal of the Blessing (Genesis 22:15–19):
Because Abraham obeyed, God renewed His promises and assured him that his
descendants would be as numerous as the stars and the sand by the sea. Nations of the
earth would be blessed through his seed.
This story foreshadows God’s sacrifice of His only Son, Jesus Christ, for the salvation of
humanity. Just as Abraham trusted God with Isaac, Christians are called to trust God with their
lives and obey Him without reservation.
Lessons on Obedience from Abraham
 Obedience requires faith: Abraham trusted God’s word, even when it seemed
impossible.
 Obedience may require sacrifice: Abraham was ready to give up his most precious
possession for God.
 Obedience leads to blessings: Abraham’s faithfulness brought generational blessings.
 God provides for the obedient: Just as He provided a ram for Abraham, He always
makes a way for those who trust Him.
 Obedience glorifies God: By obeying, Abraham honored God, and his story continues to
inspire believers today.

Meaning of Service
Service means working for the benefit of others, contributing positively to their welfare, and

In Christian teaching, service is not only about helping others but also about doing God’s will.

but to serve (Mark 10:45).
Service can be expressed in different ways:
 Serving God by obeying His commandments and living righteously.
 Serving parents, teachers, and elders by showing obedience and respect.
 Serving the community by contributing to peace, cleanliness, and development.
 Serving the nation by being patriotic, honest, and hardworking.

Joseph as an Example of Service (Genesis 37:12–36; 41:37–57)

 Reward for service: Ruth’s faithfulness and service brought blessings. She eventually

Meaning of Family
The family is the smallest unit of society. It is made up of people who are related by blood,
marriage, or adoption. It is within the family that individuals first learn values, morals, love,
respect, and responsibility. The family is the foundation of society because it trains and nurtures
children who will later become members of the larger community.
In the Bible, God created the first family when He made Adam and Eve in the Garden of Eden
(Genesis 2:18–25). From them came children and descendants who spread across the earth. God
designed the family to be a place of love, support, and guidance.
62
Types of Family
There are two major types of families:
1. Nuclear Family
o This consists of the father, mother, and their children.
o It is the immediate or basic family unit.
o Example: A man, his wife, and their children living together in one household.
2. Extended Family
o This includes the nuclear family plus other relatives such as grandparents, uncles,
aunts, cousins, nephews, and nieces.
o In African societies, the extended family is very common because it strengthens
bonds and provides support to members.
Other forms of family can also be recognized in society, such as:
 Single-Parent Family: A family where either the mother or father takes care of the
children due to death, divorce, or separation.
 Polygamous Family: A family where a man has more than one wife or, less commonly,
a woman has more than one husband.
Recognition of Family Members
Recognizing family members means knowing and identifying the different people who make up
the family and understanding their roles.
 Father: Head of the family, protector, provider, and guide.
 Mother: Supporter, caretaker, nurturer, and teacher of moral and spiritual values.
 Children: Offspring of the parents who are expected to love, respect, and obey their
parents and contribute to family unity.
 Extended Members (grandparents, uncles, aunts, cousins, etc.): They give advice,
guidance, and support.
63

example:
 Children must honor their parents as commanded in Exodus 20:12.
 Parents should love and care for their children (Ephesians 6:4).
 Members of the extended family should support one another in times of need.
Importance of Recognizing Family Members
1. It strengthens love, unity, and peace within the family.
2. It ensures order, respect, and discipline in the home.
3. It helps children to know their identity and heritage.
4. It promotes care and support among relatives.
5. It reflects God’s plan for harmony and fellowship in human society.
Biblical Teachings about the Family

Human beings are created to live together and to relate with one another. A school is a

communication easier, while choosing friends wisely helps us to live peacefully, avoid trouble,
and achieve success in school.
74

inconvenient times, to help others.

especially when they are in need.
 Persistence and Understanding: The passage also teaches that friends should be patient
and persistent in helping each other.

Him. Thus, friendship also reflects God’s love.

2. Trust and Loyalty: Friendship is built on trust and faithfulness, not betrayal.
3. Sacrifice: A friend should be willing to give up something valuable for the sake of the

4. Support in Times of Trouble: True friends stand by each other in difficult times.
5. Generosity: Friendship involves giving, sharing, and meeting each other’s needs.
6. Godliness: Christian friendship should honor God and help each person grow spiritually

Community Leaders
Every community is made up of different people who live, work, and interact together. For peace

people. These leaders are responsible for maintaining law and order, ensuring justice, and
promoting unity within the community.
Community leaders are individuals who occupy positions of authority and respect within the
society. They may be traditional, political, or religious leaders, and each plays a special role in
the life of the community.
 Traditional leaders: These include kings, chiefs, elders, and family heads who preserve
the customs, traditions, and cultural heritage of the people. They often settle disputes,
maintain peace, and serve as custodians of the land.
86
 Political leaders: These include elected representatives such as councillors, local
government chairmen, governors, and other officials. They make policies, provide
infrastructure, and ensure good governance.
 Religious leaders: These are pastors, priests, imams, and other spiritual heads who guide
people morally and spiritually. They teach values such as honesty, kindness, obedience,
and love.
 Other leaders in the community: Teachers, heads of associations, and youth leaders
also contribute to guiding, training, and shaping the younger generation.
Community leaders are expected to lead by example, be honest, and seek the welfare of the
people under them.

teaches Christians to “love your neighbour as yourself” (Mark 12:31), which is the foundation
for sustaining community life.

one another regardless of age, tribe, or religion.
2. Obedience to community leaders – Members should obey laws, rules, and regulations
made for the good of the community.
3. Peaceful co-existence – Avoiding quarrels, violence, and fighting helps to maintain
harmony. Conflicts should be resolved peacefully.
4. Cooperation and participation – Members should take part in community development
projects such as sanitation, building roads, schools, and health centres.
5. Honesty and trustworthiness – People should keep promises, avoid stealing, and be
truthful in their dealings with one another.

6. Care for the needy – The poor, widows, orphans, and the elderly should be cared for
through collective support.
7. Tolerance – Members should learn to tolerate differences in opinion, belief, and

moral and spiritual values to ensure lasting peace.
A community where relationships are sustained through these values will enjoy peace, progress,
and God’s blessings.

RELATIONSHIP IN THE CHURCH
Relationship in the church refers to how members of the Christian community interact with one
another and with God. A good relationship in the church is based on love, respect, service,
forgiveness, and obedience to God’s word.
Ways Christians relate in the church include:
1. Worshipping Together: Singing, praying, and praising God in unity.
2. Studying the Word of God: Listening to sermons, attending Bible study, and Sunday
school classes.
3. Praying for One Another: Supporting each other in times of need through intercession.
4. Showing Love and Care: Visiting the sick, giving to the poor, and comforting the
sorrowful.
5. Working Together in Service: Participating in church activities like choir, ushers,
drama group, or evangelism.
6. Obeying Leaders: Respecting pastors, elders, and other leaders God has placed in
authority.
7. Forgiving One Another: Avoiding quarrels, gossip, and divisions, and living in peace
with everyone.
A healthy church relationship makes the congregation a family of believers, where everyone
feels welcomed and accepted.
IMPORTANCE OF FELLOWSHIP AND UNITY
The word fellowship means coming together to share in worship, prayers, and Christian love.
Unity means oneness and agreement among church members. Both are very important for the
life of the church.
1. Strengthens the Faith of Believers: Fellowship helps members encourage one another
and grow spiritually.
90
2. Creates Love and Care Among Members: Unity makes believers support one another
in times of joy or sorrow.
3. Promotes Peace in the Church: When there is unity, there will be no quarrels, divisions,
or hatred.
4. Helps the Church to Grow: Just as in Acts 2, unity and fellowship attract outsiders who
want to experience the love of Christ.
5. Encourages Sharing of Resources: Members can share food, money, and skills to help
one another.
6. Shows the World the Love of Christ: Jesus prayed in John 17:21 that His followers
may be one, so that the world will know that God sent Him. Unity makes the church a
living witness of God’s love.
7. Brings God’s Blessings: Psalm 133:1–3 says that where brethren dwell together in unity,
the Lord commands His blessings.
BIBLICAL CONNECTIONS
 Acts 2:42–47: The early church devoted themselves to teaching, fellowship, breaking of
bread, and prayers.
 Hebrews 10:25: Christians are encouraged not to neglect meeting together but to
encourage one another.
 John 17:21: Jesus prayed for unity among believers.
 Psalm 133:1: “Behold, how good and pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in
unity.”
These passages show that fellowship and unity are God’s will for His church.
